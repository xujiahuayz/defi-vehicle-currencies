- Scientific: The appendix extends a study of how product-portfolio life cycles relate to investment, leverage, cash, valuation, and product introductions. Measurement work defines alternative portfolio age (pp. 1–3), effective portfolio size as inverse product-revenue concentration (p. 5, Eq. (1)), and net product entry as entry less exit (p. 6, Eq. (2)); the latter uses future withdrawal status and excludes the first and last two sample years (p. 6 n. 1). Figs. A.2–A.4 provide descriptive or residualized local-polynomial relationships, not causal identification, and the authors expressly acknowledge contamination by other firm characteristics (p. 5). The alternative age proxy is only moderately correlated with the baseline measure, 0.478 (p. 2). The structural mechanism combines costly product introduction, declining old-product revenue, product exit, costly external finance, tax shields, and precautionary preservation of debt capacity: product introduction has an inaction region, investment generally falls with portfolio age, and leverage falls as portfolios age (pp. 10–12, Fig. B.1). The Euler-equation derivation separates ordinary marginal benefits and costs from a product-portfolio term (pp. 9–10, Eqs. (5)–(13)). Estimation uses simulated method of moments with operating-profit, investment, net-leverage, and old-product-share moments; local sensitivities are reported in Table C.1, but are thresholded at 0.4, local, and acknowledged to depend on numerical gradients (pp. 13–14). The alternative-proxy estimates are qualitatively similar, but model fit is materially imperfect for leverage persistence and several age/profit moments (Table C.2, p. 19: t-statistics 4.283, −2.335, −2.477, and −2.274). Cash-as-negative-debt is examined through selected subsamples rather than a model allowing separate cash and debt choices; the assertion that parameters are statistically indistinguishable is not accompanied by formal cross-subsample tests (pp. 14–15, Table C.3). Tables C.5–C.12 show extensive heterogeneity estimates but omit difference tests and, unlike Table C.2, omit moment-difference t-statistics. The reported 20–22% contribution of product dynamics to investment and leverage variance uses Type III partial sums of squares without a displayed table or uncertainty interval (pp. 12–13 n. 2). External validity is limited by retail-product coverage and a sample characterized as primarily large, profitable, old firms (p. 15).
- Structure: Page 1 supplies the appendix roadmap. Internet Appendix A first tests an alternative age measure (pp. 1–2, Fig. A.1), then alternative product and age definitions (pp. 2–3, Fig. A.2), develops age, size, and adjustment measures and stylized facts (pp. 2–7, Eqs. (1)–(2), Fig. A.3), and distinguishes product age from firm age (p. 7, Fig. A.4). Internet Appendix B constructs the product-state transition matrix (p. 8, Eqs. (3)–(4)), derives the investment Euler equation (pp. 9–10, Eqs. (5)–(13)), and interprets numerical policy functions and a variance decomposition (pp. 10–13, Fig. B.1). Internet Appendix C discusses local-identification diagnostics, alternative-age estimation, cash/debt treatment, transition probabilities, and eight sample splits (pp. 13–16); references appear on p. 17 and Tables C.1–C.12 occupy pp. 18–29.
- Depth: Effort is concentrated in structural-estimation reporting: twelve pages of diagnostic, fit, parameter, transition, and subsample tables (pp. 18–29). Measurement receives useful definitions and graphical robustness checks (pp. 1–7), although the alternative age measure relies on a 16-quarter lifespan assumption and its validation is chiefly distributional and qualitative. Analytical depth is moderate: the Euler equation is derived explicitly (pp. 9–10), but only two of the stated 32 transition cases are shown, old-to-new transitions are temporarily allowed contrary to the main specification, and boundary-grid normalization robustness is asserted without an exhibit (p. 8). Identification analysis is local rather than global, and the many subsample estimations provide breadth of output without formal parameter-difference inference.
- Breadth: The supplement covers portfolio age, effective size, entry and exit, firm age, product durability, product variety, between- and within-firm concentration, uniqueness proxies, revenue growth, cash/debt composition, volatility, and time periods (pp. 1–7, 14–16, Tables C.3–C.12). It considers complementarity or substitution between capital investment and product introductions, market-power interpretations, fixed introduction costs, revenue decay, tax shields, external-financing costs, and precautionary debt capacity (pp. 6–7, 10–12). Alternatives are acknowledged—firm age (p. 7), separate cash and debt (pp. 14–15), proxy construction (pp. 1–3), and transition normalization (p. 8)—but competitive explanations and selection behind cross-sectional splits are not discriminated causally.
- Optics: The title precisely labels the artifact as an Internet Appendix, and the opening paragraph gives an efficient three-part roadmap (p. 1); there is no abstract or standalone statement of the parent paper’s contribution. The exhibit ladder is coherent—measurement figures, model policy functions, identification diagnostics, then robustness and heterogeneity tables—and most captions identify variables, estimators, panels, and parameters. Claim calibration is mixed: explicit caveats about confounding, local sensitivities, numerical gradients, and sample composition strengthen credibility (pp. 5, 13–15), while “qualitatively similar,” normalization robustness, statistical indistinguishability, and the 20–22% variance contribution are stated more strongly than their displayed inference supports (pp. 8, 12–15). Cross-references to absent main-paper figures, equations, and tables impede standalone auditability, and `[Table C.x about here.]` placeholders on pp. 13–16 make the file read like a production-stage manuscript despite the tables appearing on pp. 18–29. Equations are compact and interpretable, but the artifact contains visible copyediting defects and cramped notation. Page allocation is disciplined: empirical extensions pp. 1–7, model details pp. 8–13, estimation narrative pp. 13–16, references p. 17, and tables pp. 18–29.

- Implication: The appendix materially improves auditability of measurement choices, model mechanics, local identification, and heterogeneity, but it supports robustness and structural consistency rather than causal identification. The moderate correlation between age proxies, visible moment misfit, local and thresholded sensitivity diagnostics, absent cross-subsample tests, and unreported uncertainty for the variance decomposition require narrower interpretation than several narrative claims suggest. The parent paper must be read before its central contribution, baseline results, attribution, or JFE-style publication execution can be closed.
