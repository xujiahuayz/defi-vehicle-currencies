# The Making of Dominant Vehicle Currencies: scientific framing

## 0. The finding this framing must earn

On exact reconstructed decentralised-exchange routes over 2024 to 2026, the vehicle-currency role, the position an asset occupies when a trade routes *through* it without wanting to hold it, rotates from the native platform asset (ETH) toward fiat-reserve stable numéraires (USDT and USDC). The sharp measure is the vehicle excess-use ratio: an asset's share of intermediary legs net of its own endpoint (source-or-sink) demand. In 2026 the ratio is 1.41 by count and 1.19 by strict-support value for stable assets, against 0.77 and 0.68 for native. USDT is the transition margin: its count ratio moves 1.06 to 1.23 and its strict-value ratio 0.59 to 1.42 across the window, and its 120-day rolling value ratio last sits below one on 2025-04-11. The counterfactual, every alternative route available at the same reconstructed state, is observed, and the router that chose the realised route keeps no memory between blocks.

Three properties of this setting are what make the finding a contribution and not a description: the counterfactual route is *observed and priced*, not inferred; the intermediary role is *separated from endpoint demand* on the same routes; and user-side habit is *mechanically absent* because deterministic routers re-optimise each block. Sections 1 to 4 establish what the literature predicts, what those three properties let this paper establish that the FX literature structurally cannot, the strongest rival and the design's answer, and the novel measures this data enables.

---

## 1. The mechanism the literature predicts for vehicle emergence and rotation

The theory of why one asset becomes the object others route through rests on three forces. They are analytically distinct, they are confounded in every prior empirical setting, and they make *different* predictions about which asset should carry the vehicle role and about how a rotation should look. This section states each with its exact textual claim and its DeFi prediction.

### 1.1 The thick-market (volume-cost) externality: Krugman 1980

Krugman's mechanism is a decreasing-returns cost that a currency's own use drives down. He assumes "that transaction costs as a proportion of the transaction are decreasing in the volume of transactions" (`1980-Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies…txt` L324-325), and is explicit that this is "economies of market size, not … economies to large transactions, which are assumed away" (L331-332). The feedback is self-validating: once "the currency of an economically dominant country becomes established as a vehicle … This role will be self-reinforcing, swelling transactions in the currency. Even if the country's predominant position then vanishes, its currency's special role may then persist" (L459-462). This is the source of Krugman's own excess-use intuition, which the project's excess-use ratio operationalises but does not attribute as his statistic: the vehicle "enters into more transactions than [its] role in world payments would by itself justify" (L296-297).

Krugman also supplies the shape of displacement. A vehicle can lose its role, and when it does the loss is not gradual: "When change comes, however, it will be 'catastrophic' in the formal sense. Once a currency begins to lose its role as vehicle, there will be a cumulative process in which declining trading volume leads to higher transaction costs and higher transaction costs lead to lower trading volume" (L464-467).

- **Prediction for DeFi.** The vehicle is whichever asset carries the deepest, most-connected exchange volume, because depth is what lowers the cost of routing through it. At the start of the window that asset is the native one: WETH "appears in 99,863 of 105,098 pools, or 95.02%" (Lehar-Parlour, per `literature-audit.md` L530). A rotation toward a stable numéraire is predicted only once that numéraire comes to carry the routing volume that drives its cost down. The transition, if it occurs, should be cumulative and capable of tipping abruptly, and the mechanism is asset-specific volume, not any property of the router. The catastrophic-displacement claim is directly testable on the USDT margin, where the observed crossing is cumulative but noisy, not a clean discontinuity.

### 1.2 Coordination and strategic complementarity: Kiyotaki-Wright 1989, Gopinath et al. 2020, Gopinath-Stein 2021, Amiti-Itskhoki-Konings 2022, Mukhin 2022

The second force is that an asset is used as a medium because others use it, so the choice is a coordination equilibrium with possible multiplicity. Kiyotaki and Wright give the pure form: in their speculative equilibrium "agents trade a lower- for a higher-storage-cost commodity not because they wish to consume it, but because they rationally expect that this is the best way to ultimately trade for another good that they … want to consume, that is, because it is more marketable" (`1989-KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium…txt` L425-429). This produces an object "being used as a medium of exchange in spite of the fact that it is dominated in rate of return by another object" (L560-562), and the equilibrium is belief-selected: two configurations "coexist … depending solely on extrinsic beliefs and in spite of the fact that fundamentals suggest that good 1 is a much better medium of exchange" (L646-650). Lagos and Wright (2005) supply the modern search-theoretic foundation that makes this medium-of-exchange role "essential" and tractable (`2005-LagosWright2005Unified…txt` L15, L162).

The international-macro literature turns the same complementarity into a theory of a single dominant currency. Gopinath et al. (2020) name "strategic complementarities in pricing" as a defining feature of the dominant-currency paradigm and document that "the vast majority of trade is invoiced in a small number of 'dominant currencies,' with the US dollar playing an outsized role" (`2020-GopinathEtAl2020DCP…txt` L11-13, L77-78); their complementarity governs markup setting and takes dominance as given. Gopinath and Stein (2021) supply the "making of" mechanism: "a currency's role as a unit of account for invoicing decisions is complementary to its role as a safe store of value … this complementarity can lead to the emergence of a single dominant currency … even when multiple large candidate countries share similar economic fundamentals" (`2021-GopinathStein2021Making…txt` L9-17). The loop "feeds on itself" and "can entrench the dollar as the dominant currency … even if there is initially no fundamental difference between the two" (L1291-1299), while which currency wins "is indeterminate" absent history (L1670-1675). Amiti, Itskhoki and Konings (2022) confirm the complementarity at the firm level: "the currency used by the firm's competitors has a strong effect on the firm's own currency choice" (`2022-AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant…txt` L150-152), quantified as "a 10 percentage point increase in the incidence of foreign-currency pricing by competitors increases the likelihood of a given firm to adopt foreign-currency invoicing by 5.4 percentage points" (L1701-1704). Mukhin (2022) closes the system: complementarities make "exporters coordinate on the same currency of invoicing" and "can also generate multiple equilibria despite unique optimal choice at the firm level" (`2022-Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem…txt` L13-16, L628-631), producing path dependence in which "no firm wants to be the first to adopt the dollar. As a result, the pound remains the dominant currency even when the US economy overtakes the UK economy in terms of size" (L815-819).

Two of these models point to *which* asset the vehicle rotates toward. Gopinath-Stein's complementarity requires the vehicle to double as a safe store of value; Mukhin states that "high inflation and volatility in emerging economies create global demand for a vehicle currency" (L2035-2036), so low return-volatility favours the vehicle role. Somogyi's demand-based mechanism agrees from the price-impact side: indirect trade through the dollar is cheaper when the dollar pairs have "half as volatile exchange rate returns as the nondollar pair" (`2026-Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX…txt` L70-74).

- **Prediction for DeFi.** The vehicle is a coordination equilibrium, so multiplicity and incumbency are both live, and an incumbent can persist even after being overtaken on fundamentals (Mukhin L815-819). But the safe-store-of-value and low-volatility versions of the mechanism predict the *direction* of any rotation: away from the volatile native asset and toward a stable numéraire that can serve as a low-variance unit of account for the intermediate leg. This is the mechanism prediction that matches the observed ETH-to-stable rotation, with the incumbency force predicting that the move should be slow and sticky.

### 1.3 Switching costs, lock-in and excess inertia (Dowd-Greenaway 1993), and the persistence-versus-lock-in distinction (Flandreau-Jobst 2009)

The third force is a user-side cost of change. Dowd and Greenaway make it concrete: "Changing currencies is costly - we must learn to reckon in the new currency, we must change the units in which we quote prices, we might have to change our records, and so on" (`1993-DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition…txt` L46-48). The consequence is that agents "are often reluctant to switch currencies, even when the currency they are using appears to be manifestly inferior to some other" (L52-54), producing "excess inertia … an undue reluctance to switch" (L238), and ultimately "lock-in … agents might continue to use an inferior currency even though they might all be aware of its inferiority" (L466-469). Mukhin adds the same friction to the coordination model: "The hysteresis is even stronger if firms have to pay fixed costs when switching to a new currency" (L96-99).

Flandreau and Jobst make the persistence question empirical and, crucially, separate two regimes. Their liquidity-popularity feedback is the empirical face of the thick-market externality, "a unit cost function that is decreasing with the volume of transactions using a given vehicle currency. This creates persistence" (`2009-FlandreauJobst2009Empirics…txt` L117-119). The regime turns on the product of the feedback coefficients: "If 0 < c1c2 < 1 there will be persistence but no path dependence" while "If c1c2 > 1 then the dynamics are explosive and lock-in … emerges" (L799, L805-806). Their 1900 estimate is decisive on the distinction: "c1c2 = 0.463 … 95% confidence interval of [0.22, 0.72] … The empirical evidence thus suggests that there is substantial persistence but no lock-in effects" (L886-889). Persistence is slow convergence under stable feedback; lock-in is explosive or multiple-equilibrium dependence; the two are not the same object and only the second is a trap.

- **Prediction for DeFi, and why this strand is the pivotal lever.** A dominated incumbent persists because users bear a cost to change and are reluctant even when they know the incumbent is inferior. In decentralised exchange that cost is mechanically absent: a deterministic router has nothing to learn, no records to change, no quotes to re-key, and it re-solves the route each block. Dowd-Greenaway's mechanism is therefore switched off in this setting. Any observed persistence of a dominated vehicle here cannot be a user switching cost and must come from the thick-market or coordination channel. This is what lets the paper separate forces that are inseparable in FX, and it is developed in Section 2.

### 1.4 Who pays for intermediation: Ho-Stoll 1981, Glosten-Milgrom 1985, Milionis et al. 2022, Lehar-Parlour 2024

The vehicle role is a service that someone is paid for and someone pays for, and the microstructure literature names the payment. In an inventory dealer, the spread compensates for holding a position the intermediary does not want: the dealer's "costs arise from the non-optimal portfolio the dealer stands ready to accept … The benefits arise from the fee he is able to charge for his service of immediate trading" (`1981-HoStoll1981OptimalDealer…txt` L240-244). In an information dealer, the spread is pure adverse selection: "the specialist must recoup the losses suffered in trades with the well informed by gains in trades with liquidity traders. These gains are achieved by setting a spread" (`1985-GlostenMilgrom1985BidAsk…txt` L61-66), and it exists "even when all the specialist's … transactions costs (including his time, inventory costs, etc.) are zero" (L57-60).

Decentralised exchange makes both explicit and removes the dealer's discretion. In an AMM "price impact is determined by the bonding curve and is perfectly predictable given the size of the liquidity pool and the size of the incoming order" (`2024-LeharParlour2024Uniswap…txt` L79-81); "the AMM cannot adjust its price and has to rely on arbitrageurs to align the quoted price with the fundamental value. Thus … liquidity provision and price discovery are separate and provided by different agents" (L504-508). The adverse-selection cost the vehicle's liquidity providers pay has a name and a closed form: loss-versus-rebalancing, "costs incurred by AMM LPs due to stale prices that are picked off by better informed arbitrageurs" (`2022-MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR…txt` L28-30), equal to "the cumulative profits of rebalancing arbitrageurs" (L711).

- **Role in the framing.** This strand does two things. It grounds the excess-use measure in a real cost: an asset carried through more than its endpoint demand warrants is a position its LPs finance and are picked off on, so vehicle dominance is not a free accounting artefact. And it supplies the habit-free agent of Section 4: the arbitrageur is the informed, profit-driven trader whose route the deterministic AMM forces, and whose numéraire choice is a candidate benchmark for what routing looks like with no relationship and no memory.

### 1.5 What the three strands jointly predict for the observed rotation

| Force | Textual primitive | Predicts the vehicle is | Predicts about a rotation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thick-market externality (Krugman; Flandreau-Jobst feedback) | cost falls with own volume; self-reinforcing | the deepest, most-used asset; initially native (WETH 95% of pools) | slow, self-validating, capable of catastrophic tipping |
| Coordination / complementarity (Kiyotaki-Wright; Gopinath-Stein; AIK; Mukhin) | accept what others accept; unit of account complements safe store of value | a coordination equilibrium; a low-volatility safe numéraire; incumbent can persist while overtaken | direction is toward a stable numéraire; sticky; multiple equilibria possible |
| Switching costs / lock-in (Dowd-Greenaway; Mukhin fixed cost) | learning, requoting, records; reluctance to leave an inferior incumbent | whatever is already established, held by inertia | a dominated incumbent persists; switching is discouraged |
| Debt-view counterpoint (Eren-Malamud) | dominant debt currency is the *riskiest*, "precisely because of it" (L139-145) | for debt denomination, the high-covariance risky asset | opposite direction to the safe-numéraire prediction |

The strands agree that the incumbent (native ETH) should be sticky and disagree on the terminal asset. Gopinath-Stein and Mukhin predict rotation toward a safe numéraire; Krugman's depth mechanism predicts the incumbent holds until a challenger out-carries it; Eren-Malamud (2022) predict, for the debt-denomination function, that the *riskiest* currency dominates, "not despite being the riskiest … but precisely because of it" (`2022-ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt…txt` L139-145), and that this "can arise without relying on network effects, price stickiness, pricing complementarities, and safety demand" (L371-374). The observed rotation toward fiat-reserve stables is therefore an adjudication, not a confirmation: it selects the safe-numéraire mechanism for the medium-of-exchange function and, read together with DeFi collateral behaviour where ETH and BTC are posted to borrow stablecoins (`2022-MakarovSchoar2022DeFi…txt` L1324-1325, L1399-1402), it separates the medium-of-exchange function from the store-of-value function that "dollar dominance" bundles together.

## 2. The contribution, stated sharply

### 2.1 What every prior setting structurally cannot see

The FX and currency-network literatures share two blind spots that are not incidental but built into their data.

**They never observe an incumbent while it is strictly dominated, because there the incumbent's cost advantage is a consequence of its incumbency.** In FX, the dollar is cheap to trade through *because* it is the vehicle; depth and incumbency are the same variable, so no observation can hold one fixed and vary the other. The project's prior-art sweep records this as an existing gap: "The FX inertia literature cannot observe an incumbent holding the vehicle role while strictly cost-dominated, because there an incumbent's cost advantage is a consequence of its incumbency" (`paper-spine.md` L105). The identification consequence is exact and the FX literature states it against itself: a lagged dependent variable plus fundamentals controls "cannot separate switching costs from a serially correlated unobserved fundamental" (`paper-spine.md` L389). Persistence measured this way is uninterpretable between the three forces of Section 1.

**They never observe the paths not taken.** Somogyi (2026) is the closest analogue and shows the ceiling of inference-based measurement. He must recover the vehicle share from an exclusion restriction because routes are latent: his "measure of vehicle currency trading activity is the difference between interdealer volume and my implied measure of fundamental trading demand based on nonoverlapping holidays" (`2026-Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX…txt` L135-138), yielding "on average 13% of the daily trading volume in dollar currency pairs" (L60-62). The number is holiday-inferred, not observed, and the alternative routes are never priced. Flandreau-Jobst measure the role one level coarser still, as directed in-degree, a binary link that "gives an indication of the international 'popularity' of alternative currencies" (`2009-FlandreauJobst2009Empirics…txt` L256) and can see neither the route nor its alternatives. Makarov-Schoar (2020), the closest crypto analogue, observe base currencies and cross-exchange arbitrage but compute cross-rates through the local fiat base and never designate or measure a vehicle on a route (`literature-audit.md` L811 uses; their text carries no vehicle, numéraire, or routing vocabulary).

### 2.2 What this setting establishes that they cannot

Because the realised route is reconstructed exactly, every alternative route is priceable at the same state, and the router that chose keeps no memory, this paper can establish three things the FX literature cannot:

1. **The vehicle role and its rotation are measured, not inferred.** The excess-use ratio is built from observed numerator and denominator on the same routes, with no exclusion restriction and no structural sign assumption. Somogyi's 13% is the best the inference approach can do; here the analogous object is a direct count.

2. **An incumbent can be caught carrying flow it is, at that instant, the wrong asset to carry.** Cost domination, a route that an available alternative would have beaten at the same reconstructed state, is observable here and is the exact object FX cannot form. This is the paper's signature counterfactual object. Its incidence estimate is currently withdrawn pending the transaction-state frontier rebuild (Section 2.3), but the *observability* is the contribution and it is already demonstrated on validated routes.

3. **The three confounded forces are separable, because one of them is off.** Deterministic routers remove the Dowd-Greenaway switching cost and habit by construction, so persistence of a dominated vehicle in this setting cannot be a user switching cost and must be depth or coordination. This converts Flandreau-Jobst's persistence-versus-lock-in distinction from a structural inference about a feedback coefficient into a direct measurement: the counterfactual route is visible, so slow convergence under stable feedback (persistence) and explosive or multiple-equilibrium dependence (lock-in) can be told apart on the routes themselves.

### 2.3 The sharpest statement, and an honest boundary

Sharpest framing sentence:

> In decentralised exchange the counterfactual route is observed and priced at the same block state and the router keeps no habit, so this setting can watch a vehicle currency lose its cost advantage and keep its role at the same instant, which is the one observation the FX inertia literature is structurally unable to make because there an incumbent's cost advantage is a consequence of its incumbency.

Boundary the paper must keep visible: the *rotation* finding is the admitted primary candidate; the *cost-domination incidence* and the *persistence-while-dominated* estimate are construction-complete but inference-reopened, pending the exact transaction-state counterfactual frontier and the forced-versus-chosen search-efficiency split (`findings-freeze.md`). The contribution in 2.2 is what the design *enables* and what the rotation already shows; it does not pre-spend the pending persistence number.

## 3. The strongest rival a referee raises, and how the design answers it

### 3.1 The rival: market maturation and aggregator composition

A referee's best attack does not dispute the excess-use arithmetic. It reinterprets it: the apparent vehicle rotation is not a currency phenomenon at all but the diffusion of routing technology over 2020 to 2026, universal routers, aggregators, cross-venue execution and faster arbitrage, together with a change in the composition of who routes and how many legs they use. Two sub-channels make this precise and they cut opposite ways, which is what makes the rival strong. Better routing "can find direct liquidity across previously siloed pools and reduce indirect routing, or it can combine fragmented spoke liquidity and make a stable intermediary more usable" (`research-questions-and-empirical-design.md` L111-114). The first is disintermediation; the second, activation, would raise the stable share for a reason that has nothing to do with any asset's vehicle-specific feedback. Chen-Duffie (2021) supply the theoretical warning that connectivity changes the object: fragmentation results can reverse "when orders condition on cross-venue prices" (`research-questions-and-empirical-design.md` L245), which is exactly what aggregators do. Lehar-Parlour's own Binance comparison "embeds exchange entry and routing change but cannot causally isolate aggregator or cross-venue integration" (`literature-audit.md` L529).

### 3.2 How the design answers it

The design does not wave the rival away; it makes it a falsifier and conditions it out where the data allow.

- **Condition inside opportunity sets and search strata.** Re-estimate vehicle extent and candidate choice "within date-pair-size opportunity sets, stratified by search sophistication, and interact candidate type with the daily efficiency state" (L116), using transaction-level direct splitting, sequential intermediation, route complexity and cross-venue execution as separate search-sophistication measures (L115).
- **Separate the margins that maturation would move.** Report "true intermediary-route incidence among economic routes separately from direct pool splitting and from the intermediary composition of the remaining indirect routes, on both the full and balanced venue perimeters" (L114). A fall in true intermediation supports disintermediation; stable-share growth conditional on remaining indirect supports activation.
- **A pre-committed interpretation bound.** "If stable dominance disappears after conditioning on these measures, the result is a routing-technology transition. If it survives within comparable opportunity and search sets, liquidity composition remains the live mechanism. If sophisticated routing disproportionately selects stable vehicles from a dispersed pool set, routing technology is a mechanism that activates stable liquidity, not a nuisance control" (L117).

### 3.3 What the data already say, and the honest status

Two facts from the freeze already bite on the rival. The 2024-to-2026 rotation "survives … every integration-by-complexity count cell," and the existing integration and complexity strata "reject those two composition margins as a complete account" (`findings-freeze.md`). And the stable premium is specifically a cross-venue object: the change is larger across venues than within one venue by 7.45 points for episodes and 8.17 points for strict-support value, and USDT's cross-venue share premium relative to single-venue routing rises 7.59 points by count and 12.00 points by strict value. Under the interpretation bound, a cross-venue-concentrated stable rise is activation of fragmented stable liquidity, which is a vehicle mechanism (the stable becomes more usable as an intermediary), not a nuisance that explains the finding away.

Honest status the paper must print: the maturation account is a live rival, not a defeated one. The complete transaction-state route frontier and the forced-versus-chosen search-efficiency estimates are open; public router-release windows "have mixed signs and executor identity does not recover quote authorship" (`findings-freeze.md`), so venue-integration events are usable only where the affected opportunity set and an admissible control group are observed. The falsifier is pre-registered: the rotation claim dies "if the rebound disappears within fixed opportunity and search-efficiency cells." Stating the rival this way is a strength; a JFE referee trusts a design that names the test that would kill its own headline.

## 4. Above and beyond: the sharpest story and three novel estimands

### 4.1 The sharpest scientific story

The paper is the first direct observation of a dominant vehicle currency being made and unmade. Krugman, Kiyotaki-Wright, Gopinath-Stein and Mukhin wrote the theory of how a medium of exchange coalesces and entrenches; none of their empirical descendants could watch it happen, because in every prior setting the incumbent's cost advantage, its incumbency, and the paths not taken are the same unobserved thing. Decentralised exchange breaks the three-way confound: it removes the switching cost mechanically, it leaves the thick-market force as observable pool depth, and it prices every route not taken at the same state. In that laboratory the vehicle role rotates from the native platform asset to a fiat-reserve stable numéraire, and the rotation adjudicates a live theoretical disagreement, Gopinath-Stein's safe-store-of-value complementarity and Mukhin's low-volatility demand predict exactly this direction, while Eren-Malamud's debt view predicts the opposite for the borrowing function, so the data separate the medium-of-exchange function from the store-of-value function that the phrase "dollar dominance" fuses. The making of a dominant currency, which the FX literature could only model, is here a measured event with its counterfactual attached.

### 4.2 Novel measure 1: the vehicle excess-use ratio, the clean vehicle object

- **Estimand.** For asset a at a given support and period, the ratio of a's share of intermediary (middle-leg) episodes or value to a's share of endpoint (source-or-sink) demand, on the same reconstructed routes. A ratio above one is Krugman's "more transactions than payments would justify," turned into a per-asset, per-period statistic.
- **Why novel.** It nets out endpoint demand directly, with no exclusion restriction and no structural model. Somogyi must infer the excess through holidays because routes are latent; Flandreau-Jobst's in-degree cannot net endpoint demand at all; the stablecoin literature (Catalini et al. 2022) "contains no vehicle, dominance, numeraire, route, routing or notional terminology" (`literature-audit.md` L698). The measure is this paper's own and already discriminates: stable 1.41 by count and 1.19 by strict-support value against native 0.77 and 0.68 in 2026, with USDT the transition margin and the increase concentrated in USDC and USDT and not shared across stablecoin designs (fiat-reserve stables carry 96.1% of supported stable intermediary value at a 1.06 ratio; on-chain-collateralized 0.06; synthetic 0.49).
- **Status.** Admitted route-only measure. It is the sharp object the whole paper turns on.

### 4.3 Novel measure 2: the arbitrageur's revealed numéraire as a candidate habit-free benchmark

- **Estimand.** Among repeated, profit-driven cyclic searchers (arbitrageurs executing atomic, all-or-nothing multi-leg trades), the asset they route rebalancing trades *through*, conditional on state-dependent profitability, compared with the acyclic population's choice on the same pairs and states. The arbitrageur is the informed rebalancing trader who prices the AMM (Milionis et al. L28-30, L711; Glosten-Milgrom's informed trader) and whose route the deterministic bonding curve forces (Lehar-Parlour L79-81), so this agent carries the least plausible habit and the shortest memory.
- **Why novel.** No prior work isolates the numéraire choice of an agent for whom switching cost and habit are near zero and uses it as a benchmark against which the general population's persistence can be read. If arbitrageurs rotate first and further, the general population's slower rotation is attributable to depth and coordination, not to habit, which sharpens the Section 2 separation.
- **Validation the estimand requires, stated honestly.** Habit-freeness is not assumed and is not yet established. The endpoint rule alone "[does not prove] the agents are habit-free" (`node-k-proposals-round1.md` L21), and the project explicitly forbids claiming that cyclic search is "provably habit-free" (`literature-audit.md` L807). The benchmark is therefore a *proposed instrument* that must first pass its own test: identify repeated searchers, absorb searcher fixed effects, condition on state-dependent profitability, and show "a weaker persistence profile" than the acyclic population (`node-k-proposals-round1.md` L21). The novelty is the estimand and its validation design together, not a finished result.

### 4.4 Novel measure 3: a Herfindahl over vehicle shares, read with leader identity, to distinguish succession from fragmentation

- **Estimand.** The concentration of the intermediary-share distribution across candidate assets, jointly with which asset leads, per pair-cell and over time. Succession is concentration that stays high while the leader's identity changes, one vehicle replacing another. Fragmentation is concentration that falls with a stable leader, the vehicle role dispersing without replacement.
- **Why novel.** Chen-Duffie (2021) separate venue-count fragmentation from allocation and informativeness in theory but "[do] not measure a real market transition" (`literature-audit.md` L511); the FX literature cannot build the object because it sees neither the route nor the full alternative set. Here both are observed, so the distinction becomes measurable on a real succession.
- **Identification caveat, stated honestly.** The naive reading, that a Herfindahl plus leader identity alone identifies the distinction, was retired: "The claimed identification, that HHI plus leader identity separates succession from fragmentation, does not hold" (`node-c-definitions-round2.md` L150). The measure must be rebuilt on the excess-use ratio and a per-cell regime label, and the direction is open, because the excess-use ratio and the older betweenness measure disagree on when WETH's vehicle role ended. The novel contribution is the regime-labelled decomposition, not the retired two-statistic shortcut.

### 4.5 The through-line

The three measures are one instrument at three altitudes. The excess-use ratio says *how much* an asset is a vehicle net of its own demand; the arbitrageur's revealed numéraire says *what a habit-free agent does*, so the population's deviation from it is attributable; the regime-labelled Herfindahl says *whether a rotation is one vehicle replacing another or a role dissolving*. Together they let the paper report not just that the vehicle role rotated from ETH to a stable numéraire, but that it rotated by succession, at a pace the habit-free benchmark bounds, driven by a depth-and-coordination force that the setting has stripped of any switching-cost explanation. That is the claim the FX literature can model and cannot measure, and it is the claim this data can measure.
