## Completion contract

Each paper is classified as load-bearing, contextual, venue exemplar, or more than one. A complete card records five distinct reading axes, not a generic prose summary:

- exact title, authors, year, publication/version and saved source;
- project roles and every bibliography key or manuscript claim that uses it;
- `Scientific`: question, contribution, theory or mechanism, data, measurement, identification, main estimates, boundary conditions and credible alternative readings;
- `Structure`: top-level and subsection architecture and the function of each major block;
- `Depth`: where the paper spends analytical, institutional and robustness effort, and what it treats lightly;
- `Breadth`: scope, literatures, mechanisms, objections, alternatives and boundary discipline;
- `Optics`: title, opening, claim calibration, tables, figures, captions, notation, prose density, page allocation and reviewer-facing credibility;
- exact page, section, table or equation supporting every project use;
- first reader, completion status and independent-reader status.

Allowed statuses are `discovered`, `abstract-screened`, `full-text-read`, `claim-verified` and `independently-re-read`. Keyword retrieval, text extraction and corpus-level counts never advance a paper beyond `abstract-screened` on their own.

### MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR
- Status: claim-verified
- Roles: load-bearing, mechanism, measurement
- Source: literature/papers/2022-MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR-preprint-automated-market-making-and-loss-versus-rebalancing.pdf
- Version: arXiv v5, 27 May 2024, 63 pages
- Uses: Supplies the loss-versus-rebalancing definition and the constant-product closed form used in Section 5, equations `eq:lvr` and `eq:lvrrate`, and in the rent-incidence finding; does not support the Myersonian liquidity-provision claims, which belong to a different paper
- Scientific: Builds a continuous-time risk-neutral model with an infinitely deep external market, a locally smooth AMM demand curve, passive LP capital, frictionless arbitrage and exogenous noise-trader fees. The rebalancing portfolio holds the AMM's risky-asset inventory but trades at the external price. Theorem 1 shows that the AMM's predictable loss relative to that portfolio is one half of instantaneous price variance times marginal liquidity and equals rebalancing-arbitrage profit. For the equal-weight constant-product invariant, instantaneous LVR divided by pool value is variance divided by eight. The one-pair empirical exercise uses the Uniswap v2 WETH-USDC pool from 1 August 2021 to 31 July 2022, Binance minute prices, and Dune pool events; it validates the accounting approximation but is not causal identification. Its sharp boundaries are the external-price benchmark, continuous trading, no arbitrage fees or gas in the baseline, passive liquidity, and a single large pool.
- Structure: Sections 1 to 3 move from the Black-Scholes analogy and contribution preview through institutional background to the model. Section 4 derives the generic LVR theorem and mechanism. Section 5 specializes it to Balancer, Uniswap v2, Uniswap v3 ranges and a limit-order boundary case. Section 6 validates the model empirically. Sections 7 and 8 connect the object to option replication, variance swaps and impermanent loss. Section 9 discusses oracle, auction and batch-auction design responses; Section 10 concludes. Appendices prove the theorem, derive curvature, give a binomial intuition and multidimensional extension, and disclose the data construction and SQL.
- Depth: Roughly half the main text is devoted to the mechanism, theorem, intuition and AMM examples before the eight-page empirical validation. The appendices spend twelve pages on proofs and extensions and four on data construction. The paper treats equilibrium LP supply, cross-pool competition, fees, gas, block discreteness, strategic liquidity adjustment and multi-venue routing lightly or as follow-on work.
- Breadth: Connects AMM market making to stale-quote sniping, adverse selection, Black-Scholes replication, delta hedging, European options, variance swaps, impermanent loss and mechanism design. It covers generic smooth AMMs plus weighted-product, constant-product and concentrated-liquidity examples, while explicitly declining claims about equilibrium liquidity levels and the response of supplied capital to market design.
- Optics: The title names one new object; the abstract and first two pages state the analogy, mechanism, formula class, empirical validation and design implication. The paper spends fourteen pages earning the theorem before naming the constant-product shortcut, then uses seven figures and one compact results table to make the abstraction concrete. Captions are unusually explanatory, equations carry the argument, and limitations are placed before the empirical section rather than hidden in robustness. The 63-page preprint is theory-led and denser than an empirical JFE template, so its architecture is a mechanism model benchmark, not a direct section-length target.
- Locations: The rebalancing definition is in Section 4, pages 17 to 19, equations (5) and (6); Theorem 1 and the generic LVR rate are pages 19 to 20, equations (7) and (8); the constant-product result is Example 3, pages 25 to 26, equation (16); the empirical integrated form is page 30, equation (23); model limitations are Section 3.1, pages 13 to 15; the validation sample and results are pages 30 to 36, Figures 3 to 6 and Table 1; the exact empirical construction uses Binance prices at Appendix C, pages 60 to 62, especially equation (45).
- Implication: Keep the corrected attribution and variance-over-eight formula, but do not imply that the citation validates variance measured from each pool's own marginal price. The cited estimand uses the external market price and the paper implements it with Binance data. External-price reconstruction or a transparent bound is therefore required before rent incidence freezes. Preserve the source's separation of fees, LVR and market risk, and its explicit limits; decline its single-pool empirical breadth as evidence for cross-pool or vehicle-rotation claims.
- First reader: glotl, full-text pass completed 2026-08-07
- Independent: not required; this source is load-bearing for one measurement formula but is not classified as a central paper-level framing source

### MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage
- Status: claim-verified
- Roles: load-bearing, rival, empirical-design, contextual, venue exemplar
- Source: literature/papers/2020-MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage-trading-and-arbitrage-in-cryptocurrency-markets.pdf
- Version: Published Journal of Financial Economics article, volume 135, 2020, pages 293 to 319; accepted 12 January 2019 and available online 11 July 2019
- Uses: Supports the introduction's cross-exchange segmentation claim, the Section 6 choice to give a contested measured object its own defence, the Korea-Japan-Europe magnitude and friction contrast, and the choice to report economic magnitudes in dollars as well as incidence. It does not support classifying DEX round trips, claiming atomic flash-loan capture, describing arbitrage and uninformed volume shares at an AMM pool, or equating route notional times a hypothetical gap with its executable arbitrage-profit object.
- Scientific: Uses tick data for 34 centralized exchanges in 19 countries from January 2017 through February 2018, with a 15-exchange Kaiko core, to document recurring cross-exchange bitcoin price deviations, their geographic segmentation, co-movement with buying pressure, relation to capital controls, cross-cryptocurrency falsification and order-flow price pressure. The arbitrage index is the minute-level maximum volume-weighted price divided by the minimum and then averaged daily. Potential profit is constructed at second frequency from the minimum of executable sell-initiated low-price volume and buy-initiated high-price volume, normally only above a 2% spread, with a capped more aggressive rule above 10%. Korea-US price differences averaged above 15% from December 2017 to early February 2018 and reached 40% on several days; Japan averaged about 10%, Europe about 3%, and the authors estimate at least $2 billion total potential profit over December through February. Pairwise capital-control exposure predicts spread-correlation with slope 0.29, t-statistic 3 and R-squared 0.07; the country premium-beta relation has slope 0.3, t-statistic 4.6 and adjusted R-squared 0.56. Crypto-to-crypto deviations on the same exchanges are an order of magnitude smaller than crypto-to-fiat deviations, strengthening the segmentation account. Factor and canonical-correlation decompositions show common signed volume strongly explains common returns, while exchange-specific signed volume and lagged price deviations explain slow local convergence. The paper is descriptive and mechanism-discriminating, not a causal estimate of capital controls; exchange access, cleaning, possible wash trading, short sample length and endogenous exchange choice remain boundaries.
- Structure: Section 1 spends four pages stating five numbered findings, magnitudes, mechanism conjecture, order-flow contribution, literature and roadmap. Section 2 gives exchange/data construction and cleaning; Section 3 establishes volume and return facts. Section 4 builds the arbitrage index from within-region to cross-region prices and executable-volume profits. Section 5 moves from spread correlation to buying pressure and capital controls; Section 6 uses fiat-versus-crypto and crypto-versus-crypto comparisons as a mechanism check. Section 7 develops the factor and VAR order-flow model. Section 8 is a standalone defence of the measured arbitrage object through strategy mechanics and constraints. Section 9 concludes. A two-page appendix records exchange rules, fees, transfer delays and the full cross-country correlation matrix.
- Depth: The article spends about five pages on data and baseline market facts, four on arbitrage measurement and magnitudes, four on cross-country correlation and capital controls, three on the crypto-crypto falsification, five on order-flow modelling, and two and a half on executability and constraints. Ten numbered tables including two appendix tables, eleven figures and eight equations carry most evidence. Its deepest work is measurement triangulation and economic feasibility, while formal identification of capital controls, trader identity, institutional change over time and welfare are treated lightly or explicitly left unresolved.
- Breadth: Connects international market segmentation, limits to arbitrage, capital controls, investor demand, convenience yield, market microstructure, price discovery, order flow, governance risk and crypto institutional detail. It covers within-country, cross-country, crypto-fiat and crypto-crypto comparisons at second, minute, five-minute, hourly and daily frequencies. It does not cover decentralized exchanges, automated market makers, path routing, flash loans, same-block atomic execution, liquidity-provider returns or vehicle currencies.
- Optics: The broad descriptive title matches the paper's two contribution blocks. The abstract is a single compact paragraph, while the first three introduction pages preview the findings as an ordered sequence with concrete percentages and dollars before offering the mechanism. Figures 2 to 5 establish the phenomenon visually before regression tables appear; Section 8 then answers the obvious referee objection that the gaps should be arbitraged away. Captions define samples and construction in full. Equations are confined to the order-flow section and do not interrupt the descriptive opening. At 27 journal pages, the paper earns breadth through a clear results ladder and a late standalone measurement defence, not through a generic robustness section.
- Locations: Published pages 293 to 296 contain the abstract, ordered contribution preview, 15%/40%/10%/3% differences and $2 billion magnitude; pages 296 to 298 and Table 1 document the data; pages 301 to 305, Figures 2 to 5 and Sections 4.1 to 4.4 define and measure the arbitrage index and volume-matched profit; pages 305 to 308, Figures 6 to 9 and Tables 4 to 5 report buying-pressure and capital-control evidence; pages 308 to 311 and Figures 10 to 11 give the crypto-crypto comparison; pages 311 to 315, equations (1) to (8) and Tables 6 to 10 give the order-flow analysis; pages 315 to 317, Section 8 and Appendix Table A1 state latency, short-sale, fee, governance and capital-control constraints.
- Implication: Preserve the paper's sequence of phenomenon, economic magnitude, mechanism discrimination and explicit executability defence. Keep the verified Section 6 comparison. Remove this citation from DEX round-trip and same-block flash-loan claims, and do not use it as evidence about AMM pool-flow composition. Label the project's route-size-times-improvement quantity as counterfactual execution improvement or savings, subject to exact-size quote support, not as Makarov-Schoar potential arbitrage profit. Match its optics by placing the key continuous magnitude and denominator early, then giving the constructed quote object a dedicated quantitative defence.
- First reader: glotl, full published-text pass completed 2026-08-07
- Independent: not required at this stage; the paper is load-bearing for a rival and measurement benchmark but is not the project's central vehicle-dominance theory source

### venue:makarov-schoar-crypto-arbitrage
- Status: full-text-read
- Roles: venue
- Source: literature/papers/2020-MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage-trading-and-arbitrage-in-cryptocurrency-markets.pdf
- Version: Published Journal of Financial Economics article, volume 135, 2020, pages 293 to 319
- Uses: Venue benchmark for overall architecture, section allocation, result ordering, quantitative opening, exhibit design, boundary placement and the exceptional case in which a contested measured object earns a standalone defence section
- Scientific: A descriptive market-microstructure paper turns global crypto-exchange fragmentation into a finance contribution by measuring price dispersion and executable-volume profit, distinguishing within- from cross-country gaps, testing capital-control implications with crypto-crypto comparisons and country openness, and explaining price formation through common and idiosyncratic order flow. It presents multiple mutually reinforcing diagnostics while keeping the capital-control conclusion correlational and explicitly discussing alternative frictions.
- Structure: Four-page introduction; two-page data section; three-page market-facts section; four-page arbitrage measurement section; four-page correlation and segmentation section; three-page crypto-crypto comparison; five-page order-flow model and results; two-and-a-half-page arbitrage-constraints defence; half-page conclusion; two-page institutional appendix. The architecture moves from visible fact to magnitude, mechanism, price formation and object defence.
- Depth: Most pages and exhibits go to establishing that the deviation is real, large, executable at observed volume and economically explainable. The paper uses eleven figures to establish patterns before leaning on ten tables and eight equations for mechanism and price pressure. Robustness is embedded beside each claim, except that the feasibility of the measured object is important enough to receive Section 8.
- Breadth: The paper spans 34 exchanges, 19 countries, three cryptocurrencies, multiple base currencies and frequencies from seconds to days, while linking international finance and market microstructure. It keeps this breadth coherent through one object, cross-market price deviation, and does not branch into protocol design, welfare or causal policy evaluation.
- Optics: Concrete magnitudes appear on the second journal page and are repeated selectively, not everywhere. Eleven largely time-series and comparison figures make the empirical object visible; tables carry samples, moments, factors and regressions. Long captions make exhibits portable. The introduction uses an explicit first-through-final finding ladder. The penultimate standalone defence is justified by the object, then the conclusion stays under a page. Prose is dense but sentences remain direct and notation appears only after the descriptive evidence has earned it.
- Locations: Published pages 293 to 296 show the opening and contribution ladder; pages 301 to 305 and Figures 2 to 5 show the magnitude-first exhibit sequence; pages 305 to 311 and Figures 6 to 11 show mechanism triangulation; pages 311 to 315 and Tables 6 to 10 show the compact formal block; pages 315 to 317 show the standalone defence; pages 318 to 319 show the short appendix and institutional-detail strategy.
- Implication: Use this paper as a licence, not a template to copy wholesale. The present paper should open with degree of vehicle dominance and the priced declined alternative, state basis points and dollars early, keep mechanisms in a ladder, and retain a standalone executability section because the constructed route quote is contested. It should not inherit the exemplar's broad descriptive title, 11-figure density or correlational mechanism language where the on-chain design can provide sharper transaction-state tests.
- First reader: glotl, full published-text venue pass completed 2026-08-07
- Independent: not required for a venue-only card
