## Completion contract

Each paper is classified as load-bearing, contextual, venue exemplar, or more than one. A complete card records five distinct reading axes, not a generic prose summary:

- exact title, authors, year, publication/version, saved source, and canonical `Source key` shared by scientific and venue cards;
- `Companions`: every cited online appendix, internet appendix, supplement, correction, and data appendix, each saved and read, or a documented article/DOI/author-page check that found none;
- project roles and every bibliography key or manuscript claim that uses it;
- `Scientific`: question, contribution, theory or mechanism, data, measurement, identification, main estimates, boundary conditions and credible alternative readings;
- `Structure`: top-level and subsection architecture and the function of each major block;
- `Depth`: where the paper spends analytical, institutional and robustness effort, and what it treats lightly;
- `Breadth`: scope, literatures, mechanisms, objections, alternatives and boundary discipline;
- `Optics`: title, opening, claim calibration, tables, figures, captions, notation, prose density, page allocation and reviewer-facing credibility;
- exact page, section, table or equation supporting every project use;
- first reader, completion status and independent-reader status.

Allowed statuses are `discovered`, `abstract-screened`, `full-text-read`, `claim-verified` and `independently-re-read`. Keyword retrieval, text extraction and corpus-level counts never advance a paper beyond `abstract-screened` on their own.

For a published venue exemplar, `full-text-read` closes the venue row only when the `Version` field starts with `Published` and `Companions` begins with `Complete:` or `None:`. A complete working-paper read may populate every scientific axis and exact location, but it does not establish the journal version's final structure, page allocation or optics. A main article that delegates proofs, robustness, identification, measurement, or institutional detail to an unread companion is not a complete full-text read. The executable gate enforces these distinctions. Each required card must also have a `source_sets` record in `literature/pdf-sources.json`: the tracked article, a publisher or DOI page, and an author or repository page must each be checked; every discovered companion key must match the card and resolve to durable text or an official source note. This prevents a fluent `None:` sentence from closing discovery without evidence.

Source-set work may run in parallel only across independent papers. One owner keeps each paper family sequential from published main article through appendices, supplements, corrections, replication artifacts, full reading and claim adjudication. Parallel workers stage downloads and manifests in isolated locations and never edit the shared ledger; one reconciler validates exact versions and hashes, removes superseded artifacts, writes the source-set record and closes the card. A paper must not be split across readers because conclusions in the main article often depend on companion evidence, and parallel retrieval must not become parallel interpretation of an incomplete set.

- Source key: GopinathEtAl2020DCP
- Version: Published American Economic Review article, volume 110, number 3, March 2020, pages 677 to 719, DOI 10.1257/aer.20171201
- Companions: Complete: the official 30-page Online Appendix is saved under `GopinathEtAl2020DCPAppendix`, read sequentially in full, and reconciled with the published article. - Uses: Supplies the dominant-currency-pricing benchmark in which common-currency price stickiness makes the importer-to-dominant-currency exchange rate more predictive of bilateral prices and trade than the bilateral exchange rate; measures the intensity of the invoicing role through observed shares; provides a mechanism-discrimination template spanning aggregate and micro evidence. Applying that logic to a continuous degree of vehicleness is this project's inference. It does not endogenize why one currency becomes dominant, instrument invoicing choice, causally identify dollar movements, or validate DeFi route dominance, liquidity supply, routing efficiency, or aggregator effects.
- Scientific: Builds a three-region dynamic New Keynesian model with Kimball demand, Calvo price and wage rigidity, imported inputs, variable markups and incomplete international asset markets under producer-, local- and dominant-currency pricing. Proposition 1 predicts locally stable bilateral terms of trade, price and quantity adjustment tied chiefly to the importer-dollar rate, unusually weak US import responses, and global trade contraction after broad dollar appreciation. The aggregate evidence constructs chained Fisher bilateral price and quantity indices from HS6 UN Comtrade data for more than 2,500 ordered country pairs over 1992 to 2015, covering 91 percent of world trade after broad commodity exclusions. Adding the dollar rate reduces the unweighted bilateral price-pass-through coefficient from 0.757 to 0.164 while the dollar coefficient is 0.781; in volume regressions the analogous bilateral coefficient moves from -0.119 to -0.031 while the dollar coefficient is -0.186. A uniform 1 percent dollar appreciation predicts about a 0.6 percent contraction in rest-of-world trade within a year. Colombian firm-product-country customs data from 2005 to 2015 address aggregation and composition concerns, and calibrated DCP counterfactuals fit price dynamics better than the two extreme alternatives. The appendix validates the Comtrade index against BLS series, splits advanced and emerging markets, adds CPI/PPI and euro, franc and yen comparisons, varies windows and controls, and reports Colombian robustness plus a nine-parameter minimum-distance fit to eleven moments. Identification remains reduced-form and predictive: exchange rates and invoicing shares are not instrumented, key aggregate specifications cannot absorb importer-year effects, common global or financial shocks remain credible rivals, annual micro inference uses a short time dimension, and the structural exercise reports no parameter uncertainty, weighting sensitivity or formal fit comparison. Long-run quantity persistence is sample-dependent, with much of the full-sample response fading after one to two years.
- Structure: The abstract and five-page introduction define the three-feature paradigm, contrast it with producer- and local-currency pricing, enumerate four predictions, report headline estimates, map the literature and state the roadmap. Section I devotes roughly fourteen pages to households, production, price setting, Proposition 1, calibration and monetary-policy simulations. Section II moves through global data, terms of trade, price pass-through, quantities, US asymmetry, third-country trade and robustness. Section III introduces Colombian microdata, reports reduced-form evidence, then compares calibrated DCP, PCP and LCP counterfactuals and component shutoffs. A two-page conclusion states policy implications and the exogenous-invoicing boundary. The appendix follows the same evidentiary order: data provenance and external validation, macro extensions, Colombian details, then structural targets and moment fit.

The source set spends substantial appendix space on unit-value construction, BLS validation, alternative currencies, subsamples, added controls, differentiated goods and macro-to-micro replication. Causal identification is light by design. The model comparison adds discipline but remains conditional on calibrated assumptions, a diagonal minimum-distance weighting matrix and unreported structural uncertainty. The paper also leaves endogenous currency choice, equilibrium currency demand and the origin or persistence of dominance outside the model.
- Breadth: Integrates international New Keynesian monetary economics, trade elasticities, exchange-rate pass-through, invoicing, strategic complementarity, imported inputs, global cycles and monetary spillovers. - Uses: Provides an early empirical implementation of currency status as network breadth, a structural liquidity-popularity feedback and an explicit distinction between persistence and lock-in. It supports measuring dominance continuously at the currency level while retaining binary market-currency links underneath. It does not observe routed vehicle use, trade paths, turnover, execution prices, alternative routes or aggregator-mediated market integration.
- Scientific: The data contain every ordered pair among 45 markets and currencies. The binary indicator equals one when market i regularly quotes currency j against its domestic currency, while a static listing without an observed price or price change is zero. Currency j's popularity is therefore directed in-degree, the number of foreign markets quoting it. The complete matrix contains 194, 218 and 264 active links in 1890, 1900 and 1910. In the 35-country structural sample, the 1900 degrees reproduce Figure 1 exactly: sterling 34, franc 29, mark 21, Belgian franc and guilder 12, and dollar 7. Table 1's 1,408 observations are 32 currencies with complete policy and rate variables, each evaluated in 44 foreign markets. The data reproduce the general logit's short-rate coefficient of -0.988, distance -0.756, trade share 0.250, coverage 2.049, log likelihood -247.229, pseudo-R-squared 0.586 and every confusion-matrix cell; friction variables identify 72 percent of links and macro variables alone identify 17 percent. The preferred 2SPLS slopes are -0.91 and -0.52, whose unrounded product is reported as 0.463 with a percentile-bootstrap 95 percent interval of [0.22, 0.72]. Within the model, positive feedback below one implies stable persistence and a product above one implies explosive dynamics and lock-in. This is not an autoregressive persistence coefficient, half-life or causally identified network effect. Identification relies on functional form, Gaussian errors, exclusion restrictions, generated latent utilities and an undocumented weighting matrix. The supplied artifacts omit country weights, exact matrix construction, estimation code, bootstrap resampling unit and seeds, so the exact product and interval cannot be independently regenerated. GDP per capita and democracy identify the rate equation while distance and bilateral trade identify quote utility, but the paper reports no instrument-strength, exclusion or overidentification tests; common financial-centre quality, banking networks, institutions and monetary expectations remain rivals. The coverage control is mechanically the log of market i's 1900 out-degree, including the dependent observation itself. Removing it leaves qualitative friction results intact but moves distance from -0.756 to -0.971, the short-rate coefficient from -0.988 to -0.889, trade share from 0.250 to 0.219 and log likelihood from -247.23 to -281.63. The paper's claim that estimates without coverage are identical means qualitatively similar, not numerically identical. The codebook also reveals that missing bilateral trade reports are coded zero despite acknowledged positive flows, mirror reports are averaged, the shortest distance is used for multicentre countries and country activity is one if any domestic centre actively trades the currency.
- Structure: The opening motivates monetary leadership and gives the roadmap. Section 1 contrasts credibility and market-size explanations and develops search, transaction-cost and strategic-externality foundations. Section 2 moves from the complete directed exchange matrix to limited-dependent-variable evidence. Section 3 builds simultaneous liquidity and popularity schedules and estimates the 2SPLS system. Section 4 distinguishes stable persistence from lock-in, constructs a history-free equilibrium and simulates a US trade expansion. Section 5 concludes. Three tables and six figures sit beside the claims they support.
- Depth: The paper defines the complete directed matrix, derives 13 equations, separates friction from policy variables, models simultaneity and computes structural counterfactuals. The codebook permits exact reconstruction of descriptive counts, Table 1 and Table 2. Depth stops short of modern reproducibility and identification standards because the transformation matrices, estimation code, bootstrap design, raw coding evidence and uncertainty for Figures 5 and 6 are absent.
- Breadth: Covers international circulation, quote-network breadth, transaction and inventory costs, geography, bilateral trade, monetary credibility, money-market liquidity, strategic externalities, persistence, lock-in, empire, financial development, historical counterfactuals and dollar-sterling transition. It does not cover transaction volumes, price impact, observed indirect exchange, vehicle-route choice, dealer or LP supply, technological integration, aggregators, intraday dynamics or empirically identified architecture changes.
- Optics: The published version is compact and well sequenced. Its exhibits progress from descriptive degree to reduced form, structural feedback, stability regimes and counterfactual transition. Figure 4 makes persistence versus lock-in unusually legible. Three defects matter. Table 1 prints the general-model GDP-per-capita coefficient and z-statistic as negative, while the supplied data reproduce every other number in the column and yield +0.1509 with z=+0.59; this is a published sign typo. Table 3's note points to footnote 19 for bootstrap details although the bootstrap is footnote 20, and Section 4.1 points to footnote 21, which discusses sample symmetry. The abstract's claim that the dollar was bound to overtake sterling exceeds Figure 6's uncertainty-free simulation, which proportionally raises every US bilateral trade flow while holding all other bilateral trade fixed; the text directly shows only that moving the US sample trade share from about 11.2 to 15.6 percent overtakes the franc and mark.
- Locations: Data and exchange matrices are journal pages 645 to 648, equations (1) to (4) and Figure 1. Table 1 and classification performance are pages 650 to 651; Table 2 is page 652. Strategic externalities are pages 652 to 657, equations (5) to (11), Figures 2 to 3 and Table 3. The bootstrap description is footnote 20 on page 656. Persistence and lock-in are pages 656 to 659 and Figure 4. The 0.463 product and [0.22, 0.72] interval are page 659. History-free equilibrium equations (12) to (13) and Figure 5 are pages 659 to 660. The US trade simulation and Figure 6 are pages 660 to 661. Alternative switching-cost lock-in is declared unidentifiable in this cross-section on page 661, footnote 26. The codebook defines quote direction and regular-trading rules on pages 1 to 2, variables on pages 2 to 3 and archival sources on pages 3 to 6.
- Implication: Use this source to motivate a continuous dominance measure built by aggregating binary market-level links, with precise vocabulary: persistence is slow convergence under stable feedback; lock-in is model-specific explosive or multiple-equilibrium dependence. Do not use 0.463 as an empirical half-life, causal elasticity or DeFi calibration. The present paper's advantage is direct observation of intermediate tokens, executable alternatives, daily dynamics and architecture changes. Aggregator integration and optimized routing must enter as an explicit rival secular mechanism. Frame the DeFi evidence as measuring the degree of vehicle routing and persistence of dominance, not merely whether a token carries a binary vehicle label.

### ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation
- Status: claim-verified
- Roles: central, theory, mechanism, rival, contextual
- Source: literature/papers/2021-ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation-market-fragmentation.pdf

- Companions: Complete: the official 52-page Online Appendix is saved under `ChenDuffie2021FragmentationAppendix`, read sequentially in full, and reconciled with the article; the publisher and author records disclosed no correction, corrigendum, code or further companion
- Uses: Supplies a theoretical distinction between venue-level depth, aggregate allocation and collective price informativeness and a possibility result that fragmentation can improve risk sharing up to an interior optimum even as each venue becomes less deep. Supports treating fragmentation and succession as different empirical objects. It does not measure a real market transition, identify a causal welfare effect, imply that more venues are generally better, or apply unchanged once routers or aggregators connect venues and condition execution on cross-venue prices.
- Scientific: Strategic traders split locally price-contingent demand schedules across disconnected exchanges. An order at one venue is partly shielded from its price impact elsewhere, and imperfect cross-exchange price correlation reduces effective aggregate impact even though venue-level impact rises. In the unique symmetric affine baseline equilibrium, aggregate aggressiveness rises with the number of exchanges, crosses the efficient allocation and converges above it; any fragmentation beats centralization within that baseline, but excessive fragmentation is worse than a finite optimum. Individual venue prices become less informative while the vector of prices becomes more informative. A variant with observable aggregate inventory isolates order splitting and approaches efficiency as venue count grows; the dynamic model constructs an efficient equilibrium only at the special optimum and does not solve arbitrary venue counts. The appendix verifies the baseline, optimal exchange count, informativeness, dynamic Bellman problem and extensions. Endogenous liquidity supply preserves the result only under parameter restrictions; private payoff information adds adverse-selection impact and can make fragmentation harmful; arbitrary covariance can create multiple equilibria and changes limiting allocation. This is analytical theory with no data, estimates, uncertainty or causal identification. Results depend on disconnected contemporaneous demand schedules, imperfect price correlation, symmetric affine strategies, quadratic holding costs, Gaussian information or covariance restrictions, at least three strategic traders and model-specific local liquidity demand. The appendix's Proposition 10 has a sign discrepancy between its statement and concluding calculation, so its arbitrary-covariance payment formula is not reusable without resolution.
- Structure: The introduction states the depth-versus-efficiency paradox, mechanism, static and dynamic results and limitations. Section I supplies institutional motivation and mixed prior evidence; Sections II and III define the baseline and equilibrium; Section IV separates price impact, allocative efficiency and informativeness; Section V exposes the order-splitting channel by making aggregate inventory observable; Section VI constructs the dynamic special case; Section VII previews reversal extensions; Section VIII calibrates policy scope. The Online Appendix progresses from admissible-deviation verification through baseline comparative statics, dynamic proofs, endogenous liquidity, private information and arbitrary covariance.
- Depth: Analytical depth lies in equilibrium construction, cross-exchange inference, closed-form comparative statics and the dynamic belief problem. The appendix supplies 52 pages of proofs and substantially develops private-information and covariance reversals. Institutional motivation is useful but secondary, and empirical measurement, calibration, external validity and policy analysis are absent. Several extension proofs are omitted as analogous, and the dynamic result applies only at the constructed optimal venue count.
- Breadth: Covers visible lit-market fragmentation, demand-function competition, order splitting, welfare, depth, information aggregation, static and dynamic trading, local liquidity demand, private information, correlated endowments and equilibrium multiplicity. It excludes dark markets, exchange entry, fees, tick sizes, queue priority, latency, trade-through effects and general policy evaluation. Most important here, its central disconnected-exchange assumption is altered by DEX aggregators and optimized cross-venue routers, making market integration a rival time-varying mechanism rather than a harmless background trend.
- Optics: The compact title and five-sentence abstract foreground a counterintuitive result and immediately separate depth, allocation and information. The article builds a disciplined theorem and proposition ladder, with intuition surrounding dense notation and qualifications visible in the opening and conclusion. Two figures make the price-impact and welfare reversals legible. The companion is proof-heavy and lacks a standalone synthesis; placeholder running heads, omitted analogous derivations and the Proposition 10 sign conflict weaken auditability at the margin.
- Locations: Baseline assumptions and market clearing are journal pages 2253 to 2255. Equilibrium demand, price impact and allocation are pages 2255 to 2257, equations (2) to (8) and Theorem 1. Price impact, optimal fragmentation and informativeness are pages 2258 to 2263, Propositions 1 to 3, equation (9) and Figures 1 to 2. Observable aggregate inventory is pages 2262 to 2264 and Theorem 2; the dynamic construction is pages 2264 to 2269, equations (12) to (20) and Theorem 3; reversal cases are pages 2269 to 2270. In the Online Appendix, verification and baseline proofs are pages 1 to 11; observable inventory is pages 12 to 14; dynamics are pages 14 to 28; endogenous liquidity is pages 28 to 32; private information is pages 32 to 42; arbitrary covariance is pages 43 to 52.
- Implication: Use this source to keep three concepts separate: fragmented venue count, per-pair vehicle leadership and aggregate vehicle concentration. The paper licenses a conditional mechanism by which disconnected fragmentation affects impact and allocation; it does not identify the empirical effect of fragmentation. For the present time series, explicitly test whether aggregator emergence and cross-venue routing reduce siloing, because that change directly weakens the model's isolation channel and may explain market-wide efficiency independently of any vehicle-specific feedback.
