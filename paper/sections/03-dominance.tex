\subsection{A route-level transition}
\label{sec:dominance:transition}

Stablecoins carry a much larger share of vehicle activity in 2026 than in 2024. Comparing the same days of the calendar year, their share among native and stable vehicles rises from \StableCountBase{} to \StableCountEnd{} by route count. The paired-day change is \StableCountChange{}, with a calendar-HAC standard error of \StableCountSE{}. Their value-weighted share rises from \StableValueBase{} to \StableValueEnd{}, an increase of \StableValueChange{} with a standard error of \StableValueSE{}. The count measure gives equal weight to each exact two-leg route. The value measure weights routes by dollar size when the values recorded along the route agree within 20\%. Table~\ref{tab:rotation} reports both because frequent intermediation and intermediation of large trades need not move together.

\subsection{Endpoint-pair composition}
\label{sec:dominance:pairs}

The aggregate rise can come from substitution within an established endpoint market or from changes in the markets in which routed trading takes place. Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition} separates changes in the set of endpoint pairs, shifts in activity across continuing pairs, changes in the use of indirect execution, and replacement of the native asset within continuing vehicle-using pairs.

By route count, endpoint pairs entering or leaving the sample contribute \MarketSupportBridge{} of the increase, activity shifting across continuing pairs contributes \MarketActivityReweight{}, and changes in how often those pairs use an intermediary contribute \VehicleIncidenceReweight{}. The appearance or disappearance of a native-or-stable intermediary contributes \VehicleRoleSupportBridge{}. Direct stable-for-native substitution within pairs that use a vehicle in both years contributes only \WithinPairStableShare{}. Among pairs observed in both endpoint years, stablecoin share still rises from \ObservedBothYearsBase{} to \ObservedBothYearsEnd{}.

The value-weighted decomposition gives a sharper version of the same result. Of the \PairValueTotal{} increase, \PairValueReweight{} comes from activity shifting across continuing pairs and \PairValueExclusive{} from pairs that carry a native-or-stable vehicle route in only one endpoint year. Within-pair substitution contributes \PairValueWithin{}. The increase in stablecoin vehicle value is thus concentrated in the allocation of routed trading across markets; repeated replacement of the intermediary within a typical continuing market contributes little.

The matched-market estimates are tightly bounded relative to the aggregate rotation. Matching the ordered endpoint pair, day of the calendar year, and single- or cross-exchange route scope gives a count-share change of \MatchedMarketCountChange{} with a standard error of \MatchedMarketCountSE{} and a value-share change of \MatchedMarketValueChange{} with a standard error of \MatchedMarketValueSE{}. The corresponding upper confidence bounds are \MatchedMarketCountCIUpper{} and \MatchedMarketValueCIUpper{}. These intervals allow modest substitution inside continuing markets and exclude a change large enough to account for the aggregate increase.

Pair entry can reflect new listings or endpoint demand; activity can move across continuing pairs with demand or liquidity; and indirect execution can respond to direct-market depth or router access. Together, these margins assign most of the stablecoin increase to a reallocation of routed activity across endpoint markets. Native-for-stable substitution within continuing vehicle-using pairs contributes little.

\subsection{USDT and USDC}
\label{sec:dominance:excess}

The category-level increase is concentrated in USDT. To distinguish vehicle use from ordinary demand for the token, we compare each stablecoin's intermediary share with its share of route endpoints. The excess-use ratio exceeds one when a token appears between other assets more often than it appears as the asset bought or sold.

USDC and USDT together account for \JointStableContribution{} of the increase in stablecoin count share, but their paths differ. USDC begins the comparison with excess vehicle use and changes little. USDT's count ratio rises from \USDTCountExcessBase{} to \USDTCountExcessEnd{}, while its value ratio moves from \USDTValueExcessBase{} to \USDTValueExcessEnd{}. The value-weighted difference between USDT's intermediary and endpoint shares rises from \USDTEndpointGapBase{} to \USDTEndpointGapEnd{}, a change of \USDTEndpointGapChange{} with a standard error of \USDTEndpointGapSE{}. USDT therefore becomes more prevalent as an intermediary even after accounting for its growing use as an endpoint.

Reweighting from single- to cross-exchange routes accounts for \USDTVenueMixCountShare{} of USDT's count-share increase and \USDTVenueMixValueShare{} of its supported-value increase. The remaining \USDTVenueWithinCountShare{} by count and \USDTVenueWithinValueShare{} by value occurs within the two venue-scope categories. The decomposition conditions on observed venue span; the feasible alternatives available to the routing system remain unobserved.

The comparison places USDT at the centre of the rotation. Explaining its gain requires separating differences in issuance, redemption access, venue coverage, and the distribution of liquidity across pools. Each could affect intermediary use, and some may themselves respond to routed activity. Section~\ref{sec:rivals} returns to these explanations after establishing how much of the aggregate change can be attributed to observed venue span.

\subsection{Venue span and route complexity}
\label{sec:dominance:integration}

Cross-exchange routing expands sharply during the sample, raising the possibility that broader venue access contributed to the rotation. Access to previously fragmented pools can favour an intermediary with liquidity on several venues. It can also reveal a direct path and reduce the need for any intermediary.

Cross-exchange routes grow from \CrossVenueCountStart{} of route count in 2022 to \CrossVenueCountEnd{} in 2026 and account for \CrossVenueValueEnd{} of supported value in 2026. Over the same period, intermediation becomes less common: its share falls from \FullIntermediationBase{} to \FullIntermediationEnd{} across the full venue set and to \BalancedIntermediationEnd{} on the balanced five-venue perimeter. The changes are \FullIntermediationChange{} and \BalancedIntermediationChange{}, with standard errors of \FullIntermediationSE{} and \BalancedIntermediationSE{}. Cross-exchange trading and the use of an intermediary thus move in opposite directions.

Among routes that remain indirect, stablecoin share rises in every observed category defined by venue span and route complexity. The increase is \CrossVenueRotationPremium{} larger across exchanges than within one exchange, with a standard error of \CrossVenueRotationSE{}. Changes in the mix of these broad route categories therefore explain little of the rotation: the stablecoin gain extends to both single- and cross-exchange routes. Isolating an effect of router access would additionally require comparable endpoint pairs, trade sizes, and feasible paths within each category.
