\subsection{Pair formation and persistence}
\label{sec:dominance:pairs}

Aggregate dominance can change inside continuing pairs or through the distribution of activity across pairs. Let $p$ denote a pair and $y\in\{2024,2026\}$. A continuing pair carries native-or-stable routes in both matched periods; a year-specific pair carries them in only one. First split total activity between these two sets. Within continuing pairs, stablecoin share can change because a given pair changes its vehicle mix or because trading shifts toward pairs with a different vehicle mix. The remaining change comes from the aggregate weight on continuing pairs and the stablecoin share of pairs observed in only one period.

The exact midpoint decomposition holds each companion quantity at its two-period average while the other changes. Its four terms add to the pooled rotation: vehicle switching within continuing pairs, activity shifting across those pairs, the aggregate weight on continuing pairs, and the stablecoin share of pairs present in only one period. We also compare the same pair, month-day, and realised single- or cross-exchange route class with pair-by-calendar-cell effects. Appendix~\ref{app:additional-composition} gives the algebra and matched specification.

Pooling route counts gives an increase from \PairPooledBase{} to \PairPooledEnd{}, or \PairPooledTotal{}, close to the equal-day change in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:rotation}. The within-pair component is \PairPooledWithin{} by route count and \PairValueWithin{} by routed value. These net estimates combine \MarginWithinGrossUp{} from pairs moving toward stablecoins and \MarginWithinGrossDown{} from pairs moving toward the native asset. Activity shifting across continuing pairs contributes \PairPooledReweight{} by count and \PairValueReweight{} by value. The changing aggregate weight on continuing pairs contributes \PairPooledSupportMass{} and \PairValueSupportMass{} (Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition}, panels A and B).

The period-specific contribution is also a net. Pairs first observed after 2024 H1 contribute \PairLifecycleCountEntry{} by route count and \PairLifecycleValueEntry{} by routed value. Reactivation, vehicle-role turnover, and exit together contribute \PairLifecycleCountOffset{} and \PairLifecycleValueOffset{}, leaving the period-specific terms at \PairLifecycleCountNet{} and \PairLifecycleValueNet{}. The underlying rows add exactly before rounding. First-observed pair entry therefore supplies \PairLifecycleCountEntryShare{} of the total route-count rotation and \PairLifecycleValueEntryShare{} of the dollar-weighted rotation. The matched specification reaches the same conclusion on the more restrictive sample: stablecoin share changes by \MatchedMarketCountChange{} by count and \MatchedMarketValueChange{} by value (Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition}, panel C). A large aggregate rotation therefore coexists with little net switching inside established cells defined by pair, calendar date, and route class.

The pattern extends through time and across endpoint sets. In every adjacent January--June comparison from 2019 through 2026, the within-pair term lies between \AdjacentWithinMin{} and \AdjacentWithinMax{} even as the aggregate moves from \AdjacentLargestDecline{} in \AdjacentLargestDeclineYears{} to \AdjacentLargestIncrease{} in \AdjacentLargestIncreaseYears{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:rotation-boundaries}, panel A). Removing every pair with WETH or a stablecoin at either endpoint still gives an increase from \NonvehicleEndpointStableBase{} to \NonvehicleEndpointStableEnd{} by count and from \NonvehicleEndpointValueBase{} to \NonvehicleEndpointValueEnd{} by supported value (panel B). Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:endpoint-direction} then allocates the headline change across endpoint directions and shows that USDT supplies \StableStableUsdtValueContribution{} of the routed-value increase between two stablecoin endpoints. Pair composition therefore explains stablecoin gains and reversals even when endpoint identity leaves both broad vehicle families available.
