\section{The vehicle role under cost dominance}
\label{sec:dominance}

\subsection{Dominance on realised routing}
\label{sec:dominance:realised}

An asset occupies the vehicle role for the trades between other assets that route through it. The classical account of that role turns on the thickness of the markets quoting the asset \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}, with acceptability reinforced by the routing choices of other traders \citep{KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium,LagosWright2005Unified}. One configuration goes untested, an incumbent used at a moment when routing through it was the more expensive way to trade. Foreign exchange cannot observe that state in principle: an incumbent's cost advantage is a consequence of its incumbency, and the counterfactual network of quotes is absent from the data \citep{GopinathStein2021Making,Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. On an automated market maker the counterfactual is a pool that existed at the block of the realised trade, priceable at the state it executed in \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}, and the configuration is common.

Of realised multi-leg routing, 27.2\% was strictly dominated by a direct pool available at the same reconstructed state and size. The figure is population-weighted, it covers 79.0\% of realised routing, and it rests on 1,762 routes matched to a counterfactual, out of 90,705 realised multi-leg routes over four consecutively priced days, 2023-06-01 to 2023-06-04, a sample that binds the estimate and leaves its construction intact. An earlier single-venue measurement of this market put that quantity at 30.0\%. The agreement is not corroboration. The 30.0\% is the retired 17.9\% figure, recomputed on the identical 103,857 routes over the identical 186 days, with a flat gas charge added for the extra hop, which makes it one computation reported twice. It is also an all-in figure, where 27.2\% is gross of gas, and the gas charge on an extra hop is first order at the fee levels these venues clear \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys}, so both differences point one way, and neither supports the newer number.

\subsection{Migration of the role}
\label{sec:dominance:transition}

Dominance incidence, read at one state, is a statement about a moment. The moments in this sample are not exchangeable: the vehicle role itself moves across the period. Value-weighted, the native asset's share of intermediation falls from 73.0\% to 14.8\% over 2,240 days, while the stable numeraire's rises from 21.2\% to 50.1\%, and the value-weighted crossover arrives in 2022-Q1, sustained from 2022-Q4. The count-weighted crossover appears only in 2026-H1, in the last two quarters of the sample, so the count series is described as crossing, and Figure~\ref{fig:transition} plots both series. Section~\ref{sec:rivals} takes up the gap between the two crossover dates, where value moves to the stable numeraire years before the count of routes does, the signature of a transition led by large trades. An intermediary asset is held for the duration of the hop, the cost of its volatility scales with trade size, and a unit pegged to the dollar removes that cost where it is largest \citep{CataliniDeGortariShah2022Stablecoins,LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}.

Two worlds the international currency literature separates in principle are separated here in the data. Under succession one vehicle replaces another and concentration persists, the sterling to dollar transition, and the pre-1914 record of that transition shows persistence in a currency's position without lock-in \citep{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. Under fragmentation the role spreads across several assets and concentration falls, and the incumbent may still lead, the pattern documented for trading venues \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. Switching between vehicles carries the network externality and the switching cost that fall on whoever moves first \citep{DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition}. The value basis here shows both worlds together. The distinction is the one at issue in the dollar's position relative to the euro and the renminbi \citep{GopinathEtAl2020DCP,ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt}, where invoicing and debt shares are observed, while the network of quotes that was available is not \citep{Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem,AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant}.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:dominance:robustness}

The incidence figure is a frequency, a count over states. A frequency is a statement about a state, and it does not require the router to have had a choice. An error in the state at which a route is priced changes the magnitude of the frequency and leaves its kind intact. That property separates this measurement from the retention estimand of Section~\ref{sec:survival}. Section~\ref{sec:executability} measures the error and reports its effect on the classification.

Two restrictions applied throughout move the number. Round trips are excluded before anything is measured, since a route whose first input equals its last output moved no value, and it belongs to the arbitrage traffic that runs across these venues \citep{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage,HeimbachPahariSchertenleib2024NonAtomic}. The support bound of Section~\ref{sec:setting:support} declines to quote any leg whose price impact would exceed 5\%, which removes between 70\% and 86\% of quotable routes and moves the median gap from thousands of basis points to tens. The retired single-venue figure of 17.9\% was constant-product only and unrestricted, and both changes move it upward, which accounts for the whole distance between it and the figures above.
