Comparing the same days of the calendar year, their share among native and stable vehicles rises from \StableCountBase{} to \StableCountEnd{} by route count. \subsection{Composition of source--destination pairs}
\label{sec:dominance:pairs}

The aggregate rise can come from substitution within an established ordered source--destination pair or from changes in the pairs across which routed trading takes place. Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition} separates changes in the set of source--destination pairs, shifts in activity across continuing pairs, changes in the use of indirect execution, and replacement of the native asset within continuing vehicle-using pairs.

By route count, source--destination pairs entering or leaving the sample contribute \MarketSupportBridge{} of the increase, activity shifting across continuing pairs contributes \MarketActivityReweight{}, and changes in how often those pairs use an intermediary contribute \VehicleIncidenceReweight{}. The appearance or disappearance of a native-or-stable intermediary contributes \VehicleRoleSupportBridge{}. Direct stable-for-native substitution within pairs that use a vehicle in both years contributes only \WithinPairStableShare{}, even though stablecoin share rises from \ObservedBothYearsBase{} to \ObservedBothYearsEnd{} among pairs observed in both comparison years. The value-weighted decomposition sharpens this result: of the \PairValueTotal{} increase, \PairValueReweight{} comes from activity shifting across continuing pairs and \PairValueExclusive{} from pairs that carry a native-or-stable vehicle route in only one comparison year, while within-pair substitution contributes \PairValueWithin{}.

A matched comparison reaches the same conclusion. Matching the ordered source--destination asset pair, day of the calendar year, and single- or cross-exchange route type gives a count-share change of \MatchedMarketCountChange{} with a standard error of \MatchedMarketCountSE{} and a value-share change of \MatchedMarketValueChange{} with a standard error of \MatchedMarketValueSE{}. The corresponding upper confidence bounds, \MatchedMarketCountCIUpper{} and \MatchedMarketValueCIUpper{}, allow modest substitution inside continuing pairs but exclude a change large enough to account for the aggregate increase.

Pair entry can reflect new listings or endpoint demand; activity can move across continuing pairs with demand or liquidity; and indirect execution can respond to direct-market depth or router access. Together, these margins assign most of the stablecoin increase to a reallocation of routed activity across source--destination pairs. Native-for-stable substitution within continuing vehicle-using pairs contributes little.

\subsection{USDT and USDC}
\label{sec:dominance:excess}

The category-level increase is concentrated in USDT. To distinguish vehicle use from ordinary demand for the token, we compare each stablecoin's share of intermediary episodes with its share of endpoint appearances on the prespecified currency perimeter. Direct routes contribute to the endpoint denominator, and routes with several intermediaries contribute one episode for each intermediary. The excess-use ratio exceeds one when the token's intermediary share is larger than its endpoint share.

USDC and USDT together account for \JointStableContribution{} of the increase in stablecoin count share, but their paths differ. USDC begins the comparison with excess vehicle use and changes little. USDT's count ratio rises from \USDTCountExcessBase{} to \USDTCountExcessEnd{}, while its value ratio moves from \USDTValueExcessBase{} to \USDTValueExcessEnd{}. The value-weighted difference between USDT's intermediary and endpoint shares rises from \USDTEndpointGapBase{} to \USDTEndpointGapEnd{}, a change of \USDTEndpointGapChange{} with a standard error of \USDTEndpointGapSE{}. USDT therefore becomes more prevalent as an intermediary even after accounting for its growing use as an endpoint.

Reweighting from single- to cross-exchange routes accounts for \USDTVenueMixCountShare{} of USDT's count-share increase and \USDTVenueMixValueShare{} of its supported-value increase. The remaining \USDTVenueWithinCountShare{} by count and \USDTVenueWithinValueShare{} by value occurs within the two venue-scope categories. The decomposition conditions on observed venue span; the feasible alternatives available to the routing system remain unobserved.

The comparison places USDT at the centre of the rotation. Explaining its gain requires separating differences in issuance, redemption access, venue coverage, and the distribution of liquidity across pools. Each could affect intermediary use, and some may themselves respond to routed activity. Section~\ref{sec:rivals} returns to these explanations after establishing how much of the aggregate change can be attributed to observed venue span.

\subsection{Venue span and route complexity}
\label{sec:dominance:integration}

Cross-exchange routing expands sharply during the sample, raising the possibility that broader venue access contributed to the rotation. Access to previously fragmented pools can favour an intermediary with liquidity on several venues. It can also reveal a direct path and reduce the need for any intermediary.

Cross-exchange routes grow from \CrossVenueCountStart{} of route count in 2022 to \CrossVenueCountEnd{} in 2026 and account for \CrossVenueValueEnd{} of supported value in 2026. Over the same period, intermediation becomes less common: its share falls from \FullIntermediationBase{} to \FullIntermediationEnd{} across the full venue set and to \BalancedIntermediationEnd{} on the balanced five-venue perimeter, changes of \FullIntermediationChange{} and \BalancedIntermediationChange{} with standard errors of \FullIntermediationSE{} and \BalancedIntermediationSE{}. Among routes that remain indirect, stablecoin share rises in every observed category defined by venue span and route complexity. The increase is \CrossVenueRotationPremium{} larger across exchanges than within one exchange, with a standard error of \CrossVenueRotationSE{}.

These estimates separate three observed margins: cross-exchange trading, the incidence of intermediation, and the composition of intermediaries. Wider venue span coincides with less intermediation overall, while the stablecoin gain extends to the indirect routes that remain in both single- and cross-exchange groups. Estimating the effect of router access requires comparable ordered source--destination asset pairs, trade sizes, and feasible paths within each category.
