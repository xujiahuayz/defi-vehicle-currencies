\section{Rotation in the vehicle role}
\label{sec:dominance}

\subsection{A route-level transition}
\label{sec:dominance:transition}

The first object is the choice made inside an observed route. For every exact two-leg route $i\rightarrow k\rightarrow o$, the intermediary $k$ receives one vote. Aggregating those votes gives a count measure that does not allow a few large transactions to define the monetary role. A parallel value measure asks whether the same ordering survives when economic size matters. Because route values are not equally well supported, the main value series retains only routes whose intermediary flow is within 20\% of the route's input value. The count and value measures answer different questions and are reported together.

The value margin has moved further than the count margin. Stable vehicles lead by supported value before they sustain leadership by number of routes. That ordering is consistent with a transition led by economically large routes, but it does not identify why those routes moved. A larger value change can reflect liquidity placement, venue reach, router search, or the holding properties of the intermediary. The evidence establishes rotation; the mechanism remains a separate estimand.

Nor is this a monotone displacement. Native intermediation rebounds during 2023 and 2024. Stable value regains the lead from 2025-Q1, whereas count leadership does not persist over a long post-transition window. I therefore use \emph{rotation}, not succession. A date trend would average over the reversal and confound time with the market designs and liquidity states that happened to arrive at particular dates.

\subsection{Intermediation and endpoint demand}
\label{sec:dominance:excess}

A stable asset can become common because traders want to acquire or sell it, without becoming a vehicle between other assets. The excess-use ratio separates the two margins. It divides the asset's share of intermediary positions by its share of route endpoints on the same perimeter. A ratio above one means that the asset appears in the middle more often than endpoint demand alone would imply.

The aggregate shift is not common to all stablecoin designs. USDC and USDT jointly account for \JointStableContribution{} of the increase in stable count share. USDC already has excess vehicle use at the start of the comparison and changes little. USDT supplies the transition margin. Its count excess-use ratio rises from \USDTCountExcessBase{} to \USDTCountExcessEnd{}, while its strict-value ratio moves from \USDTValueExcessBase{} to \USDTValueExcessEnd{}. The strict-value intermediary-minus-endpoint gap moves from \USDTEndpointGapBase{} to \USDTEndpointGapEnd{}, a \USDTEndpointGapChange{} change with a \USDTEndpointGapSE{} standard error.

This decomposition rejects an interpretation based only on growing stablecoin payments. It also rules out language in which ``the stablecoin'' becomes the new vehicle. Fiat-reserve, on-chain-collateralised, and synthetic claims have different balance sheets and redemption technologies. The present result says which token moved into the intermediary position; it does not yet say which of those institutional attributes caused the move.

\subsection{The integration rival}
\label{sec:dominance:integration}

The strongest mechanical rival is the expansion of the trading network. As routers span more venues, they can reach liquidity that was previously fragmented. A stable intermediary might then gain share because its pools become reachable, not because monetary coordination has strengthened. Conversely, the same integration can make direct paths easier to find and reduce the need for an intermediary altogether.

The data show both forces. Cross-venue routes grow from \CrossVenueCountStart{} to \CrossVenueCountEnd{} of route count, reaching \CrossVenueValueEnd{} of value. Yet the share of economic routes that use a true intermediary falls from \FullIntermediationBase{} in 2022 to \FullIntermediationEnd{} in 2026. On a balanced five-venue perimeter it falls to \BalancedIntermediationEnd{}. The declines are \FullIntermediationChange{} and \BalancedIntermediationChange{}, with standard errors of \FullIntermediationSE{} and \BalancedIntermediationSE{}. Integration expands while intermediation contracts.

Within the routes that remain indirect, the stable rotation survives every observed integration-by-complexity count cell. It is also \CrossVenueRotationPremium{} larger across venues than within one venue, with a \CrossVenueRotationSE{} standard error. The evidence therefore rejects two simple accounts: rising stable share merely reflects more multi-venue routes, or a changing number of legs mechanically reclassifies the intermediary.

It does not reject routing technology as a mechanism. Search can activate dispersed stable liquidity even after conditioning on observed venue span and route complexity. The discriminating exercise compares vehicle choice within a fixed endpoint pair, notional, feasible opportunity set, and search-efficiency cell. Until the transaction-state frontier fixes that opportunity set, the cross-venue interaction is descriptive evidence about where the rotation is strongest, not a technology coefficient.

\subsection{Time, architecture, and reversal}
\label{sec:dominance:architecture}

Protocol architecture changes during the same period, but protocol chronology does not convert date into treatment. The current event audit defines entry and exit by realised use of the newest architecture and asks for isolated windows, complete panels, and an unchanged comparison set. Every candidate within-cell substitution event fails at least one of those requirements. Role appearances and disappearances offer some usable observations, but their magnitude is partly mechanical because the event itself is a crossing of zero.

This support failure is informative. Entry into realised architecture exposure is endogenous, and exit reverses exposure without removing the design from the feasible set. A design-reversal claim would require independently measured availability or exact cost and depth states that can move in both directions. Hysteresis would then be an asymmetry: vehicle use retained after the favourable state disappears relative to vehicle use displaced when the state arrives. The present data do not supply that contrast, so no architecture coefficient is promoted.

\subsection{What the route evidence establishes}
\label{sec:dominance:robustness}

The route-only evidence establishes three facts. Vehicle use rotates sharply from the native asset toward stable num\'eraires; the change is stronger by supported value than by route count; and USDT, instead of a pooled stablecoin category, is the principal transition margin. The rotation is not monotone, and it survives observed stratification by venue integration and complexity.

These facts leave the paper's central mechanism contest open. Cost dominance requires exact transaction-state counterfactuals. Persistence requires a final pair-candidate risk set and fixed-effect design. Liquidity provision requires independent external reference prices and validated capital quantities. Those objects are evaluated in the following sections but do not enter the rotation claim before their own support requirements are met.
