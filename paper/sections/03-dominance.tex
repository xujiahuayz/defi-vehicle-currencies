\subsection{Endpoint-pair formation and persistence}
\label{sec:dominance:pairs}

Aggregate dominance can change inside continuing endpoint pairs or through the distribution of activity across pairs. Let $p$ denote an ordered endpoint pair and $y\in\{2024,2026\}$. A continuing pair carries native-or-stable routes in both matched periods; a year-specific pair carries them in only one. Let $W_y$ be the share of native-plus-stable activity in continuing pairs, $\omega_{p,y}$ pair $p$'s weight within that activity, and $s_{p,y}$ its stablecoin share. Continuing pairs have stablecoin share $S_{C,y}=\sum_p\omega_{p,y}s_{p,y}$, while year-specific pairs have share $S_{E,y}$. Writing $\Delta x=x_{2026}-x_{2024}$ and $\bar{x}=(x_{2024}+x_{2026})/2$ gives the exact midpoint decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pair-decomposition}
\Delta S=
\underbrace{\bar{W}\sum_p\bar{\omega}_p\Delta s_p}_{\text{switching within continuing pairs}}
+\underbrace{\bar{W}\sum_p\bar{s}_p\Delta\omega_p}_{\text{activity reallocation}}
+\underbrace{(\bar{S}_C-\bar{S}_E)\Delta W}_{\text{continuing-pair weight}}
+\underbrace{(1-\bar{W})\Delta S_E}_{\text{year-specific pairs}}.
\end{equation}

We also compare the same pair, date within the year, and realised single- or cross-exchange route class. For route-count or routed-value stablecoin share $S^{(m)}_{pds,y}$, the matched regression is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pair-fe}
S^{(m)}_{pds,y}=\alpha^{(m)}_{pds}+\beta^{(m)}\mathbf 1\{y=2026\}+\varepsilon^{(m)}_{pds,y},
\qquad m\in\{N,V\}.
\end{equation}
Here $d$ is the month-day and $s$ the realised route class. Observations are weighted by native-plus-stable route count or supported routed value; standard errors cluster by endpoint pair and date. The comparison is descriptive because route class and trading activity can respond to the same forces as vehicle choice.

Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition} applies Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-decomposition} to route counts and supported value and reports Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-fe} on the matched pair-date-route-class cells.

Endpoint demand sharpens the composition result. A pair with WETH at one endpoint cannot also use WETH as its intermediary, so the native-versus-stable choice is mechanically fixed. Such pairs supply \LockedReweight{} of the \PairPooledReweight{} count reallocation and \LockedNewPair{} of the \MarginNewPairTotal{} entry component. An exact decomposition of the headline daily change by endpoint direction shows that pairs with one native and one stable endpoint contribute \EndpointNativeStableCountContribution{} of the \EndpointCountChange{} count increase and \EndpointNativeStableValueContribution{} of the \EndpointValueChange{} value increase. Pairs with two stable endpoints contribute only \EndpointStableStableCountContribution{} by count but \EndpointStableStableValueContribution{} by value. Within that value channel, USDT alone contributes \StableStableUsdtValueContribution{}; USDC, DAI, and the remaining stablecoin intermediaries together contribute \StableStableOtherValueContribution{}. The stable-to-stable result is present on all \StableStableActiveDays{} common days, and trimming the largest decile of daily contributions leaves a \StableStableTrimmedValueChange{} increase. The largest component still comes from all other endpoint pairs, at \EndpointOtherCountContribution{} by count and \EndpointOtherValueContribution{} by value. Stablecoin dominance therefore spreads beyond pairs whose endpoint identity limits the native vehicle, while routing between stable currencies concentrates on one intermediary issuer.

The first routes in a new pair predict its subsequent vehicle. For entrant pair $p$ first observed on date $t$, let $S^{\mathrm{entry}}_{p,t}$ be its initial stablecoin share and $S_{p,t+h}$ its stablecoin share through horizon $h$. We estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:entry-path}
S_{p,t+h}=\alpha_h+\beta_h S^{\mathrm{entry}}_{p,t}+\gamma_h\mathbf 1\{S^{\mathrm{entry}}_{p,t}>1/2\}+X_{p,t}'\delta_h+\varepsilon_{p,t+h},
\end{equation}
where $X_{p,t}$ contains entry size, cohort year, endpoint class, direct-route share, and route-complexity share. The sample excludes WETH-endpoint pairs and requires complete follow-up support.

\subsection{Stablecoin arrival and network reach}
\label{sec:dominance:formation}

Stablecoin arrival is concentrated among active native-only pairs. For an endpoint pair active on date $t$ with no stablecoin vehicle, define $H_{p,t+30}$ as an indicator that a stablecoin route appears within 30 days. The rolling-hazard specification is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stable-turn-on}
H_{p,t+30}=\alpha+\beta_N\log(1+N_{p,t})+\beta_A\log(1+A_{p,t})+\mu_d+\varepsilon_{p,t+30},
\end{equation}
where $N_{p,t}$ is origin-date route count, $A_{p,t}$ is pair age, and $\mu_d$ absorbs month-day effects. The mixed-vehicle analysis instead compares vehicles inside the same pair-date-route-class set and relates each vehicle's route share to its network reach outside that set.

\subsection{Issuer-level intermediation}
\label{sec:dominance:excess}

USDC and USDT together account for \JointStableContribution{} of the increase in stablecoin route-count share. The direct endpoint decomposition identifies the issuer inside the high-value stable-to-stable channel: USDT contributes \StableStableUsdtValueContribution{}, while USDC, DAI, and the remaining stablecoin intermediaries together contribute \StableStableOtherValueContribution{}. Table~\ref{tab:usdt-transition} asks the narrower question of whether USDT becomes more specialised in intermediation conditional on its observed endpoint demand. A ratio of zero means no intermediary use, one means proportional use in the two roles, and a value above one means the token is overrepresented as an intermediary. This ratio measures role specialisation, not aggregate importance: a currency unused in both roles has a ratio of zero, and a heavily traded endpoint can have a ratio below one despite substantial intermediary use.

\subsection{Endpoint demand within a day}
\label{sec:dominance:withinday}

The issuer comparison can be made within each date. Let $I_{a,t}$ be currency $a$'s share of the day's intermediary episodes and $D_{a,t}$ its share of route-endpoint appearances, both in pp. We estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:within-day-role-regression}
I_{a,t}=\alpha+\beta D_{a,t}+\mathbf A_a'\theta+\mu_t+\eta_a+\varepsilon_{a,t},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf A_a$ contains asset-class indicators, $\mu_t$ date effects, and $\eta_a$ currency effects.

Within a date, cross-exchange routes carry \WithinDayVenueGapValue{} more stablecoin share by routed value than single-exchange routes; the count difference is \WithinDayVenueGapCount{}. Exchange span is selected by the router, so the comparison describes where stablecoin-intermediated value travels and does not identify the effect of widening venue access.
