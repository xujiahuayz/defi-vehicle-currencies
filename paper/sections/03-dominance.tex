\subsection{Ultimate-pair formation and persistence}
\label{sec:dominance:pairs}

Aggregate dominance can change inside continuing ultimate pairs or through the distribution of activity across ultimate pairs. Let $p$ denote an ordered ultimate pair and $y\in\{2024,2026\}$. A continuing ultimate pair carries native-or-stable routes in both matched periods; a year-specific ultimate pair carries them in only one. Let $W_y$ be the share of native-plus-stable activity in continuing ultimate pairs, $\omega_{p,y}$ ultimate pair $p$'s weight within that activity, and $s_{p,y}$ its stablecoin share. Continuing ultimate pairs have stablecoin share $S_{C,y}=\sum_p\omega_{p,y}s_{p,y}$, while year-specific ultimate pairs have share $S_{E,y}$. Writing $\Delta x=x_{2026}-x_{2024}$ and $\bar{x}=(x_{2024}+x_{2026})/2$ gives the exact midpoint decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pair-decomposition}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S={}&
\underbrace{\bar{W}\sum_p\bar{\omega}_p\Delta s_p}_{\text{switching within continuing ultimate pairs}}
+\underbrace{\bar{W}\sum_p\bar{s}_p\Delta\omega_p}_{\text{activity reallocation}}\\
&+\underbrace{(\bar{S}_C-\bar{S}_E)\Delta W}_{\text{continuing-ultimate-pair weight}}
+\underbrace{(1-\bar{W})\Delta S_E}_{\text{year-specific ultimate pairs}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

We also compare the same ultimate pair, date within the year, and realised single- or cross-exchange route class. For route-count or routed-value stablecoin share $S^{(m)}_{pds,y}$, the matched regression is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pair-fe}
S^{(m)}_{pds,y}=\alpha^{(m)}_{pds}+\beta^{(m)}\mathbf 1\{y=2026\}+\varepsilon^{(m)}_{pds,y},
\qquad m\in\{N,V\}.
\end{equation}
Here $d$ is the month-day and $s$ the realised route class. Observations are weighted by native-plus-stable route count or supported routed value; standard errors cluster by ultimate pair and date. The comparison is descriptive because route class and trading activity can respond to the same forces as vehicle choice.

Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition} applies Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-decomposition} to route counts and supported value and reports Equation~\eqref{eq:pair-fe} on the matched ultimate-pair-date-route-class cells.

Endpoint demand sharpens the composition result. An ultimate pair with WETH at one endpoint cannot also use WETH as its intermediary, so the native-versus-stable choice is mechanically fixed. Such ultimate pairs supply \LockedReweight{} of the \PairPooledReweight{} count reallocation and \LockedNewPair{} of the \MarginNewPairTotal{} entry component. An exact decomposition of the headline daily change by endpoint direction shows that ultimate pairs with one native and one stable endpoint contribute \EndpointNativeStableCountContribution{} of the \EndpointCountChange{} count increase and \EndpointNativeStableValueContribution{} of the \EndpointValueChange{} value increase. Ultimate pairs with two stable endpoints contribute only \EndpointStableStableCountContribution{} by count but \EndpointStableStableValueContribution{} by value. Within that value channel, USDT alone contributes \StableStableUsdtValueContribution{}; USDC, DAI, and the remaining stablecoin intermediaries together contribute \StableStableOtherValueContribution{}. The stable-to-stable result is present on all \StableStableActiveDays{} common days, and trimming the largest decile of daily contributions leaves a \StableStableTrimmedValueChange{} increase. The largest component still comes from all other ultimate pairs, at \EndpointOtherCountContribution{} by count and \EndpointOtherValueContribution{} by value. Stablecoin dominance therefore spreads beyond ultimate pairs whose endpoint identity limits the native vehicle, while routing between stable currencies concentrates on one intermediary issuer.

The first routes in a new pair predict its subsequent vehicle. For entrant pair $p$ first observed on date $t$, let $S^{\mathrm{entry}}_{p,t}$ be its initial stablecoin share and $S_{p,t+h}$ its stablecoin share through horizon $h$. We estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:entry-path}
S_{p,t+h}=\alpha_h+\beta_h S^{\mathrm{entry}}_{p,t}+\gamma_h\mathbf 1\{S^{\mathrm{entry}}_{p,t}>1/2\}+X_{p,t}'\delta_h+\varepsilon_{p,t+h},
\end{equation}
where $X_{p,t}$ contains entry size, cohort year, endpoint class, direct-route share, and route-complexity share. The sample excludes ultimate pairs with WETH at an endpoint and requires complete follow-up support.

Network reach, issuer-level specialisation, within-day endpoint demand, and exchange span provide supplementary views of the same formation result. Appendix~\ref{app:additional-composition} reports these estimates. They show that stablecoin arrival is more common in older and more active ultimate pairs, intermediary use moves closely with a currency's endpoint demand within a day, and the stablecoin rotation appears in both single- and cross-exchange routes. These comparisons help locate the result but do not replace the direct decomposition in Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition}.
