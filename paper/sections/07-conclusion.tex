\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The theory of vehicle currencies rests on a state that its usual data cannot contain. If an incumbent is cheap to trade because it is used and used because it is cheap, then observing the incumbent being used tells us nothing about how much of its position the cost advantage is doing, and the moments that would be informative, when the incumbent holds the role while some other path is cheaper, are exactly the moments the counterfactual is unavailable for. This paper takes the question to a currency system where the counterfactual is computable, and finds that those moments are common: on the days priced for it, more than a quarter of realised intermediated routing would have done better going direct at the state it executed in.

That fact is worth having on its own, and it is not the interesting part. What the setting makes possible is the duration question, whether an incumbent that has lost its cost advantage holds the role longer than a challenger with the same advantage takes to take it. That asymmetry is the difference between a market that adjusts slowly in both directions and one that protects whoever arrived first, and it has been out of reach because the two arms have never been observable on the same pairs with the same instrument. Here they are, and this paper specifies both.

Three descriptive results stand independent of that estimand. The vehicle role migrated from the platform's native asset to a stable numeraire within six years. It fragmented at the same time, with the effective number of vehicles rising roughly sixfold as the leader's share fell by seventy per cent, and that combination separates succession from fragmentation in a way currency data cannot. Whether the role also changed hands turns on whether use is counted by value or by transaction, and the paper reports both. And architecture proved not to be destiny: removing the technical mandate to route through the native asset did not reduce native pairing at all.

The paper is candid about what it does not do. It identifies nothing causally, and it substitutes the validation apparatus that the majority of comparable empirical work substitutes, which is a weaker thing and is labelled as such. Its matched sample is a small and non-random slice of realised routing, inverted on the very dimension that drives the outcome, which is why the headline is a reweighted figure and why nothing conditional on trade size generalises. And the state at which routes are priced is coarser than the state a router quotes at, which misclassifies a measurable minority of dominance verdicts and is the reason the survival arms are estimated at block level.

Two directions follow. The first is that the arbitrageur is a measuring instrument this paper has excluded and should use. Round-trip cyclic routes are removed everywhere here as wash trading, and they are the one population that provably carries no habit, since a searcher re-optimises every block and dies if it routes badly. Which asset such a searcher opens and closes its cycles in is a revealed ranking of transaction cost with the stickiness stripped out, and comparing it against the acyclic population would give a habit-free benchmark for the vehicle role. The second is that this system's vehicle role changed hands once already inside six years, which the dollar's has not in eighty. Whatever governs the speed of that turnover is measurable here in a way it is not anywhere else, and the duration estimates this paper specifies are the first quantity to ask it for.
