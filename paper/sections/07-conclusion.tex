\section{Discussion and policy implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

We group stablecoins by their shared vehicle function while retaining the token that supplies each route. USDC, USDT, DAI, and other stablecoins target a common unit of account while differing in issuer, backing, redemption, peg risk, and local pool liquidity. Peg stress can reallocate trading across issuers \citep{LiuMakarovSchoar2023Terra,AnaduEtAl2023StablecoinRuns}. Our family measure captures tokenised-dollar intermediation; the issuer results show how that role is divided within the family. Stable-to-stable endpoint pairs make this distinction especially clear because USDT supplies most of their increase in routed value.

A one-for-one peg aligns prices while preserving currency identity. The Bahamian dollar, for example, is conventionally pegged at one Bahamian dollar per U.S. dollar and remains a separately issued currency with its own monetary and exchange-control arrangements \citep{IMF2025BahamasArticleIV}. The same distinction guides our stablecoin aggregation. A unit-of-account measure would consolidate dollar-pegged instruments. Our route measure records competition among separately issued dollar instruments and between that family and WETH.

The provider results suggest that vehicle networks reproduce themselves through specialised participation. Origins that supplied a vehicle across earlier endpoint pools are more likely to supply that vehicle when a new spoke becomes material. The stablecoin-core estimates are consistent with liquidity in DAI, USDC, and USDT core pools extending into new endpoint pools, although their uncertainty is wider. A newly issued stablecoin may gain convertibility through links to incumbent dollar tokens, and issuer-sponsored or affiliated market makers may seed those links alongside independent providers. Transaction origins reveal participation. Provider identity, affiliation, funding relationships, hedges, fee expectations, and beneficial ownership remain outside the observed records.

Demand for dollar-linked assets offers another interpretation of the upstream allocation. Demand for a stable store of value can raise expected stablecoin trading and fee income, encouraging liquidity provision on stablecoin legs. Traditional models similarly connect demand for safe dollar assets and trade finance to the currency's durable international role \citep{GopinathStein2021Making,ChahrourValchev2022TradeFinance}. Our ETH-stress comparisons capture a narrower implication. In Uniswap v3, the stable-minus-WETH slopes on gross additions and withdrawals are nearly identical and individually imprecise after Holm adjustment; the net coefficient is \EthStressVThreeNetSupplyVolatilityEffect{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:eth-stress-supply-transmission}, panel A). The net response to an ETH decline is \EthStressVThreeNetSupplyDeclineEffect{} with Holm-adjusted \EthStressVThreeNetSupplyDeclineHolmP{}, while its addition and withdrawal components are also imprecise.

ETH prices mechanically revalue deposited capital; route competition depends on the relative weak-leg depth of the stablecoin and WETH alternatives. In the monthly common-support route sample, a 10 percentage point ETH decline predicts \EthStressDeclineRelativeDepthEffect{} log point of stable-relative weak-leg dollar depth, \EthStressDeclineOutputEffect{} of stablecoin exact-output advantage, and \EthStressDeclineChoiceEffect{} of realised stablecoin use (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:eth-stress-supply-transmission}, panel B, first row). An ETH decline can open temporary price discrepancies across endpoint--WETH and endpoint--stablecoin pools. Arbitrage can restore consistent cross-rates, including through WETH--endpoint-token--stablecoin trades when outside markets and trading costs permit \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys}. This process aligns prices. Capital remains a separate margin: holding endpoint price and liquidity units fixed, a constant-product endpoint--WETH pool loses dollar value as WETH becomes cheaper. The near-zero monthly coefficient therefore describes relative weak-leg dollar depth at that horizon; price efficiency requires a high-frequency price-convergence test. Cross-market segmentation can slow that convergence in crypto markets \citep{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage}. Stablecoin use is more closely associated with vehicle-specific capital placement and executable prices than with market-wide ETH declines. Direct measures of stable-asset demand would be needed to separate prospective fee demand from issuer support and provider specialisation.

Relative-price risk can also influence where capital is supplied. While two stablecoins hold their pegs, their relative price typically varies less than either token's price against WETH. In a constant-product pool, smaller relative-price movements reduce loss versus holding, commonly called impermanent or divergence loss. Lower prior endpoint--vehicle risk predicts deeper constant-product bridge capital at the measurement date and 30 days later (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:bridge-lp-risk}, panel A, columns 1--4). Stablecoin bridges nevertheless have higher median relative volatility than WETH bridges and carry the lower risk in fewer than one third of comparable pair-months (panel B). Divergence risk helps locate bridge capital locally and leaves room for expected fees, hedging, issuer support, and network experience to account for the aggregate rotation. Impermanent loss is distinct from loss-versus-rebalancing, the running adverse-selection cost of stale \ac{amm} prices \citep{MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR}.

Vehicle use connects otherwise unrelated pairs to the liquidity and issuer conditions of the asset in the middle. Concentration can lower trading costs through deeper two-leg markets, while fragmentation can raise price impact \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. The same concentration transmits disruption across many endpoint pairs. This is a network form of commonality in liquidity \citep{ChordiaRollSubrahmanyam2000Commonality}. Payment-system design faces the same extensive margin. Nexus assigns foreign-exchange conversion to a provider, and Rialto uses a third currency when direct exchange is unavailable \citep{BISNexusFX2024,BISRialto2025}. Access policy can affect both the price quoted inside an established relationship and which two-sided markets form early enough to become the bridge for new ones. Welfare analysis would additionally require user surplus, provider returns, and the incidence of vehicle-specific risk.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vehicle dominance can rotate even when established trading relationships show almost no net substitution. Stablecoin use rises sharply from 2024 H1 to 2026 H1 because activity shifts toward pairs that already use stablecoins and because new pairs first appear using stablecoin routes. Movements in both directions remain substantial within continuing pairs, yet they nearly cancel in the aggregate. The vehicle present when a pair enters then predicts its later vehicle mix.

Gross stable-facing liquidity additions rotate with the trading network. Stable-facing additions rise on both an action and dollar basis, period-specific transaction origins account for much of the change, and experienced vehicle suppliers are more likely to appear when new pools become material. Capital on both required legs precedes route adoption and predicts the durability of exact-price leadership. Together, these results associate later vehicle use with the network of funded endpoint--vehicle links.

Executable prices transmit that network into realised vehicle use. A router responds to the price offered for the current trade, while prior capital on both legs predicts whether the vehicle retains its lead. Together, the estimates suggest that dominant vehicle currencies are made through liquidity networks and sustained through the prices those networks deliver. The resulting concentration lowers exchange costs and spreads vehicle-specific risks across trading relationships.
