\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Realised trading paths make vehicle-currency use directly observable. They separate the assets that ever intermediate a trade from the smaller set that carries most routed activity. In our sample, stablecoins become the leading vehicles by value relative to native assets, although the transition reverses more than once. USDT accounts for most of the increase within the stablecoin category.

Most of the aggregate increase comes from changes in the endpoint pairs observed, shifts in trading across continuing pairs, and changes in how often those pairs use an intermediary. Stable-for-native switching within matched continuing markets is small and imprecisely estimated. This is an accounting result: the appearance of an endpoint pair in the route sample does not by itself show that a market was newly created.

The familiar liquidity feedback begins with currency choice inside an established market: greater use deepens liquidity and makes future use more attractive. Our decomposition points to an additional empirical margin. A currency can gain aggregate route share as trading moves toward endpoint pairs connected to its pools, even when few matched pairs replace one intermediary with another. Whether this reallocation reflects market creation, endpoint demand, liquidity supply, or router reach remains an open mechanism question.

Liquidity provision, venue reach, endpoint demand, and routing software may explain why activity moved toward USDT-centred routes; realised paths alone cannot separate these mechanisms. The paths establish that the stablecoin gain is concentrated in USDT and occurs mainly through changes in routed activity and route structure rather than repeated stable-for-native replacement within matched continuing markets.
