\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vehicle dominance on decentralised exchanges has shifted markedly from native assets toward stablecoins. The shift is neither monotone nor a uniform replacement of one intermediary by another. Native assets regain share in 2023 and 2024 before stablecoins recover the value lead, and USDT's intermediary use rises relative to its appearance at the observed endpoints of pool routes. Most of the aggregate rotation comes from changes in the source--destination pairs observed and the distribution of activity across continuing pairs. Stable-for-native substitution within matched pairs is close to zero.

Vehicle-currency theories usually emphasise a reinforcing choice for a given pair of assets: trading deepens an intermediary's markets, lower costs attract more trading, and the vehicle role persists. The decomposition shows why aggregate evidence alone cannot reveal that choice. A vehicle's share can change sharply as routed activity moves across source--destination pairs even when its use within matched pairs barely changes.

This distinction sets the empirical target for explanations of changing dominance. Relative execution costs must be evaluated within comparable opportunity sets. Liquidity supply, router reach, asset risk, and protocol architecture must be linked to subsequent route use. Pair reallocation alone does not establish those mechanisms. The path data make the tests possible, but the route-composition result does not prejudge them.

Aggregate vehicle dominance combines two observed allocations: the intermediary used within a matched source--destination pair and the pairs over which routed activity occurs. The stablecoin rotation runs primarily through the second.
