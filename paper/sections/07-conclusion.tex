\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vehicle currencies are usually observed through aggregate turnover, quotation patterns, or prices. Realised routes reveal the role directly. They also reveal why vehicle status and vehicle dominance are different objects: many assets can appear in the middle of a trade, while only a few carry a large share of those choices.

In decentralised exchange, vehicle use rotates sharply but not monotonically from the native asset toward fiat-backed stablecoins. Stablecoins lead by routed value in 2022, lose that lead in 2023 and 2024, and regain it in 2025. Their count share moves later. Within the stablecoin category, USDT accounts for the principal increase in intermediary use relative to its endpoint use. This is a change in the asset carrying routes, not simply a rise in trading demand for stablecoin endpoints.

The expansion of cross-venue routing does not mechanically explain the rotation. Cross-venue reach expands while the overall use of intermediaries contracts, and the stablecoin shift also appears within observed route types. Better search may nevertheless help traders reach dispersed stablecoin liquidity. Protocol launches do not isolate this channel because changes in architecture coincide with pair entry, exit, and other market changes.

The route evidence therefore establishes a non-monotonic change in vehicle leadership, but not its cause. Distinguishing monetary coordination from liquidity reallocation, router access, and software defaults requires the same ordered endpoint pairs to be followed through reversals in relative cost and liquidity. Direct trading need not eliminate a vehicle currency: the role depends on where liquidity is placed and which alternatives routers can reach, not only on whether a direct pool exists.
