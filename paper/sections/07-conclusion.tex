\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Ordered admitted pool-call sequences reveal whether an asset was bought or sold or carried an exchange between two other assets, a distinction that pair-level turnover conceals. On decentralised exchanges, this distinction shows a substantial rotation from native assets toward stablecoins. The path includes reversals in 2023 and 2024 before stablecoins regain the value lead. Within the same prespecified route universe, USDT's share of intermediary episodes rises relative to its share of endpoint appearances. Most of the rotation comes from changes in the pairs observed, shifts in activity across continuing pairs, and changes in how often those pairs use an intermediary. Stable-for-native substitution within matched continuing pairs is small, and its confidence interval excludes a change large enough to account for the aggregate movement.

Vehicle-currency theories usually emphasise a reinforcing choice for a given pair of assets: trading deepens an intermediary's markets, lower costs attract more trading, and the vehicle role persists. Our evidence adds the allocation of trading across source--destination pairs. Aggregate dominance changes as activity moves toward pairs connected through a different vehicle, even when intermediary substitution within matched pairs is modest.

The transition from Uniswap v1 to v2 provides a descriptive setting in which a pairing mandate ended. V1 required token-to-token trades to cross ETH; v2 allowed arbitrary ERC-20 pairs. Native-asset pairing remained common after the requirement disappeared. The persistence is an economic fact; attributing it to the earlier architecture would require a fixed opportunity set.

Changing vehicle dominance therefore joins two allocations: the intermediary used for a given source and destination, and the source--destination pairs to which trading flows. Vehicle-currency theory has concentrated on the first; the stablecoin transition in our data runs primarily through the second. A theory of changing dominance must explain both. The making of a dominant vehicle currency is also the making of the markets in which it is used.
