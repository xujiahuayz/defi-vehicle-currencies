\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vehicle currencies are usually observed through aggregate turnover, quotation patterns, or prices. Realised routes reveal the role directly. They also reveal why vehicle status and vehicle dominance are different objects: many assets can appear in the middle of a trade, while only a few carry a large share of those choices.

In decentralised exchange, that share rotates sharply from the native asset toward fiat-backed stablecoins. The change is late and non-monotone. It is more advanced by supported value than by route count, and USDT drives the increase after endpoint demand is removed. Market integration is not a complete mechanical explanation: cross-venue reach expands while the overall use of intermediaries contracts, and stable rotation remains inside observed integration and complexity cells. Routing technology may nevertheless be part of the mechanism because it changes which dispersed liquidity a trader can reach.

The negative result on architecture is equally useful. Realised protocol-share entry and exit do not yet furnish clean substitution events, and calendar time is not a treatment. A convincing design test must distinguish exposure from availability and ask whether vehicle use reverses when the underlying cost or liquidity state reverses. That is also the relevant test of hysteresis.

The remaining work separates three explanations that the descriptive rotation cannot adjudicate: whether vehicle use persists despite an executable cheaper route, whether liquidity placement leads or follows vehicle choice, and whether router search activates a stable vehicle within a fixed opportunity set. The transparent route graph makes those questions measurable. Their answers, and not the chronology alone, will determine whether this episode is best understood as monetary coordination, market integration, or a change in market design.
