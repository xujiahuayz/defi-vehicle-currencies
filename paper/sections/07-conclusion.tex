\section{Interpretation and implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

We group stablecoins by their shared vehicle function while retaining the token that supplies each route. USDC, USDT, DAI, and other stablecoins target a common unit of account while differing in issuer, backing, redemption, peg risk, and local pool liquidity. Peg stress can reallocate trading across issuers \citep{LiuMakarovSchoar2023Terra,AnaduEtAl2023StablecoinRuns}. Our family measure captures tokenised-dollar intermediation; the issuer results show how that role is divided within the family. Stable-to-stable endpoint pairs make this distinction especially clear because USDT supplies most of their increase in routed value (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:endpoint-direction}, panel B).

A one-for-one peg aligns prices while preserving currency identity. The Bahamian dollar, for example, is conventionally pegged at one Bahamian dollar per U.S. dollar and remains a separately issued currency with its own monetary and exchange-control arrangements \citep{IMF2025BahamasArticleIV}. The same distinction guides our stablecoin aggregation. A unit-of-account measure would consolidate dollar-pegged instruments. Our route measure records competition among separately issued dollar instruments and between that family and WETH.

The provider results suggest that vehicle networks reproduce themselves through specialised participation. Origins that supplied a vehicle across earlier endpoint pools are more likely to supply that vehicle when a new spoke becomes material. The stablecoin-core estimates are consistent with liquidity in DAI, USDC, and USDT core pools extending into new endpoint pools, although their uncertainty is wider. A newly issued stablecoin may gain convertibility through links to incumbent dollar tokens, and issuer-sponsored or affiliated market makers may seed those links alongside independent providers. Transaction origins reveal participation. Provider identity, affiliation, funding relationships, hedges, fee expectations, and beneficial ownership remain outside the observed records.

Demand for dollar-linked assets offers another interpretation of the upstream allocation. Demand for a stable store of value can raise expected stablecoin trading and fee income, encouraging liquidity provision on stablecoin legs \citep{CataliniDeGortariShah2022Stablecoins,LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}. Traditional models similarly connect demand for safe dollar assets and trade finance to the currency's durable international role \citep{GopinathStein2021Making,ChahrourValchev2022TradeFinance}. Monthly growth in circulating stablecoin supply provides a broad equilibrium proxy for this demand. Its four coefficients for later core or spoke capital growth and first-link formation are imprecise after Holm adjustment (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:stablecoin-supply-lp}, panels A and B). Circulating supply combines issuance, redemption, transaction demand, and portfolio demand. It is too coarse to distinguish user demand from issuer support.

\subsection{Provider actions and price revaluation}
\label{sec:liquidity:eth-accounting}

Aggregate stress can change market-making capacity in traditional markets \citep{BrunnermeierPedersen2009Liquidity,Nagel2012EvaporatingLiquidity}. In Uniswap v3, the stable-minus-WETH slopes on gross additions and withdrawals are nearly identical and individually imprecise after Holm adjustment; the net coefficient is \EthStressVThreeNetSupplyVolatilityEffect{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:eth-stress-supply-transmission}, panel A, column 1). The coefficient on an ETH-price fall is \EthStressVThreeNetSupplyDeclineEffect{} with Holm-adjusted value (\EthStressVThreeNetSupplyDeclineHolmValue{}), while its addition and withdrawal components are also imprecise (column 2). Endpoint-by-week comparisons isolate stablecoin-minus-WETH differences in decoded external flows. Total activity, within-pool range management, and adjustments between external actions remain separate margins.

Provider choices establish pools and their liquidity units. Prices and reserve adjustment can then change dollar capital before another provider action. After arbitrage aligns the marginal cross-price in a constant-product pool between endpoint $A$ and WETH $W$, its dollar capital is
\[
V^{A,W}=2\sqrt{kP_A P_W}.
\]
Holding $k$ and the endpoint's dollar price $P_A$ fixed, the elasticity of $V^{A,W}$ to the WETH price $P_W$ is one half. A 0.10-log-point ETH-price fall therefore lowers WETH-facing dollar capital by about 5\%, while a stablecoin-facing pool has no corresponding $P_W$ revaluation. Triangular arbitrage changes reserve ratios to align cross-prices when the gap exceeds fees and other trading costs; it does not align dollar capital across pools \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys}.

We separate provider and price margins in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 by selecting, for each nonstable endpoint and date, material stablecoin-facing and WETH-facing pools and holding that pool set fixed for one, three, or seven days. For each vehicle family, a symmetric decomposition assigns the log change in dollar capital, $V$, to changes in square-root reserves, $\sqrt{k}$, and unit value, $V/\sqrt{k}$. Square-root reserves can change through provider actions, retained swap fees, and token transfers. Unit value combines token-price revaluation with reserve adjustment.

A 0.10-log-point fall in ETH's dollar price, about 9.5\%, corresponds to \EthVTwoAccountingCapitalApproxPercentRange{} more stablecoin-relative dollar capital over the three horizons (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:eth-decline-v2-accounting}, column 1). The $\sqrt{k}$ component ranges from \EthVTwoAccountingQuantityApproxPercentRange{} and is imprecise after Holm adjustment (column 2). Unit value contributes \EthVTwoAccountingUnitValueOneDayEffectApproxPercent{}, \EthVTwoAccountingUnitValueThreeDayEffectApproxPercent{}, and \EthVTwoAccountingUnitValueSevenDayEffectApproxPercent{} after one, three, and seven days (column 3). The three- and seven-day estimates are numerically close to the conditional \EthVTwoAccountingUnitValueBenchmarkApproxPercent{} constant-product reference. For \EthVTwoAccountingSinglePricedEndpointShare{} of primary intervals, WETH-side capital equals twice the WETH reserve value because the endpoint lacks an anchored price; the WETH price therefore enters both predictor and outcome. Decoded external flows appear in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:eth-stress-supply-transmission}, panel A. Daily closing states supply the comparison, and only pools with a surviving validated state enter each horizon.

The monthly route sample separately relates trailing ETH-price declines to prior-day V2 and SushiSwap v2 weak-leg capital, exact output for the observed trade size, and realised vehicle choice. Its stable-relative capital coefficient is \EthStressDeclineRelativeDepthEffect{}, and the exact-output and route-use coefficients are \EthStressDeclineOutputEffect{} and \EthStressDeclineChoiceEffect{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:eth-stress-supply-transmission}, panel B, first row). Pool membership and the binding leg can change between monthly observations; all three coefficients are near zero. At intraday frequency, a 0.10-log-point ETH decline is associated with a \EthIntradayDeclineOutputEffect{} stablecoin output lead and \EthIntradayDeclineChoiceEffect{} stablecoin use; both estimates are imprecise (panel C, first row). Conditional on the ETH move, a 100 basis point exact-output advantage predicts \EthIntradayOutputChoiceEffect{} higher stablecoin use (second row). The short-horizon evidence separates dollar revaluation of standing positions from vehicle prices and use. Direct measures of stable-asset demand remain necessary to separate prospective fee demand from issuer support and provider specialisation.

Relative-price risk can also influence where capital is supplied. While two stablecoins hold their pegs, their relative price typically varies less than either token's price against WETH. This low-risk argument applies most directly to stablecoin-core pools; endpoint--stablecoin spokes still bear endpoint-token price risk. In a constant-product pool, smaller relative-price movements reduce loss versus holding, commonly called impermanent or divergence loss. Lower prior endpoint--vehicle risk is associated with larger constant-product weak-leg capital at the measurement date and 30 days later (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:bridge-lp-risk}, panel A, columns 1--4). Stablecoin two-leg connections nevertheless have higher median relative volatility than WETH connections and are the lower-risk alternative in fewer than one third of comparable pair-months (panel B). The aggregate rotation may also reflect expected fees, hedging, issuer support, and network experience. Impermanent loss is distinct from loss-versus-rebalancing, the running adverse-selection cost of stale \ac{amm} prices \citep{MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR}.

Vehicle use connects otherwise unrelated pairs to the liquidity and issuer conditions of the asset in the middle. Concentration can lower trading costs through deeper two-leg markets, while fragmentation can raise price impact \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. The same topology exposes many endpoint pairs to a common vehicle-specific disruption. This is a network form of commonality in liquidity \citep{ChordiaRollSubrahmanyam2000Commonality}. Payment-system design faces the same extensive margin. Nexus assigns foreign-exchange conversion to a provider, and Rialto uses a third currency when direct exchange is unavailable \citep{BISNexusFX2024,BISRialto2025}. Access policy can affect both the price quoted within an established pair and which vehicle links form early enough to support new pairs. Welfare analysis would additionally require user surplus, provider returns, and the incidence of vehicle-specific risk.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vehicle dominance can rotate even when established trading relationships show almost no net substitution. Stablecoin use rises sharply from 2024 H1 to 2026 H1 because activity shifts toward pairs that already use stablecoins and because new pairs first appear using stablecoin routes. Movements in both directions remain substantial within continuing pairs, yet they nearly cancel in the aggregate. The vehicle present when a pair enters then predicts its later vehicle mix.

Gross stable-facing liquidity additions rotate with the trading network. Stable-facing additions rise on both an action and dollar basis, period-specific transaction origins account for much of the change, and prior same-vehicle participation by an origin predicts its participation when a new pool becomes material. Before a pair's first observed stablecoin route, measured capital on both required legs is associated with a \BridgeAdoptionRiskAnySupport{} higher weekly probability of first use; conditional on support, a tenfold increase in stablecoin relative to WETH weak-leg capital adds \BridgeAdoptionRiskIntensiveTenfold{} (Table~\ref{tab:bridge-adoption-risk}, panel A, columns 1--2). Together, these results associate later vehicle use with funded endpoint--vehicle links.

Exact-output rankings predict realised use, while prior capital on both legs predicts route allocation and one-month price-lead persistence. Providers choose funded links and liquidity units; prices revalue standing positions between those actions. The estimates are consistent with vehicle dominance forming through funded networks and persisting when those networks deliver competitive quotes. Lower-output retention is infrequent and modest by input value. Common vehicle liquidity lowers exchange costs but transmits vehicle-specific risk across unrelated pairs.
