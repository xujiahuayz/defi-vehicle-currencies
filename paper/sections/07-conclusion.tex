\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Complete trading paths reveal whether an asset was bought or sold or carried an exchange between two other assets, a distinction that pair-level turnover conceals. Applying this distinction to decentralised exchanges shows a substantial rotation from native assets toward stablecoins, with reversals in 2023 and 2024 before stablecoins regain the value lead. USDT drives the increase, and its intermediary use rises relative to its presence at the endpoints of the same routes. The rotation occurs chiefly through the allocation of routed trading across source--destination pairs: most of the increase comes from changes in the pairs observed, shifts in activity across continuing pairs, and changes in how often those pairs use an intermediary, while stable-for-native substitution within matched continuing pairs is small and its confidence interval excludes a change large enough to account for the aggregate movement.

Vehicle-currency theories usually emphasise a reinforcing choice for a given pair of assets: trading deepens an intermediary's markets, lower costs attract more trading, and the vehicle role persists. Our evidence adds the allocation of trading across source--destination pairs. Aggregate dominance changes as activity moves toward pairs connected through a different vehicle, even when intermediary substitution within matched pairs is modest.

The institutional contrast between Uniswap v1 and v2 illustrates why protocol rules and market formation must be separated. Uniswap v1 required token-to-token trades to cross ETH, whereas Uniswap v2 allowed arbitrary ERC-20 pairs. Native-asset pairing remained common after the requirement disappeared, although that persistence alone does not identify the effect of the architecture.

Changing vehicle dominance therefore joins two allocations: the intermediary used for a given source and destination, and the source--destination pairs to which trading flows. The first allocation is the familiar focus of vehicle-currency theory. The second carries the stablecoin transition in our data. A theory of changing vehicle dominance must explain both.
