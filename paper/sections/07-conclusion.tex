\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vehicle dominance on decentralised exchanges has shifted markedly from native assets toward stablecoins through a reorganisation of the trading network. Net stable-for-native substitution is close to zero across matched ultimate pairs without conditioning on route feasibility because positive and negative pair-level movements offset; the aggregate moves with activity reallocating toward ultimate pairs that already route through stablecoins and with ultimate pairs observed in only one comparison year. The bridge-establishment sample identifies the local liquidity margin. During the first month, stable weak-leg depth is below one-tenth of WETH depth on \BridgeDepthThinFirstDays{} of \BridgeDepthDoseFirstRows{} comparable ultimate-pair days. Within a bridge event, a 10 percentage point increase in stable relative depth predicts \BridgeDepthDoseFirstCoef{} higher stable route share. That share reaches \BridgeDepthEqualFirstShare{} once stable depth reaches WETH depth and \BridgeDepthDoubleFirstShare{} when stable depth is at least twice WETH depth. Native assets regain share in 2023 and 2024 before stablecoins recover the value lead, and USDT's intermediary use rises relative to its appearance at the observed endpoints of pool routes.

Vehicle-currency theories usually emphasise a reinforcing choice for a given ultimate pair of assets: trading deepens an intermediary's markets, lower costs attract more trading, and the vehicle role persists. The decomposition adds the network distribution of trading to this account. A vehicle gains aggregate share when it becomes more common within an ultimate pair, when activity shifts toward ultimate pairs that already use it, or when newly active ultimate pairs route through it. In our sample, the latter two margins dominate. This distinction changes how aggregate vehicle shares should be interpreted: a rising share can reflect a reorganisation of trading toward parts of the network in which the challenger already connects markets even when positive and negative changes inside continuing ultimate pairs cancel. Models and empirical tests that hold the ultimate pair fixed describe the first margin; aggregate turnover combines both.

The mechanism evidence locates where such a role is made. Stable vehicles appear in new and repeatedly active ultimate pairs, and their route share rises with the relative deposited depth of the two-leg bridge. Deposited capital is a proximate liquidity-formation state variable; executable depth and provider-supply shocks are distinct objects. The entry analysis separately documents persistence in the first intermediary's identity. Liquidity provision moves on a slower margin: deposited capital remains native-heavy, route-capital gaps covary with later capital-share convergence and provider activity, and the USDC/SVB window shows stable-route demand rising while capital shifts toward WETH. The capital-share convergence regressions retain a mechanical mean-reversion component, so they do not identify provider reallocation. Route opportunities, local depth, entry identity, and provider activity therefore move together on distinct horizons.

The accounting is not specific to decentralised exchanges. A currency's share of settled trade can decline while every corridor that uses it today continues to do so, provided trade grows fastest along corridors that never adopted it. The renminbi arrangements between China and Brazil described in Section~\ref{sec:intro} illustrate one such corridor. Aggregate currency shares are always measured over a set of corridors that is itself changing, and the two margins quantified here are the margins through which such a decline would run. Records that report only the legs of an exchange cannot separate them from a change of intermediary within a corridor. A transaction log that preserves the whole path can.
