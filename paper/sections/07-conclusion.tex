\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Complete trading paths reveal whether an asset was bought or sold or carried an exchange between two other assets, a distinction that pair-level turnover conceals. Applying it to decentralised exchanges shows a substantial rotation from native assets toward stablecoins, with reversals in 2023 and 2024 before stablecoins regain the value lead. USDT drives the increase, and its intermediary use rises relative to its presence at the endpoints of the same routes.

The rotation occurs chiefly through the allocation of routed trading across markets. Most of the increase comes from changes in the endpoint pairs observed, shifts in activity across continuing pairs, and changes in how often those pairs use an intermediary. Stable-for-native substitution within matched continuing markets is small, and its confidence interval excludes a change large enough to account for the aggregate movement.

Vehicle-currency theories usually emphasise a reinforcing choice within an established market: trading deepens an intermediary's markets, lower costs attract more trading, and the vehicle role persists. Our evidence adds the allocation of trading across markets. Aggregate dominance changes as activity moves toward endpoint markets connected by a different vehicle, even when intermediary substitution within matched markets is modest. The transition from Uniswap v1 to v2 illustrates how market design can move this margin: V2 converted ETH intermediation from a protocol rule into a market choice, while native-asset pairing remained dominant on that venue. Changing vehicle dominance therefore joins two allocations, the intermediary a market uses and the markets to which trading flows. The second allocation carries the stablecoin transition in our data and belongs at the centre of theories of vehicle-currency change.
