\section{Discussion and policy implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

We group stablecoins by their shared vehicle function while retaining the token that supplies each route. USDC, USDT, DAI, and other stablecoins can target the same unit of account while differing in issuer, backing, redemption, peg risk, and local pool liquidity. The family-level result measures the rise of tokenised-dollar intermediation; the issuer-level estimates show how that role is allocated within the family. Stable-to-stable endpoint pairs identify this distinction directly because USDT supplies most of their increase in routed value.

A one-for-one peg aligns prices while preserving currency identity. The Bahamian dollar, for example, is conventionally pegged at one Bahamian dollar per U.S. dollar and remains a separately issued currency with its own monetary and exchange-control arrangements \citep{IMF2025BahamasArticleIV}. The same distinction disciplines our stablecoin aggregation. A currency-equivalent measure would consolidate dollar-pegged instruments by unit of account; our route measure records competition among separately issued dollar instruments and between that family and WETH.

This distinction may also affect liquidity supply. While two stablecoins hold their pegs, their relative price typically varies less than either token's price against WETH. In a constant-product pool, smaller relative-price movements imply smaller loss versus holding, commonly called impermanent or divergence loss. That risk profile may make stablecoin--stablecoin links attractive to liquidity providers and help tokenised dollars build dense cross-issuer markets. Lower prior endpoint--vehicle risk predicts deeper constant-product bridge capital at the measurement date and 30 days later (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:bridge-lp-risk}, panel A, columns 1--4). Stablecoin bridges nevertheless have higher median relative volatility than WETH bridges and carry the lower risk in fewer than one third of comparable pair-months (panel B). Divergence risk helps explain where capital sits; it does not account for the aggregate stablecoin rotation. Impermanent loss remains distinct from loss-versus-rebalancing, the running adverse-selection cost of stale \ac{amm} prices \citep{MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR}.

A newly issued stablecoin also gains convertibility by linking to incumbent dollar tokens, so issuer-sponsored or affiliated-market-maker capital may seed cross-issuer pools alongside independent liquidity supply. Deposited capital reveals the pool stock; provider identities, funding relationships, entry prices, fee income, hedges, and expectations determine profitability. We therefore use these channels as economic interpretations of pool formation.

Vehicle use connects otherwise unrelated pairs to the liquidity and issuer conditions of the asset in the middle. This concentration can lower trading costs through deeper two-leg markets while transmitting a disruption in those markets across many endpoint pairs. The pair-entry estimates show why route allocation may adjust slowly after those links form. These are market-structure implications of observed route allocation. Welfare effects require additional evidence on user surplus, provider returns, and the incidence of stablecoin risk.

Payment-system design faces the same extensive margin. Nexus assigns foreign-exchange conversion to a provider, and Rialto uses a third currency when direct exchange is unavailable \citep{BISNexusFX2024,BISRialto2025}. The route evidence suggests that access policy affects more than the price quoted inside an existing relationship. It also affects which two-sided markets form early enough and deeply enough to become the bridge for new relationships.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Stablecoin vehicle dominance rises sharply on decentralised exchanges. Between matched January--June periods in 2024 and 2026, the equal-day stablecoin share among native and stable intermediaries rises from \StableCountBase{} to \StableCountEnd{} by route count and from \StableValueBase{} to \StableValueEnd{} by routed value (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:rotation}). In the pooled decomposition, movements toward and away from stablecoins offset inside continuing pairs, while activity shifting across continuing pairs contributes \PairPooledReweight{} by route count. Pairs first observed after 2024 H1 contribute \PairLifecycleCountEntry{} before reactivation, vehicle-role turnover, and exit reduce the period-specific net to \PairLifecycleCountNet{} (Table~\ref{tab:pair-composition}, panel A). First-observed pair entry contributes \PairLifecycleValueEntry{} by routed value (panel B). Longer routes show the same movement.

The vehicle used at pair entry predicts later use after the entry day is removed from every outcome (Table~\ref{tab:entry-persistence}). First-month stablecoin use rises from \BridgeTimingShallowMonthShare{} below one-tenth of WETH depth to \BridgeTimingCompetitiveMonthShare{} once that threshold is reached (Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment}, panel C); capital on the weaker leg builds before first use (Figure~\ref{fig:bridge-adoption-capital-path}). The exact stablecoin-versus-WETH contest sharpens the persistence result. The incumbent-retention coefficient on challenger price leadership is \ContestChallengerEffect{}, while the coefficient on a 10 pp incumbent lagged full-range constant-product capital advantage is \ContestCapitalAdvantageTenPpEffect{} after current output advantage is held fixed (Table~\ref{tab:contestable-vehicle-choice}, columns 2 and 3). The price--capital interaction is \ContestPriceCapitalInteractionEffect{} in column 4 and points toward a larger capital association when the incumbent's quote is weaker. Among routes that retain a mature incumbent, the input-value-weighted exact-output shortfall is \ContestRetainedForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} (Table~\ref{tab:contestable-vehicle-consequences}, panel B). Incumbent retention tracks the current price ranking and remains higher where the incumbent has more lagged full-range constant-product capital.

Aggregate dominance combines use within continuing relationships with the vehicle identity of new ones. The large stablecoin rotation comes mainly from pair entry and where trading grows across continuing pairs. Early vehicle use and two-sided depth tie new relationships to the markets around them; contemporaneous prices determine how firmly that tie holds. Currency competition therefore operates inside established relationships and when new relationships form the markets that later support them.
