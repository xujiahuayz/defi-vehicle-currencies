\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Stablecoins become dominant vehicle currencies on decentralised exchanges through pair formation and local liquidity. Between matched January--June periods in 2024 and 2026, their share among native and stable intermediaries rises from \StableCountBase{} to \StableCountEnd{} by route count and from \StableValueBase{} to \StableValueEnd{} by routed value. Positive and negative substitutions offset inside continuing pairs. Trading grows in continuing pairs that already use stablecoins more intensively, and pairs present in only one period supply the largest additional component. The same rotation appears on routes with one intermediary and on longer routes with several intermediary positions.

The vehicle used at pair entry predicts later use. A 1 pp higher initial stablecoin share predicts \FormationPathEntryShareOneTwentyCoef{} higher stablecoin share over 120 days. Local capital helps explain when another vehicle can compete. First-month stablecoin use is \BridgeTimingShallowMonthShare{} when its two-leg bridge has less than one-tenth of WETH depth and \BridgeTimingCompetitiveMonthShare{} once it reaches that threshold. Among eventual users, capital on the weaker stablecoin leg rises before the first observed stablecoin route.

Exact pre-transaction prices separate venue competition from vehicle competition. Opening all exact venues finds a better same-vehicle quote on \ExactFrontierSameVehicleShare{} of common-support routes. Opening other vehicles and the direct path raises that incidence by \ExactFrontierVehicleAddition{} and lowers stablecoin vehicle share by \ExactFrontierStableDecline{}. Public prices frequently favour another venue while preserving the intermediary asset.

The distinction extends beyond decentralised exchange. Cross-border payment systems still require an institution to convert the source currency into the destination currency. Project Nexus assigns this function to an FX provider, and Project Rialto uses a vehicle currency when direct exchange is unavailable \citep{BISNexusFX2024,BISRialto2025}. Aggregate dominance in such systems reflects both the vehicle selected inside continuing trading relationships and the vehicles inherited by relationships that enter and grow. For a currency seeking a larger international role, the evidence points to two complementary margins: establish deep markets on both sides of new trading relationships, and be available when those relationships form. Price improvement inside an established relationship addresses only the first margin after the route structure already exists.

A vehicle currency is made at two connected points: when a new trading relationship adopts it and when liquidity on both legs makes the route competitive. Aggregate dominance records both.
