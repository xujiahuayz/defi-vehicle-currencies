\section{The returns to liquidity provision behind the vehicle role}
\label{sec:liquidity}

\subsection{Fee revenue against the loss to arbitrageurs}
\label{sec:liquidity:accounting}

Vehicle routes concentrate in deep pools, but those pools exist only because liquidity providers commit capital. If providers move capital slowly away from the incumbent's two legs, liquidity can preserve a vehicle role after its relative cost changes. The return to supplying this depth is therefore part of the persistence question. \citet{GrossmanMiller1988Liquidity} set the price of immediacy against the inventory risk a market maker carries. \citet{AmihudMendelson1980Dealership} and \citet{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer} show how inventory and return risk enter dealer quotes, while \citet{Nagel2012EvaporatingLiquidity} uses short-horizon reversals as a proxy for gross returns to liquidity provision. An automated market maker replaces the dealer quote with an invariant but leaves liquidity providers exposed to fees, price risk, and the cost of adjusting their positions \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

Providers earn swap fees and bear two costs: loss-versus-rebalancing and the gas paid to add or remove liquidity. Write $W_{p,t}$ for the contemporaneous dollar value of pool $p$ on day $t$, $C_{p,t-1}$ for deposited capital on the prior calendar day, $V_{p,t}$ for dollar volume, $\phi_p$ for the pool's swap fee, and $n_{p,t}$ for the number of mints and burns. With $g$ denoting the gas used by one liquidity event and $\varpi_tP_t$ its dollar price, the daily return before scaling by capital is
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{p,t}=\phi_pV_{p,t}-\mathrm{LVR}_{p,t}-n_{p,t}g\varpi_tP_t.
\label{eq:rent}
\end{equation}

For a constant-product pool, \citet{MilionisMoallemiRoughgardenZhang2022LVR} show that the instantaneous loss-versus-rebalancing rate equals one eighth of the external reference price's variance rate times pool value. Over a day,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{LVR}_{p,t}=\frac{W_{p,t}}{8}\int_t^{t+1}\sigma^2_{p,u}\,du
=\frac{W_{p,t}}{8}\mathrm{RV}_{p,t},
\qquad
\mathrm{RV}_{p,t}=\sum_h\bigl(\Delta\log P^{\mathrm{ext}}_{p,h}\bigr)^2,
\label{eq:lvr}
\end{equation}
where $P^{\mathrm{ext}}_{p,h}$ is an external reference price. Inverting the price changes the sign of each log return but not its square. This loss is related to, but distinct from, classical adverse selection: \citet{GlostenMilgrom1985BidAsk} price private-information order flow into a spread, whereas loss-versus-rebalancing measures arbitrage against a stale pool price under its own external-price and frictionless-arbitrage assumptions. \citet{KleinKozhanViswanathNatrajWang2026InformedLP} document that signed liquidity additions and withdrawals predict short-horizon returns. Their result describes provider timing but does not measure providers' losses.

Dividing by lagged capital does not eliminate the contemporaneous pool-value scale:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{p,t}\equiv\frac{\mathrm{LVR}_{p,t}}{C_{p,t-1}}
=\frac{W_{p,t}}{C_{p,t-1}}\frac{\mathrm{RV}_{p,t}}{8}.
\label{eq:lvrrate}
\end{equation}
The ratio $W_{p,t}/C_{p,t-1}$ must therefore be measured directly. Net yield per lagged dollar supplied is
\begin{equation}
y_{p,t}=\frac{\Pi_{p,t}}{C_{p,t-1}}
=\frac{\phi_pV_{p,t}}{C_{p,t-1}}
-\frac{W_{p,t}}{C_{p,t-1}}\frac{\mathrm{RV}_{p,t}}{8}
-\frac{n_{p,t}g\varpi_tP_t}{C_{p,t-1}}.
\label{eq:netyield}
\end{equation}

Estimating \eqref{eq:netyield} requires deposited capital and an external reference price measured on a consistent calendar. The historical Uniswap V3 balance series does not meet that requirement because local virtual depth is not invested capital and cannot scale provider returns. We therefore report no provider-return estimate from that series. The Uniswap V2 estimates are also withheld while deposited capital and contemporaneous pool value are reconstructed on the calendar used in \eqref{eq:netyield}.
