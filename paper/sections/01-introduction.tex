\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The U.S. dollar appears on more than 85\% of foreign-exchange transactions, far more often than the United States' share of cross-border economic activity would suggest \citep{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. Its use as a vehicle between other currencies helps explain this prominence. Vehicle use concentrates trading in dollar markets, deeper markets lower trading costs, and lower costs reinforce the dollar's intermediary role \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies,KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium,FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. This feedback explains the persistence of an established vehicle, so the central question is how vehicle dominance changes when new routes and market technologies emerge.
% literature/text/2026-Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX-dollar-dominance-in-fx-trading.txt; dollar involvement exceeds 85 percent

Vehicle dominance has two margins: the intermediary chosen for a given ultimate pair and the allocation of activity across ultimate pairs. A 2025 agreement between China and Brazil to establish renminbi clearing arrangements illustrates the second margin. As bilateral activity moves toward a new settlement link, the dollar's aggregate share can fall even if its role in every existing dollar-settled relationship persists. We need data that attach each transaction's intermediary to the ultimate exchange it serves.\footnote{See the \href{https://www.pbc.gov.cn/en/3688241/3688636/3688657/2025080817522611695/index.html}{memorandum of understanding} between the People's Bank of China and the Banco Central do Brasil. The comparison is illustrative; the arrangement and its consequences lie outside our evidence.}

Standard foreign-exchange records report turnover by atomic currency pair without linking the two legs of an ultimate exchange.\footnote{For example, see the \href{https://www.bis.org/statistics/triennialrep/guidelines_cbanks.htm}{BIS 2025 Triennial Survey reporting guidelines and templates}.} A yen--euro exchange routed through dollars therefore appears as separate yen--dollar and dollar--euro trades. \citet{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX} uses atomic-pair volume responses around non-overlapping holidays to infer which trades serve a vehicle role, but those inferences are indirect. In \ac{defi}, logs preserve the connected pool sequence, so we observe vehicle role directly.

An ultimate pair is an intended source--destination pair from a user's instruction. A trade may be executed as one atomic pair or several atomic pairs across smart-contract liquidity pools. We reconstruct connected routes in 472 million swaps on eight Ethereum \ac{amm} deployments between February 2020 and June 2026. Each token sequence identifies the ultimate source and destination, the intermediary, and movement across exchanges. This links observed atomic trades to the route it serves and distinguishes vehicle use from appearance at either endpoint.

Stablecoins, including USDC, USDT, DAI, and other pegged tokens, become substantially more prominent vehicles during the sample. Comparing the same January-through-June dates in 2024 and 2026, stablecoin dominance rises from \StableCountBase{} to \StableCountEnd{} by route count and from \StableValueBase{} to \StableValueEnd{} by dollar-weighted volume. Within continuing ultimate pairs, this margin changes little in net because pair-level switches partly offset. The aggregate rise comes from activity moving toward pairs that already rely more on stablecoins and from ultimate pairs present in only one comparison year.

The rotation unfolds unevenly over time and across tokens. Native assets regain share in 2023 and 2024 before stablecoins recover the dollar-weighted lead in 2025. Within the stablecoin class, USDT's intermediary use rises especially sharply relative to its appearance at route endpoints. The stablecoin gain also appears on routes confined to one exchange and on routes spanning several exchanges.

Local liquidity helps explain which vehicle carries an ultimate trade once an alternative route becomes available. During the first month after a persistent stablecoin bridge forms, stable depth on the shallower atomic leg is below one-tenth of WETH depth on most ultimate-pair days. Stablecoins carry \BridgeDepthEqualFirstShare{} of routes when that depth is at least WETH depth and \BridgeDepthDoubleFirstShare{} when it is at least twice WETH depth. The dollar cost of the annual difference between the median gas units used by stablecoin and direct routes, evaluated at each year's median gas and WETH prices, falls from \GasStableExtraCostBase{} in 2024 to \GasStableExtraCostEnd{} in 2026. Deposited depth and fixed execution costs thus describe two conditions under which a stablecoin route can compete; executable all-in prices and exogenous liquidity-supply changes remain separate objects.

Directly observed routes change the interpretation of vehicle-currency dominance. They show that a large aggregate rotation can arise through the trading network even when continuing ultimate pairs exhibit little net substitution. This network-composition margin complements the holiday-based inference of \citet{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX} and the historical turnover evidence of \citet{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. The depth results then connect route allocation to the self-reinforcing liquidity mechanism in classical vehicle-currency theory and to fragmented-market research in which liquidity supply shapes the opportunities available to traders \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies,ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.
