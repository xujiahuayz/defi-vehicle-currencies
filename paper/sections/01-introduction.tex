\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A small number of currencies carry most of the world's indirect exchange. A trade between two minor currencies can go through a third, and modern foreign-exchange evidence estimates a disproportionate dollar share of that vehicle use \citep{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. \citet{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies} accounts for concentration with a volume-cost externality: vehicle use expands transaction volume in two bilateral markets, lower brokerage costs reinforce that use, and under some payment structures the feedback supports more than one resting point. Search-theoretic monetary theory derives multiplicity from primitives \citep{KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium,LagosWright2005Unified}, and \citet{DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition} add switching costs, which hold an established medium in place once traders have settled on it.

The account is old and widely accepted, and it carries a measurement problem no exchange-rate dataset resolves. An incumbent's cost advantage, on that account, is an equilibrium consequence of its incumbency. The state that would price the role, an asset in the vehicle position at a moment when routing through it is the more expensive available way to trade, is the state the record never holds. Exchange-rate series carry the path taken, they are silent on the path passed over, and the cost of a declined path is therefore missing at every date in the sample. Whether the role is worth anything once the cost advantage disappears cannot be asked of that record.

This paper takes the question to a currency system where the state is recorded. Decentralised exchange on Ethereum settles trades into public pools, and the reserves of any pool at any past block are recoverable \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,Schar2021DeFi,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. Quoting a route the market declined uses the invariant the venue applied to the route it took, at the block the declined route would have settled in. A trader moving from one asset to another through a third leaves a direct pool joining the two endpoints, that pool has a known state at the block the route settled in, and the cost of the path passed over is computable to the basis point. The configuration foreign exchange cannot observe is therefore observable here, and it is common: of realised routing that passed through an intermediary, 27.2\% would have done better going direct at the state it executed in, on 1,762 matched routes over four consecutively priced days.

The setting also removes a channel of inertia, one the currency literature cannot switch off. Over-the-counter trading requires bilateral search before a quote \citep{DuffieGarleanuPedersen2005OTC}. Aggregate interdealer data do not reveal routes or counterparties, so they cannot separate search or dealer-relationship channels from other sources of dollar vehicle use \citep{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. Route selection here is performed by graph optimisers, they re-solve at every quote, they carry no memory of yesterday's path, and the searchers who close any residual mispricing re-optimise block by block \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys,HeimbachPahariSchertenleib2024NonAtomic}.

Whatever incumbency remains has to operate through state variables, namely the placement of liquidity, the set of pairs that exist, and the depth quoted on each. That object is narrower than the one foreign exchange studies, and a null on it speaks to those state variables while staying silent on habit. An incidence is a statement about states, and the inertia literature asks about duration. The estimand is one event, leadership on a pair passing to a different asset between consecutive days, conditioned on the dominance status of the leading asset the day before.

Both conditions run on one set of pairs and one set of days, with dominance read at the block each route executed in. Over sixty consecutive days the role changes hands on 12.88\% of the pair-days whose incumbent was losing on cost, compared with 11.01\% of the pair-days whose incumbent was the cheapest available, a turnover hazard ratio of 1.17 across 1,553 pair-days, of which 109 sit in the undominated condition. Strict cost dominance raises the daily chance of losing the role by about a sixth of its base rate. A large ratio is the competitive case in which the role tracks cost, and a ratio near one is incumbent protection no symmetric friction generates.

Posing the same question as two duration arms answers something else. One arm times how long a dominated incumbent leads, and the other times how long a cheaper challenger takes to take over. The first spell ends when any candidate takes the lead, the second asks about one named candidate, so the first is a minimum over the candidate set and the second a single draw from it. The gap between the two arms widens with the number of candidates quoted on a pair. Section~\ref{sec:survival} reports both arms as description, with that arithmetic attached, and estimates the hazard ratio as the quantity the single-event comparison delivers.

Three descriptive results frame the exercise, and each concerns how a vehicle role is made and unmade. The role migrated. Value-weighted, the native platform asset's share of intermediation falls from 73.0\% to 14.8\% across six years, the stable numeraire's rises from 21.2\% to 50.1\%, and a dollar-denominated claim now sits at the centre of a market that opened around a native unit of its own. The value-weighted crossover arrives in 2022-Q1, and the count-weighted crossover only in 2026-H1.
% docs/finding-intermediation-transition.md, value-weighted and count-weighted intermediation shares

The role also fragmented. The effective number of vehicles, the inverse of the Herfindahl index over intermediation shares, rises from 1.42 to 8.43 by value and from 1.86 to 7.31 by transaction count. The leader's share falls from 83.7\% to 25.5\% by value and from 71.5\% to 27.9\% by count, which makes the spreading common to both ways of counting use. A change of hands is a separate question, and the two bases disagree on it. Leadership alternates between the native asset and the stable numeraire from 2022 onward on the value basis alone, and on the count basis the native asset leads in all seven years of the sample.
% output/exhibits/vehicle_concentration.jsonl, share_volume and share_count bases

Read together, migration and fragmentation separate two worlds the currency literature can state and cannot distinguish. Succession keeps concentration where it is and changes the identity of the leader, the sterling-to-dollar transition. Fragmentation lowers concentration and can leave the incumbent leading, the dollar's position relative to the euro and the renminbi. The distinction here is an empirical taxonomy defined by the joint movement of leader identity and concentration, not a result imported from a venue-fragmentation model. The third result is that architecture did not determine the outcome. The platform withdrew a technical mandate to route all token-to-token trades through the native asset, native pairing held after the withdrawal, and the share of newly created pairs quoted on the native asset rose from 84.1\% to 97.9\%.

Four accounts would each produce a role outlasting its cost advantage, and Section~\ref{sec:rivals} runs them as a horse race under the labels \emph{slow capital}, \emph{integration scope}, \emph{holding cost} and \emph{defaults}. Under \emph{slow capital}, finite risk-bearing makes immediacy costly \citep{GrossmanMiller1988Liquidity}, while capital and margin constraints can make liquidity supply state-dependent \citep{BrunnermeierPedersen2009Liquidity} and participation can withdraw when risk rises \citep{AnandVenkataraman2016MarketMaking}; the hypothesis specific to this setting is that AMM depth then reallocates slowly across routes, which requires provider-position and flow evidence. Under \emph{integration scope} a cheaper route outside an aggregator's search universe is a route no trader using that aggregator can take, and the duration is the cadence at which that search universe changes. Under \emph{holding cost} the volatility of the asset carried across the hop scales with size, and large trades abandon a volatile vehicle before small ones do. Under \emph{defaults} routing software and pool-creation templates name the incumbent before any trader chooses, and the duration is a software release cadence with no allocative content.

The paper reports one rejection and one leader. The size gradient \emph{holding cost} requires is absent from the data, and \emph{defaults} leads on the only completed test that came out in an account's favour. Nothing below is identified causally. In place of an identifying design stand a data advantage, the counterfactual network of quotes, and the validation apparatus that licenses reading it.

A venue's pricing function is accepted only after reproducing swaps that executed, at 0.0000\% median error on three of the six venue families and 0.033\% on a fourth. Past pool state is reconstructed by unwinding backward from stored period-end values, because replaying forward returns a median 11.8\% error and returns it silently. The panel declines to quote any route whose price impact in the pool it would traverse exceeds 5\%, a bound read off the distribution of 932,270 realised trades, and that restriction removes most quotable routes and moves the median measured gap from thousands of basis points to tens. Section~\ref{sec:executability} then asks whether the remaining gaps were takeable, since an atomic cycle paying after fees and gas should be closed by the searchers who monitor each block.

The relation to four literatures follows. The theory of vehicle currencies explains the concentration of exchange on a small number of media, and it predicts multiple equilibria supported by thick-market externalities \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies,KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium,DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition}. Modern descendants of that theory carry the dominant currency into price setting, and into balance sheets. \citet{GopinathEtAl2020DCP} and \citet{Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem} put a dominant invoicing currency at the centre of international transmission, \citet{AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant} recover the invoicing choice from firm-level data, \citet{GopinathStein2021Making} joins the trade-invoicing role to the banking one, and \citet{ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt} derive the currency denomination of debt from the same forces. These sources study role-specific concentration without observing the market-wide set of alternative trading routes and quote paths at the state in which a trade executes. The empirical extension here is to price those alternatives and measure the value of the role after its cost advantage is gone.

\citet{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics} supply the closest empirical precedent. Working on a matrix of nineteenth-century quotations, they measure a currency's international standing by the number of foreign markets quoting it, a degree statistic on that matrix, and they estimate the feedback running between a currency's international circulation and its interest rate. The product of the two feedback parameters is 0.463, below the unit value at which the dynamics would become explosive, and their reading is persistence without lock-in. Two things separate the exercise here from theirs. A degree statistic counts the markets quoting a currency, it is silent on how much trade routes through it, and the data used here separate quotation from realised use. Quotation records also carry no counterfactual cost for the route declined, and that cost is the quantity the turnover hazard conditions on.
% literature/text/2009-FlandreauJobst2009Empirics-*.txt, published Section 4.1 and Table 3 column III

Market microstructure supplies the price of immediacy, through the inventory a liquidity supplier carries and through the informed order flow it faces. \citet{AmihudMendelson1980Dealership} derives a spread from inventory risk, while \citet{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer} separates a risk-bearing spread from the quote-midpoint adjustment induced by current inventory; \citet{GlostenMilgrom1985BidAsk} and \citet{Kyle1985ContinuousAuctions} derive pricing from adverse selection. Empirical descendants trace inventory, intermediary wealth and price pressure through realised positions and spreads \citep{ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories,HendershottMenkveld2014PricePressures,GarleanuPedersenPoteshman2009DemandBased,CoughenourSaad2004CommonMarketMakers}. That literature prices one asset's spread against one counterparty. The object here is a choice among paths joining the same two endpoints, where every leg carries a spread of its own, and the microstructure results enter as the cost decomposition on each leg.

Work on decentralised exchange has established the cost structure this paper routes over. \citet{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage} document cross-venue price deviations in cryptocurrency that persist for weeks and name the frictions holding them open, \citet{MakarovSchoar2022DeFi} and \citet{Schar2021DeFi} set out the institutional architecture, and \citet{LeharParlour2024Uniswap} model the automated market maker as a competitive market in liquidity provision. \citet{MilionisMoallemiRoughgarden2023Myersonian} characterise adverse-selection incentives, while \citet{KleinKozhanViswanathNatrajWang2026InformedLP} document short-horizon return predictability from signed liquidity additions and withdrawals; \citet{CaparrosChaudharyKlein2024BlockchainScaling} instead measure active repositioning, liquidity concentration and hypothetical single-pool slippage. The unit of analysis throughout that work is a pool or a venue. The unit here is the intermediating asset, and the question is which asset a market keeps routing through once the pools quoting it stop being the cheapest way across.
