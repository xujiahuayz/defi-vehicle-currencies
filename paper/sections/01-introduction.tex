\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A small number of currencies carry most of the world's indirect exchange. A trade between two minor currencies is executed by going through a third, and the third is almost always the dollar. \citet{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies} accounts for that arrangement with a thick-market externality: a currency is cheap to trade because many others trade it, and many others trade it because it is cheap. Search-theoretic monetary theory derives that multiplicity from primitives \citep{KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium,LagosWright2005Unified}, and \citet{DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition} add the switching costs that make an established medium hard to dislodge once adopted. The account is old and widely accepted, and it carries a measurement problem that no exchange-rate dataset resolves. An incumbent's cost advantage is an equilibrium consequence of its incumbency. The state that would price the role, an asset occupying the vehicle position at a moment when routing through it is the more expensive available way to trade, stays outside the record.

This paper takes that question to a currency system where the state is recorded. Decentralised exchange on Ethereum settles each trade into public pools whose reserves at any past block can be recovered, and quoting a route the market declined uses the invariant the venue applied to the route it took \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,Schar2021DeFi,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. When a trader moves from one asset to another through a third, the direct pool joining the two endpoints has a known state at that block, and the cost of the path declined is computable to the basis point. The configuration that foreign exchange cannot observe is therefore observable here, and it is common: of realised routing that passed through an intermediary, 27.2\% would have done better going direct at the state it executed in, on 1,762 matched routes over four consecutively priced days.

The setting also removes a channel the currency literature cannot switch off. Dealer relationships and the search that precedes a quote are what carry inertia in over-the-counter currency trading \citep{DuffieGarleanuPedersen2005OTC}, and dollar dominance in interdealer foreign exchange is estimated on markets where those relationships are themselves the object under study \citep{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. Route selection here is performed by graph optimisers that re-solve at each quote and carry no memory of yesterday's path, and the searchers who close any residual mispricing re-optimise block by block \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys,HeimbachPahariSchertenleib2024NonAtomic}. Whatever incumbency remains has to operate through state variables, namely the placement of liquidity and the set of pairs that exist. That is a narrower object than the one foreign exchange studies, and a null on it speaks to those state variables while staying silent on habit.

An incidence is a statement about states, and the inertia literature asks about duration. Given that an incumbent has been beaten on cost, by how much does that raise the chance the vehicle role changes hands? The estimand is one event, leadership on a pair passing to a different asset between consecutive days, conditioned on whether the leading asset was dominated the day before. Both conditions are evaluated on one set of pairs, over one set of days, with dominance read at the block each route executed in. Over sixty consecutive days the role changes hands on 12.88\% of the pair-days whose incumbent was losing on cost, compared with 11.01\% of the pair-days whose incumbent was the cheapest available, a turnover hazard ratio of 1.17 across 1,553 pair-days, of which 109 sit in the undominated condition. Strict cost dominance raises the daily chance of losing the role by about a sixth of its base rate. A large ratio is the competitive case in which the role tracks cost, and a ratio near one is incumbent protection that no symmetric friction generates.

Posing that question as two duration arms, one timing how long a dominated incumbent leads and the other timing how long a cheaper challenger takes to take over, answers something else. The first spell ends when any candidate takes the lead while the second asks about one named candidate, so the first is a minimum over the candidate set and the second a single draw from it, and the gap between them widens with the number of candidates quoted on a pair. Section~\ref{sec:survival} reports both arms as description, with that arithmetic attached to them, and estimates the hazard ratio as the quantity the single-event comparison delivers.

Three descriptive results frame the exercise, and each concerns how a vehicle role is made and unmade. The role migrated. Value-weighted, the native platform asset's share of intermediation falls from 73.0\% to 14.8\% across six years while the stable numeraire's rises from 21.2\% to 50.1\%, putting a dollar-denominated claim at the centre of a market that opened around a native unit of its own \citep{CataliniDeGortariShah2022Stablecoins,LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins,GortonZhang2023Wildcat}. The role also fragmented. The effective number of vehicles rises from 1.42 to 8.43 by value and from 1.86 to 7.31 by transaction count, while the leader's share falls from 83.7\% to 25.5\% by value and from 71.5\% to 27.9\% by count, making the spreading common to both ways of counting use. Whether the role also changed hands is a separate question and the two bases disagree on it: leadership alternates between the native asset and the stable numeraire from 2022 onward on the value basis alone, and the native asset leads in all seven years of the sample on the count basis.

Reading migration and fragmentation together separates two worlds the currency literature can state and cannot distinguish. Succession keeps concentration where it is and changes the identity of the leader, the sterling-to-dollar transition. Fragmentation lowers concentration and can leave the incumbent leading, the dollar's position relative to the euro and the renminbi, and it is the outcome a market splitting across venues and instruments tends toward \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. The third result is that architecture did not determine the outcome. When the platform withdrew a technical mandate to route all token-to-token trades through the native asset, native pairing held, and the share of newly created pairs quoted on it rose from 84.1\% to 97.9\%.

Four accounts would each produce a role that outlasts its cost advantage, and Section~\ref{sec:rivals} runs them as a horse race under the labels \emph{slow capital}, \emph{integration scope}, \emph{holding cost} and \emph{defaults}. Under \emph{slow capital} the depth backing a vehicle is committed capital moving on the cadence of an allocation decision, the friction that makes liquidity supply slow to arrive where it is wanted \citep{GrossmanMiller1988Liquidity,BrunnermeierPedersen2009Liquidity,Nagel2012EvaporatingLiquidity,AnandVenkataraman2016MarketMaking}. Under \emph{integration scope} a cheaper route that no aggregator has integrated is a route no trader can take, placing the binding friction on the routing layer and making the duration a cadence of business decisions taken at the aggregators \citep{HendershottJonesMenkveld2011Algorithmic,LiWangYe2021WhoProvides}. Under \emph{holding cost} the volatility of the asset carried across the hop scales with size, and large trades abandon a volatile vehicle before small ones do. Under \emph{defaults} routing software and pool-creation templates name the incumbent and the duration is a software release cadence with no allocative content. The paper reports one rejection and one leader. The size gradient that \emph{holding cost} requires is absent from the data, and \emph{defaults} leads on the only completed test that came out in an account's favour.

Nothing below is identified causally. In place of an identifying design stands a data advantage and the validation apparatus that licenses it. A venue's pricing function is accepted only after reproducing swaps that executed, at 0.0000\% median error on three of the six venue families and 0.033\% on a fourth. Past pool state is reconstructed by unwinding backward from stored period-end values, because replaying forward returns a median 11.8\% error and returns it silently. The panel declines to quote any route whose price impact in the pool it would traverse exceeds 5\%, a bound read off the distribution of 932,270 realised trades, which removes most quotable routes and moves the median measured gap from thousands of basis points to tens. Section~\ref{sec:executability} then asks whether the remaining gaps were takeable. An atomic cycle paying after fees and gas should be closed by the searchers who monitor each block, and a gap that persists after those costs is evidence about the pricing engine.

The relation to four literatures follows. The theory of vehicle currencies explains why exchange concentrates on a small number of media and predicts multiple equilibria supported by thick-market externalities \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies,KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium,DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition}. Its modern descendants carry the dominant currency into price setting and into balance sheets: \citet{GopinathEtAl2020DCP} and \citet{Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem} put a single invoicing currency at the centre of international transmission, \citet{AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant} recover the invoicing choice from firm-level data, \citet{GopinathStein2021Making} joins the trade-invoicing role to the banking one, and \citet{ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt} derive the currency denomination of debt from the same forces. Each is estimated on a system whose alternative paths are unpriced, which is why the theory's central comparative static has stayed theoretical. Here the alternative paths are priced, and what the role is worth once the cost advantage is gone becomes a measured quantity.

\citet{FlandreauJobst2006Empirics} supply the closest empirical precedent. Working on a matrix of nineteenth-century quotations, they measure a currency's international standing by the number of foreign markets quoting it, which is a degree statistic on that matrix, and they estimate the feedback running between a currency's international circulation and its interest rate. The product of the two feedback parameters is 0.463, below the unit value at which the dynamics would become explosive, and their reading is persistence without lock-in. Two things separate the exercise here from theirs. A degree statistic counts the markets that quote a currency and is silent on how much trade routes through it, and the data used here separate quotation from realised use. Quotation records also contain no counterfactual cost for the route declined, and that cost is precisely what the turnover hazard conditions on.

Market microstructure supplies the price of immediacy, through the inventory a liquidity supplier carries and through the informed order flow it faces. \citet{AmihudMendelson1980Dealership} and \citet{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer} derive the spread from inventory risk, \citet{GlostenMilgrom1985BidAsk} and \citet{Kyle1985ContinuousAuctions} derive it from adverse selection, and the empirical descendants trace both channels through realised inventories and price pressures \citep{ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories,HendershottMenkveld2014PricePressures,GarleanuPedersenPoteshman2009DemandBased,CoughenourSaad2004CommonMarketMakers}. That literature prices one asset's spread against one counterparty. The object here is a choice among paths joining the same two endpoints, where every leg carries a spread of its own, and the microstructure results enter here as the cost decomposition on each leg.

Work on decentralised exchange has established the cost structure this paper routes over. \citet{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage} document cross-venue price deviations in cryptocurrency that persist for weeks and defend them by naming the frictions that keep them open, \citet{MakarovSchoar2022DeFi} and \citet{Schar2021DeFi} set out the institutional architecture, \citet{LeharParlour2024Uniswap} model the automated market maker as a competitive market in liquidity provision, and the adverse-selection cost borne by liquidity providers is characterised in \citet{MilionisMoallemiRoughgarden2023Myersonian} and measured in \citet{KleinKozhanViswanathNatrajWang2026InformedLP} and \citet{CaparrosChaudharyKlein2024BlockchainScaling}. The unit of analysis throughout that work is a pool or a venue. The unit here is the intermediating asset, and the question is which asset a market keeps routing through once the pools quoting it stop being the cheapest way across.
