\section{Quoter construction and validation}
\label{app:quoters}

The constructed side of every comparison in the body is an exact-input quote: given a pool's state and an input amount, what the pool would have paid out. Each venue family is priced on its own invariant in the venue's own arithmetic, and each quoter was accepted only after reproducing swaps that executed. Acceptance is decided by achieved error relative to realised output, because a type string is a label and an error is a measurement. The two families that need a parameter the subgraph does not serve, Curve's amplification coefficient and Balancer's weight ratio, identify that parameter by fitting realised trades, which turns an assumption into a testable restriction on data the panel already carries.

\subsection{Constant-product pools}
\label{app:quoters:cp}

Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 price the product of reserves \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. With input reserve $R_{\mathrm{in}}$, output reserve $R_{\mathrm{out}}$, input amount $x$ and the fee factor $\phi$, an exact-input swap moves the pool along
\[
(R_{\mathrm{in}} + \phi x)\,(R_{\mathrm{out}} - y) \;=\; R_{\mathrm{in}} R_{\mathrm{out}},
\]
which inverts in closed form to
\[
y(x) \;=\; \frac{\phi\, x\, R_{\mathrm{out}}}{R_{\mathrm{in}} + \phi\, x}, \qquad \phi = \tfrac{997}{1000},
\]
the 30 basis point fee both venues charge, applied on chain as the integer ratio above.
% src/ddvc/cpquote.py, FEE_NUM and FEE_DEN, Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2

A multi-leg route composes these one hop at a time, threading the output token of one pool into the next, and the route-cost gap the body reports is the basis-point margin by which the best direct quote beats the best intermediated quote at the same state and the same size,
\[
g \;=\; 10^{4}\,\frac{q_{\mathrm{direct}} - q_{\mathrm{via}}}{q_{\mathrm{via}}}.
\]
Both sides of $g$ are computed from one reconstructed state, which is the property the realised-trade comparison lacked and the reason the counterfactual exists at all.

Validation is direct: quote each executed swap's own input against the state standing immediately before it and compare with what the swap paid. Across 8,024 executed swaps the median absolute error is 0.0000\%, with 96.7\% of quotes inside 1\% and 95.2\% inside 0.01\% of realised output. The archive-node quoter this replaces reached 93.7\% inside 1\% on the same class of comparison.
% src/ddvc/cpquote.py module docstring, 8,024 executed swaps

\subsection{Concentrated liquidity}
\label{app:quoters:cl}

Uniswap v3 and v4 distribute reserves across price ranges, so a pool has no single reserve pair and a quote has to walk the price axis \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3}. Inside one range the pool behaves as a constant-product pool with virtual reserves, and liquidity $L$ with price $P$ satisfies
\[
L = \sqrt{x\,y}, \qquad \sqrt{P} = \sqrt{y/x},
\]
so the amounts consumed and paid out over a price move from $\sqrt{P_a}$ to $\sqrt{P_b}$ are
\[
\Delta x = L\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_a}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{P_b}}\right), \qquad \Delta y = L\left(\sqrt{P_b} - \sqrt{P_a}\right).
\]
An exact-input step inverts those. Paying in token1 raises the price by a linear increment in root price, and paying in token0 lowers it through a reciprocal update,
\[
\sqrt{P'} = \sqrt{P} + \frac{\Delta y}{L}, \qquad \sqrt{P'} = \frac{L\sqrt{P}}{L + \Delta x \sqrt{P}}.
\]
Range boundaries sit on a geometric tick grid and each initialised tick carries a signed liquidity delta applied on crossing,
\[
\sqrt{P(i)} = 1.0001^{\,i/2}, \qquad L \mapsto L \pm \Delta L_i ,
\]
with the sign set by the direction of travel. The quoter carries the contract's own integer Q96 arithmetic, including the round-up conventions on the input side and the round-down conventions on the output side, so a quote is exact given the state.
% src/ddvc/pricing/v3quote.py, integer port of SwapMath.computeSwapStep and TickMath

The active tick map is accumulated from every mint and burn strictly before the validation day, then same-day liquidity changes and swaps are replayed together by block and log index. Consecutive swaps in one pool supply the price state: the earlier event leaves \texttt{sqrtPriceX96} and \texttt{tick} immediately before the later swap, while the interleaved event stream supplies the active liquidity it actually faced. Errors are reported by trade direction crossed with whether the quote traversed an initialised tick, because a traversal fault is directional by construction and a pooled error statistic averages a broken direction against a working one. That is not a hypothetical: an upward search that returned the tick just crossed produced a zero-width step and under-quoted upward tick-crossing swaps by a median 62.6\%, while every non-crossing quote read exact and the downward direction validated at $-0.0006\%$.
% src/ddvc/pricing/v3quote.py, _next_init_tick measured docstring against realised swaps

Table~\ref{tab:app:cl} reports the four cells on Uniswap v4, whose supported pools exercise the same code path on static fee tiers v3 never carried, including 0, 7, 8 and 20,000 hundredths of a basis point. Hook-bearing and dynamic-fee pools are excluded because their per-swap hook cash flows and realised fee are not present in the raw rows; Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} measures that support boundary.

\subsection{StableSwap pools}
\label{app:quoters:stable}

Curve interpolates between a constant-sum curve, which is flat and charges no slippage, and the constant-product curve of Section~\ref{app:quoters:cp}. For $n$ tokens with balances $x_i$, amplification $A$ and invariant $D$,
\[
A n^{n} \sum_i x_i + D \;=\; A D n^{n} + \frac{D^{\,n+1}}{n^{n} \prod_i x_i},
\]
where $A \to 0$ recovers the constant product and $A \to \infty$ recovers the constant sum. The contract solves $D$ by Newton iteration and an exact-input quote solves the same equation for the output balance once the input balance is raised, so with $D_{p} = D \prod_k D / (n x_k)$ the two loops are
\[
D \;\leftarrow\; \frac{\left(A n \sum_i x_i + n D_p\right) D}{(A n - 1) D + (n+1) D_p}, \qquad y \;\leftarrow\; \frac{y^{2} + c}{2y + b - D},
\]
with $b$ and $c$ formed from the balances of the tokens the swap does not touch. Balances are rescaled to eighteen decimals before either loop runs, because the invariant treats its tokens as interchangeable at par and a six-decimal stablecoin read on an eighteen-decimal scale is off by a factor of $10^{12}$. Output is net of the pool fee,
\[
y_{\mathrm{net}} = (x_j - y - 1)\,(1 - f),
\]
carrying the contract's own off-by-one guard on the raw difference.
% src/ddvc/pricing/stableswap.py, get_d, get_y and quote_exact_input

$A$ is Curve-specific and the standardised subgraph schema drops it, so it is identified from data instead of fetched. Every other quantity in the invariant is observed and every Curve swap reports both amounts, which leaves $A$ as the only unknown,
\[
\widehat{A} \;=\; \arg\min_{A} \; Q_{0.90}\!\left\{ \frac{|q(A; x, f, \Delta_{\mathrm{in}}) - \Delta_{\mathrm{out}}|}{\Delta_{\mathrm{out}}} \right\},
\]
fitted on the first half of a pool-day's trades over a logarithmic grid with a ternary refinement, then scored on the trades the fit never saw. The gate reads the 90th percentile of fitting error and demands it clear 1\%. A median gate is gameable and was measured to be gamed: a pool cleared a 0.1\% median fit gate on alternate trades and then returned 34\% median error on the trades in between, because half a fitting sample can sit inside a median gate while the other half is arbitrarily bad.
% src/ddvc/pricing/stableswap.py, FIT_QUANTILE 0.90 and MAX_CALIBRATION_ERROR 0.01

Rejection is the honest outcome for a pool running a different curve. Curve has launched crypto-pools since 2021 which price the CryptoSwap invariant, and an early version of the gate that bounded $A$ to a plausible-looking range let them through to be fitted with the wrong invariant, returning a best-fit $A$ of 3 and a 36\% median quote error instead of an exclusion.
% src/ddvc/pricing/stableswap.py, A_MIN comment recording the measured failure

\subsection{Weighted pools}
\label{app:quoters:weighted}

Balancer weighted pools keep a weighted geometric mean constant,
\[
\prod_i B_i^{\,W_i} = \mathrm{const}, \qquad \sum_i W_i = 1,
\]
which gives the two-token exact-input form
\[
y_{\mathrm{out}} \;=\; B_{\mathrm{out}}\left[\,1 - \left(\frac{B_{\mathrm{in}}}{B_{\mathrm{in}} + \phi\, x_{\mathrm{in}}}\right)^{W_{\mathrm{in}}/W_{\mathrm{out}}}\right].
\]
Only the ratio $W_{\mathrm{in}}/W_{\mathrm{out}}$ enters, so an equal-weight Balancer pool and a constant-product pair with the same balances quote identically and every departure is the weight ratio doing work. Quotes are refused past the vault's cap of 30\% of the input token's balance, because a swap beyond that reverts on chain and a number returned there would be a price the panel could not have traded.
% src/ddvc/pricing/weighted.py, MAX_IN_RATIO at 30% of the input balance

The weight ratio and the swap fee are both reported by the subgraph and both can be stale, since the schema serves them at the head block while balances come from a historical snapshot. Liquidity-bootstrapping pools move weights on a schedule and any weighted pool's owner can reset the fee, and the fee case showed up in validation before it was diagnosed: read parameters reproduced 2024 trades to $10^{-9}$ percent and 2022 trades only to 0.19\%, a fee-sized gap, since the fee enters through $\phi$ and a fee wrong by a basis point moves a quote by about a basis point. Both parameters are therefore identified the way Curve's $A$ is, with reading tried first and each fit introducing exactly one free scalar over at least four observations on the pair.
% src/ddvc/pricing/weighted.py, parameter identification docstring and MIN_PAIR_OBSERVATIONS

The acceptance gate sits at 0.1\% on the 90th percentile of fitting error, ten times tighter than Curve's, because the achieved errors separate cleanly there. At a 1\% gate the stable, composable-stable and elliptic-curve families clear on a fitted weight ratio and then score between 0.05\% and 1.08\% on held-out trades with a 99th percentile of 12\%, while pools that really are weighted score between $10^{-9}$ and 0.004\%. Two populations two orders of magnitude apart put 1\% inside the gap instead of below it.

\section{Backward state reconstruction}
\label{app:state}

Every quote in the panel needs the state that stood immediately before a trade, and none of the venue schemas stores that state. What they store is a period-end value: the constant-product subgraph updates a pool-hour's reserves on every swap in the hour, and the weighted-pool schema overwrites a pool-day's balances on every event of the day. The stored record is therefore the state after the last event of its period, and the direction in which a reader unwinds it is the whole of the reconstruction.

Let $R^{\mathrm{end}}$ be the stored period-end reserve pair and $\Delta_t$ the net signed amounts of the $t$-th event in execution order. The state before event $s$ is the stored value with every later event removed,
\[
R^{\mathrm{pre}}_{s} \;=\; R^{\mathrm{end}} - \sum_{t \ge s} \Delta_t \;=\; R^{\mathrm{pre}}_{s+1} - \Delta_s ,
\]
which is a single reverse pass over the period. The alternative reading takes the previous period's stored value as the current period's opening state and replays forward,
\[
\widetilde{R}^{\,\mathrm{pre}}_{s} \;=\; R^{\mathrm{end}}_{\mathrm{prev}} + \sum_{t < s} \Delta_t ,
\]
which accumulates every unrecorded balance change between the two stored points into the state it hands the quoter. The two readings use identical inputs and differ only in direction, and they do not perform alike. Replaying forward returns a median absolute quote error of 11.8\% against realised outputs, and unwinding backward returns 0.0000\%.
% src/ddvc/cpquote.py, unwind_hour measured docstring on 8,024 executed v2 swaps

The reconstruction also audits itself, which is what removes the need to fetch mint and burn events for correctness. Unwinding a period all the way to its start and comparing with the previous period's stored end value tests whether swaps were the only thing that moved reserves,
\[
\left|\frac{R^{\mathrm{pre}}_{1} - R^{\mathrm{end}}_{\mathrm{prev}}}{R^{\mathrm{end}}_{\mathrm{prev}}}\right| < \tau .
\]
Agreement means the unwind is exact. Disagreement means a mint, a burn or a direct transfer intervened, and every pre-state in that period is displaced by the same amount. On 2024-01-15, 96.8\% of comparable pool-hours reconcile and 3.2\% are flagged, and the flagged share coincides with the 3.3\% of quotes that miss 1\% accuracy, so the continuity test identifies the inaccurate cases and does not merely correlate with them. Dropping flagged hours is not random sampling, since liquidity events concentrate in actively managed and newly launched pools, and that selection is carried as a limitation of the constant-product legs.
% src/ddvc/cpquote.py, hour_is_clean measured docstring on 2024-01-15

The same result appeared independently on the weighted-pool family, where the reconstruction question takes a different form and the answer repeats. Three readings of \texttt{poolSnapshots.amounts} were scored against realised Balancer trades on the twelve days of Table~\ref{tab:app:weighted}: holding the snapshot flat across the day gives a median 1.7\%, treating it as the day's opening state and rolling forward gives 2.8\%, and treating it as the day's closing state and rolling the event sequence backward gives 0.0000\%.
% output/exhibits/weighted_quoter_validation.jsonl, medians across 12 sampled days

Two features of that replication make it informative about the constant-product result as well as about Balancer. The venues share no code, no schema and no event stream, so a common bug is not available as an explanation. And the weighted-pool walk had to merge joins and exits into the same ordered sequence as swaps, because a swap-only walk charges every liquidity event to the invariant and reads exactly like a pool running different mathematics, which collapsed coverage from 87 of 105 testable pool-days to 177 of 367 and left 96.9\% of tested volume excluded. The correction there was a second event stream.
% src/ddvc/pricing/weighted.py, balance-reconstruction measured docstring on the same twelve days

\section{The support screen}
\label{app:support}

Each quoter above was accepted against swaps that executed, and a swap that executed is a trade whose pool was deep enough to serve it. The panel then applies those quoters to hypothetical routes, including routes through pools no router would touch, and in that region a quote extrapolates beyond any measured error. The screen bounds that region, and its threshold is read off the validation population instead of chosen.

The screening statistic is the leg's own price impact in the pool it would traverse,
\[
\iota \;=\; \frac{x_{\mathrm{in}}}{R_{\mathrm{in}}},
\]
the input amount as a fraction of the input-side reserve, which is known before any quote is formed. A screen stated in the size of the measured gap would condition on a monotone function of the outcome, which is selection on the dependent variable. This one conditions on pool state and on nothing downstream of it.

\section{Block-level pricing and the triangle residual}
\label{app:timing}

The counterfactual panel prices at the close of an hour and a router quotes at a block, and Section~\ref{sec:executability:timing} reports the level deviation between those two states. A dominance classification compares both sides at the same state, so a level deviation common to both sides cancels and only the relative part can change the sign. The test that isolates the relative part is the triangle.

For endpoints $a$ and $b$ and intermediary $k$, with a direct pool $ab$ and legs $ak$ and $kb$, the residual
\[
m_{a,k,b} \;=\; \log P(a \to b) - \log P(a \to k) - \log P(k \to b)
\]
is positive exactly when the direct pool returns more of $b$ per unit of $a$ than the two-leg route does, in the zero-size limit where the marginal price is the pool price. The sign of $m$ determines the dominance classification at that limit.

Two properties make the test exact. A raw pool price $\widetilde{P}$ and the human-units price $P$ differ by the token decimals of the pair,
\[
\log P_{ab} \;=\; \log \widetilde{P}_{ab} + (d_a - d_b)\log 10 ,
\]
and around a closed triangle those constants cancel,
\[
(d_a - d_b) - (d_a - d_k) - (d_k - d_b) \;=\; 0 ,
\]
so $m$ can be formed in raw units and inherits no decimals-resolution error. On the concentrated-liquidity venues the raw price is carried on the swap event itself,
\[
\widetilde{P} = \left(\frac{\texttt{sqrtPriceX96}}{2^{96}}\right)^{2},
\]
so the state immediately after any swap is observed and never reconstructed, and the block-side and hour-side numbers come from the same field.

\section{Round-trip exclusion}
\label{app:roundtrip}

A route whose first input token equals its last output token,
\[
\mathbf{1}\{\,a_{\mathrm{first}} = b_{\mathrm{last}}\,\},
\]
does not represent endpoint-to-endpoint conversion under the paper's route unit. Such routes are removed everywhere before anything is measured because they would otherwise enter a routing statistic as though a trader had chosen an intermediary between distinct endpoints. The excluded population can contain cyclic arbitrage and other self-returning activity; the endpoint rule does not label every route as arbitrage or wash trading. Multi-trade arbitrage can be bundled atomically \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys}, which supports the mechanism but not an exhaustive behavioural classification.

The magnitude of the contamination governs how much weight the exclusion argument can carry, and it drifted once in this project's own records. A single day, 2025-12-06, was measured at 25.9\% of multi-leg routes by count and 91.3\% by value, and that pair propagated into eleven places before anyone noticed it was the most extreme day in the sample. The figure was never arithmetically wrong, which is what made it durable, since anyone re-checking it against its stated source reproduced it exactly.
