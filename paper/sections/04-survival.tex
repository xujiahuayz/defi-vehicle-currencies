\section{Persistence in vehicle-currency use}
\label{sec:survival}

Vehicle shares can rotate because new endpoint markets form around a different intermediary even when the vehicle used in established markets never changes. To separate such composition changes from turnover, we follow the leading intermediary within each ordered endpoint pair. The outcome is the time until that asset loses the role after its relative execution cost deteriorates.

Persistence and hysteresis are distinct. Slow turnover is persistence, but it can arise from symmetric adjustment costs that delay both adoption and abandonment. Hysteresis requires an asymmetry: an intermediary retains the role after losing a cost advantage more readily than it acquires the role after gaining an equivalent advantage. A market may therefore exhibit persistent vehicle use while remaining responsive to current execution costs. Conversely, weak cost discipline alone does not establish hysteresis because an omitted market condition can move the incumbent and its alternatives together.

Historical studies infer a currency's international role from the number of markets in which it is quoted \citep{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. Realised routes allow us to identify the leading intermediary separately for each endpoint pair, while pre-transaction quotes show whether it remains cost competitive. The comparison uses both objects: duration measures persistence in use, and a reversal in relative execution cost identifies whether incumbency changes the response to price.

\subsection{Incumbency and relative execution cost}
\label{sec:survival:question}
\label{sec:survival:notation}

Fix an ordered endpoint pair $(i,o)$ and date $t$. Let $K_{iot}$ contain the intermediary assets for which both legs can be quoted before the transaction, and let $s_{iokt}$ be asset $k$'s share of exact two-leg routes. The incumbent is

\begin{equation}
\ell_{iot}=\arg\max_{k\in K_{iot}}s_{iokt},
\qquad \sum_{k\in K_{iot}}s_{iokt}=1.
\label{eq:incumbent}
\end{equation}

Membership in $K_{iot}$ is determined before the realised route is observed. For notional $q$, let $O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t}$ denote output from the indirect route through $k$, evaluated at the pre-transaction state, and let $O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}=\max_{j\in K_{iot}}O^{I}_{i,o,j,q,t}$ be the largest output among feasible indirect routes. The incumbent's relative output is

\begin{equation}
G_{iot}(q)=\frac{O^{I}_{i,o,\ell_{iot},q,t}-O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}}{O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}},
\qquad D_{iot}(q)=\mathbf{1}\{G_{iot}(q)<0\}.
\label{eq:incumbent-gap}
\end{equation}

Every asset in $K_{iot}$ is quoted at the same finite trade size and transaction state. Gas is included only when route-specific gas units for intermediary $k$ are observed. Marginal prices and closing hourly states do not provide the pre-trade comparison in \eqref{eq:incumbent-gap}.

\subsection{Turnover following a cost disadvantage}
\label{sec:survival:hazard}

Leadership changes between $t$ and $t+\tau$ when

\begin{equation}
Y_{io,t+\tau}=\mathbf{1}\{\ell_{io,t+\tau}\neq\ell_{iot}\},
\qquad \tau\in\{1,7,30,120\}\text{ calendar days}.
\label{eq:event}
\end{equation}

We compare the probability of turnover according to whether the incumbent offered the largest output before the outcome window:

\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\tau,d}=\Pr\!\left(Y_{io,t+\tau}=1\mid D_{iot}(q)=d\right),
\qquad \theta_{\tau}=\frac{\lambda_{\tau,1}}{\lambda_{\tau,0}}.
\label{eq:hazard}
\end{equation}

When $\theta_{\tau}>1$, an incumbent that has lost its cost advantage is more likely to be displaced. Low turnover in both states together with $\theta_{\tau}>1$ indicates slow adjustment with continued cost discipline. Similar turnover rates across states show that current cost has little relation to incumbency, but they do not by themselves distinguish hysteresis from an omitted common state.

The regression specification absorbs endpoint-pair and calendar effects, allows the relation to vary with the size of the cost gap, and conditions on the number of feasible intermediary assets:

\begin{equation}
\Pr(Y_{io,t+\tau}=1\mid\cdot)=F\!\left(\alpha_{io}+\delta_t+\gamma_{\tau}D_{iot}(q)+f\!\left(G_{iot}(q)\right)+\psi_{\tau}|K_{iot}|\right).
\label{eq:turnover-regression}
\end{equation}

The term $|K_{iot}|$ is necessary because adding feasible alternatives raises the chance that some asset displaces the incumbent even when the quality of each alternative is unchanged. Identification comes from endpoint pairs observed in both cost states, and inference is clustered where shocks to those states are shared.

Turnover frequency, routed value, and execution loss answer different questions. Pair activity determines how precisely the leading share and turnover date are measured. Routed value measures the exposure attached to a switch, while the output gap measures the loss relative to the best supported alternative. We therefore report duration, exposure, and output forgone separately.

\subsection{Reversals in relative execution cost}
\label{sec:survival:reversal}

A comparison of raw spell lengths can create a mechanical asymmetry if incumbent retention ends when any alternative wins but displacement ends only when one named asset becomes the incumbent. The minimum of several arrival times is shorter even when all intermediary assets have identical arrival rates. Retention and displacement spells are therefore descriptive unless they are constructed on the same asset and risk set.

The hysteresis test follows intermediary $k$ within endpoint pair $(i,o)$ through a reversal in its relative cost. Let $A_{iokt}(q)$ be the output advantage of the route through $k$ over the best other feasible intermediary at the same transaction state and notional. An improvement asks whether $k$ acquires the vehicle role when $A_{iokt}(q)$ crosses from weakly negative to positive. A deterioration asks whether the same asset retains the role when the advantage crosses from weakly positive to negative. The feasible set, quote support, and notional remain fixed around both crossings.

This design separates state dependence from a calendar trend. Vehicle use that rises with an improvement and falls with a deterioration responds to current relative cost. Retention after the advantage disappears is consistent with hysteresis only when it exceeds the response to the corresponding improvement, conditional on prior use and cost. Protocol launch dates and realised changes in architecture exposure cannot substitute for this reversal comparison.

\subsection{Empirical requirements}
\label{sec:survival:obstacle}

The turnover comparison requires a pre-transaction quote for every feasible intermediary at the same fixed notional. The panel must link observations at exact calendar horizons, retain unsupported alternatives as missing rather than absent, and define the feasible set independently of the realised route. An earlier diagnostic used marginal prices, an assumed fee wedge, incomplete quote coverage, and an unconditional rate ratio. It cannot estimate the turnover probability in \eqref{eq:hazard}, and we report no persistence estimate from it.

Economic importance does not follow from duration alone. Flow through a cost-disadvantaged incumbent measures exposure, and output forgone relative to the best feasible route measures loss. A weak cost-state coefficient can coexist with large exposure if economically important routes remain with the incumbent; rapid turnover can have little welfare relevance when the alternatives are nearly tied. The empirical analysis therefore relates cost to turnover first and then measures the routed activity and output associated with the state.
