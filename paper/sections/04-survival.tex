\section{Persistence and vehicle-role turnover}
\label{sec:survival}

\subsection{The economic distinction}
\label{sec:survival:question}

Section~\ref{sec:dominance} documents rotation in aggregate vehicle use. Aggregate shares can change without turnover in existing markets: new endpoint pairs may form around one vehicle even when the incumbent on older pairs never changes. We therefore follow leadership within each ordered endpoint pair and ask how quickly another candidate takes its place after the incumbent ceases to be the cheapest feasible intermediary.

Slow turnover is not by itself hysteresis. Symmetric switching frictions can slow gains and losses alike, while the incumbent's relative cost still affects the probability of turnover. Hysteresis makes the stronger prediction that vehicle use responds asymmetrically when the same candidate first gains and later loses a cost advantage. We use duration to describe persistence and reserve hysteresis for this candidate-level response to a reversal in relative cost.

Historical studies infer a currency's role from the number of markets quoting it \,\citep{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. Here, realised routes identify the incumbent on each endpoint pair, and exact-state counterfactual quotes reveal whether that incumbent remains cost competitive. Duration alone measures persistence in use; hysteresis requires the role to survive a reversal in relative cost.

\subsection{Incumbency and the cost state}
\label{sec:survival:notation}

Fix an ordered endpoint pair $(i,o)$ and date $t$. Let $K_{iot}$ be the set of feasible intermediaries and let $s_{iokt}$ be candidate $k$'s share of exact two-leg routes. The incumbent is

\begin{equation}
\ell_{iot}=\arg\max_{k\in K_{iot}}s_{iokt},
\qquad \sum_{k\in K_{iot}}s_{iokt}=1.
\label{eq:incumbent}
\end{equation}

Feasibility is determined before realised vehicle choice is observed. For notional $q$, let $O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t}$ denote output from the indirect route through $k$, evaluated at strict pre-transaction state, and let $O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}=\max_{j\in K_{iot}}O^{I}_{i,o,j,q,t}$ be the best feasible indirect output. The incumbent's cost gap is

\begin{equation}
G_{iot}(q)=\frac{O^{I}_{i,o,\ell_{iot},q,t}-O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}}{O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}},
\qquad D_{iot}(q)=\mathbf{1}\{G_{iot}(q)<0\}.
\label{eq:incumbent-gap}
\end{equation}

Every candidate is quoted at the same finite trade size and transaction state. Gas enters only when candidate-specific route units are observed, and the feasible set is defined independently of the realised winner. Marginal prices and hourly closing states do not satisfy the pre-trade comparison in \eqref{eq:incumbent-gap}.

\subsection{The turnover hazard}
\label{sec:survival:hazard}

Leadership changes between $t$ and $t+\tau$ when

\begin{equation}
Y_{io,t+\tau}=\mathbf{1}\{\ell_{io,t+\tau}\neq\ell_{iot}\},
\qquad \tau\in\{1,7,30,120\}\text{ calendar days}.
\label{eq:event}
\end{equation}

The primary contrast conditions the same turnover event on the incumbent's cost state before the outcome window:

\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\tau,d}=\Pr\!\left(Y_{io,t+\tau}=1\mid D_{iot}(q)=d\right),
\qquad \theta_{\tau}=\frac{\lambda_{\tau,1}}{\lambda_{\tau,0}}.
\label{eq:hazard}
\end{equation}

A value of $\theta_{\tau}$ above one means that losing the cost advantage predicts faster turnover. Slow hazards in both states with $\theta_{\tau}>1$ describe persistence with continued cost discipline. Similar hazards across states imply that current cost has little grip on incumbency; they indicate hysteresis only if the same candidate responds asymmetrically to favourable and adverse cost crossings.

The regression form absorbs endpoint-pair and calendar effects and conditions on the size of the feasible candidate set:

\begin{equation}
\Pr(Y_{io,t+\tau}=1\mid\cdot)=F\!\left(\alpha_{io}+\delta_t+\gamma_{\tau}D_{iot}(q)+f\!\left(G_{iot}(q)\right)+\psi_{\tau}|K_{iot}|\right).
\label{eq:turnover-regression}
\end{equation}

More candidates create more ways for any incumbent to lose, so omitting $|K_{iot}|$ mechanically raises turnover in richer markets. Identification requires within-pair observations in both cost states, and inference is clustered at the level at which cost-state shocks are shared.

\subsection{Retention, displacement, and reversal}
\label{sec:survival:reversal}

A duration comparison that ends incumbent retention when any challenger wins but ends challenger displacement only when one named candidate wins is mechanically asymmetric. The minimum over many challengers is shorter even when every candidate has the same independent arrival rate. For that reason, raw retention and displacement spells are reported as descriptions and never interpreted through their ratio.

The hysteresis test instead follows the same pair-candidate unit through a cost-state crossing. Let $A_{iokt}(q)$ be candidate $k$'s output advantage over the best other feasible intermediary at the same state and notional. A favourable crossing asks whether $k$ gains the vehicle role when $A_{iokt}(q)$ moves from weakly negative to positive; an adverse crossing asks whether the same candidate retains the role when that advantage moves from weakly positive to negative. The comparison set, quote support, and notional must remain fixed across the window. Hysteresis is an asymmetric response of vehicle use to those opposed crossings, conditional on pre-crossing use and cost. A permanent protocol launch date or a realised architecture-share exit cannot substitute for this reversal.

The reversal design also separates state dependence from calendar trend. If vehicle use rises when a candidate's cost position improves and falls when it deteriorates, current relative cost helps explain route choice. If use remains after the advantage disappears, the asymmetric retention response is consistent with hysteresis. A one-way late-sample rise is uninformative about both patterns.

\subsection{The pair-candidate opportunity set}
\label{sec:survival:obstacle}

Turnover is identified only when every feasible intermediary for an ordered endpoint pair is quoted at the same fixed notional immediately before execution. The panel must also carry exact calendar lags, record unsupported alternatives, and define the candidate set independently of the realised winner. The earlier block-level diagnostic instead used marginal prices, an assumed fee wedge, incomplete candidate support, and an unconditional rate ratio, so it cannot estimate the turnover hazard in \eqref{eq:hazard}. We report no persistence estimate from that diagnostic.

Turnover and economic magnitude are separate outcomes. Flow through a cost-disadvantaged incumbent measures exposure, while output forgone relative to the best feasible route measures loss. Spell length identifies no such magnitude; both outcomes require the exact-state comparison used to measure persistence.
