\section{Persistence and vehicle-role turnover}
\label{sec:survival}

\subsection{The economic distinction}
\label{sec:survival:question}

Section~\ref{sec:dominance} documents a rotation in aggregate vehicle use. Rotation does not reveal how individual markets change hands. A vehicle may lose aggregate share because new endpoint pairs form elsewhere, even if incumbency on existing pairs is permanent. The persistence question is therefore defined within an endpoint pair: when the incumbent ceases to be the cheapest feasible intermediary, how quickly does another candidate take its place?

Persistence and hysteresis make different predictions. Under persistence, turnover is slow in both directions, but the incumbent's cost position still changes the probability that the role moves. Under hysteresis, vehicle choice becomes weakly responsive to a reversal in the cost state. Symmetric switching frictions can generate slow entry and exit; they do not by themselves generate an asymmetric response to a state that moves in both directions. The paper consequently treats duration as a descriptive quantity and reserves hysteresis for a cost-conditioned asymmetry.

This object differs from quotation-network inertia. Historical studies measure a currency's role through the number of markets quoting it \,\citep{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. Vehicle routes make the incumbent observable on each endpoint pair, while exact-state counterfactual quotes can determine whether its cost advantage remains. Both ingredients are needed. A long spell without a measured reversal is persistence in use, not evidence of hysteresis.

\subsection{Incumbency and the cost state}
\label{sec:survival:notation}

Fix an unordered endpoint pair $(i,o)$ and date $t$. Let $K_{iot}$ be the set of feasible intermediaries and let $s_{iokt}$ be candidate $k$'s share of exact two-leg routes. The incumbent is

\begin{equation}
\ell_{iot}=\arg\max_{k\in K_{iot}}s_{iokt},
\qquad \sum_{k\in K_{iot}}s_{iokt}=1.
\label{eq:incumbent}
\end{equation}

Feasibility is determined before realised vehicle choice is observed. For notional $q$, let $O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t}$ denote output from the indirect route through $k$, evaluated at strict pre-transaction state, and let $O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}=\max_{j\in K_{iot}}O^{I}_{i,o,j,q,t}$ be the best feasible indirect output. The incumbent's cost gap is

\begin{equation}
G_{iot}(q)=\frac{O^{I}_{i,o,\ell_{iot},q,t}-O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}}{O^{*}_{i,o,q,t}},
\qquad D_{iot}(q)=\mathbf{1}\{G_{iot}(q)<0\}.
\label{eq:incumbent-gap}
\end{equation}

The gap uses finite-size output from the same transaction-state quote engine for every candidate. Gas enters only after candidate-specific route units are available. Marginal prices, hourly closing states, assumed fee wedges, and a candidate observed only because it was chosen do not satisfy this definition.

\subsection{The turnover hazard}
\label{sec:survival:hazard}

Leadership changes between $t$ and $t+\tau$ when

\begin{equation}
Y_{io,t+\tau}=\mathbf{1}\{\ell_{io,t+\tau}\neq\ell_{iot}\},
\qquad \tau\in\{1,7,30,120\}\text{ calendar days}.
\label{eq:event}
\end{equation}

The primary contrast conditions the same turnover event on the incumbent's cost state before the outcome window:

\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\tau,d}=\Pr\!\left(Y_{io,t+\tau}=1\mid D_{iot}(q)=d\right),
\qquad \theta_{\tau}=\frac{\lambda_{\tau,1}}{\lambda_{\tau,0}}.
\label{eq:hazard}
\end{equation}

A value of $\theta_{\tau}$ above one means that losing the cost advantage predicts faster turnover. Slow hazards in both states with $\theta_{\tau}>1$ describe persistence with continued cost discipline. Similar hazards across states imply that current cost has little grip on incumbency, but this pattern is called hysteresis only if the corresponding candidate-level reversal design shows asymmetric retention and displacement.

The regression form absorbs endpoint-pair and calendar effects and conditions on the size of the feasible candidate set:

\begin{equation}
\Pr(Y_{io,t+\tau}=1\mid\cdot)=F\!\left(\alpha_{io}+\delta_t+\gamma_{\tau}D_{iot}(q)+f\!\left(G_{iot}(q)\right)+\psi_{\tau}|K_{iot}|\right).
\label{eq:turnover-regression}
\end{equation}

Candidate count is not a routine control. More candidates create more ways for any incumbent to lose, so omitting $|K_{iot}|$ mechanically raises turnover in richer markets. The final estimator also requires within-pair observations in both cost states and clusters inference at the level at which cost-state shocks are shared.

\subsection{Retention, displacement, and reversal}
\label{sec:survival:reversal}

A duration comparison that ends incumbent retention when any challenger wins but ends challenger displacement only when one named candidate wins is mechanically asymmetric. The minimum over many challengers is shorter even when every candidate has the same independent arrival rate. For that reason, raw retention and displacement spells are reported as descriptions and never interpreted through their ratio.

The hysteresis test instead follows the same pair-candidate unit through a cost-state crossing. A favourable crossing asks whether candidate $k$ gains the vehicle role after $G_{iokt}(q)$ turns positive; an adverse crossing asks whether the same candidate loses the role after the gap turns negative. The comparison set, quote support, and notional must remain fixed across the window. Hysteresis is an asymmetric response of vehicle use to those opposed crossings, conditional on pre-crossing use and cost. A permanent protocol launch date or a realised architecture-share exit cannot substitute for this reversal.

This design also separates state dependence from calendar trend. If stable vehicle use rises when its cost state improves and falls when the state reverses, the evidence supports a contemporaneous comparative-static mechanism. If use remains after the advantage disappears, the retention response supplies the object needed for hysteresis. A one-way late-sample rise supplies neither conclusion.

\subsection{Evidence gate}
\label{sec:survival:obstacle}

The earlier block-level diagnostic is excluded because it used marginal prices, an assumed fee wedge, incomplete candidate support, and an unconditional rate ratio. The final panel must quote every feasible candidate at strict pre-transaction state and fixed notional, carry exact calendar lags, record unsupported alternatives, and establish that the candidate set was defined independently of the realised winner. Estimates are withheld until those conditions pass on the certified transaction-state frontier.

Economic magnitude is reported separately from turnover. Flow through a cost-disadvantaged incumbent measures exposure; the output forgone relative to the best feasible route measures loss. The two cannot be inferred from spell length, and neither enters the persistence result unless the same exact-state quote contract supports it.
