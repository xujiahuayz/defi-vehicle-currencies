\section{Comparing direct and intermediated execution}
\label{sec:executability}

Realised routes identify the assets that carried each trade. Execution cost is a separate object: it compares those routes with alternatives available immediately beforehand, quoted at the same trade size and over a common set of exchanges. We hold the source $i$, destination $o$, dollar input $q$, and pre-transaction pool state $e^-$ fixed. The best direct and two-leg outputs through intermediary $k$ are
\begin{equation*}
O^{D}_{i,o,q,e}=P_{o,e}\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,o,e^-}}Q_p\!\left(i\to o,\Delta_i(q,e);\chi_{p,e^-}\right),
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,e}=P_{o,e}\max_{p'\in\mathcal P_{k,o,e^-}}Q_{p'}\!\left(k\to o,\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,k,e^-}}Q_p\!\left(i\to k,\Delta_i(q,e);\chi_{p,e^-}\right);\chi_{p',e^-}\right)
\end{equation*}
This comparison extends the realised-versus-optimised allocation benchmark of \citet{XiMoallemi2026Routing} from parallel pools for one atomic asset pair to sequential routes through an intermediary.

The vehicle-route shortfall relative to direct execution is
\begin{equation*}
\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,e}=\frac{O^{D}_{i,o,q,e}-O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,e}}{O^{D}_{i,o,q,e}},
\end{equation*}
reported in basis points as $10^{4}\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,e}$. Positive values mean that the direct route returns more output before gas. Swaps and liquidity changes are replayed in on-chain order because a daily or hourly closing state incorporates events that occurred after the trade. The observed event marks settlement; a quote formed earlier can reflect a different opportunity set.

We compare hypothetical legs only within a common executable range. Let $\widetilde Q_{\ell}$ denote leg $\ell$'s output at the pool's marginal rate. Each leg satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:support}
\pi_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)=\frac{\widetilde Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)-Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)}{\widetilde Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)}\leq \bar\pi,
\qquad \bar\pi=0.05.
\end{equation}
The 5\% own-price-impact ceiling applies across pool families; contract-specific limits add further restrictions. Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} gives the pricing equations and Appendix~\ref{app:support} calibrates the support rule.

Executable price differences can persist when trading is segmented across cryptocurrency markets \citep{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage}. Prices therefore offer a demanding rival explanation for persistent vehicle use. If the intermediary merely records the price in one locally attractive pool, widening the same-state opportunity set should often change the intermediary. We test that prediction on the fifteenth day of every month from June 2020 through June 2026. Each chosen two-leg path must reproduce realised output within one basis point. We then widen the opportunity set in three stages: the same vehicle among the venue families used by the realised route, the same vehicle across Uniswap v2, SushiSwap v2, and Uniswap v3, and any named public vehicle or direct route across those three exchanges. The main sample also requires coherent leg values, at least \$100 of input, and the 5\% price-impact bound on the realised path.

Table~\ref{tab:exact-vehicle-frontier} shows that the chosen vehicle survives broader price competition. Only \ExactFrontierWithinShare{} of routes have a same-vehicle alternative that improves gross output by more than one basis point within the venue families already used. Opening all three exact venues raises that share to \ExactFrontierSameVehicleShare{}. Opening the vehicle set and the direct path raises it only another \ExactFrontierVehicleAddition{}, to \ExactFrontierAllPathShare{}. Among routes with an improvement, the median gain is \ExactFrontierMedianGain{}. Thus public venue prices often favour a different venue while preserving the intermediary asset.
% output/exhibits/exact_vehicle_frontier_monthly.jsonl; pooled common-support sample

The vehicle-share comparison gives the same result. The full price set lowers stablecoin vehicle share by \ExactFrontierStableDecline{} on a route-weighted basis and \ExactFrontierStableValueDecline{} when routes are weighted by input value. The route-weighted standard error is \ExactFrontierStableChangeSE{}. Restricting the comparison to the \ExactFrontierHighNotionalRoutes{} routes with at least \$10,000 of input lowers the route-weighted share by \ExactFrontierHighNotionalDecline{}. Vehicle identity is therefore much less sensitive to broader public prices than venue choice. This separation supports an asset-level coordination role alongside venue-level execution frictions; it does not identify why a router omitted an available pool.

Receipt gas measures the fixed execution toll attached to adding a vehicle leg. That toll matters because gas fees can change both trading costs and liquidity concentration in decentralised exchange \citep{CaparrosChaudharyKlein2024BlockchainScaling}. In the executed-route gas sample, a stable-vehicle route in 2024 uses \GasStableExtraGasUnitsBase{} more gas than the median direct route, \GasStableExtraGasPctBase{} of the direct-route median. Evaluated at that year's median gas price and WETH price, the extra toll is \GasStableExtraCostBase{}, so a trade needs about \GasStableOneBpNotionalBase{} of notional before the extra gas is below one basis point. In 2026 the median stable-vehicle route uses \GasStableExtraGasUnitsEnd{} more gas than the direct-route median, but the dollar toll falls to \GasStableExtraCostEnd{} and the one-basis-point notional falls to \GasStableOneBpNotionalEnd{}. Pricing the same fixed toll at each stable-vehicle route's own gas price sharpens the interpretation: median stable-vehicle notional falls from \GasStableRouteMedianNotionalBase{} to \GasStableRouteMedianNotionalEnd{}, while the median fixed toll falls from \GasStableRouteMedianTollBpsBase{} to \GasStableRouteMedianTollBpsEnd{} and the share of stable-vehicle routes with a fixed toll below 25 basis points rises from \GasStableRouteTwentyFiveBpShareBase{} to \GasStableRouteTwentyFiveBpShareEnd{}, the trade-size version of the fixed-cost channel in \citet{CaparrosChaudharyKlein2024BlockchainScaling}. Thus the extra-hop overhead remains visible in gas units while becoming economically small for ordinary routed notionals. This evidence supports the interpretation that vehicle formation spread to routes for which fixed execution overhead had ceased to be a large barrier. The estimand is the executed route's fixed gas toll; a stable-versus-WETH all-in price ranking remains outside this check.

The benchmark represents public on-chain liquidity in the declared exchange set. Private order flow, financing costs, losses from reverted transactions, and pools outside that set can alter a router's choice. Pool-family coverage is economically material. In the sampled Curve exercise, pools outside the StableSwap model carry 34.2\% of assessed volume, including 65.2\% of native-leg volume and 21.1\% of stablecoin-leg volume. Hook-bearing or dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools, SushiSwap v3, and several Balancer families also use pricing states outside the benchmark. Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} quantifies these boundaries.
