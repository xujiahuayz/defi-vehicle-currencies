\section{Exact prices, full-range capital, and vehicle retention}
\label{sec:executability}

Executable price differences can persist when trading is segmented across cryptocurrency markets \citep{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage}. We therefore compare stablecoin and WETH routes at the same input and pretrade state. For each observed two-leg route on the fifteenth day of every month from June 2020 through June 2026, we quote the best WETH route and the best stablecoin route through DAI, USDC, or USDT across Uniswap v2, SushiSwap v2, and Uniswap v3. These venues carry \ExactFrontierVenueCoverage{} of linear routes on the sampled dates, and \ExactFrontierReproduction{} of mapped observed routes reproduce realised output within one basis point. Both vehicle families must be feasible; every observed and hypothetical leg must remain below 5\% own-price impact; leg values must agree within 20\%; and input value must be at least \$100. Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} gives the pricing equations, Appendix~\ref{app:support} calibrates the common support rule, and Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:exact-vehicle-frontier} reports the broader price frontier.

\subsection{The first exact contest after pair entry}
\label{sec:executability:first-contest}

The entry-day estimates show persistence across later realised routes, including pairs for which only one vehicle family may be feasible. We now locate the first sampled monthly date after pair entry on which the same observed trade can use either WETH or a stablecoin path under the exact common-support rules. Pair entry requires at least \FirstContestEntryValueThreshold{} of coherent route value on its first active day. Among \FirstContestEntryPairs{} such entrants, \FirstContestPairs{}, or \FirstContestCoverage{}, reach a sampled exact contest. The median lag is \FirstContestMedianLagDays{} days, and \FirstContestWithinOneTwenty{} reach it within 120 days (Table~\ref{tab:first-contestable-choice}, panel A). The estimates describe this strict monthly opportunity set.
% output/exhibits/first_contestable_vehicle_choice.jsonl; output/exhibits/first_contestable_vehicle_choice_support.jsonl

For route $r$ on pair $p$'s first contestable date $t$, we estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first-contestable-choice}
C_{rpt}=\alpha+\beta A^O_{rpt}+\gamma A^K_{pt-1}
       +\beta_q\log q_r+\delta_t+\eta_i+\xi_j+\phi_s+\varepsilon_{rpt}.
\end{equation}
In column 1 of panel B, $C_{rpt}$ indicates stablecoin use and $A^O_{rpt}$ is the stablecoin's exact-output advantage. In columns 2 and 3, $C_{rpt}$ indicates retention of the entry family, while $A^O_{rpt}$ and $A^K_{pt-1}$ are oriented toward that family. The capital term is its share of prior-calendar full-range constant-product weak-leg capital. Date effects $\delta_t$, source effects $\eta_i$, destination effects $\xi_j$, and observed-route-scope effects $\phi_s$ compare routes inside the same dated opportunity structure.

The entry family carries \FirstContestRouteRetention{} of routes at the first sampled exact contest; equal weighting across pairs gives \FirstContestPairRetention{} (Table~\ref{tab:first-contestable-choice}, panel A). Across all first-contest routes, the coefficient on a 100 bp stablecoin exact-output advantage is \FirstContestStableOutputEffect{} (panel B, column 1). On the common capital sample, the coefficient on a 100 bp exact-output advantage for the entry family is \FirstContestRetentionOutputOnlyEffect{} in column 2 and \FirstContestRetentionJointOutputEffect{} after prior capital enters in column 3. The prior-capital coefficient is \FirstContestRetentionJointCapitalEffect{} per 10 pp, with a standard error of \FirstContestRetentionJointCapitalSE{} (column 3). At the first contest, executable price is the precise predictor; the separate prior-capital coefficient is imprecise. In established pairs and at price-rank crossings, prior capital predicts mature retention and the durability of a new price lead.

\subsection{Prices and capital across established pairs}
\label{sec:executability:established}

We call a family the incumbent when it alone carries the pair on the first observed vehicle day and begin measuring retention 30 days later. For route $r$, pair $p$, and date $t$, the price-and-capital specification is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contestable-retention}
R_{rpt}=\beta_O A^O_{rpt}+\beta_K A^K_{pt-1}+\beta_q\log q_r+\alpha_p+\delta_t+\varepsilon_{rpt}.
\end{equation}
Here $R_{rpt}$ equals one when the route retains the incumbent family. The exact-output advantage is $A^O_{rpt}=\left[10^4(O^{\mathrm{inc}}_{rpt}-O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt})/O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt}\right]_{-1000}^{1000}/100$, so one unit represents 100 basis points. For family $v$, weak-leg capital is $K^v_{pt-1}=\min\{K_{i,v,t-1},K_{v,o,t-1}\}$, where each leg aggregates prior-calendar deposited capital across full-range Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools. Its relative advantage is $A^K_{pt-1}=\{K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}/(K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}+K^{\mathrm{chall}}_{pt-1})-1/2\}/0.10$, so one unit represents a 10-percentage-point shift from an equal capital split. Pair fixed effects $\alpha_p$ absorb persistent pair-level routing preferences, and date fixed effects $\delta_t$ absorb market-wide conditions.

Table~\ref{tab:contestable-vehicle-choice} reports the route-choice estimates. Column 1 relates contemporaneous price leadership to route choice; the coefficient on stablecoin price leadership is \ContestPriceLeaderEffect{}. Among mature pairs with an exclusive incumbent, the incumbent carries \ContestIncumbentLeaderRetention{} of routes when it leads on exact output and \ContestChallengerLeaderRetention{} when the challenger leads. In column 2, the within-pair, within-date coefficient on challenger leadership is \ContestChallengerEffect{}. Columns 3 and 4 use the same \ContestOutputAdvantageHundredBpN{} routes, each with positive prior-day full-range constant-product capital for both vehicle families. A 10-percentage-point incumbent capital-share advantage is associated with \ContestCapitalAdvantageTenPpEffect{} higher retention in column 4. The exact-output coefficient changes little across the two columns, while lagged two-leg capital adds explanatory power beyond the current quote.

\subsection{When the price lead changes hands}
\label{sec:executability:crossings}

The retention regressions compare routes across many price configurations. We next follow the same pair when its exact-output ranking reverses. A price-rank crossing occurs when a challenger moves from at least one basis point behind in month $t-1$ to at least one basis point ahead in month $t$. The ranking uses the pair-month median exact-output gap across common-support quotes. Event dating uses only months $t-1$ and $t$. The principal comparison requires at least two common-support routes and at least \$1,000 of observed input in each crossing month. It contains \RankCrossingEvents{} crossings across \RankCrossingPairs{} pairs, split equally between stablecoin and WETH challengers.
% output/exhibits/price_rank_crossing.jsonl; output/exhibits/price_rank_crossing_support.jsonl

Let $R_{e\tau}$ be the incumbent family's route share in event $e$ and relative month $\tau$, and let $Q_{e,-1}$ be the challenger's share of prior-calendar full-range constant-product weak-leg capital on the eve of the crossing. We estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:price-rank-crossing}
\begin{aligned}
R_{e0}-R_{e,-1}
  &=\alpha+\gamma Q_{e,-1}+X_e'\delta+\lambda_{m(e)}+\varepsilon_e,\\
L_{e,1}
  &=a+\theta Q_{e,-1}+X_e'c+\lambda_{m(e)}+u_e,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $L_{e,1}$ equals one when the challenger remains the exact-output leader one month later. The controls $X_e$ contain the challenger family, the exact-output gaps in months $t-1$ and $t$, prior incumbent route share, and log route activity across the two crossing months. Calendar-month effects $\lambda_{m(e)}$ absorb common market conditions.

Among the \RankCrossingBalancedEvents{} crossings observed from month $-3$ through month $+3$, the incumbent carries \RankCrossingPriorShare{} of routes immediately before the crossing and \RankCrossingCurrentShare{} in the crossing month (Table~\ref{tab:price-rank-crossing}, panel A). One month later, its share is \RankCrossingNextShare{}. Incumbent share falls \RankCrossingPlaceboDrop{} more at the crossing than during the same event's earlier move from month $-3$ to month $-2$ (panel B, column 4). Route allocation therefore moves sharply when the sampled pair-month exact-output ranking changes.

The next month separates a temporary quote from a durable challenge. The challenger remains ahead in \RankCrossingStillAhead{} of observed events, and the incumbent retakes the lead in \RankCrossingRetaken{}. Incumbent route share is \RankCrossingStillAheadShare{} in the first group and \RankCrossingRetakenShare{} in the second. A 10 pp larger challenger share of prior weak-leg capital predicts \RankCrossingCapitalPersistence{} greater probability that the challenger remains ahead one month later (Table~\ref{tab:price-rank-crossing}, panel B, column 2). Its immediate route-share coefficient in column 1 is small. Exact output tracks immediate allocation; prior two-leg capital predicts whether the new price lead persists.

The same comparison gives the output consequence of vehicle choice. Panel A of Table~\ref{tab:contestable-vehicle-consequences} shows that \ContestLowerOutputFamilyShare{} of routes use the family that returns at least one basis point less. Conditional on that choice, the median shortfall is \ContestForegoneMedianBps{} and the 90th percentile is \ContestForegonePNinetyBps{}; weighting every contestable route by input value gives \ContestForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{}. Among routes that retain a mature exclusive incumbent, \ContestRetainedLowerOutputShare{} leave more than one basis point of gross output, with a \ContestRetainedForegoneMedianBps{} conditional median and \ContestRetainedForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} across all retained routes (panel B). Panel C reports value-weighted shortfalls of \ContestYoungForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} for pairs aged 0--89 days, \ContestMiddleForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} for pairs aged 90--364 days, and \ContestMatureForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} after one year.

Panel D matches the exact-route comparison to transaction receipts and predicts gas separately for the chosen and rival paths. On the common receipt-matched sample, the input-value-weighted shortfall rises from \GasConsequenceOverallGrossShortfallBp{} bp gross to \GasConsequenceOverallNetShortfallBp{} bp net of gas. For inputs below \$1,000, it rises from \GasConsequenceSmallGrossShortfallBp{} to \GasConsequenceSmallNetShortfallBp{} bp; above \$100,000, the estimates are \GasConsequenceLargeGrossShortfallBp{} and \GasConsequenceLargeNetShortfallBp{} bp. Gas therefore sharpens the consequence for small trades while leaving the aggregate magnitude nearly unchanged. The price cost is concentrated earlier in a pair's life and at smaller trade sizes.

The benchmark represents public on-chain liquidity in the declared exchange set. Private order flow, financing costs, losses from reverted transactions, and pools outside that set can alter a router's choice. Across sampled Curve pool-days, pools outside the StableSwap model carry 34.2\% of assessed volume, including 65.2\% of native-leg volume and 21.1\% of stablecoin-leg volume (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:curveleg}, panel A). Hook-bearing or dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools, SushiSwap v3, and several Balancer families also use pricing states outside the benchmark.
