\section{Capital, exact prices, and route use}
\label{sec:executability}

Once both vehicle families are executable, current exact prices and lagged full-range capital jointly predict vehicle use. Full-range capital captures one part of pool state; size-specific pretrade quotes determine what routers can execute. For each observed two-leg route on the fifteenth day of every month from June 2020 through June 2026, we quote the best WETH path and the best stablecoin path through DAI, USDC, or USDT at the same input amount and pre-transaction state. The comparison covers Uniswap v2, SushiSwap v2, and Uniswap v3, which carry \ExactFrontierVenueCoverage{} of linear routes on the sampled dates. Among routes for which all required states are observed, \ExactFrontierReproduction{} reproduce realised output within one basis point (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:exact-vehicle-frontier}, panel A). Both vehicle families must be feasible, each observed and hypothetical leg remains below 5\% own-price impact, leg values agree within 20\%, and input value is at least \$100. Exact price is central to automated routing, while gas, venue access, and support can still separate an available quote from an executable path \citep{XiMoallemi2026Routing}. Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} gives the pricing equations, Appendix~\ref{app:support} defines the common-support rule, and Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:exact-vehicle-frontier} reports results for additional venues, vehicles, and direct paths.

We call a vehicle family the incumbent when it alone carries a pair on the first observed vehicle day and begin measuring retention 30 days later. For route $r$, pair $p$, and date $t$, we estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contestable-retention}
R_{rpt}=\beta_O A^O_{rpt}+\beta_K A^K_{pt-1}+\beta_q\log q_r+\alpha_p+\delta_t+\varepsilon_{rpt}.
\end{equation}
Here $R_{rpt}$ indicates retention of the incumbent family and $q_r$ is the route's observed dollar input value. We define the two advantages as
\begin{align*}
A^O_{rpt}
&=\frac{1}{100}\left[
10^4\frac{O^{\mathrm{inc}}_{rpt}-O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt}}
{O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt}}
\right]_{-1000}^{1000},\\
K^v_{pt-1}&=\min\{K_{i,v,t-1},K_{v,j,t-1}\},\\
A^K_{pt-1}
&=\frac{K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}/
(K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}+K^{\mathrm{chall}}_{pt-1})-1/2}{0.10}.
% scripts/analyze/run_contestable_vehicle_choice.py; exact regressor scaling.
\end{align*}
One unit of $A^O_{rpt}$ represents 100 basis points. Each leg of $K^v_{pt-1}$ aggregates prior-calendar deposited capital across full-range Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools. One unit of $A^K_{pt-1}$ represents a 10 pp shift from an equal capital split. Pair effects $\alpha_p$ absorb persistent routing conditions, and date effects $\delta_t$ absorb market-wide conditions.

On the common sample in columns 1 and 2, a 100 basis point incumbent output advantage predicts \ContestSameSampleOutputAdvantageHundredBpEffect{} higher retention before controlling for prior capital and \ContestOutputAdvantageHundredBpEffect{} after controlling for it. A 10 pp incumbent capital-share advantage predicts another \ContestCapitalAdvantageTenPpEffect{} in column 2. The exact-output coefficient remains similar across columns. Current quotes predict route choice, while prior two-leg capital contains additional information about the incumbent's position.

We then follow the same pair when exact-price leadership changes. A crossing occurs when the pair-month median exact-output gap moves from at least one basis point in favour of the incumbent in month $t-1$ to at least one basis point in favour of the challenger in month $t$. Let $L_{e,1}$ indicate that the challenger remains the price leader one month later, and let $Q_{e,-1}$ be its prior-calendar weak-leg capital share. We estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:crossing-transmission}
\begin{aligned}
L_{e,1}&=a+\theta Q_{e,-1}+X_e'c+\lambda_{m(e)}+u_e,\\
\Delta R_{e\tau}&=\mu_e+\rho\,\mathbf{1}\{\tau=0\}+Z_{e\tau}'d+\nu_{e\tau},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the second line stacks the crossing-month change from $t-1$ to $t$ with the same event's earlier change from $t-3$ to $t-2$. Event effects $\mu_e$ compare the two changes within a crossing. The first line controls for challenger family, the price gaps in months $t-1$ and $t$, prior incumbent route share, log route activity, and crossing-calendar-month effects. In the second line, $Z_{e\tau}$ contains interactions of the crossing-month indicator with prior challenger capital share and an indicator for a stablecoin challenger.

Incumbent route share falls \RankCrossingPlaceboDrop{} more at the crossing than during the same event's earlier movement (Table~\ref{tab:capital-price-transmission}, panel B, column 2). A 10 pp larger challenger share of prior weak-leg capital predicts \RankCrossingCapitalPersistence{} greater probability that the new price lead lasts one month (panel B, column 1). Route share moves with the exact-output ranking; prior two-leg capital predicts whether the challenger keeps the lead.

The first sampled contest arrives slowly for most entering pairs that reach one. Only \FirstContestCoverage{} of material entrants enter the monthly common-support sample, after a median of \FirstContestMedianLagDays{} days; the entry family carries \FirstContestRouteRetention{} of routes at that first contest (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:first-contestable-choice}, panel A). Much of the persistence documented in Section~\ref{sec:dominance:entry-persistence} therefore accumulates before a sampled rival becomes feasible. At the first contest and in the mature sample above, current exact prices strongly predict the chosen family.

Persistence has a modest average execution cost in the common-support sample. The observed vehicle family offers at least one basis point less output on \ContestLowerOutputFamilyShare{} of contestable routes, with an input-value-weighted shortfall of \ContestForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:contestable-vehicle-consequences}, panel A). Among routes that retain a mature exclusive incumbent, the corresponding incidence is \ContestRetainedLowerOutputShare{} and the weighted shortfall is \ContestRetainedForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} (panel B). Incorporating predicted path-specific gas changes the overall weighted shortfall from \GasConsequenceOverallGrossShortfallBp{} to \GasConsequenceOverallNetShortfallBp{} basis points (panel D). The same table shows that the shortfall is concentrated in younger and smaller pairs.
