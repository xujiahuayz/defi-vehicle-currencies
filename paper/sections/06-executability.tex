\section{What the price comparison can identify}
\label{sec:executability}

The realised routes identify which assets carried trades. Explaining route choice requires the alternatives available immediately before execution, their output at the same trade size, and the costs of reaching them. We therefore keep the vehicle-dominance results in Section~\ref{sec:dominance} separate from the counterfactual price comparison.

The comparison in Section~\ref{sec:setting:pricing} holds the endpoints, input amount, and pool state fixed. For each trade, it asks how much the best direct path and the best two-leg path through a particular intermediary would have returned. A quote difference describes the reconstructed opportunity set. Interpreting it as route choice additionally requires the router's quote time, searched exchanges, access to private order flow, and financing and reversion costs, which the public record generally leaves latent.

Two implementation conditions are essential. First, pool balances and liquidity must be measured immediately before the transaction. A closing hourly or daily state can incorporate trades and liquidity changes that occurred after the route was chosen. We reconstruct constant-product balances by replaying swaps, liquidity additions, and withdrawals in transaction order. For concentrated-liquidity pools, we replay initialization, liquidity changes, and swaps in block and log order. A pool-period enters the comparison only when the required event history is complete and the reconstructed state reproduces the realised trade. Appendix~\ref{app:state} describes these checks.

Second, a hypothetical trade should resemble the trades on which the pricing rule has been evaluated. We retain a leg only when its size relative to available depth lies within the range represented among realised swaps. Because the support restriction is set from trade size and pool state before outputs are compared, selection is independent of the measured price difference. Appendix~\ref{app:support} reports the calibration.

A third condition is market coverage. The price comparison covers a narrower market than the realised-route panel. The largest omission is concentrated in Curve pools that connect native assets and stablecoins but use a different invariant from the StableSwap pools we can price. These exclusions account for 34.2\% of assessed Curve volume and are concentrated in native-asset depth: 65.2\% of native-leg volume is excluded, compared with 21.1\% of stable-leg volume. Hook-bearing and dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools are omitted because their transaction-level cash-flow rules cannot be recovered from the available records. Historical state is also unavailable for SushiSwap v3 and for several Balancer pool families. These omissions can improve either the direct or the intermediated alternative, so their effect on the difference between the two is not known in advance. Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} reports their magnitude and composition.

Pre-transaction state, trade-size support, and market coverage leave a sharp division in the evidence. Route incidence and vehicle dominance use observed token flows and remain measurable whenever the route itself is recovered. Tests that connect vehicle use to relative execution cost require the stricter pre-transaction state and market-coverage conditions. Route-composition results do not depend on the counterfactual price comparison; relative-cost tests use only observations with complete pre-transaction state and market coverage.
