\section{Realised routes and execution costs}
\label{sec:executability}

Realised routes identify the assets that carried each trade; execution costs can explain that choice only relative to the alternatives available immediately beforehand. Those alternatives must be quoted at the same trade size and over the venues the trader or router could reach. Section~\ref{sec:dominance} uses the observed paths directly. Testing the cost mechanism adds the counterfactual opportunity set.

We hold the endpoints, input amount, and pre-transaction pool state fixed and compare the output of direct and two-leg paths. This extends the realised-versus-optimised benchmark for allocations among parallel pools \citep{XiMoallemi2026Routing} to sequential paths through an intermediary. Our public on-chain opportunity set may be broader than the router's search set and omits private order flow, financing costs, and the cost of a reverted transaction. It nevertheless requires pool balances and liquidity immediately before each trade. Because hourly or daily closing states can contain information that arrived after route choice, we replay swaps and liquidity events in their on-chain order and restrict hypothetical trades to the common 5\% own-price-impact perimeter described in Appendices~\ref{app:state} and~\ref{app:support}. Component exercises reproduce selected realised swaps. Complete pre-transaction states for every included pool family remain in progress.

Component audits locate the parts of the market for which the price comparison is most selective. In the sampled Curve audit, pools inconsistent with the StableSwap quoter account for 34.2\% of assessed Curve volume and are concentrated in native-asset depth: 65.2\% of native-leg volume, compared with 21.1\% of stable-leg volume. Hook-bearing or dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools, SushiSwap v3 pools without historical state, and several Balancer pool families require separate treatment. Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} reports these historical component exercises; their shares do not measure complete coverage across every pool family and date.

Observed token flows establish which intermediaries carried realised trading and where the rotation occurred. The fixed-opportunity analysis will determine whether execution costs help explain that allocation once complete pre-transaction states permit comparable direct and indirect paths. Until then, execution costs remain a mechanism under test rather than a reported estimate.
