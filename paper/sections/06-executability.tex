\section{Exact prices, full-range capital, and vehicle retention}
\label{sec:executability}

Executable price differences can persist when trading is segmented across cryptocurrency markets \citep{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage}. We therefore compare stablecoin and WETH routes at the same input and pretrade state. For each observed two-leg route on the fifteenth day of every month from June 2020 through June 2026, we quote the best WETH route and the best stablecoin route through DAI, USDC, or USDT across Uniswap v2, SushiSwap v2, and Uniswap v3. The observed quote must reproduce realised output within one basis point; both vehicle families must be feasible; every observed and hypothetical leg must remain below 5\% own-price impact; leg values must agree within 20\%; and input value must be at least \$100. Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} gives the pricing equations, Appendix~\ref{app:support} calibrates the common support rule, and Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:exact-vehicle-frontier} reports the broader price frontier.
% output/exhibits/exact_vehicle_frontier_monthly.jsonl; output/exhibits/contestable_vehicle_choice_support.jsonl

We call a family the incumbent when it alone carries the pair on the first observed vehicle day and begin measuring retention 30 days later. For route $r$, pair $p$, and date $t$, the price-and-capital specification is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contestable-retention}
R_{rpt}=\beta_O A^O_{rpt}+\beta_K A^K_{pt-1}+\beta_q\log q_r+\alpha_p+\delta_t+\varepsilon_{rpt}.
\end{equation}
Here $R_{rpt}$ equals one when the route retains the incumbent family. The exact-output advantage is $A^O_{rpt}=\left[10^4(O^{\mathrm{inc}}_{rpt}-O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt})/O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt}\right]_{-1000}^{1000}/100$, so one unit represents 100 basis points. For family $v$, weak-leg capital is $K^v_{pt-1}=\min\{K_{i,v,t-1},K_{v,o,t-1}\}$, where each leg aggregates prior-calendar deposited capital across full-range Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools. Its relative advantage is $A^K_{pt-1}=\{K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}/(K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}+K^{\mathrm{chall}}_{pt-1})-1/2\}/0.10$, so one unit represents a 10-percentage-point shift from an equal capital split. Pair fixed effects $\alpha_p$ absorb persistent pair-level routing preferences, and date fixed effects $\delta_t$ absorb market-wide conditions.

Table~\ref{tab:contestable-vehicle-choice} reports the route-choice estimates. Column 1 relates contemporaneous price leadership to route choice; the coefficient on stablecoin price leadership is \ContestPriceLeaderEffect{}. Among mature pairs with an exclusive incumbent, the incumbent carries \ContestIncumbentLeaderRetention{} of routes when it leads on exact output and \ContestChallengerLeaderRetention{} when the challenger leads. In column 2, the within-pair, within-date coefficient on challenger leadership is \ContestChallengerEffect{}. Columns 3 and 4 use the same \ContestOutputAdvantageHundredBpN{} routes, each with positive prior-day full-range constant-product capital for both vehicle families. The coefficient on a 100-basis-point incumbent output advantage is \ContestSameSampleOutputAdvantageHundredBpEffect{} when entered alone in column 3 and \ContestOutputAdvantageHundredBpEffect{} after capital enters in column 4. A 10-percentage-point incumbent capital-share advantage is associated with \ContestCapitalAdvantageTenPpEffect{} higher retention in column 4. The exact-output coefficient changes little across the two columns, while lagged two-leg capital adds explanatory power beyond the current quote.

The same comparison gives the output consequence of vehicle choice. Panel A of Table~\ref{tab:contestable-vehicle-consequences} shows that \ContestLowerOutputFamilyShare{} of routes use the family that returns at least one basis point less. Conditional on that choice, the median shortfall is \ContestForegoneMedianBps{} and the 90th percentile is \ContestForegonePNinetyBps{}; weighting every contestable route by input value gives \ContestForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{}. Among routes that retain a mature exclusive incumbent, \ContestRetainedLowerOutputShare{} leave more than one basis point of exact output, with a \ContestRetainedForegoneMedianBps{} conditional median and \ContestRetainedForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} across all retained routes (panel B). Panel C reports value-weighted shortfalls of \ContestYoungForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} for pairs aged 0--89 days, \ContestMiddleForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} for pairs aged 90--364 days, and \ContestMatureForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} after one year. The price cost is concentrated earlier in a pair's life. Established routes remain responsive to public prices, while weak-leg capital retains an independent association with vehicle use.

The benchmark represents public on-chain liquidity in the declared exchange set. Private order flow, financing costs, losses from reverted transactions, and pools outside that set can alter a router's choice. Across sampled Curve pool-days, pools outside the StableSwap model carry 34.2\% of assessed volume, including 65.2\% of native-leg volume and 21.1\% of stablecoin-leg volume (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:curveleg}, panel A). Hook-bearing or dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools, SushiSwap v3, and several Balancer families also use pricing states outside the benchmark.
