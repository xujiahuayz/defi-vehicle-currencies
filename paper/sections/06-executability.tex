\section{Comparing direct and intermediated execution}
\label{sec:executability}

Realised routes identify the assets that carried each trade. Execution cost is a separate object: it compares those routes with alternatives available immediately beforehand, quoted at the same trade size and over a common set of exchanges. We hold the source $i$, destination $o$, dollar input $q$, and pre-transaction pool state $e^-$ fixed. The best direct and two-leg outputs through intermediary $k$ are
\begin{equation*}
O^{D}_{i,o,q,e}=P_{o,e}\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,o,e^-}}Q_p\!\left(i\to o,\Delta_i(q,e);\chi_{p,e^-}\right),
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,e}=P_{o,e}\max_{p'\in\mathcal P_{k,o,e^-}}Q_{p'}\!\left(k\to o,\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,k,e^-}}Q_p\!\left(i\to k,\Delta_i(q,e);\chi_{p,e^-}\right);\chi_{p',e^-}\right)
\end{equation*}
This comparison extends the realised-versus-optimised allocation benchmark of \citet{XiMoallemi2026Routing} from parallel pools connecting the same two assets to sequential routes through an intermediary.

The vehicle-route shortfall relative to direct execution is
\begin{equation*}
\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,e}=\frac{O^{D}_{i,o,q,e}-O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,e}}{O^{D}_{i,o,q,e}},
\end{equation*}
reported in basis points as $10^{4}\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,e}$. Positive values mean that the direct route returns more output before gas. Swaps and liquidity changes are replayed in on-chain order because a daily or hourly closing state incorporates events that occurred after the trade. The observed event marks settlement; a quote formed earlier can reflect a different opportunity set.

We compare hypothetical legs only within a common executable range. Let $\widetilde Q_{\ell}$ denote leg $\ell$'s output at the pool's marginal rate. Each leg satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:support}
\pi_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)=\frac{\widetilde Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)-Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)}{\widetilde Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,e^-)}\leq \bar\pi,
\qquad \bar\pi=0.05.
\end{equation}
The 5\% own-price-impact ceiling applies across pool families; contract-specific limits add further restrictions. Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} gives the pricing equations and Appendix~\ref{app:support} calibrates the support rule.

Executable price differences can persist when trading is segmented across cryptocurrency markets \citep{MakarovSchoar2020Arbitrage}. Prices therefore offer a demanding rival explanation for persistent vehicle use. If the intermediary merely records the price in one locally attractive pool, widening the same-state opportunity set should often change the intermediary. We test that prediction on the fifteenth day of every month from June 2020 through June 2026. Each observed two-leg route must reproduce realised output within one basis point. We then widen the opportunity set in three stages: alternative paths using the same vehicle and venue families as the observed route, paths using the same vehicle across Uniswap v2, SushiSwap v2, and Uniswap v3, and paths using any named public vehicle or the direct connection across those three exchanges. The main sample also requires coherent leg values, at least \$100 of input, and the 5\% price-impact bound on the realised route.

Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:exact-vehicle-frontier} shows that the chosen vehicle survives broader price competition. Only \ExactFrontierWithinShare{} of routes have a same-vehicle alternative that improves gross output by more than one basis point within the venue families already used. Opening all three exact venues raises that share to \ExactFrontierSameVehicleShare{}. Opening the vehicle set and the direct path raises it only another \ExactFrontierVehicleAddition{}, to \ExactFrontierAllPathShare{}. Among routes with an improvement, the median gain is \ExactFrontierMedianGain{}. Thus public venue prices often favour a different venue while preserving the intermediary asset.

The full price set lowers stablecoin vehicle share by \ExactFrontierStableDecline{} on a route-weighted basis and \ExactFrontierStableValueDecline{} when routes are weighted by input value. We next compare stablecoin and WETH routes directly. For each observed route, we quote the best route through WETH and the best route through DAI, USDC, or USDT while holding the pair, input amount, and pretrade state fixed. Both families must satisfy the support condition in Equation~\eqref{eq:support}. We call a family the incumbent when it alone carries the pair on the first observed vehicle day and begin measuring retention 30 days later. For route $r$, pair $p$, and date $t$, the joint price-and-capital specification is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contestable-retention}
R_{rpt}=\beta_O A^O_{rpt}+\beta_K A^K_{pt-1}+\beta_q\log q_r+\alpha_p+\delta_t+\varepsilon_{rpt}.
\end{equation}
Here $R_{rpt}$ equals one when the route retains the incumbent family. The exact-output advantage is $A^O_{rpt}=\left[10^4(O^{\mathrm{inc}}_{rpt}-O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt})/O^{\mathrm{chall}}_{rpt}\right]_{-1000}^{1000}/100$, so one unit represents 100 basis points. For family $v$, weak-leg capital is $K^v_{pt-1}=\min\{K_{i,v,t-1},K_{v,o,t-1}\}$, using deposited capital on the prior calendar day. Its relative advantage is $A^K_{pt-1}=\{K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}/(K^{\mathrm{inc}}_{pt-1}+K^{\mathrm{chall}}_{pt-1})-1/2\}/0.10$, so one unit represents a 10-percentage-point shift from an equal capital split. Pair fixed effects $\alpha_p$ absorb persistent pair-level routing preferences, and date fixed effects $\delta_t$ absorb market-wide conditions.

The same comparison gives the output consequence of vehicle choice. Across the pooled common-support sample, \ContestLowerOutputFamilyShare{} of routes use the family that returns at least one basis point less. Conditional on that choice, the median shortfall is \ContestForegoneMedianBps{} and the 90th percentile is \ContestForegonePNinetyBps{}; weighting every contestable route by input value gives \ContestForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{}. Among routes that retain a mature exclusive incumbent, \ContestRetainedLowerOutputShare{} leave more than one basis point of exact output, with a \ContestRetainedForegoneMedianBps{} conditional median and \ContestRetainedForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} across all retained routes. The corresponding value-weighted shortfall is \ContestYoungForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} for pairs aged 0--89 days, \ContestMiddleForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} for pairs aged 90--364 days, and \ContestMatureForegoneInputValueWeightedBps{} after one year. The price cost is concentrated earlier in a pair's life. Route allocation remains closely related to public prices, while full-range capital retains an independent association with vehicle use.

The benchmark represents public on-chain liquidity in the declared exchange set. Private order flow, financing costs, losses from reverted transactions, and pools outside that set can alter a router's choice. Pool-family coverage is economically material. In the sampled Curve exercise, pools outside the StableSwap model carry 34.2\% of assessed volume, including 65.2\% of native-leg volume and 21.1\% of stablecoin-leg volume. Hook-bearing or dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools, SushiSwap v3, and several Balancer families also use pricing states outside the benchmark. Appendix~\ref{app:coverage} quantifies these coverage limits.
