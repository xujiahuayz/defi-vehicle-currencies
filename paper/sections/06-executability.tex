\section{Realised routes and execution costs}
\label{sec:executability}

Realised routes identify the assets that carried each trade. Explaining route choice through execution costs requires the alternatives available immediately before execution, quoted at the same trade size and over the venues the trader or router could reach. The route-composition results in Section~\ref{sec:dominance} follow directly from observed paths. The cost mechanism adds this counterfactual opportunity set.

The comparison holds the endpoints, input amount, and pre-transaction pool state fixed and compares the output of direct and two-leg paths. It measures alternatives in public on-chain states, although a router may also condition on private order flow, financing costs, the cost of a reverted transaction, and a search set narrower than the public market. Building the public comparison requires pool balances and liquidity immediately before each transaction. Closing hourly or daily states can contain information that arrived after route choice, so the design replays swaps and liquidity events in their on-chain order and restricts hypothetical trades to the common 5\% own-price-impact perimeter described in Appendices~\ref{app:state} and~\ref{app:support}. Component exercises reproduce selected realised swaps; complete pre-transaction states for every included pool family are not yet available.

The component audits show where the price comparison is most selective. In the sampled Curve audit, pools inconsistent with the StableSwap quoter account for 34.2\% of assessed Curve volume and are concentrated in native-asset depth: 65.2\% of native-leg volume, compared with 21.1\% of stable-leg volume. Hook-bearing or dynamic-fee Uniswap v4 pools, SushiSwap v3 pools without historical state, and several Balancer pool families require separate treatment. These shares describe the historical components examined in Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}, rather than complete coverage of every pool family and date.

Observed token flows establish which intermediaries carried realised trading and where the rotation occurred. The fixed-opportunity analysis asks whether execution costs help explain that allocation, using complete pre-transaction states and comparable direct and indirect paths. Because those inputs are incomplete, we treat execution costs as a mechanism to be tested and do not report a cost estimate.
