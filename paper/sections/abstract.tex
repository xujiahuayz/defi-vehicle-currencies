We study how vehicle-currency dominance changes when the intermediary is observed on each transaction. Decentralised exchanges record the full trading route, which allows us to distinguish an asset's intermediary use from demand to buy or sell it. From 2020 to 2026, vehicle use rotates sharply but unevenly from the native asset toward fiat-backed stablecoins, with USDT accounting for most of the increase. Yet the increase disappears when we compare the same ordered endpoint market on the same calendar day and within the same broad route scope: the stable share changes by 0.22 percentage points by count and $-1.35$ points by value. The aggregate rotation therefore reflects changes in which markets are active and where routed activity takes place; comparable markets exhibit no uniform drift. We also find two-way predictability between vehicle use and the concentration of liquidity in vehicle-linked pools. In matched route receipts, Uniswap V4 sometimes records no transfer of the intermediary token even though the economic route continues to pass through it. Vehicle dominance develops through the organisation of markets and liquidity, while settlement architecture can change how the same role is implemented on chain.
