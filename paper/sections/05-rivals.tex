\section{Interpreting the rotation}
\label{sec:rivals}

Stablecoin dominance rose chiefly as routed activity moved across source--destination pairs and the incidence of intermediation changed. Repeated replacement of a native intermediary within continuing pairs contributed little. Any explanation for the rotation must therefore account for the assets being exchanged as well as the intermediary carrying the route.

\subsection{Liquidity and router reach}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. On a decentralised exchange, the relevant markets are the pools connecting an intermediary to other assets. Trading can attract provider capital, deeper pools can lower price impact, and lower price impact can draw further trading. Pool size remains an equilibrium outcome because providers balance fee income against adverse-selection risk and the cost of adjusting their positions \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. Timing helps distinguish the two directions of this feedback. Capital arriving before vehicle use is consistent with liquidity supply drawing routes toward an intermediary; capital arriving afterwards is consistent with providers responding to routed demand. Deposited capital and executable liquidity also measure different objects. The former captures intermediation capacity, whereas concentrated-liquidity providers can change the latter at a given trade size by repositioning capital within a pool \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3}. This distinction resembles the separation of dealer capacity from liquidity supplied at a particular level of trading demand \citep{HuangRanaldoSchrimpfSomogyi2025Constrained}.

A low-cost path can attract trading only when the routing system reaches the venue that offers it. Cross-exchange trading becomes much more common during the sample, yet stablecoin share also rises within single-exchange routes. The rotation therefore reaches both route scopes. Fragmented liquidity can still matter within each category because routers may search different pools and paths \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}.

\subsection{Asset risk and software architecture}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

USDT's rising intermediary use relative to its endpoint use makes differences within the stablecoin category economically relevant. USDT and USDC differ in issuance, redemption access, venue coverage, and the risks borne by holders and liquidity providers \citep{LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}. Inventory models predict that changes in those risks alter the cost of supporting an intermediary's markets \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. A native-asset drawdown exposes providers to volatile inventory; a stablecoin depeg changes the risk of an asset whose usefulness depends partly on price stability. Under a balance-sheet explanation, these risk changes should precede changes in USDT's intermediary use relative to its endpoint use.

Protocol design changes the set of feasible routes. Uniswap v1 formed pools between an ERC-20 token and ETH, so a token-to-token trade crossed ETH; Uniswap v2 enabled arbitrary ERC-20--ERC-20 pairs and allowed the two tokens to trade in one pool.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} Native-asset pairing persisted after this requirement disappeared. The share of single-leg Uniswap v2 trades executed in WETH pools was 95.1\% in 2020 and 95.5\% in 2026, and ranged from 93.4\% to 97.9\% in the intervening years. Among the 477,633 pairs that ever traded on Uniswap v2, 97.1\% include WETH, while the WETH share of newly traded pairs rose from 84.1\% in 2020 to 97.9\% in 2026. Deep WETH pools, launch conventions, and inherited search paths can all produce that persistence; estimating an additional effect on route choice requires a fixed-opportunity comparison.

Uniswap v4 separates route choice from physical settlement more sharply. All pools reside in one singleton, and flash accounting records balance changes internally before settling the final net amounts at the end of a call.\footnote{See Adams et al., \href{https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper-v4.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v4 Core}}, Sections 1 and 3.} An economic route can therefore cross an intermediary pool without generating a separate external token transfer at each step. The mechanism test uses decoded pool actions and holds the ordered source--destination asset pair and feasible alternatives fixed.

Liquidity, router reach, asset risk, and software can move together for the same source and destination assets, and each can raise USDT's intermediary use relative to its endpoint use. Their distinct timing predictions provide the separating evidence. The tests therefore align capital flows, risk changes, and route costs for the same ordered asset pair, feasible routes, and trade size.
