\section{Liquidity provision and network formation}
\label{sec:liquidity}

Routers can choose only among paths whose two legs have already received capital. Liquidity providers choose which links to fund, and automated routers compare the resulting size-specific quotes when selecting a route. Liquidity supply is voluntary and responds to expected trading conditions \citep{LiWangYe2021WhoProvides}. In concentrated-liquidity pools, providers also choose the price range in which their capital is active \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. We study two complementary samples. Uniswap v3 additions reveal the participation and prior experience of transaction origins that supply stablecoin- and WETH-facing pools. Full-range Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 reserves then date the appearance of two-leg stablecoin capital before the first observed stablecoin route. The samples connect provider participation, capital placement, and route use without tracking the same position across pool designs.

\subsection{Stable-facing liquidity additions}
\label{sec:liquidity:providers}

Uniswap v3 records each addition to a liquidity position and the transaction origin that initiates it. We call a pool a \emph{spoke} when it pairs exactly one of WETH, DAI, USDC, or USDT with an asset outside that set; a \emph{stablecoin core} pool pairs two of DAI, USDC, and USDT. The stable-facing share of spoke-pool addition actions rises from \VThreeLPAddActionBaselineStableShare{} in 2024 H1 to \VThreeLPAddActionComparisonStableShare{} in 2026 H1. The share of vehicle-side dollar additions follows a similar rotation, from \VThreeLPUSDFlowBaselineStableShare{} to \VThreeLPUSDFlowComparisonStableShare{} (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel A). Thus gross additions shift sharply toward stable-facing pools as routed activity rotates.
% output/exhibits/v3_lp_origin_supply_decomposition.jsonl

We decompose this change across transaction origins using the same midpoint identity as the pair decomposition. Period-specific origins contribute \VThreeLPAddActionPeriodSpecificOrigins{} of the \VThreeLPAddActionStableShareChange{} increase in the stable-facing action share, while changes within continuing origins contribute \VThreeLPAddActionWithinContinuingOrigins{}. The corresponding contributions for dollar additions are \VThreeLPUSDFlowPeriodSpecificOrigins{} and \VThreeLPUSDFlowWithinContinuingOrigins{} (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel A). A transaction origin measures participation in the liquidity addition; beneficial ownership and coordinated control remain unobserved.

Pool maturity separates this rotation from short-lived launches. Pools older than 90 days supply \VThreeLPMaturePoolIncreaseShare{} of the increase in stable-facing addition actions (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:v3-lp-launch-supply}, panel A). After excluding each pool's first seven observed days, the stable-facing action share rises from \VThreeLPExLaunchStableShareBaseline{} to \VThreeLPExLaunchStableShareComparison{} (panel B). Among origins present only in 2026 H1, one-day, one-pool participation supplies \VThreeLPOneDayOnePoolActionShare{} of stable-facing actions and \VThreeLPOneDayOnePoolFlowShare{} of vehicle-side dollar additions (panel C). Mature pools and repeated participation therefore carry most of the rotation in addition activity.

The formation of a material spoke pool also draws disproportionately on origins with experience supplying the same vehicle elsewhere. For every endpoint--vehicle pool, we date the first week in which reported total value locked reaches \VThreeLPFormationTVLThreshold{}. We then compare the vehicles supplied by each transaction origin during that week and measure its supply during the preceding \VThreeLPFormationLookback{}, excluding the focal pool and all pools attached to the focal endpoint. For formation event $e$, origin $o$, and vehicle $v$, let $Y_{eov}$ equal one when $v$ is the vehicle in the newly material pool. Let $H_{ov,e-1}$ denote the origin's prior same-vehicle supply elsewhere in the network, measured either as an indicator or as log endpoint breadth. We estimate
\begin{equation}
Y_{eov}=\beta H_{ov,e-1}+\alpha_{eo}+\lambda_{v,q(e)}+\varepsilon_{eov},
\label{eq:provider-specialization}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{eo}$ are formation-event-by-origin effects and $\lambda_{v,q(e)}$ are vehicle-by-calendar-quarter effects. Prior supply of the same vehicle predicts a \VThreeLPSameVehicleSupplyEffect{} higher probability that the origin supplies that vehicle in the new pool (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel B, column 1). A one-unit increase in log prior endpoint breadth predicts \VThreeLPSameVehicleBreadthEffect{} higher probability (column 2). Both estimates remain statistically significant after Holm adjustment across the four reported models. This specialisation is consistent with existing vehicle networks supplying new spokes. Common market makers can similarly link liquidity conditions across securities \citep{CoughenourSaad2004CommonMarketMakers}.

Within stablecoins, prior supply to a same-token core pool has a \VThreeLPSameStablecoinCoreSupplyEffect{} coefficient (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel B, column 3), and prior same-token core-pool breadth has a \VThreeLPSameStablecoinCoreBreadthEffect{} coefficient (column 4). The first estimate is imprecise after multiplicity adjustment, while the breadth estimate is significant at the 10\% level. Cross-vehicle specialisation is precisely estimated; the stablecoin-core estimates are weaker and suggestive of core dollar-token liquidity extending into spokes. We observe the pools that receive liquidity; a provider choice model would additionally require each participant's full opportunity set.

\subsection{Full-range constant-product capital before route use}
\label{sec:liquidity:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. Here an indirect route needs two legs: one connecting the source to the vehicle and another connecting the vehicle to the destination. We measure deposited capital as validated full-range reserve value in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools. The weaker leg determines this two-leg capital measure. Dollar capital changes with reserve quantity and valuation; square-root reserves can change through provider actions, retained fees, and token transfers. Deposited capital is an equilibrium stock on those constant-product venues; size-specific pretrade quotes record the output available at a given trade size across the broader venue set. Concentrated-liquidity providers can change local executable depth by moving capital along the price curve \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

We follow each pair by calendar week before its first observed route through DAI, USDC, or USDT. Neither endpoint belongs to the native or stablecoin vehicle family, every pair has recent WETH-mediated activity, and pairs that never adopt remain in the weekly sample. Let \(A_{pt}\) equal one when pair \(p\), with endpoints \(i\) and \(j\), first uses a stablecoin during week \(t\). Let \(K_{av,t-1}=K_{va,t-1}\) denote full-range capital at the start of the week on the leg joining endpoint \(a\) to vehicle \(v\). For \(\mathcal S=\{\mathrm{DAI},\mathrm{USDC},\mathrm{USDT}\}\), Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge-adoption-risk} defines the vehicle capital measures and adoption model:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B^S_{pt}&=\max_{v\in\mathcal S}\min\{K_{iv,t-1},K_{jv,t-1}\},\qquad
B^W_{pt}=\min\{K_{iW,t-1},K_{jW,t-1}\},\\
A_{pt}&=\beta Z_{pt}+\alpha_p+\lambda_t+g(a_{pt})+\gamma'X_{pt}+\varepsilon_{pt}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bridge-adoption-risk}
\end{equation}
Here \(W\) denotes WETH, while \(B^S_{pt}\) and \(B^W_{pt}\) are stablecoin and WETH weak-leg capital. Depending on the column, \(Z_{pt}\) is any positive stablecoin support, the stablecoin-to-WETH weak-leg capital ratio among positive-support weeks, or stablecoin's share of joint weak-leg capital. Pair and week effects absorb persistent pair conditions and market-wide changes; \(g(a_{pt})\) controls flexibly for pair age, and \(X_{pt}\) contains WETH weak-leg capital and recent WETH-route activity.

Any measured stablecoin capital on both legs at the start of the week predicts \BridgeAdoptionRiskAnySupport{} higher probability of first observed stablecoin route use (Table~\ref{tab:bridge-adoption-risk}, panel A, column 1). Among repeated positive-support weeks, a tenfold increase in the stablecoin-to-WETH weak-leg capital ratio predicts \BridgeAdoptionRiskIntensiveTenfold{} higher first-use probability (column 2). Requiring at least 50 WETH routes on five days during the prior 28 days gives \BridgeAdoptionRiskStrictAnySupport{} and \BridgeAdoptionRiskStrictIntensiveTenfold{}, respectively (panel B, columns 1--2). The presence of measured capital on both legs and its scale relative to WETH therefore provide separate extensive and intensive margins before first use.
% output/exhibits/bridge_adoption_risk_set.jsonl; primary and strict weekly risk-set models.

A 10 pp larger preweek stablecoin capital share predicts \BridgeAdoptionRiskPreweek{} higher adoption (Table~\ref{tab:bridge-adoption-risk}, panel A, column 3). The next-week association is \BridgeAdoptionRiskFuture{} (column 4); when both enter together, the coefficients are \BridgeAdoptionRiskJointPreweek{} and \BridgeAdoptionRiskJointFuture{} (column 5). Measured stablecoin capital can precede first use, while next-week capital also co-moves with adoption. The next-week measure combines provider decisions, valuation, reserve adjustment, and any response to use, so the joint timing is consistent with feedback around formation.
