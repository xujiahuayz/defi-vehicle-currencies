\section{How vehicle use becomes established}
\label{sec:rivals}

The pair decomposition locates the aggregate rotation in pairs that become active and attract trading. We first ask whether the vehicle used when a pair enters persists. We then study how two-leg capital makes a competing vehicle available and how exact prices allocate trading once stablecoin and WETH paths coexist.

\subsection{Vehicle persistence after pair entry}
\label{sec:rivals:entry-persistence}

The vehicle used when a pair enters strongly predicts its subsequent use. For pair $p$, let $E_p$ be the stablecoin share on its entry day. For each of the disjoint windows $h\in\{1\text{--}30,31\text{--}120\}$, let $R_{p,h}$ indicate that the pair carries another native- or stablecoin-intermediated route, and let $S_{p,h}$ be the stablecoin share when $R_{p,h}=1$. We estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:entry-persistence}
\begin{aligned}
S_{p,h}&=\alpha_h+\beta_h E_p+X_p'\delta_h+\varepsilon_{p,h},
&& R_{p,h}=1,\\
R_{p,h}&=a_h+b_h E_p+X_p'c_h+u_{p,h},
&& \text{all entrant pairs}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second line is a linear probability model. The controls $X_p$ contain the cohort year, a stablecoin-endpoint indicator, log entry-day route count, direct-route share, and complex-route share. The sample uses pairs entering by March 2 in 2024 or 2026, giving every pair 120 complete calendar days of follow-up, and keeps pairs whose endpoints lie outside the native group.

We separate later vehicle use from pair survival. Of the \EntryPersistenceEntrants{} entrants, \EntryPersistenceEarlyRetradeRate{} trade again during days 1--30 and \EntryPersistenceLateRetradeRate{} do so during days 31--120 (Table~\ref{tab:entry-persistence}, panel B). Among pairs that trade again, a 10 pp higher entry-day stablecoin share predicts \EntryPersistenceEarlyPairEffectPerTen{} higher stablecoin share during days 1--30 and \EntryPersistenceLatePairEffectPerTen{} during days 31--120 (panel A, columns 2 and 5). The route-activity-weighted estimates are \EntryPersistenceEarlyActivityEffectPerTen{} and \EntryPersistenceLateActivityEffectPerTen{}, respectively (columns 3 and 6). Every follow-up outcome begins after the entry day, so these estimates capture continued vehicle use across subsequent routes.

Entry-day stablecoin share also predicts a modestly higher probability of subsequent trading: a 10 pp increase corresponds to \EntryPersistenceEarlyRetradeEffectPerTen{} during days 1--30 and \EntryPersistenceLateRetradeEffectPerTen{} during days 31--120 (Table~\ref{tab:entry-persistence}, panel B). Vehicle identity therefore persists strongly among pairs active in each window, alongside a smaller survival margin. We interpret both as descriptive equilibrium relationships: relative prices, depth, and venue access can shape the vehicle at entry and remain relevant for later trading.

Protocol history gives a benchmark for inherited vehicle use. Uniswap v1 required each token-to-token trade to cross ETH; v2 allowed any two ERC-20 tokens to form a pool.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} We reconstruct \VOneForcedRoutes{} connected v1 token-to-token routes. In 2026, after six years of open pair formation, \VTwoWethTradeShare{} of single-leg v2 trades still execute in WETH pools and \VTwoWethNewPairShare{} of newly traded token combinations include WETH (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:v1-architecture}, panels A and B). Venue migration changed the available token set, so this chronology serves as descriptive context. The bridge and price estimates below compare pairs in which stablecoin and WETH routes coexist.

\subsection{Full-range constant-product capital and route allocation}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. Here an indirect route needs two legs: one connecting the source to the vehicle and another connecting the vehicle to the destination. We measure deposited capital as validated full-range reserve value in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools. The weaker leg determines this two-leg capital measure. It is an equilibrium stock on those constant-product venues; size-specific pretrade quotes provide the closer measure of executable liquidity across the broader venue set. Concentrated-liquidity providers can also change executable depth by moving capital along the price curve \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

We date bridge formation using only information available before route choice. The event is the first date on which DAI, USDC, or USDT has at least \BridgeExanteThreshold{} of prior-calendar full-range capital in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools on each of the two legs required by the pair. Every pair routed through WETH earlier, and the event predates any observed stablecoin route in the pair. This definition yields \BridgeExanteEvents{} bridge events. Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment}, panel A, shows that \BridgeExanteAdoptionThirty{} carry a supported stablecoin route within 30 days and \BridgeExanteAdoptionOneTwenty{} do so within 120 days. Among the first-month adopters, \BridgeExanteLaterUse{} use a supported stablecoin again during days 30--119, although stablecoins account for only \BridgeExanteLaterShare{} of their route activity in that later window. The two-leg capital threshold therefore precedes adoption, while initial use often leaves WETH as the principal vehicle.

The capital comparison follows each bridge after support appears. Let $K_{av,t-1}$ denote prior-calendar full-range capital in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools on the leg joining endpoint $a$ to vehicle $v$. The stablecoin set is $\mathcal S=\{\mathrm{DAI},\mathrm{USDC},\mathrm{USDT}\}$. For pair \((i,j)\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D^S_{et}=\max_{v\in\mathcal S}\min\{K_{iv,t-1},K_{vj,t-1}\},\qquad
&D^W_{et}=\min\{K_{iW,t-1},K_{Wj,t-1}\},\\
Q_{et}=\frac{D^S_{et}}{D^S_{et}+D^W_{et}},\qquad
&S^R_{et}=\beta Q_{et}+\alpha_e+\lambda_{m(t)}+\eta_{a(t)}+\varepsilon_{et}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bridge-relative-depth}
\end{equation}
Here $W$ denotes WETH, $S^R_{et}$ is stablecoin route share, $m(t)$ is calendar month, and $a(t)$ groups days since support into seven-day intervals. Event effects compare changes in relative full-range constant-product capital within the same pair and route scope.

Panel B compares route allocation after that prior-information event with the preceding 30 days. Stablecoin route share rises by \BridgeExanteChangeThirty{} during days 0--29 and by \BridgeExanteChangeOneTwenty{} during days 30--119. Panel C uses the continuous relative-capital share within the same event. A 10 pp increase in the stablecoin share of prior-day weak-leg full-range capital is associated with \BridgeExanteDepthThirty{} more stablecoin route activity during days 0--29 and \BridgeExanteDepthOneTwenty{} during days 30--119. The later coefficient is larger even though adoption remains incomplete. The threshold marks full-range support on both legs; relative capital continues to divide trading after the bridge appears.

The threshold event and subsequent relative-capital estimates connect pair formation to full-range liquidity on Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 using information available at the event date. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:bridge-persistent-support} and Figure~\ref{fig:app:bridge-adoption-capital-path} follow the alternative persistent-support and first-use timing definitions. They show that most pre-use capital growth occurs in pools already active one week earlier (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:bridge-pool-margins}). This pattern is suggestive of providers deepening a bridge in anticipation of route demand, traders waiting for usable depth, or both forces operating together.
