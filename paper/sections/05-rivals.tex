\section{Mechanisms behind incumbency}
\label{sec:rivals}

If an incumbent remains in use after another feasible intermediary becomes cheaper, four frictions could account for the delay. Liquidity providers may move capital slowly across pools; routers may not yet reach the venue carrying the cheaper route; holding risk may make some intermediaries more costly to support; or routing software and pool-creation templates may continue to favour the incumbent. These mechanisms can generate similar persistence in aggregate, but they make different predictions within an ordered endpoint pair.

The classical volume-cost feedback provides the starting point. In \citet{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}, current transaction volume lowers proportional brokerage cost. Provider capital, liquidity reallocation and router access are empirical extensions to that mechanism. In this setting those quantities can in principle be measured separately, allowing the persistence of a vehicle role to be related to the state variable that may sustain it.

\subsection{Liquidity reallocation}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

Past vehicle use may concentrate liquidity in the two pools that connect an incumbent to an endpoint pair. If reallocating that liquidity is costly or infrequent, a cost-disadvantaged incumbent should lose its role more slowly where incumbent-linked depth remains concentrated. Let $C_{abt}$ measure that concentration and add it to the turnover hazard:

\begin{equation}
\Pr\bigl(y_{ab,\,t+1}=1 \mid \cdot\bigr)
=1-\exp\!\left[-\exp\!\left(\alpha_{ab}+\gamma d_{ab\ell t}
+\beta C_{abt}+\zeta d_{ab\ell t}C_{abt}+\eta g_{abt}\right)\right],
\label{eq:capital}
\end{equation}
where $g_{abt}$ is the incumbent's cost gap. Slow liquidity reallocation predicts $\zeta<0$.

Timing distinguishes this mechanism from liquidity that merely follows routing. For horizon $h$,

\begin{equation}
\Delta s_{ab\ell,t+h}=a_h+b_h\Delta C_{abt}+\varphi_h'x_{abt}+\epsilon_{ab,t+h},
\qquad h=-H,\ldots,H .
\label{eq:leadlag}
\end{equation}

Capital moving before depth and routing is consistent with slow reallocation; routing moving first points to liquidity following order flow. A change in executable depth at fixed capital instead points to range placement or market design. The test requires candidate-linked deposited capital and executable depth across the venues available to routers. Venue-level depth alone is insufficient when orders split across exchanges \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. The historical V3 balance series measures virtual depth, leaving deposited capital and this mechanism unestimated.

\subsection{Router access}
\label{sec:rivals:integration}

The router's opportunity set excludes cheaper routes outside its reach. Router access therefore predicts a discrete increase in turnover when the venue carrying the cheaper route becomes reachable. Let $t^*_{ab}$ denote that date and $R_{abt}$ the share of routing volume served by systems that can reach the venue:

\begin{equation}
\log \lambda_{abt}=\alpha_{ab}+\gamma d_{ab\ell t}+\kappa R_{abt}
+\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\pi_h\mathbf{1}\{t-t^*_{ab}=h\}+\epsilon_{abt}.
\label{eq:integration}
\end{equation}

This mechanism predicts $\kappa>0$, no differential movement before integration, and a change at $h=0$. The public chain identifies the contract that executes a route but not necessarily the off-chain system that selected the path. Request-for-quote responses, solver alternatives, and private order flow are also unobserved. Estimating \eqref{eq:integration} therefore requires versioned documentation or implementation evidence that identifies route authorship and a reported measure of the routing volume those systems represent.

\subsection{Holding risk}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

Inventory models motivate a third mechanism \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. If supporting a route through an intermediary ties up risk-bearing capacity, deterioration in that asset should make a cost-disadvantaged incumbent turn over faster. Let $z_{kt}$ denote candidate $k$'s own drawdown over the preceding week:

\begin{equation}
\theta_{kt}=\exp\bigl(\gamma+\xi z_{kt}\bigr).
\label{eq:stress}
\end{equation}

The sign depends on the asset. A native-asset drawdown raises the cost of bearing volatile inventory, while a stablecoin depeg changes the safety of an asset whose usual attraction is low volatility. Candidate-specific episodes can test this prediction. Repeated changes in value-weighted leadership and calendar ordering leave the holding-cost mechanism unidentified in the aggregate rotation.

\subsection{Software defaults}
\label{sec:rivals:defaults}

Routing software and pool-creation templates may continue to point toward an incumbent after cheaper alternatives appear. Unlike gradual liquidity reallocation, a software default predicts a change at a release date, concentration among pairs served by the same software, and little response on venues whose software did not change. For an outcome $X_{vt}$ on venues outside the changed architecture, the corresponding locality test is

\begin{equation}
X_{vt}=\alpha_v+\pi\mathbf{1}\{t\geq t^*\}+\varrho t+\varepsilon_{vt},
\qquad v\notin\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{treated}}.
\label{eq:break}
\end{equation}

The transition from mandatory native-asset routing in the first protocol version to arbitrary token pairs in the second shows that architecture can remove a routing mandate. Continued native-asset pairing after that change is consistent both with software templates and with a thick-market externality. Discrimination between them requires dated software changes and turnover measured among pairs whose feasible route set is otherwise unchanged.

\subsection{Empirical scope}
\label{sec:rivals:scope}

These mechanisms generate distinct predictions, while the current evidence leaves their ranking open. Liquidity reallocation requires validated capital and depth; router access requires route authorship and venue reach; holding risk requires candidate-specific stress; and software defaults require dated, local changes in routing behaviour. Each test is applied to the exact-state turnover hazard of Section~\ref{sec:survival}. Until that hazard is estimated on the common ordered-pair opportunity set, the aggregate facts motivate the mechanisms without selecting among them.
