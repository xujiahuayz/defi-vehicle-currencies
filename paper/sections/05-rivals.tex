\section{How vehicle use becomes established}
\label{sec:rivals}

The pair decomposition locates the aggregate rotation in the pairs that become active and attract trading. The next question is how a vehicle becomes available inside those pairs and why its use persists. Longer routes carry the same rotation. The transition from Uniswap v1 to v2 separates inherited route structure from voluntary vehicle choice. Realised vehicle use then links the aggregate result to the capital on the two legs required to complete the route.

\subsection{The rotation spans short and long routes}
\label{sec:rivals:complexity}

Figure~\ref{fig:vehicle-composition} includes every complete route, with one observation for each intermediary position. Stablecoins gain across both short and long routes and within single- and cross-exchange routes. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:rotation} reports the mutually exclusive vehicle choice on exact two-leg routes; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:network-position}, panel A, shows that stablecoin participation rises from \NetworkStableParticipationTwentyFour{} in 2024 to \NetworkStableParticipationTwentySix{} in 2026 H1 when routes of every length enter. The full route network therefore moves in the same direction as the one-intermediary choice sample.
% output/exhibits/intermediation_complexity_rival.jsonl; output/exhibits/presentation_values.tex

Panel B compares realised use with betweenness in the observed trading graph. WETH remains the highest-betweenness node in every annual graph even as its intermediary-position share falls and the combined USDC--USDT share rises. Binary network position therefore changes much less than realised intermediary use. An edge records that at least one leg connects two assets; local depth and execution cost determine how useful that connection is for a particular route.

\subsection{Protocol architecture and inherited vehicle use}
\label{sec:rivals:architecture}

Uniswap v1 created pools between an ERC-20 token and ETH, so token-to-token trades crossed ETH. Uniswap v2 allowed arbitrary ERC-20--ERC-20 pools and made direct token-to-token trading possible.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} We reconstruct \VOneForcedRoutes{} connected v1 token-to-token routes, as reported in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:v1-architecture}, panel A. In a March 2020 transaction, a trader selling \VOneCaseTokenIn{} \VOneCaseTokenInSymbol{} received \VOneCaseEth{} ETH at one exchange contract and spent the identical ETH amount at a second for \VOneCaseTokenOut{} \VOneCaseTokenOutSymbol{}.\footnote{Transaction \ethtx{\VOneCaseTx}{\VOneCaseTxShort}, block \VOneCaseBlock, March 15, 2020. The transaction is the largest ETH route among the \VOneCaseExactCount{} mandate-era token-to-token transactions whose two ETH legs match exactly. The exact exchange registry identifies the endpoint tokens; public transfer records provide an independent cross-check.}

Native-asset pairing remained pervasive after v2 opened direct token-to-token pools. In 2026, \VTwoWethTradeShare{} of single-leg Uniswap v2 trades execute in WETH pools and \VTwoWethNewPairShare{} of newly traded token combinations include WETH. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:v1-architecture}, panel B, reports both estimates. Native pairing therefore persisted after any two ERC-20 tokens could form a pool. We treat the v1--v2 transition as descriptive because v1 network thinning and migration to v2 changed available token combinations when the mandate disappeared. The pair-entry and bridge-depth estimates below examine settings in which stablecoin and WETH routes coexist.

\subsection{Local bridge depth and route allocation}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. Here an indirect route needs two legs: one connecting the source to the vehicle and another connecting the vehicle to the destination. Capital on only one leg is insufficient. The weaker leg therefore determines the deposited-capital depth of the bridge. Deposited capital remains an equilibrium stock; a size-specific pretrade quote gives the closer measure of executable liquidity. Concentrated-liquidity providers can also change executable depth by moving capital along the price curve \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

We date the first persistent stablecoin bridge for a pair that previously routed through WETH and had no observed stablecoin route. A bridge begins when DAI, USDC, or USDT has positive prior-calendar deposited capital on both required legs for at least 24 of the next 30 days. Panel A of Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment} compares the 30 days before support with the first month and with days 30 through 119. Across \BridgeEstablishmentEvents{} events, stablecoin route share rises by \BridgeEstablishmentCountCoef{} in the first month and supported-value share by \BridgeEstablishmentValueCoef{}. The corresponding count-share increase over days 30 through 119 is 8.68 pp.
% output/exhibits/bridge_liquidity_dominance.jsonl; output/tables/bridge_establishment_regressions.tex

The depth comparison follows each bridge after support appears. Let $K_{av,t-1}$ denote prior-calendar deposited capital on the leg joining endpoint $a$ to vehicle $v$. The stablecoin set is $\mathcal S=\{\mathrm{DAI},\mathrm{USDC},\mathrm{USDT}\}$. For pair \((i,j)\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D^S_{et}=\max_{v\in\mathcal S}\min\{K_{iv,t-1},K_{vj,t-1}\},\qquad
&D^W_{et}=\min\{K_{iW,t-1},K_{Wj,t-1}\},\\
Q_{et}=\frac{D^S_{et}}{D^S_{et}+D^W_{et}},\qquad
&S^R_{et}=\beta Q_{et}+\alpha_e+\lambda_{m(t)}+\eta_{a(t)}+\varepsilon_{et}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bridge-relative-depth}
\end{equation}
Here $W$ denotes WETH, $S^R_{et}$ is stablecoin route share, $m(t)$ is calendar month, and $a(t)$ groups days since support into seven-day intervals. Event effects compare changes in relative depth within the same pair and route scope.

Panel B of Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment} reports the relation. During the first month, the best stablecoin bridge has less than one-tenth of WETH depth on \BridgeDepthThinFirstDays{} of \BridgeDepthDoseFirstRows{} comparable pair-days. Stablecoins carry \BridgeDepthThinFirstShare{} of routes in that range. Their route share reaches \BridgeDepthEqualFirstShare{} when stablecoin depth matches WETH depth and \BridgeDepthDoubleFirstShare{} when it reaches twice WETH depth. Within the same bridge event, a 1 pp increase in $Q_{et}$ is associated with \BridgeDepthDoseFirstCoef{} higher stablecoin route share.

Panel C separates support from use. Only \BridgeTimingSameDayShare{} of the \BridgeTimingEvents{} events use one of their supported stablecoins on the support date; \BridgeTimingMonthShare{} do so within 30 days and \BridgeTimingLongShare{} within 120 days. First-month use is \BridgeTimingShallowMonthShare{} when stablecoin depth is below one-tenth of WETH depth and \BridgeTimingCompetitiveMonthShare{} once it reaches that threshold. Controls for prior WETH activity, endpoint direction, route scope, the supported stablecoins, and event month leave a \BridgeTimingControlledDiff{} difference. Among pairs with two non-stablecoin endpoints, the difference is \BridgeTimingNoStableEndpointDiff{}.

Figure~\ref{fig:bridge-adoption-capital-path} follows the \BridgeAdoptionCapitalEvents{} events whose supported stablecoin is first used 7 to 119 days after persistent support. Stablecoin bottleneck capital rises \BridgeAdoptionCapitalPreCoef{} log points during the week before use and falls \BridgeAdoptionCapitalPostCoef{} over the following week. Relative to the matched WETH bridge, the corresponding changes are \BridgeAdoptionCapitalMatchedPreCoef{} and \BridgeAdoptionCapitalMatchedPostCoef{}; the pre-minus-post contrast is \BridgeAdoptionCapitalMatchedDifferenceCoef{}. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:bridge-pool-margins} locates the pre-use increase: \BridgePoolMarginContinuingCapitalShare{} of stablecoin weak-leg capital on the first-use day lies in pools active one week earlier, while \BridgePoolMarginNewCapitalShare{} lies in newly active pools. The pattern is consistent with providers deepening a bridge in anticipation of route demand, traders waiting for usable depth, or both forces operating together. Most formation occurs through deeper existing pools, with a smaller pool-activation margin. Because first use is jointly determined with capital, we treat the timing as suggestive evidence about formation and leave the two forces observationally combined.

These results connect pair formation to local liquidity. A stablecoin route often becomes technically available while its bridge remains shallow. Use rises sharply as the two-leg bridge approaches WETH depth, and capital builds before first observed use. The post-entry persistence estimates remain similar among pairs with more entry-day trades (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:entry-persistence-robustness}). At later horizons, route use and bridge depth remain positively related in both chronological and time-reversed comparisons (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:bridge-feedback}), consistent with persistent equilibrium comovement.
