\section{How vehicle use becomes established}
\label{sec:rivals}

The pair decomposition locates the aggregate rotation in the pairs that become active and attract trading. The next question is how a vehicle becomes available inside those pairs and why its use persists. Longer routes carry the same rotation. The transition from Uniswap v1 to v2 separates inherited route structure from voluntary vehicle choice. Realised vehicle use then links the aggregate result to the capital on the two legs required to complete the route.

\label{sec:rivals:complexity}

Stablecoins gain across both short and long routes. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:network-position} reports two all-route normalisations. Intermediary-position share divides a currency's uses by all intermediary positions and sums to one across currencies. Route-participation share divides routes containing the currency by all routes and can sum above one because a longer route can use several currencies. Stablecoin participation rises from \NetworkStableParticipationTwentyFour{} in 2024 to \NetworkStableParticipationTwentySix{} in 2026 H1.

The same table compares realised use with betweenness in the observed trading graph. WETH ranks first in all seven annual graphs and retains betweenness of \NetworkWethBetweennessTwentySix{} in 2026. On the monthly graph dates, however, its intermediary-position share falls from \NetworkWethPositionTwentyFour{} in 2024 to \NetworkWethPositionTwentySix{} in 2026, while USDC and USDT together rise from \NetworkStableDuoPositionTwentyFour{} to \NetworkStableDuoPositionTwentySix{}. Binary network position therefore changes much less than realised intermediary use. An edge records that at least one leg connects the two assets; local depth and execution cost determine how useful that connection is for a particular route.

\subsection{Protocol architecture and inherited vehicle use}
\label{sec:rivals:architecture}

Uniswap v1 created pools between an ERC-20 token and ETH, so token-to-token trades crossed ETH. Uniswap v2 allowed arbitrary ERC-20--ERC-20 pools and made direct token-to-token trading possible.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} Forced ETH intermediation accounts for 8.0\% of Uniswap v1 swap transactions and 8.1\% of its ETH volume over the venue's life, and about one trade in ten in 2019 and 2020. One transaction from March 2020 makes the route visible: a trader selling \VOneCaseTokenIn{} \VOneCaseTokenInSymbol{} received \VOneCaseEth{} ETH at one exchange contract and spent the identical ETH amount at a second for \VOneCaseTokenOut{} \VOneCaseTokenOutSymbol{}.\footnote{Transaction \ethtx{\VOneCaseTx}{\VOneCaseTxShort}, block \VOneCaseBlock, March 15, 2020. The transaction is the largest ETH route among the \VOneCaseExactCount{} mandate-era token-to-token transactions whose two ETH legs match exactly. The exact exchange registry identifies the endpoint tokens; public transfer records provide an independent cross-check.}
% output/exhibits/v1_forced_vehicle_report.md; output/exhibits/v1_route_case.json

Native-asset pairing remained pervasive after v2 opened direct token-to-token pools. The share of single-leg Uniswap v2 trades executed in WETH pools was 95.1\% in 2020 and 95.5\% in 2026. Among the 477,633 token combinations that ever traded on Uniswap v2, 97.1\% include WETH, and WETH's share of newly traded token combinations rose from 84.1\% in 2020 to 97.9\% in 2026. Early v2 pools inherited liquidity, users, and routing conventions from v1, which makes part of the early persistence mechanical. Deep WETH pools and later launch conventions then carried that structure forward. The pair-entry and bridge-depth estimates below examine settings in which stablecoin and WETH routes can coexist.

\subsection{Local bridge depth and route allocation}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. Here an indirect route needs two legs: one connecting the source to the vehicle and another connecting the vehicle to the destination. Capital on only one leg is insufficient. The weaker leg therefore determines the deposited-capital depth of the bridge. Deposited capital remains an equilibrium stock; a size-specific pretrade quote gives the closer measure of executable liquidity. Concentrated-liquidity providers can also change executable depth by moving capital along the price curve \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

We date the first persistent stablecoin bridge for a pair that previously routed through WETH and had no observed stablecoin route. A bridge begins when DAI, USDC, or USDT has positive prior-calendar deposited capital on both required legs for at least 24 of the next 30 days. Panel A of Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment} compares the 30 days before support with the first month and with days 30 through 119. Across \BridgeEstablishmentEvents{} events, stablecoin route share rises by \BridgeEstablishmentCountCoef{} in the first month and supported-value share by \BridgeEstablishmentValueCoef{}. The corresponding count-share increase over days 30 through 119 is 8.68 pp.
% output/exhibits/bridge_liquidity_dominance.jsonl; output/tables/bridge_establishment_regressions.tex

The depth comparison follows each bridge after support appears. Let $K_{av,t-1}$ denote prior-calendar deposited capital on the leg joining endpoint $a$ to vehicle $v$. The stablecoin set is $\mathcal S=\{\mathrm{DAI},\mathrm{USDC},\mathrm{USDT}\}$. For pair \((i,j)\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D^S_{et}=\max_{v\in\mathcal S}\min\{K_{iv,t-1},K_{vj,t-1}\},\qquad
&D^W_{et}=\min\{K_{iW,t-1},K_{Wj,t-1}\},\\
Q_{et}=\frac{D^S_{et}}{D^S_{et}+D^W_{et}},\qquad
&S^R_{et}=\beta Q_{et}+\alpha_e+\lambda_{m(t)}+\eta_{a(t)}+\varepsilon_{et}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bridge-relative-depth}
\end{equation}
Here $W$ denotes WETH, $S^R_{et}$ is stablecoin route share, $m(t)$ is calendar month, and $a(t)$ groups days since support into seven-day intervals. Event effects compare changes in relative depth within the same pair and route scope.

Panel B of Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment} reports the relation. During the first month, the best stablecoin bridge has less than one-tenth of WETH depth on \BridgeDepthThinFirstDays{} of \BridgeDepthDoseFirstRows{} comparable pair-days. Stablecoins carry \BridgeDepthThinFirstShare{} of routes in that range. Their route share reaches \BridgeDepthEqualFirstShare{} when stablecoin depth matches WETH depth and \BridgeDepthDoubleFirstShare{} when it reaches twice WETH depth. Within the same bridge event, a 1 pp increase in $Q_{et}$ is associated with \BridgeDepthDoseFirstCoef{} higher stablecoin route share.

Panel C separates support from use. Only \BridgeTimingSameDayShare{} of the \BridgeTimingEvents{} events use one of their supported stablecoins on the support date; \BridgeTimingMonthShare{} do so within 30 days and \BridgeTimingLongShare{} within 120 days. First-month use is \BridgeTimingShallowMonthShare{} when stablecoin depth is below one-tenth of WETH depth and \BridgeTimingCompetitiveMonthShare{} once it reaches that threshold. Controls for prior WETH activity, endpoint direction, route scope, the supported stablecoins, and event month leave a \BridgeTimingControlledDiff{} difference. Among pairs with two non-stablecoin endpoints, the difference is \BridgeTimingNoStableEndpointDiff{}.

Stablecoin bottleneck capital rises \BridgeAdoptionCapitalPreCoef{} log points during the week before use and falls \BridgeAdoptionCapitalPostCoef{} over the following week. Capital is already present before the first observed stablecoin route and is concentrated around its first use. The rise is consistent with traders waiting for depth and with providers anticipating later demand. The subsequent decline is consistent with temporary capital or later provider reallocation. Because first use is an endogenous event date, it can also align the sample around a local capital peak. The observed sequence does not distinguish these explanations.

These results connect pair formation to local liquidity. A stablecoin route often becomes technically available while its bridge remains shallow. Use rises sharply as the two-leg bridge approaches WETH depth, and capital builds before first observed use. Pool activation, route-capital feedback, router releases, venue pricing families, and V3/V4 provider activity offer further descriptive evidence. Appendix~\ref{app:secondary-mechanisms} reports those comparisons and keeps them separate from the paper's central decomposition and bridge-depth result.
