\section{Interpreting the rotation}
\label{sec:rivals}

Stablecoin dominance rose chiefly as routed activity moved across source--destination pairs and the incidence of intermediation changed. Repeated replacement of a native intermediary within continuing pairs contributed little. Explaining the rotation therefore requires an account of which assets were exchanged as well as which intermediary carried the route.

\subsection{Liquidity and router reach}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. On a decentralised exchange, the link operates through the pools that connect an intermediary to other assets: trading can attract provider capital, deeper pools can lower price impact, and lower price impact can draw further trading, although pool size remains an equilibrium outcome because providers balance fee income against adverse-selection risk and the cost of adjusting their positions \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. This mechanism yields a timing prediction. Capital that arrives before vehicle use is consistent with liquidity supply drawing routes toward an intermediary; capital that follows is consistent with providers responding to routed demand. The test also distinguishes deposited capital, which measures intermediation capacity, from executable liquidity at a given trade size, which concentrated-liquidity providers can change by repositioning capital within a pool while leaving the amount invested unchanged. This distinction resembles the separation of dealer capacity from liquidity supplied at a particular level of trading demand \citep{HuangRanaldoSchrimpfSomogyi2025Constrained}.

Router reach affects the same feedback because a low-cost path matters only if the trading system can use the venue that offers it. Cross-exchange trading becomes much more common during the sample. Stablecoin share also rises within single-exchange routes, so the rotation extends to both route scopes. Fragmented liquidity may still matter within each category, since routers can differ in the particular pools and paths they search \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}.

\subsection{Asset risk and software architecture}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

USDT's rise relative to USDC makes differences within the stablecoin category economically relevant. The two tokens differ in issuance, redemption access, venue coverage, and the risks borne by holders and liquidity providers. Inventory models predict that changes in those risks affect the cost of supporting an intermediary's markets \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. A native-asset drawdown exposes providers to volatile inventory; a stablecoin depeg changes the risk of an asset whose usefulness depends partly on price stability. A balance-sheet explanation therefore predicts that changes in these risks precede changes in USDT's intermediary use relative to its endpoint use.

Protocol design changes the set of feasible routes. Uniswap v1 formed pools between an ERC-20 token and ETH, so a token-to-token trade crossed ETH; Uniswap v2 enabled arbitrary ERC-20--ERC-20 pairs and thereby allowed the two tokens to trade in one pool.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} Native-asset pairing remained prevalent after this requirement disappeared. The share of single-leg Uniswap v2 trades executed in WETH pools was 95.1\% in 2020 and 95.5\% in 2026, and ranged from 93.4\% to 97.9\% in the intervening years; among the 477,633 pairs that ever traded on Uniswap v2, 97.1\% include WETH, while the WETH share of newly traded pairs rose from 84.1\% in 2020 to 97.9\% in 2026. These figures establish persistence in native-asset pair formation and pool use after the protocol mandate disappeared. Deep WETH pools, launch conventions, and inherited search paths can all produce that persistence. A fixed-opportunity comparison is needed to estimate any additional effect on route choice.

Uniswap v4 separates route choice from physical settlement even more clearly. All pools reside in one singleton, and flash accounting records balance changes internally and settles only the final net amounts at the end of a call.\footnote{See Adams et al., \href{https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper-v4.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v4 Core}}, Sections 1 and 3.} An economic route can therefore cross an intermediary pool without generating a separate external token transfer at each step. The Uniswap v4 mechanism test compares decoded pool actions while holding the ordered source--destination asset pair and feasible alternatives fixed.

Liquidity, router reach, asset risk, and software can move together for the same source and destination assets. Each can produce a USDT-centred increase across asset pairs and within both single- and cross-exchange routes. Their distinct timing predictions provide the separating evidence: capital flows, risk changes, and route costs measured for the same ordered asset pair, feasible routes, and trade size.
