\section{Mechanisms suggested by the route evidence}
\label{sec:rivals}

Stablecoin dominance rose chiefly as routed activity moved across source--destination pairs and the incidence of intermediation changed. Repeated replacement of a native intermediary within continuing pairs contributed little. Any explanation for the rotation must therefore account for the assets being exchanged as well as the intermediary carrying the route.

\subsection{Liquidity supply}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. On a decentralised exchange, the relevant markets are the pools connecting an intermediary to other assets. Trading can attract provider capital, deeper pools can lower price impact, and lower price impact can draw further trading. Pool size remains an equilibrium outcome because providers balance fee income against adverse-selection risk and the cost of adjusting their positions \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. Timing helps distinguish the two directions of this feedback. Capital arriving before vehicle use is consistent with liquidity supply drawing routes toward an intermediary; capital arriving afterwards is consistent with providers responding to routed demand. Deposited capital and executable liquidity also measure different objects. The former captures intermediation capacity, whereas concentrated-liquidity providers can change the latter at a given trade size by repositioning capital within a pool \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3}. This distinction resembles the separation of dealer capacity from liquidity supplied at a particular level of trading demand \citep{HuangRanaldoSchrimpfSomogyi2025Constrained}.

\subsection{Router reach}

A low-cost path attracts trading only when the routing system reaches the exchange that offers it. Cross-exchange trading becomes much more common during the sample, and stablecoin share rises among both single- and cross-exchange routes. Broader reach therefore contributes to the composition of observed routes without accounting for the entire stablecoin increase. Search can also differ within each group because routers inspect different pools and paths through fragmented liquidity \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. A sharper comparison holds the source and destination, trade size, and set of available routes fixed.

\subsection{Asset risk}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

USDT's rising intermediary use relative to its observed route-endpoint use makes differences within the stablecoin category economically relevant. USDT and USDC differ in issuance, redemption access, venue coverage, and the risks borne by holders and liquidity providers \citep{LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}. Inventory models predict that changes in those risks alter the cost of supporting an intermediary's markets \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. A native-asset drawdown exposes providers to volatile inventory; a stablecoin depeg changes the risk of an asset whose usefulness depends partly on price stability. Under a balance-sheet explanation, these risk changes should precede changes in USDT's intermediary use relative to its observed route-endpoint use.

\subsection{Protocol design}

Protocol design changes the set of routes that can be formed. Uniswap v1 created pools between an ERC-20 token and ETH, so token-to-token trades crossed ETH. Uniswap v2 allowed arbitrary ERC-20--ERC-20 pools and made direct token-to-token trading possible.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} Native-asset pairing remained pervasive after this mandate ended. The share of single-leg Uniswap v2 trades executed in WETH pools was 95.1\% in 2020 and 95.5\% in 2026, remaining between 93.4\% and 97.9\% in every intervening year. Among the 477,633 pairs that ever traded on Uniswap v2, 97.1\% include WETH, and WETH's share of newly traded pairs rose from 84.1\% in 2020 to 97.9\% in 2026. This persistence is consistent with deep WETH pools, launch conventions, and inherited search paths. Separating those forces from the original protocol constraint requires comparison among pairs that had the same alternatives available.

Uniswap v4 separates route choice from physical settlement more sharply. All pools reside in one singleton, and flash accounting records balance changes internally before settling the final net amounts at the end of a call.\footnote{See Adams et al., \href{https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper-v4.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v4 Core}}, Sections 1 and 3.} An economic route can therefore cross an intermediary pool without generating an external token transfer at every step. Decoded pool actions preserve the trading path despite this netting, while the architecture changes how liquidity, fees, and settlement are organised around that path.

Liquidity, router reach, asset risk, and protocol design can move together for the same source and destination assets. They nevertheless imply different sequences. A liquidity-supply explanation predicts that capital or executable depth moves before route share; a provider response predicts that capital follows routed activity. A risk explanation links changes in the cost of holding inventory to subsequent intermediary use. Router reach and protocol design work through the routes available for the same assets and trade size. These are empirical predictions, not findings established by the route decomposition; each requires its own comparison before it can explain the rotation.
