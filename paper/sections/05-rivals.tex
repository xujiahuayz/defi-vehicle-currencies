\section{Interpreting the rotation}
\label{sec:rivals}

Stablecoin dominance rose chiefly as routed activity moved across endpoint markets and the incidence of intermediation changed. Repeated replacement of a native intermediary within continuing markets contributed little. Explaining the rotation therefore requires an account of where routed trading occurred as well as which intermediary a given market used.

\subsection{Liquidity and router reach}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. On a decentralised exchange, the link operates through the pools that connect an intermediary to other assets. Trading can attract provider capital, deeper pools can lower price impact, and lower price impact can draw further trading. Pool size is an equilibrium outcome because providers balance fee income against adverse-selection risk and the cost of adjusting their positions \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. The timing of capital and route use distinguishes liquidity that draws trading from liquidity supplied in response to trading.

A liquidity-based explanation makes a timing prediction. Capital that arrives before vehicle use is consistent with liquidity supply drawing routes toward an intermediary; capital that follows is consistent with providers responding to routed demand. Deposited capital measures intermediation capacity, whereas executable liquidity measures depth at a given trade size. Concentrated-liquidity providers can change the latter by repositioning capital within a pool while leaving the amount invested unchanged. This distinction resembles the separation of dealer capacity from liquidity supplied at a particular level of trading demand \citep{HuangRanaldoSchrimpfSomogyi2025Constrained}.

Router reach affects the same feedback because a low-cost path matters only if the trading system can use the venue that offers it. Cross-exchange trading becomes much more common during the sample. Stablecoin share also rises within single-exchange routes, so the rotation extends to both route scopes. Fragmented liquidity may still matter within each category, since routers can differ in the particular pools and paths they search \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}.

\subsection{Asset risk and software architecture}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

USDT's rise relative to USDC makes differences within the stablecoin category economically relevant. The two tokens differ in issuance, redemption access, venue coverage, and the risks borne by holders and liquidity providers. Inventory models predict that changes in those risks affect the cost of supporting an intermediary's markets \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. A native-asset drawdown exposes providers to volatile inventory; a stablecoin depeg changes the risk of an asset whose usefulness depends partly on price stability. A balance-sheet explanation therefore predicts that changes in these risks precede changes in USDT's intermediary use relative to its endpoint use.

Protocol design changes the set of feasible routes. Uniswap v1 made ETH intermediation mechanical by requiring every token-to-token trade to pass through ETH. Uniswap v2 permitted arbitrary ERC20 pairs, shifting the relevant margin from a mandatory intermediary toward the markets that form and the routes chosen among them \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

Native-asset pairing remained dominant on V2 after it became optional. The share of single-leg V2 trades executed in WETH pools was 95.1\% in 2020 and 95.5\% in 2026, and ranged from 93.4\% to 97.9\% in the intervening years. Among the 477,633 pairs that ever traded on V2, 97.1\% include WETH. The WETH share of newly traded pairs rose from 84.1\% in 2020 to 97.9\% in 2026. These figures establish persistence in native-asset pair formation and pool use on V2 after the mandate was withdrawn. Market-wide route dominance is a separate object. Deep WETH pools, launch conventions, and inherited search paths can all produce this persistence. A fixed-opportunity comparison would isolate any additional effect on route choice.

Uniswap V4 separates route choice from physical settlement even more clearly. Its singleton can net token movements across calls within one transaction, so an economic route may pass through an intermediary without recording a separate transfer of that token at each step. A V4 mechanism test therefore compares decoded economic routes while holding endpoint markets and feasible alternatives fixed.

Liquidity, router reach, asset risk, and software can move together within the same endpoint market. Each can produce a USDT-centred increase across endpoint markets and within both single- and cross-exchange routes. Their distinct timing predictions provide the separating evidence: capital flows, risk changes, and route costs measured for the same endpoint market, feasible routes, and trade size.
