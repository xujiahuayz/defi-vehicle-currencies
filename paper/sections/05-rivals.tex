\section{Liquidity provision and network formation}
\label{sec:liquidity}

Liquidity providers fund the pools before routers choose a route. Every indirect exchange requires capital on both legs; providers choose which links to fund, and automated routers translate the resulting prices into route use. Liquidity supply is voluntary and responds to expected trading conditions \citep{LiWangYe2021WhoProvides}. We first study the participation and prior experience of transaction origins that add liquidity to stablecoin- and WETH-facing pools. We then date the appearance of two-leg stablecoin capital before the first observed stablecoin route.

\subsection{Stable-facing liquidity additions}
\label{sec:liquidity:providers}

Uniswap v3 records each addition to a liquidity position and the transaction origin that initiates it. We call a pool a \emph{spoke} when it pairs exactly one of WETH, DAI, USDC, or USDT with an asset outside that set; a \emph{stablecoin core} pool pairs two of DAI, USDC, and USDT. The stable-facing share of spoke-pool addition actions rises from \VThreeLPAddActionBaselineStableShare{} in 2024 H1 to \VThreeLPAddActionComparisonStableShare{} in 2026 H1. The share of vehicle-side dollar additions follows a similar rotation, from \VThreeLPUSDFlowBaselineStableShare{} to \VThreeLPUSDFlowComparisonStableShare{} (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel A). Thus the change in routed activity coincides with a large upstream shift in gross liquidity additions.
% output/exhibits/v3_lp_origin_supply_decomposition.jsonl

We decompose this change across transaction origins using the same midpoint identity as the pair decomposition. Period-specific origins contribute \VThreeLPAddActionPeriodSpecificOrigins{} of the \VThreeLPAddActionStableShareChange{} increase in the stable-facing action share, while changes within continuing origins contribute \VThreeLPAddActionWithinContinuingOrigins{}. The corresponding contributions for dollar additions are \VThreeLPUSDFlowPeriodSpecificOrigins{} and \VThreeLPUSDFlowWithinContinuingOrigins{} (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel A). A transaction origin measures participation in the liquidity addition; beneficial ownership and coordinated control remain unobserved.

Pool maturity separates this rotation from short-lived launches. Pools older than 90 days supply \VThreeLPMaturePoolIncreaseShare{} of the increase in stable-facing addition actions (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:v3-lp-launch-supply}, panel A). After excluding each pool's first seven observed days, the stable-facing action share rises from \VThreeLPExLaunchStableShareBaseline{} to \VThreeLPExLaunchStableShareComparison{} (panel B). Among origins present only in 2026 H1, one-day, one-pool participation supplies \VThreeLPOneDayOnePoolActionShare{} of stable-facing actions and \VThreeLPOneDayOnePoolFlowShare{} of vehicle-side dollar additions (panel C). Mature pools and repeated participation therefore carry most of the rotation in addition activity.

The formation of a material spoke pool also draws disproportionately on origins with experience supplying the same vehicle elsewhere. For every endpoint--vehicle pool, we date the first week in which reported total value locked reaches \VThreeLPFormationTVLThreshold{}. We then compare the vehicles supplied by each transaction origin during that week and measure its supply during the preceding \VThreeLPFormationLookback{}, excluding the focal pool and all pools attached to the focal endpoint. For formation event $e$, origin $o$, and vehicle $v$, let $Y_{eov}$ equal one when $v$ is the vehicle in the newly material pool. Let $H_{ov,e-1}$ denote the origin's prior same-vehicle supply elsewhere in the network, measured either as an indicator or as log endpoint breadth. We estimate
\begin{equation}
Y_{eov}=\beta H_{ov,e-1}+\alpha_{eo}+\lambda_{v,q(e)}+\varepsilon_{eov},
\label{eq:provider-specialization}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{eo}$ are formation-event-by-origin effects and $\lambda_{v,q(e)}$ are vehicle-by-calendar-quarter effects. Prior supply of the same vehicle predicts a \VThreeLPSameVehicleSupplyEffect{} higher probability that the origin supplies that vehicle in the new pool (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel B, column 1). A one-unit increase in log prior endpoint breadth predicts \VThreeLPSameVehicleBreadthEffect{} higher probability (column 2). Both estimates remain statistically significant after Holm adjustment across the four reported models. This specialisation is consistent with existing vehicle networks supplying new spokes. Common market makers can similarly link liquidity conditions across securities \citep{CoughenourSaad2004CommonMarketMakers}.

Within stablecoins, prior supply to a same-token core pool has a \VThreeLPSameStablecoinCoreSupplyEffect{} coefficient (Table~\ref{tab:v3-provider-formation}, panel B, column 3), and prior same-token core-pool breadth has a \VThreeLPSameStablecoinCoreBreadthEffect{} coefficient (column 4). The first estimate is imprecise after multiplicity adjustment, while the breadth estimate is significant at the 10\% level. We treat cross-vehicle specialization as the primary result and stablecoin-core experience as suggestive evidence on how core dollar-token liquidity can extend into spokes. We observe the pools that receive liquidity; a provider choice model would additionally require each participant's full opportunity set.

\subsection{Full-range constant-product capital before route use}
\label{sec:liquidity:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. Here an indirect route needs two legs: one connecting the source to the vehicle and another connecting the vehicle to the destination. We measure deposited capital as validated full-range reserve value in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools. The weaker leg determines this two-leg capital measure. It is an equilibrium stock on those constant-product venues; size-specific pretrade quotes provide the closer measure of executable liquidity across the broader venue set. Concentrated-liquidity providers can also change executable depth by moving capital along the price curve \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

We date bridge formation using only information available before route choice. The event is the first date on which DAI, USDC, or USDT has at least \BridgeExanteThreshold{} of prior-calendar full-range capital in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools on each of the two legs required by the pair. Every pair routed through WETH earlier, and the event predates any observed stablecoin route in the pair. This definition yields \BridgeExanteEvents{} bridge events. Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment}, panel A, shows that \BridgeExanteAdoptionThirty{} carry a supported stablecoin route within 30 days and \BridgeExanteAdoptionOneTwenty{} do so within 120 days. Among the first-month adopters, \BridgeExanteLaterUse{} use a supported stablecoin again during days 30--119, although stablecoins account for only \BridgeExanteLaterShare{} of their route activity in that later window. The two-leg capital threshold therefore precedes adoption, while initial use often leaves WETH as the principal vehicle.
% output/exhibits/bridge_exante.jsonl; output/tables/bridge_exante.tex

The capital comparison follows each bridge after support appears. Let $K_{av,t-1}=K_{va,t-1}$ denote prior-calendar full-range capital in Uniswap v2 and SushiSwap v2 pools on the leg joining endpoint $a$ to vehicle $v$. The stablecoin set is $\mathcal S=\{\mathrm{DAI},\mathrm{USDC},\mathrm{USDT}\}$. For bridge event $e$ associated with pair \((i_e,j_e)\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D^S_{et}=\max_{v\in\mathcal S}\min\{K_{i_ev,t-1},K_{j_ev,t-1}\},\qquad
&D^W_{et}=\min\{K_{i_eW,t-1},K_{j_eW,t-1}\},\\
Q_{et}=\frac{D^S_{et}}{D^S_{et}+D^W_{et}},\qquad
&S^R_{et}=\beta Q_{et}+\alpha_e+\lambda_{m(t)}+\eta_{a_e(t)}+\varepsilon_{et}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bridge-relative-depth}
\end{equation}
Here $W$ denotes WETH, $S^R_{et}$ is stablecoin route share, $m(t)$ is calendar month, and $a_e(t)$ groups elapsed days since event $e$ into seven-day intervals. Event effects compare changes in relative full-range constant-product capital within the same pair, separately for realised single- and cross-venue route classes.

Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment}, panel B, compares route allocation after that prior-information event with the preceding 30 days. Stablecoin route share rises by \BridgeExanteChangeThirty{} during days 0--29 and by \BridgeExanteChangeOneTwenty{} during days 30--119. Panel C estimates Equation~\eqref{eq:bridge-relative-depth} using the continuous relative-capital share within the same event. A 10 pp increase in the stablecoin share of prior-day weak-leg full-range capital is associated with \BridgeExanteDepthThirty{} more stablecoin route activity during days 0--29 and \BridgeExanteDepthOneTwenty{} during days 30--119. The later coefficient is larger even though adoption remains incomplete. The threshold marks full-range support on both legs; relative capital remains associated with route shares after bridge support appears.

Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:bridge-persistent-support} and Figure~\ref{fig:app:bridge-adoption-capital-path} use persistent-support and first-use timing definitions. Most pre-use capital growth occurs in pools already active one week earlier (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:bridge-pool-margins}). This timing is consistent with providers adding capital in anticipation of route demand, traders waiting for usable depth, reserve revaluation, or these forces operating together. Liquidity supply is voluntary and can respond to expected trading conditions \citep{LiWangYe2021WhoProvides}.
