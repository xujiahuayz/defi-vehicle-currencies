\section{Mechanisms behind incumbency}
\label{sec:rivals}

A vehicle that stops being the cheapest route keeps the largest share of routing on its pair for some number of days, and the hazard ratio $\theta$ of \eqref{eq:hazard} measures how weakly the incumbent's cost position governs that. Four accounts each generate a $\theta$ near one and each names a different state variable as the thing moving too slowly for cost to bind. They are \emph{slow capital}, \emph{integration scope}, \emph{holding cost} and \emph{defaults}. Table~\ref{tab:rivals} states the fact that separates each from the other three and the evidence standing behind it today.

The classical volume-cost feedback is not excluded by the asset taxonomy. Native status is a platform identity, while pairing degree, transaction volume, depth and route cost are measured properties. \citet{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}'s primitive is current transaction volume lowering proportional brokerage cost; his model contains neither provider capital nor liquidity reallocation. The \emph{slow capital} account is this paper's extension: past vehicle use may concentrate liquidity in the two markets through the vehicle and lower their current costs. What the four accounts have to explain is why routing does not leave at the moment that cost advantage does.

The determinism of the counterfactual route search narrows the field in a way the currency literature cannot. It is recomputed at every quote and contains no lagged-choice state, which removes habit from the benchmark and leaves none of the four accounts resting on it. Each instead names a state variable that updates slowly, and $\theta$ near one is a measurement of how fast that variable moves. The race is therefore about a speed. A level fact about the incumbent pairing network says nothing about the rate at which that network can be rebuilt around a different asset.

\subsection{Slow capital}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

Routing follows depth within a block, and depth follows the capital that liquidity providers have committed. The \emph{slow capital} account hypothesises that reallocating this capital across routes is infrequent or costly; neither the cadence nor its cause is supplied by the finite-risk-bearing model of \citet{GrossmanMiller1988Liquidity}. Under this account the role outlasts dominance for exactly as long as reallocation takes, and $\theta$ near one is a statement about provider stickiness carrying no behavioural content on the trading side.

The prediction that separates \emph{slow capital} from the other three is a lead-lag between depth and routing. Write $C_{abt}$ for the concentration of provider capital in the pools linking the incumbent to the pair, and extend \eqref{eq:cloglog} with it:
\begin{equation}
\Pr\bigl(y_{ab,\,t+1}=1 \mid \cdot\bigr) \;=\; 1 - \exp\Bigl(-\exp\bigl(\alpha_{ab} + \gamma\, d_{ab\ell t} + \beta\, C_{abt} + \zeta\, d_{ab\ell t} C_{abt} + \eta\, g_{abt}\bigr)\Bigr),
\label{eq:capital}
\end{equation}
with $g_{abt}$ the contemporaneous cost gap in basis points and the account requiring $\zeta < 0$, meaning a dominated incumbent backed by more concentrated capital loses the role more slowly. The sharp half is the timing, estimated as a local projection of the incumbent's share on capital at leads and lags:
\begin{equation}
\Delta s_{ab\,\ell,\,t+h} \;=\; a_h \;+\; b_h\, \Delta C_{abt} \;+\; \varphi_h' x_{abt} \;+\; \epsilon_{ab,t+h}, \qquad h = -H,\dots,H .
\label{eq:leadlag}
\end{equation}
The mechanism has two ordered margins: deposited capital $C$ changes, protocol-state capital efficiency maps it into executable depth $D$, and routing responds to $D$. The design must therefore estimate $C\rightarrow D$ and $D\rightarrow s$ separately. Capital leading depth and routing supports the slow-capital account; routing leading capital refutes it. A change in depth at fixed capital is an architecture or range-placement channel, not capital reallocation.

A lagged dependent variable with observed-fundamentals controls is retained as diagnostic, not discriminating evidence. A serially persistent unobserved fundamental can generate the same pattern as a switching cost, so that regression cannot identify the slow-capital account even if its lag is large.

The premise is currently untested. The prior rent panel is withdrawn while V2 is regenerated with contemporaneous pool value as the LVR scale and exact prior-calendar deposited capital as the return denominator. Uniswap V3 provider TVL failed the historical-balance audit, and local virtual depth is neither capital nor an LVR scale. No earlier V2 magnitude or V3 sign is admitted here. Token-denominated subsidies also sit outside the accounting, so even a validated trading-revenue loss would not by itself distinguish subsidy-supported participation from provider stickiness.
% docs/findings-freeze.md; docs/finding-rent-incidence.md

The duration test itself is blocked by a measure. Candidate-linked liquidity and provider concentration are currently reconstructed from one venue's data alone, while routers split a single trade across venues. \citet{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation} establish the narrower point that venue-level depth and aggregate allocation can diverge when orders in one asset split across exchanges. The cross-pool liquidity aggregate to which an on-chain router responds is this paper's measurement object. Rebuilding $C_{abt}$ on that layer is what puts \emph{slow capital} into the race, and it enters with \eqref{eq:capital} and \eqref{eq:leadlag} as stated.
% docs/retired-single-venue-round.md

\subsection{Integration scope}
\label{sec:rivals:integration}

A cheaper route no aggregator has integrated is a route no trader can take. Under \emph{integration scope} the role outlasts dominance for as long as the routing layer takes to see the alternative, and $\theta$ near one measures the cadence of a business decision taken at the aggregators. This is a supply-side friction on the routing layer, and it is distinct from \emph{slow capital} because no capital has to move for the role to end.

The prediction that separates it is a step at integration dates crossed with a cross-section in breadth. Write $t^{*}_{ab}$ for the date the venue carrying the pair's cheaper alternative became reachable through the aggregators serving it, and $R_{abt}$ for the share of routing volume whose aggregator had integrated that venue by $t$:
\begin{equation}
\log \lambda_{abt} \;=\; \alpha_{ab} + \gamma\, d_{ab\ell t} + \kappa\, R_{abt} + \sum_{h=-H}^{H} \pi_h \, \mathbf{1}\{t - t^{*}_{ab} = h\} + \epsilon_{abt},
\label{eq:integration}
\end{equation}
with the account requiring $\kappa > 0$ and a step in $\pi_h$ at $h = 0$ against a flat profile before it. Both halves need the same input.

The obstacle is quote authorship and it has been measured. The calling contract identifies the executor of a route but does not necessarily identify the system that selected its path. Authorship is classified only where versioned protocol documentation or implementation evidence establishes that an off-chain service or solver produced the route and any split proportions. An executor's share of swaps is therefore not the routing system's share of decisions, and the verified systems are not interchangeable: two run off-chain routing services that emit executable calldata, while a third is a batch auction in which competing solvers submit solutions. The executor population also fragments across the sample, from 171 distinct senders on 62,657 swaps in mid-2022 to 241 on 74,323 in early 2024 and 397 on 130,282 in late 2025, while the share captured by the ten largest labelled executors falls from 67.7\% to 11.8\% between the last two of those snapshots.
% docs/router-identification-feasibility.md

Parts of an aggregator's opportunity set are private and stay private. Losing request-for-quote responses, market-maker inventory, the full set of solutions submitted to a batch auction, and the ex ante value of private transaction inclusion are all absent from the public record, and a route declined for any of them is indistinguishable here from a route the routing layer could not see. That is a ceiling on how sharply this account can ever be tested, and it is a property of the data.
% docs/router-identification-feasibility.md

Two inputs make \eqref{eq:integration} admissible. The first is the date each venue became reachable through each major aggregator, constructed from contract-creation traces and function selectors instead of hand curation, since hand curation is what fell from 67.7\% coverage to 11.8\% as the executor population grew. The second is the share of routing volume the labelled executors carry, which has to be reported before any design is built on those labels, because a design resting on 11.8\% of contracts and most of the volume is viable and one resting on 11.8\% of both is not.
% docs/router-identification-feasibility.md

\subsection{Holding cost}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

Risk-bearing dealer theory and inventory evidence motivate, but do not identify, a holding-cost rival in this setting \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. If supplying pools that carry an intermediate asset ties up more risk-bearing capacity when its price is volatile, a volatile incumbent should be abandoned faster under its own stress than a stable one is, and $\theta$ should move with what the role costs to occupy.

A second signature of this account is rejected on the record. If warehousing the intermediary costs something that scales with notional, the native asset's cost advantage weakens as trade size rises, and a profile read off three separate notional subsamples looks exactly like that. Estimated as an interaction on the same screened panel,
\begin{equation}
g_{rc} \;=\; \alpha_c \;+\; \beta_1 \, \mathrm{NATIVE}_r \;+\; \beta_2 \, \mathrm{NATIVE}_r \log V_r \;+\; \epsilon_{rc},
\label{eq:size}
\end{equation}
with $g_{rc}$ the route's cost gap in basis points, $V_r$ its notional and $\alpha_c$ a fixed effect for pair by time window by trade size, the estimate is $\hat{\beta}_2 = +0.0023$ with a standard error of $0.0209$ (0.914), on 732 routes in 274 cells. The apparent gradient is three draws from a flat line. No comparison anywhere in this paper describes the native cost advantage as weakening with size, and the fixed-cost reading it invited is withdrawn.
% output/exhibits/dominance_specification_curve.jsonl

One prediction stands on the record and it is the ordering of the two crossover dates. Value-weighted, stablecoins first exceed the native asset as intermediaries in 2022-Q1 and lead from 2022-Q4 onward. Count-weighted, the crossover appears only in 2026-H1, at the very end of the sample and with no four-quarter run available to call it sustained. Large trades moved to fiat-pegged assets roughly four years before small trades did, which is the ordering a cost scaling in notional implies and an ordering the mechanism predicted before it was observed.
% docs/finding-intermediation-transition.md

One competing explanation of that ordering is rejected here. Venues running a stableswap invariant make stable-to-stable legs nearly free and exist for no other asset class, and their rise coincides in time with stablecoin intermediation, which would make the whole transition an automated market maker technology fact wearing a monetary label. Recomputing intermediation shares on the constant-product subset alone, keeping a route only when every one of its legs settles on that subset so that a single stableswap leg cannot readmit the explanation, the stable share of intermediation moves by $+49.5$ percentage points across the sample while the native share moves by $-68.0$. Stable intermediation reaches 59.5\% on constant-product venues alone in 2026 against 51.1\% across all venues on the same days. The transition is larger where the technology does not exist. Those figures come from a fifteen-day sampled panel whose endpoints are one day in 2020 and two in 2026, and the comparison between the two subsets is the informative part where the endpoint levels are not the full-sample series of Section~\ref{sec:dominance:transition}, which puts the stable share at 50.1\% over 2,240 days.
% output/exhibits/venue_technology_rival.jsonl

What remains for \emph{holding cost} is a stress test on the hazard, and its specification is fixed here. Write $z_{kt}$ for candidate $k$'s own drawdown over the preceding week and interact it with the dominance indicator in \eqref{eq:cloglog}:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{kt} \;=\; \exp\bigl(\gamma + \xi\, z_{kt}\bigr), \qquad \xi > 0 \;\; \text{for the native asset}, \qquad \xi < 0 \;\; \text{for a stable candidate}.
\label{eq:stress}
\end{equation}
A stable candidate's own downward depeg reverses the sign a second time, and a placebo assigning the shock after the outcome window closes has to return $\xi$ indistinguishable from zero. March 2023 is the candidate-specific episode in this sample: exposure of USDC reserves to Silicon Valley Bank produced a sharp depeg and reversed the contemporaneous direction of stablecoin circulation flows \citep{AnaduEtAl2023StablecoinRuns}. We use that episode to test whether deterioration in one candidate's safety predicts lower subsequent vehicle use; the comparison with the native asset is this project's design, not a result supplied by the source. The May 2022 UST collapse is a separate algorithmic failure in which reflexive conversion into LUNA propagated a run across on-chain and off-chain venues \citep{LiuMakarovSchoar2023Terra}. One prediction on the record is a thinner position than this account began with, and \eqref{eq:stress} is its remaining chance.

\subsection{Defaults}
\label{sec:rivals:defaults}

Routing software and pool-creation templates may point at the incumbent. Under \emph{defaults} $\theta$ near one is the release cadence of that software and carries no allocative content, which makes it the least interesting of the four accounts and the one the evidence currently favours. This is a project hypothesis pending protocol-code and release-record evidence, not an implication of the institutional DeFi literature.

Four features separate it. A default change is a step at a release date while an allocative reallocation is a drift. A default inside one venue's software cannot move routing on venues whose software did not change, which makes cross-venue scope the discriminating design. Withdrawing an architectural mandate to route through the incumbent lets pairing retreat if optimisation had been propping it up. And turnover should cluster by the software serving a pair while differing across software populations.

The mandate withdrawal has already happened, and it favours this account. The first version of the venue studied here gave every token exactly one exchange contract paired against the native asset, and a token-to-token trade had no direct pool anywhere, which routed it through the native asset. The vehicle was mandated and traders never chose it. The second version, live 2020-05-05, allowed arbitrary token pairs and withdrew the mandate. Pairing did not retreat. Of 477,633 pairs that ever traded on the second version, 463,548, or 97.1\%, include the wrapped native asset, and the share of newly created pairs including it rose from 84.1\% in 2020 to 99.0\% in 2023 and stands at 97.9\% in 2026.
% docs/finding-v1-forced-vehicle.md

The token-level version of that event reaches a bounded null. Exit speed is regressed on forced-routing intensity across the first-version exchanges,
\begin{equation}
\log \bigl(\text{lifetime}_j\bigr) \;=\; a \;+\; b\, F_j \;+\; x_j' c \;+\; u_j,
\label{eq:exit}
\end{equation}
where $F_j$ is the share of token $j$'s early volume that the mandate forced through the native asset. On the 247 exchanges clearing the identifying filters $\hat{b} = +0.276$ log-months with a heteroskedasticity-robust standard error of $0.307$, and randomisation inference over 5,000 reshuffles of $F_j$ across exchanges returns (0.355). The mandate hypothesis requires a negative coefficient. The grouped-time proportional hazard of the same event,
% output/exhibits/v1_token_level_ols.jsonl, v1_token_level_permutation.jsonl; 247 first-version exchanges clearing the identifying filters
\begin{equation}
\Pr\bigl(\text{exit}_{jm} = 1 \mid \text{alive at } m\bigr) \;=\; 1 - \exp\bigl(-\exp(\phi_m + \beta\, F_j)\bigr),
\label{eq:v1hazard}
\end{equation}
on 2,457 exchange-months in 247 clusters with 216 failures, puts $\hat{\beta} = +0.026$ with a cluster-robust standard error of $0.431$, a hazard ratio of 1.004 for a one standard deviation move in intensity.
% docs/finding-v1-forced-vehicle.md; output/exhibits/v1_token_level_ols.jsonl, v1_token_level_hazard.jsonl, v1_token_level_permutation.jsonl

That null is bounded, and the bound was fixed before it was measured. Refitting \eqref{eq:exit} on outcomes rebuilt with a known coefficient, the design recovers a halving of exchange lifetime between the 5th and 95th percentiles of intensity in 98.4\% of samples, against a pre-stated threshold of 80\%. An effect of the size the mandate hypothesis needs would have been visible and it was not detected. The edge of the bound is reported alongside it: power against a 25\% shortening is 42.2\%, and effects in that range are out of reach here and nothing is claimed against them.
% output/exhibits/v1_token_level_power.jsonl

One placebo on that design failed its own pre-stated rule and the failure is reported as a failure. Shifting the event six months earlier and truncating follow-up so the placebo window closes before the real date, the placebo coefficient is $+0.0333$ against a real six-month estimate of $+0.0311$ on the same horizon, and the rule required the placebo to be insignificant and smaller in absolute value. It is insignificant and it is larger, by 0.0022 log-months. A rule comparing magnitudes cannot discriminate when both magnitudes sit within a quarter of a standard error of zero, and the earlier window carries a seventh of the exits, which leaves the failure bounding nothing and reading as evidence neither way.
% docs/finding-v1-forced-vehicle.md

The cross-venue scope test has been run and it yields one usable reading. The outcome is measured only on venues whose architecture did not change, and the break at the concentrated-liquidity launch of 2021-05-05 is estimated as
\begin{equation}
X_{vt} \;=\; \alpha_v \;+\; \pi\, \mathbf{1}\{t \geq t^{*}\} \;+\; \varrho\, t \;+\; \varepsilon_{vt}, \qquad v \notin \mathcal{V}^{\text{treated}},
\label{eq:break}
\end{equation}
with $X_{vt}$ the native-minus-stable share of topological betweenness and inference by randomisation over event dates. The estimate is $\hat{\pi} = +0.020$, with randomisation inference at (0.489) against a minimum detectable effect of 0.106 and a pre-event base of 0.689 \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3}. An architecture change on one venue shifted the structural indispensability of the native asset on venues that did not adopt it by under 11 percentage points, and locality of that kind is what \emph{defaults} implies and what an allocative account does not.
% docs/finding-cross-venue-spillover.md; output/exhibits/cross_venue_spillover_estimates.jsonl

That reading is a bound, and the reason is in the same exhibit. Fitting \eqref{eq:break} at the midpoint of the pre-event window using pre-event data alone returns a pseudo-break of $+0.043$ (0.000) in the same series, roughly twice the estimated jump. The series was already producing steps of that size at dates where nothing happened. What stands is the ceiling on the effect.
% output/exhibits/cross_venue_spillover_estimates.jsonl

The same design does not deliver a second reading, and the reason is a design lesson. At the 2025-01-31 launch the untreated-venue move in native-minus-stable route share is $-0.293$ (0.000) while the move across all venues including the treated one is $-0.346$ (0.000), and restricting to untreated venues recovers 85\% of a market-wide move and differences out almost nothing. Untreated venues are most of this market, and a market-wide compositional rotation passes straight through them. Early 2025 contains such a rotation, and no contrast across venues can separate it from an architecture effect.
% docs/finding-cross-venue-spillover.md; output/exhibits/cross_venue_spillover_selection.jsonl

Betweenness is the outcome carrying the verdict because it is the only outcome family whose placebo passes cleanly. A date on which block time and gas dynamics changed while no market maker architecture did moves betweenness by $+0.006$ (0.787) on one untreated set and $+0.007$ (0.783) on the other. The same date moves the new-pair composition of venues that did not change by $+0.055$ (0.000) and $+0.062$ (0.000), and the new-pair family cannot support a spillover reading at any event date.
% output/exhibits/cross_venue_spillover_estimates.jsonl

Two of the four separating features are still owed and both wait on the hazard itself. Step timing asks whether $\theta$ falls discontinuously at routing-software release dates and drifts everywhere else, and heterogeneity asks whether turnover clusters by the software serving a pair. There is also a limit on how far the pairing evidence carries this account on its own, and it cuts against reading the mandate null too hard: a token launching against the native asset because the native asset is the default in every launch template is a thick-market externality operating through a state variable, and pairing counts cannot separate that from a template.
% docs/finding-v1-forced-vehicle.md

\subsection{What the race has settled}
\label{sec:rivals:verdict}

The race has produced one rejection and one leader, and the two remaining accounts carry specified tests that are not yet admissible. The rejection and the leader are both reported against this project's own interest, which is the order in which they are taken here.

The rejection removes the more interesting half of \emph{holding cost}. A cost of warehousing the intermediary that scales with notional leaves a size gradient in the native cost advantage, that gradient was the signature joining the paper's two halves, and $\hat{\beta}_2 = +0.0023$ (0.914) in \eqref{eq:size} removes it. \emph{Holding cost} stays in the race on the ordering of the crossover dates alone, and that ordering does stand against its sharpest competing explanation, since the migration toward stablecoins is larger on venues where stableswap technology never existed.
% output/exhibits/dominance_specification_curve.jsonl; output/exhibits/venue_technology_rival.jsonl

\emph{Defaults} leads on the evidence available. It is the only account with a completed test that came out its way, and the test is a bounded null: withdrawing an architectural mandate to route through the native asset left pairing where it was, at a resolution that would have caught a halving of exchange lifetime in 98.4\% of samples. The cross-venue bound points the same way, capping the reach of an architecture change onto venues that did not adopt it at under 11 percentage points of betweenness share. An account under which $\theta$ near one is a software release cadence with no allocative content is the dullest of the four, and it is reported as leading anyway.
% output/exhibits/v1_token_level_power.jsonl; output/exhibits/cross_venue_spillover_estimates.jsonl

\emph{Slow capital} has neither its premise nor its discriminating sequence established. The replacement panel separates deposited capital, protocol-specific executable depth and routing, while the current V3 flow panel remains deliberately unnormalized. The account re-enters only after validated capital stocks and direction-specific depth allow the $C\rightarrow D\rightarrow s$ sequence to be estimated on the cross-venue opportunity set routers search over.
% docs/finding-rent-incidence.md

\emph{Integration scope} is the newest account in the race and has no completed test at all. What stands between it and one is quote authorship: the calling contract names the executor while the routing decision is authored off-chain, and the labelled share of that population fell to 11.8\% as it fragmented. The account is retained because the friction it names is the one friction consistent with a role outlasting its cost advantage on a pair where nothing about liquidity, volatility or software changed.
% docs/router-identification-feasibility.md

The verdict on $\theta$, when it can be given across the six-venue panel, is given on a curated specification curve with a joint inference test across the whole curve. Four choices are mandatory to vary: the support-screen threshold, the fee wedge $\delta$ of \eqref{eq:margin}, the notional grid point at which routes are priced, and the treatment of outliers. Discretion over ten routine choices of that kind is enough to report over 70\% of randomly generated variables as significant, so the curve and its joint test are part of the result.
% docs/paper-spine.md section 5.5, specification-curve requirement

Two outcomes of this race are findings and both will be reported as such. A tie in which two accounts stand with the same discriminating sign is a statement about which state variables move slowly and which do not. And a hazard ratio far above one, where the role tracks cost from one day to the next, is a rejection of hysteresis that belongs in the abstract. The block-level window of Section~\ref{sec:survival} puts $\theta$ at 1.17 on sixty days of one venue family, which is the first reading the four accounts are ranked against, and the ranking here moves when the six-venue panel returns its own.
% output/exhibits/survival_at_block.jsonl, arm turnover_hazard_ratio, 60 days at a 30 bps wedge
