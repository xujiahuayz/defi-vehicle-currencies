\section{How vehicle use becomes established}
\label{sec:rivals}

The pair decomposition locates the aggregate rotation in the pairs that become active and attract trading. The next question is how a vehicle becomes available inside those pairs and why its use persists. Longer routes carry the same rotation. The transition from Uniswap v1 to v2 separates inherited route structure from voluntary vehicle choice. Realised vehicle use then links the aggregate result to the capital on the two legs required to complete the route.

Figure~\ref{fig:vehicle-composition} includes every complete route, with one observation for each intermediary position. Stablecoins gain across both short and long routes and within single- and cross-exchange routes. Their all-route participation rises from \NetworkStableParticipationTwentyFour{} in 2024 to \NetworkStableParticipationTwentySix{} in 2026 H1, while WETH remains the highest-betweenness node in every annual trading graph (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:network-position}). Connectivity therefore moves less than realised intermediary use; local depth and execution cost determine how useful a connection is for a particular route.

Protocol history gives a benchmark for inherited vehicle use. Uniswap v1 required each token-to-token trade to cross ETH; v2 allowed any two ERC-20 tokens to form a pool.\footnote{See Adams, Zinsmeister, and Robinson, \href{https://docs.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf}{\emph{Uniswap v2 Core}}, pp.~1--2.} We reconstruct \VOneForcedRoutes{} connected v1 token-to-token routes. In 2026, after six years of open pair formation, \VTwoWethTradeShare{} of single-leg v2 trades still execute in WETH pools and \VTwoWethNewPairShare{} of newly traded token combinations include WETH (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:v1-architecture}, panels A and B). The chronology is descriptive because venue migration changed the available token set. The bridge and price estimates below compare pairs in which stablecoin and WETH routes coexist.

\subsection{Local bridge depth and route allocation}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction costs \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. Here an indirect route needs two legs: one connecting the source to the vehicle and another connecting the vehicle to the destination. Capital on only one leg is insufficient. The weaker leg therefore determines the deposited-capital depth of the bridge. Deposited capital remains an equilibrium stock; a size-specific pretrade quote gives the closer measure of executable liquidity. Concentrated-liquidity providers can also change executable depth by moving capital along the price curve \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3,LeharParlour2024Uniswap}.

We date bridge formation using only information available before route choice. The event is the first date on which DAI, USDC, or USDT has at least \$10,000 of prior-calendar deposited capital on each of the two legs required by the pair. Every pair routed through WETH earlier and has no earlier observed stablecoin route. This definition yields \BridgeExanteEvents{} bridge events. Table~\ref{tab:bridge-establishment}, panel A, shows that \BridgeExanteAdoptionThirty{} carry a supported stablecoin route within 30 days and \BridgeExanteAdoptionOneTwenty{} do so within 120 days. Among the first-month adopters, \BridgeExanteLaterUse{} use a supported stablecoin again during days 30--119, although stablecoins account for only \BridgeExanteLaterShare{} of their route activity in that later window. Capital sufficient to open both legs therefore precedes adoption, while initial use often leaves WETH as the principal vehicle.
% output/exhibits/bridge_exante.jsonl; output/tables/bridge_exante.tex

The depth comparison follows each bridge after support appears. Let $K_{av,t-1}$ denote prior-calendar deposited capital on the leg joining endpoint $a$ to vehicle $v$. The stablecoin set is $\mathcal S=\{\mathrm{DAI},\mathrm{USDC},\mathrm{USDT}\}$. For pair \((i,j)\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D^S_{et}=\max_{v\in\mathcal S}\min\{K_{iv,t-1},K_{vj,t-1}\},\qquad
&D^W_{et}=\min\{K_{iW,t-1},K_{Wj,t-1}\},\\
Q_{et}=\frac{D^S_{et}}{D^S_{et}+D^W_{et}},\qquad
&S^R_{et}=\beta Q_{et}+\alpha_e+\lambda_{m(t)}+\eta_{a(t)}+\varepsilon_{et}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bridge-relative-depth}
\end{equation}
Here $W$ denotes WETH, $S^R_{et}$ is stablecoin route share, $m(t)$ is calendar month, and $a(t)$ groups days since support into seven-day intervals. Event effects compare changes in relative depth within the same pair and route scope.

Panel B compares route allocation after that prior-information event with the preceding 30 days. Stablecoin route share rises by \BridgeExanteChangeThirty{} during days 0--29 and by \BridgeExanteChangeOneTwenty{} during days 30--119. Panel C uses continuous relative depth within the same event. A 10 pp increase in the stablecoin share of prior-day weak-leg depth is associated with \BridgeExanteDepthThirty{} more stablecoin route activity during days 0--29 and \BridgeExanteDepthOneTwenty{} during days 30--119. The later coefficient is larger even though adoption remains incomplete. Two-sided capital opens the contest; relative depth continues to divide trading after the bridge appears.

The threshold event and subsequent relative-depth estimates connect pair formation to local liquidity without using future support to select the event date. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:bridge-persistent-support} and Figure~\ref{fig:app:bridge-adoption-capital-path} follow the alternative persistent-support and first-use timing definitions. They show that most pre-use capital growth occurs in pools already active one week earlier (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:bridge-pool-margins}). This pattern is suggestive of providers deepening a bridge in anticipation of route demand, traders waiting for usable depth, or both forces operating together. The post-entry persistence estimates remain similar among pairs with more entry-day trades (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app:entry-persistence-robustness}).
