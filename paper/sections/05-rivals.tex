\section{Interpreting the rotation}
\label{sec:rivals}

The endpoint-pair decomposition points to a reallocation across markets; repeated stable-for-native substitution within a typical market contributes little. Several economic forces could produce that pattern. Liquidity may move slowly toward the pools used by an incumbent; routers may differ in the exchanges and paths they can reach; changes in backing or redemption risk may alter the appeal of an intermediary; and software or protocol architecture may direct trading toward particular routes. These forces can reinforce one another, so their implications must be read against the variation observed in the route data.

\subsection{Liquidity and router reach}
\label{sec:rivals:capital}

The classical vehicle-currency mechanism links trading volume to lower transaction cost \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. In a decentralised exchange, that feedback operates through the pools joining an intermediary to other assets. Pool size is endogenous to the fees and adverse-selection risk borne by liquidity providers \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. Trading through those pools can attract provider capital; deeper pools can then make the same intermediary cheaper to use. If providers move capital slowly, an established vehicle may retain deep routes after another intermediary becomes competitive.

The direction of timing is central to this explanation. Capital moving before vehicle use would support liquidity supply as a source of the change, whereas capital following route use would suggest that trading draws liquidity to the pools it already uses. Deposited capital and executable liquidity also differ. Deposited capital is the amount invested in the relevant pools; executable liquidity is the amount those pools can trade at a given order size and price impact. The analogous distinction in dealer markets is between intermediation capacity and the quantity of liquidity supplied at a given trading demand \citep{HuangRanaldoSchrimpfSomogyi2025Constrained}. Concentrated-liquidity providers can change executable liquidity by moving positions within a pool without changing total invested capital.

We estimate both directions with exact calendar leads and lags. For V2 pools, deposited capital is measured from reconciled reserves. For V3, mint and burn events measure provider flows separately from the stock of capital. The outcome is vehicle dominance for the same set of currencies used in the route results. A lead from capital to vehicle use would support liquidity supply as a predictor; a lead in the other direction would show that providers follow routed activity. Bidirectional predictability would still be compatible with common demand, volatility, or routing shocks; causal feedback requires variation that separates those shocks from capital and vehicle use.

Router reach supplies a related explanation because a cheaper path affects route choice only when the trading system can use the exchange that offers it. Cross-exchange routing becomes much more common during the sample, while stablecoin use rises among both single-exchange and cross-exchange routes. The rotation therefore occurs within each broad route type as well as through changes in their relative importance. Because exchange span is chosen jointly with the route, estimating the effect of broader access requires comparisons within the same market and feasible route set. The present result establishes that the stablecoin gain extends beyond the cross-exchange portion of the sample \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}.

\subsection{Asset risk and software architecture}
\label{sec:rivals:holding}

USDT's rise relative to USDC makes differences within the stablecoin category economically relevant. The two assets differ in issuance, redemption access, the distribution of liquidity across exchanges, and the risks borne by holders and liquidity providers. Inventory models imply that a deterioration in an intermediary can raise the cost of supporting routes through it \citep{HoStoll1981OptimalDealer,ComertonFordeEtAl2010Inventories}. A native-asset drawdown exposes providers to volatile inventory, while a stablecoin depeg changes the perceived safety of an asset valued for stability. The current route shares identify which stablecoin gains the vehicle role, but they do not isolate which of these attributes is responsible.

Software can sustain use of an incumbent intermediary after mandatory routing through it disappears. Routing systems search only the pools and exchanges they integrate, and pool-creation templates can make one intermediary the default path for new markets. Uniswap v1 required token-to-token trades to pass through ETH, whereas v2 allowed arbitrary ERC20 pairs \citep{LeharParlour2024Uniswap}. Continued native-asset use after that change is compatible with both inherited software paths and the thick-market feedback described above. The v1 evidence comes from a separate institutional comparison rather than the eight-venue route panel. Protocol launches also coincide with changes in users, liquidity, and available pairs, so their dates describe the market's chronology but do not identify an architecture effect.

Uniswap V4 can change the settlement of a route while preserving its economic sequence. Its singleton permits token movements to be netted across the calls made within one transaction. The empirical comparison therefore separates two outcomes: whether the decoded route passes through an intermediary, and whether the receipt records a physical transfer of that token. Routes are compared within the same week, ordered endpoint pair, intermediary, direction, and trade-size group. This comparison identifies physical-settlement differences conditional on a similar economic route. Effects on vehicle choice require a separate comparison of the feasible route set.

Current route composition locates the reallocation. A changing balance between single-exchange and cross-exchange routes accounts for little of the increase, and USDT's intermediary share rises relative to its endpoint share. Liquidity, router reach, asset risk, and software can nevertheless move together within the same endpoint market. Ranking these explanations requires comparisons of the same ordered pair and trade size when the router can reach the same alternatives, together with capital and provider flows measured independently of realised route choice.
