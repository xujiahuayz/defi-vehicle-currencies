\subsection{Routes and notation}
\label{sec:setting:route}

A trader who wants to exchange one token for another does not choose a pool. The trader signs a transaction addressed to a router contract, the router selects a path through the pool graph, and it splits the order across paths when splitting pays, with every leg executing inside a single atomic transaction. If any leg fails the whole transaction reverts, and a multi-leg path therefore completes in full or does not happen. The economic object is the path, and the unit of observation throughout is the route: one coherent multi-leg component within one transaction, counted once whatever its leg count.

Write $t$ for a state index, $\mathcal{A}_{t}$ for the assets tradable at $t$, $\mathcal{P}_{t}$ for the pools live at $t$, and $\mathcal{V}$ for the priced venues, with $v(p) \in \mathcal{V}$ the venue of pool $p$ and $S_{p,t}$ the reconstructed state of $p$ at $t$. A route $r$ carries an ordered pool sequence $(p_{1},\ldots,p_{L})$ of length $L(r)$, an endpoint pair $(a,b)$ with $a$ paid in and $b$ received, an intermediary set $\mathcal{M}(r) = \{k_{1},\ldots,k_{L-1}\}$, and a size $s$ measured in dollars at the input token's price. Let $q_{p}(a \to b,\, x;\, S_{p,t})$ denote the venue's own exact-input quote, the units of $b$ paid out for $x$ units of $a$ at pool $p$ in state $S_{p,t}$. The quoted output of the route composes its legs,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:route}
Q_{r}(s,t) \;=\; q_{p_{L}}\!\left(k_{L-1} \to b,\; \cdots\, q_{p_{1}}\!\left(a \to k_{1},\, s;\, S_{p_{1},t}\right) \cdots;\; S_{p_{L},t}\right),
\end{equation}
and Section~\ref{sec:setting:pricing} gives the four functional forms $q_{p}$ takes across the priced venues. Two benchmarks price the paths the router passed over. The first is the cheapest single-hop execution of the same endpoints at the same state and size, and the second is the cheapest two-hop execution through a given intermediary $k$, with each leg independently taking its own cheapest venue,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:direct}
Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t) \;=\; \max_{p \,\in\, \mathcal{P}_{ab,t}} \; q_{p}\!\left(a \to b,\, s;\, S_{p,t}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vehicle}
Q^{k}_{ab}(s,t) \;=\; \max_{p' \,\in\, \mathcal{P}_{kb,t}} \; q_{p'}\!\left(k \to b,\; \max_{p \,\in\, \mathcal{P}_{ak,t}} q_{p}\!\left(a \to k,\, s;\, S_{p,t}\right);\; S_{p',t}\right).
\end{equation}
The cost gap of routing $(a,b)$ through $k$ at size $s$ and state $t$ is the shortfall of the vehicle route against the direct benchmark, as a fraction of that benchmark,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gap}
G^{k}_{ab}(s,t) \;=\; \frac{Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t) - Q^{k}_{ab}(s,t)}{Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t)},
\end{equation}
reported in basis points as $10^{4} \, G^{k}_{ab}(s,t)$. The dominance indicator takes a gross and an all-in form, the second charging the extra hop the gas it costs,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:dom}
D^{\mathrm{g}}_{ab,k}(s,t) &\;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left\{ G^{k}_{ab}(s,t) > 0 \right\}, \\
\label{eq:domallin}
D^{\mathrm{a}}_{ab,k}(s,t) &\;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left\{ G^{k}_{ab}(s,t) > -\gamma(s,t) \right\}, \qquad
\gamma(s,t) \;=\; \frac{g \, \varpi_{t} \, P_{t}}{s},
\end{align}
with $g$ the incremental gas of the second hop, $\varpi_{t}$ the gas price and $P_{t}$ the native asset's dollar price. That gas charge separates the two forms: gross dominance is a statement about the pool graph, and all-in dominance is a statement about what a trader could have banked. The pool graph behind both statements is the whole priced venue set, and the maximisations in \eqref{eq:direct} and \eqref{eq:vehicle} run over all of it. A counterfactual that omits a venue understates $Q^{\ast}$ and $Q^{k}$ together, and it moves every cost comparison in one direction. Section~\ref{sec:executability} signs that direction and bounds its size.

Deterministic routing removes one possible incumbency channel, the habit and relationships of a human dealer, but that is not \citet{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}'s mechanism. His proportional brokerage cost in each bilateral market falls with that market's transaction volume, so using a vehicle expands its two markets, lowers their costs and can validate the original choice. \citet{KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium} make acceptability an equilibrium object: an agent accepts a good for indirect exchange when its marketability, generated by others' trading strategies and inventories, compensates for its storage cost. \citet{DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition} introduce fixed user-side costs of learning, reckoning, requoting prices and accounting in a new currency. On chain, the selection in \eqref{eq:route} is a graph search re-solved at every quote with no memory of the previous choice. Quote-time habit is absent by construction, but the volume-cost feedback can persist through state variables, including where liquidity sits and which paths the router integrates.

The dominant-currency literature studies complementary roles from the invoicing side \citep{GopinathEtAl2020DCP,AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant,Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem} and the funding side \citep{GopinathStein2021Making,ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt}, and \citet{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX} documents related concentration inside dealer trading volumes. That concentration appears in a historical network too. That network lets \citet{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics} estimate the feedback between a currency's use and its cost, and they report persistence without lock-in. The feedback is measured on the count of markets quoting a currency, a degree statistic that \eqref{eq:route} separates from realised use. The router quotes at a specific block and settles in the next, so the state a route was chosen at is a block and neither a day nor an hour. Section~\ref{sec:executability} reports what the panel pays for indexing $S_{p,t}$ at a coarser state.

\subsection{Definitions and measures}
\label{sec:setting:definitions}

\begin{definition}[Vehicle]
\label{def:vehicle}
An asset $k$ is a \emph{vehicle} on route $r$ when $k \in \mathcal{M}(r)$, meaning the route passes through $k$ while $k$ is neither endpoint of the trade the router was asked to perform.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Vehicle role]
\label{def:role}
The \emph{vehicle role} of asset $k$ on date $t$ is the extent to which routes between other assets pass through $k$, measured by value share and by betweenness centrality,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:role}
\theta_{k,t} \;=\; \frac{\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_{t}} \nu_{r} \, \mathbf{1}\{k \in \mathcal{M}(r)\}}{\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_{t}} \nu_{r} \, \mathbf{1}\{L(r) > 1\}},
\qquad
\beta_{k,t} \;=\; \sum_{a \neq k \neq b} \frac{\sigma_{ab,t}(k)}{\sigma_{ab,t}},
\end{equation}
where $\nu_{r}$ is the dollar value of route $r$, $\mathcal{R}_{t}$ the routes settled on date $t$, and $\sigma_{ab,t}(k)$ of the $\sigma_{ab,t}$ shortest paths joining $a$ and $b$ that day pass through $k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dominated route]
\label{def:dominated}
A route with endpoints $(a,b)$, intermediary $k$ and size $s$ is \emph{dominated} at state $t$ when $D^{\mathrm{g}}_{ab,k}(s,t) = 1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Round trip]
\label{def:roundtrip}
A route is a \emph{round trip} when its first input token equals its last output token, $a = b$, so that the route returns the trader to the asset it started from.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Asset types]
\label{def:types}
The type map $\tau: \mathcal{A}_{t} \to \{\mathrm{native}, \mathrm{stable}, \mathrm{imported}, \mathrm{other}\}$ labels every asset by what it is a claim on.
\end{definition}

The two role measures in \eqref{eq:role} answer different questions, $\theta_{k,t}$ asking where value moves and $\beta_{k,t}$ asking which paths must cross an asset, and Section~\ref{sec:dominance} reports both because they disagree in an informative way. The betweenness measure is the on-chain analogue of the quote-count statistic \citet{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics} use for an international role. The two measures coincide only when flow is spread evenly over the paths a graph admits. Dominance in Definition~\ref{def:dominated} says a direct pool between the endpoints, quoted at the same reconstructed state and the same size, would have returned more of the output token than the path the router took. It is a property of a state, and a dominated route is no evidence that the router erred. The direct pool may have been unreachable, the quote may have moved between choice and settlement, or the comparison may omit a cost the router priced. What $D^{\mathrm{g}}$ identifies is the configuration foreign exchange cannot observe, an asset in the vehicle role at a moment when routing through it was not the cheapest way to trade.

Round trips are excluded everywhere before anything is measured because a route that begins and ends in the same asset is not an endpoint-to-endpoint conversion under the paper's route unit. This population can contain cyclic arbitrage and other self-returning activity, but the endpoint rule does not identify every closed route as arbitrage or wash trading. \citet{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys} establish that multi-trade arbitrage can be bundled atomically; they do not supply an exhaustive behavioural classification of closed paths. Round trips run 12.7\% of multi-leg routes by count and 21.7\% by value on the median of 79 sampled days. On 2025-12-06, the most extreme day observed, they run 25.9\% and 91.3\%.
% output/exhibits/round_trip_share_by_day.jsonl, via scripts/measure_round_trip_share.py

The \emph{native} label of Definition~\ref{def:types} goes to the platform's own settlement asset, independent of how many pools quote it, how much volume it intermediates or how cheaply a route through it executes. The \emph{stable} label goes to tokens targeting a fixed value in a fiat currency, \emph{imported} covers wrapped claims on off-chain or cross-chain value, and \emph{other} is the residual. Pairing degree, centrality, depth and route cost are measured properties of those types, not ingredients in the taxonomy. The stable class spans heterogeneous designs. Reserve-backed stablecoins target par through backing and redemption, making confidence in those arrangements relevant to acceptance and run exposure \citep{GortonZhang2023Wildcat}. Peg-restoration mechanisms and solvency differ across fiat-backed, crypto-collateralized and algorithmic designs \citep{CataliniDeGortariShah2022Stablecoins,LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}. The type label is assigned independently of those mechanisms, and Section~\ref{sec:rivals} reads the migration of $\theta_{k,t}$ toward that class against them.

\label{sec:setting:data}

The sample is every swap on the eight largest Ethereum automated market makers from 2020-02-11 to 2026-06-30, carrying roughly 215,000 swap legs a day. Coverage is 2,277 days of the 2,332 in that span, the 55 absent days sit in the earliest weeks, before several of the venues existed, and legs are assembled into routes within each transaction, so multi-leg paths are recovered across venues as well as within one. The on-chain object here is different: atomic routers can condition on cross-pool state, weakening their disconnected-demand assumption, and this panel reconstructs the aggregate execution route directly.

The advantage this reconstruction confers should be described accurately. The reconstruction runs on data public to anyone with an endpoint, a point \citet{Schar2021DeFi} makes about the architecture itself, so nothing here is proprietary. What is costly is attribution: assembling multi-leg routes across eight venues at scale, resolving token decimals, and reconstructing $S_{p,t}$ at the moment each route executed. That attribution is engineering difficulty, and it does not substitute for identification. The executor of a route is recoverable from the calling contract, and the author of the routing decision is only partly so. On a 2024 snapshot of 74,323 swaps, 241 distinct senders appear, and a hand-built registry of the ten largest accounts for 67.7\% of those swaps. By a 2025 snapshot the population has fragmented to 397 senders across 130,282 swaps, and the same registry reaches 11.8\% of swaps. Results are therefore reported over routes and never over routers.

Constant-product pools carry reserves $R_{a,p,t}$ and $R_{b,p,t}$ whose product is constant within a trade, and their exact-input quote is closed form,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cp}
R_{a,p,t} \, R_{b,p,t} \;=\; \kappa_{p,t},
\qquad
q^{\mathrm{cp}}_{p}(a \to b,\, x) \;=\; \frac{(1-\phi_{p}) \, x \, R_{b,p,t}}{R_{a,p,t} + (1-\phi_{p}) \, x},
\end{equation}
with the swap fee $\phi_{p}$ fixed at 30 basis points on both constant-product venues. One pricing routine serves both of them, since they run the same invariant and the same event schema. \citet{LeharParlour2024Uniswap} study liquidity provision on exactly this invariant, and \citet{MilionisMoallemiRoughgarden2023Myersonian} characterise the provider's optimal position within it.

Concentrated-liquidity pools distribute reserves over a grid of ticks, so the quote walks the tick map with the exact swap step the contract implements \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3}. Index the pool's two tokens as $0$ and $1$, let $P$ be the price of token $0$ in units of token $1$, and let $L$ be the liquidity in range. The token amounts spanned between two prices, and the tick grid the ranges are cut on, are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clamounts}
\Delta_{0} \;=\; L \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{\alpha}}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{\beta}}} \right),
\qquad
\Delta_{1} \;=\; L \left( \sqrt{P_{\beta}} - \sqrt{P_{\alpha}} \right),
\qquad
\sqrt{P(i)} \;=\; 1.0001^{\,i/2},
\end{equation}
and one swap step of net input $x$ moves the price within the current range by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:clstep}
\sqrt{P}' &\;=\; \sqrt{P} + \frac{(1-\phi_{p}) \, x}{L}
&& \text{when token } 1 \text{ is paid in}, \\
\label{eq:clstepdown}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{P}'} &\;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{P}} + \frac{(1-\phi_{p}) \, x}{L}
&& \text{when token } 0 \text{ is paid in}.
\end{align}
When a step exhausts the range, the price lands on the boundary tick $i$ and the pool's liquidity is updated by that tick's net position before the next step,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cross}
L \;\longleftarrow\; L + \varepsilon \, \Delta L_{i},
\qquad \varepsilon = -1 \text{ downward and } +1 \text{ upward},
\end{equation}
which reproduces realised output at 0.0000\% median error in all four combinations of trade direction and tick crossing. Direction is where this routine is fragile, and the fragility is silent. Taking the tick just crossed as the next target under-quotes tick-crossing upward swaps by a median 62.6\%, it leaves every non-crossing quote exact, and a validation set drawn only from non-crossing swaps would therefore pass the routine unchanged.
% docs/venue-coverage-bounds.md; src/ddvc/pricing/v3quote.py, the direction tie-break in _next_init_tick

Curve pools interpolate between a constant-sum and a constant-product curve. For $n$ tokens with normalised balances $x_{i}$, amplification coefficient $A$ and invariant $D$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stableswap}
A n^{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i} \;+\; D \;=\; A D n^{n} \;+\; \frac{D^{\,n+1}}{n^{n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}},
\end{equation}
and an exact-input quote solves the same equation for the output balance once the input has been added,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ssquote}
q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{p}(a \to b,\, x) \;=\; (1-\phi_{p}) \left( x_{b} - y \right),
\qquad
y \;\text{ solving \eqref{eq:stableswap} at } D \text{ with } x_{a} \text{ replaced by } x_{a} + x .
\end{equation}
Both loops are the contract's own Newton iterations in integer arithmetic, so the quote is exact given the balances and $A$. The amplification coefficient is not served by the raw layer, and it is identified from trades instead. Every quantity in \eqref{eq:stableswap} except $A$ is observed, and every realised swap reports its input and its output. The coefficient is therefore recovered by fitting the pool's own trades at a high quantile of absolute quote error,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:amp}
\hat{A}_{p,t} \;=\; \arg\min_{A} \; \mathcal{Q}_{0.90} \!\left( \left\{ \left| \frac{q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{p}(x_{j};\, A) - y_{j}}{y_{j}} \right| \right\}_{j} \right),
\end{equation}
where $(x_{j}, y_{j})$ are the day's realised amounts. A median gate is gameable, and it was gamed on a neighbouring venue family. One pool there cleared a 0.1\% median gate and then returned 34\% median error on held-out trades, so the ninetieth percentile carries the gate here. A pool whose best achievable error exceeds 1\% is excluded and never quoted at a best-fit $A$, which is what happens to Curve's crypto-pools, whose own invariant is different, and which come back at 36\% median error when the wrong one is fitted to them.
% docs/venue-coverage-bounds.md; src/ddvc/pricing/stableswap.py, MAX_CALIBRATION_ERROR and FIT_QUANTILE

Balancer pools keep a weighted geometric mean of their balances constant, which nests the constant product at equal weights,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted}
\prod_{i} B_{i,p,t}^{\, w_{i,p}} \;=\; \bar{V}_{p,t},
\qquad \sum_{i} w_{i,p} = 1,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wquote}
q^{\mathrm{w}}_{p}(a \to b,\, x) \;=\; B_{b,p,t} \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{B_{a,p,t}}{B_{a,p,t} + (1-\phi_{p}) \, x} \right)^{ w_{a,p} / w_{b,p} } \right].
\end{equation}
Only the ratio $w_{a,p}/w_{b,p}$ enters, so an equal-weight Balancer pool and a constant-product pair on the same reserves quote identically, and every departure is the weight ratio doing work. The ratio and the fee are both served at the head block while balances come from a historical snapshot, and a pool that repriced itself since is then quoted with the wrong parameter at a past state. Both are therefore identified from trades on the pattern of \eqref{eq:amp} whenever the reported values fail to reproduce the pool's own swaps. One venue in the top eight is not priced, and the exclusion carries a number. SushiSwap's concentrated-liquidity deployment is 0.016\% of the priced venues' volume pooled across the sample, and on fourteen sampled days 4.1\% of its own volume sits on pairs that no priced venue hosts, so omitting it cannot move any quantity reported here.
% src/ddvc/pricing/weighted.py, calibrate_fee and calibrate_weight_ratio
% docs/venue-coverage-bounds.md

Recovering $S_{p,t}$ at a past moment has a direction, and getting it backwards is silent. State is stored at period ends and unwound backward from there,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unwind}
S_{p,t} \;=\; S_{p,\bar{t}(t)} \;-\; \sum_{u \,\in\, \mathcal{E}_{p} \,:\, t \,<\, u \,\leq\, \bar{t}(t)} \delta_{p,u},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{t}(t)$ is the end of the period containing $t$, $\mathcal{E}_{p}$ the pool's event stream and $\delta_{p,u}$ the net balance change of event $u$. Replaying forward from the previous period's stored state instead accumulates every unrecorded liquidity event, and it returns a median 11.8\% error where backward unwinding returns 0.0000\%. The same result appeared independently on a second venue family, where a flat within-period assumption gave 1.7\%, an opening-state assumption 2.8\%, and backward unwinding 0.0000\%. The event stream in \eqref{eq:unwind} must carry joins and exits alongside swaps, because a liquidity event that moves balances without moving the price ratio is otherwise charged to the invariant, and the pool then looks like one running different arithmetic.

\subsection{The support restriction}
\label{sec:setting:support}

Every pricing routine above was accepted on swaps that happened, and trades that happened are trades whose pool was deep enough to serve them. Applying those routines to hypothetical routes extends them into a region where no error has been measured, and in that region a quote is an extrapolation. The consequence is not theoretical. Before any restriction, the median cost gap at a \$100,000 trade is 4,655 basis points, which implies an arbitrage cycle paying after three pool fees and three-hop gas. A gap of this size is economically implausible as an unconstrained same-state quote. Smart-contract proxy execution can bundle multiple trades across exchanges into one all-or-nothing transaction and revert the batch when one leg fails \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys}. That mechanism removes unmatched-leg risk but not financing, gas, competition, state latency or builder frictions. The support screen is therefore validated quantitatively below instead of treating instantaneous capture as automatic.
% output/exhibits/gap_arbitrage_bound.jsonl; scripts/measure_dominance_windows.py

The restriction has to be applied before the quote and never after it. Filtering on the size of the measured gap would condition on a monotone function of the outcome in \eqref{eq:gap}, which is selection on the dependent variable and discharges nothing. The restriction is therefore stated in a quantity known in advance, namely a leg's own price impact against its own marginal price, with the threshold read off the distribution of realised behaviour,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:support}
\pi_{\ell}(x,t) \;=\; \frac{\tilde{q}_{\ell}(x,t) - q_{\ell}(x,t)}{\tilde{q}_{\ell}(x,t)} \;\leq\; \bar{\pi},
\qquad
\frac{x}{R_{a,p,t}} \;\leq\; \bar{\pi},
\qquad \bar{\pi} = 0.05,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{q}_{\ell}$ prices leg $\ell$ at the pool's marginal rate and the second form is the constant-product special case. Price impact is a property of one leg measured on its own marginal price, computed with no reference to the competing route, and rejecting a leg nobody would take does not condition on which route wins. Pooled over 932,270 validated swaps across eight sampled days, the median trade moves 0.34\% of the pool's input-side reserve, the 90th percentile 3.3\%, and the 99th 14.9\%, which places $\bar{\pi}$ between the 95th and the 99th percentile of realised behaviour.

The restriction is expensive and its cost is reported. It removes between 70\% and 86\% of quotable routes on the four measured days. It also moves the median gap from thousands of basis points to tens: 31 basis points at \$1,000, 34 at \$10,000 and 21 at \$100,000. Applying the reserve-ratio form alone was insufficient, for a compositional reason. Most quotes entering \eqref{eq:direct} and \eqref{eq:vehicle} come from pools whose reserves sit on a tick map, where a reserve ratio has no meaning, and the impact form in \eqref{eq:support} is what binds there. With the constant-product branch restricted alone the median gap at a \$100,000 trade fell only to 1,164 basis points, and 70.4\% of gaps still implied a cycle that pays.

\subsection{Costs by type}
\label{sec:setting:validation}

On the restricted panel, with a fixed effect for pair by time window by trade size, the native indicator on the continuous cost gap of \eqref{eq:gap} is $-25.3$ basis points with a standard error of $11.4$ (0.037), estimated on 732 routes in 274 cells. The binary form of the same specification, taking $D^{\mathrm{g}}$ as the outcome, is $-0.043$ (0.543). The ordering restates the thick-network content of Definition~\ref{def:types} and carries no economic claim on its own. A quoting engine that reproduces an ordering the literature already assumes is an engine behaving as expected, the standard \citet{CaparrosChaudharyKlein2024BlockchainScaling} applies to on-chain liquidity measurement, and it licenses the unknown quantities that follow. A coefficient on a binary outcome at a threshold the design absorbs is a shift in a distribution function with no mapping to basis points absent the density at that threshold. $G^{k}_{ab}$ and never $D^{\mathrm{g}}$ is therefore the estimand wherever a magnitude is reported.
