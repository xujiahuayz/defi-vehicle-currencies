\subsection{Routes and notation}
\label{sec:setting:route}

In a routed swap, the trader submits a transaction to a router, which selects the pools and may split the order across paths. All legs execute atomically: if one fails, the transaction reverts. I define one coherent directed component within a transaction as a route and count it once, regardless of its number of legs.

Write $t$ for a state index, $\mathcal{A}_{t}$ for the assets tradable at $t$, $\mathcal{P}_{t}$ for the pools live at $t$, and $\mathcal{V}$ for the priced venues, with $v(p) \in \mathcal{V}$ the venue of pool $p$ and $S_{p,t}$ the reconstructed state of $p$ at $t$. A route $r$ carries an ordered pool sequence $(p_{1},\ldots,p_{L})$ of length $L(r)$, an endpoint pair $(a,b)$ with $a$ paid in and $b$ received, an intermediary set $\mathcal{M}(r) = \{k_{1},\ldots,k_{L-1}\}$, and a size $s$ measured in dollars at the input token's price. Let $q_{p}(a \to b,\, x;\, S_{p,t})$ denote the venue's own exact-input quote, the units of $b$ paid out for $x$ units of $a$ at pool $p$ in state $S_{p,t}$. The quoted output of the route composes its legs,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:route}
Q_{r}(s,t) \;=\; q_{p_{L}}\!\left(k_{L-1} \to b,\; \cdots\, q_{p_{1}}\!\left(a \to k_{1},\, s;\, S_{p_{1},t}\right) \cdots;\; S_{p_{L},t}\right),
\end{equation}
and Section~\ref{sec:setting:pricing} gives the four functional forms $q_{p}$ takes across the priced venues. The observed route is sufficient to define vehicle status and dominance. Counterfactual direct and vehicle quotes require additional state and opportunity-set assumptions and are introduced separately in Section~\ref{sec:setting:pricing}.

Automated execution removes the relationships of a human dealer from the transaction, but that is not \citet{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}'s mechanism. His proportional brokerage cost in each bilateral market falls with transaction volume, so using a vehicle expands its two markets, lowers their costs and can validate the original choice. \citet{KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium} make acceptability an equilibrium object: an agent accepts a good for indirect exchange when its marketability, generated by others' trading strategies and inventories, compensates for its storage cost. \citet{DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition} introduce fixed user-side costs of learning, reckoning, requoting prices and accounting in a new currency. On chain, execution is deterministic conditional on the submitted route, but route selection can still reflect persistent software defaults, venue integration and liquidity placement. The setting removes dealer relationships from execution, not volume--cost feedback or software-mediated coordination.

The dominant-currency literature studies complementary roles in invoicing \citep{GopinathEtAl2020DCP,AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant,Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem}, funding \citep{GopinathStein2021Making,ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt}, and dealer trading volumes \citep{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. \citet{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics} measure a currency's international role by the number of markets quoting it and estimate a feedback between use and cost in a historical trading network. Their degree statistic describes network position; the observed routes in \eqref{eq:route} reveal realised intermediary use. A route's settlement block is observed, but the state available when the routing decision was made is not and may precede settlement. Section~\ref{sec:executability} assesses how settlement-time and latent quote-time states differ in counterfactual comparisons.

\subsection{Definitions and measures}
\label{sec:setting:definitions}

\begin{definition}[Vehicle status]
\label{def:vehicle}
Asset $k$ has \emph{vehicle status} on route $r$ when it is an observed intermediary and is not an endpoint,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vehicle-status}
Z_{r,k} \;=\; \mathbf{1}\{k \in \mathcal{M}(r)\}.
\end{equation}
Vehicle status is therefore binary at the route level.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Vehicle dominance]
\label{def:role}
The \emph{vehicle dominance} of asset $k$ on date $t$ is its continuous share of realised intermediary use. I report a count share and a value share,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dominance}
S^{N}_{k,t} \;=\;
\frac{\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}} Z_{r,k}}
     {|\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}|},
\qquad
S^{V}_{k,t} \;=\;
\frac{\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}^{(2),V}_{t}} \nu_{r} Z_{r,k}}
     {\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}^{(2),V}_{t}} \nu_{r}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}$ contains exact two-leg routes, each with one observed intermediary, $\mathcal{R}^{(2),V}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}$ is the subset with value support, and $\nu_{r}$ is the supported dollar value of route $r$. Count dominance measures how frequently $k$ carries the intermediary role; value dominance measures the economic value routed through it. Multi-leg routes with several intermediary positions enter the broader episode diagnostics, not this one-vehicle dominance measure.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Graph position]
\label{def:graph-position}
Graph position describes the paths available through an asset; vehicle dominance describes the realised routes that use it. Betweenness centrality is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:role}
\beta_{k,t} \;=\; \sum_{a \neq k \neq b} \frac{\sigma_{ab,t}(k)}{\sigma_{ab,t}},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{ab,t}(k)$ of the $\sigma_{ab,t}$ shortest paths joining $a$ and $b$ pass through $k$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Round trip]
\label{def:roundtrip}
A route is a \emph{round trip} when its first input token equals its last output token, $a = b$, so that the route returns the trader to the asset it started from.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Asset types]
\label{def:types}
The type map $\tau: \mathcal{A}_{t} \to \{\mathrm{native}, \mathrm{stable}, \mathrm{imported}, \mathrm{other}\}$ labels every asset by what it is a claim on.
\end{definition}

These objects answer different questions. Vehicle status records whether $k$ is the intermediary on one observed route. Count and value dominance aggregate that realised use across routes. Betweenness instead describes $k$'s position in the trading graph and is the on-chain analogue of the quote-count statistic used by \citet{FlandreauJobst2009Empirics}. A central asset need not carry a large share of realised routes, and a heavily used vehicle need not lie on many shortest paths. Section~\ref{sec:dominance} therefore reports count and value dominance; later tests use graph position as a separate characteristic.

I exclude round trips because they return to the starting asset without exchanging between distinct endpoints. This population can contain cyclic arbitrage and other self-returning activity, but the endpoint rule does not identify every closed route as arbitrage or wash trading. \citet{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys} establish that multi-trade arbitrage can be bundled atomically; they do not supply an exhaustive behavioural classification of closed paths. Round trips run 12.7\% of multi-leg routes by count and 21.7\% by value on the median of 79 sampled days. On 2025-12-06, the most extreme day observed, they run 25.9\% and 91.3\%.
% output/exhibits/round_trip_share_by_day.jsonl, via scripts/measure_round_trip_share.py

The \emph{native} label of Definition~\ref{def:types} goes to the platform's own settlement asset, independent of how many pools quote it, how much volume it intermediates or how cheaply a route through it executes. The \emph{stable} label goes to tokens targeting a fixed value in a fiat currency, \emph{imported} covers wrapped claims on off-chain or cross-chain value, and \emph{other} is the residual. Pairing degree, centrality, depth and route cost are measured properties of those types, not ingredients in the taxonomy. The stable class spans heterogeneous designs. Reserve-backed stablecoins target par through backing and redemption, making confidence in those arrangements relevant to acceptance and run exposure \citep{GortonZhang2023Wildcat}. Peg-restoration mechanisms and solvency differ across fiat-backed, crypto-collateralized and algorithmic designs \citep{CataliniDeGortariShah2022Stablecoins,LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}. Type assignments do not depend on these measured outcomes, and Section~\ref{sec:rivals} examines the institutional differences within the stable class.

\label{sec:setting:data}

The sample is every swap on the eight largest Ethereum automated market makers from 2020-02-11 to 2026-06-30, carrying roughly 215,000 swap legs a day. Coverage is 2,277 days of the 2,332 in that span, the 55 absent days sit in the earliest weeks, before several of the venues existed, and legs are assembled into routes within each transaction, so multi-leg paths are recovered across venues as well as within one. \subsection{Pricing the counterfactual}
\label{sec:setting:pricing}

I construct two counterfactual benchmarks at a common reconstructed settlement state and trade size. The direct benchmark is the best single-hop execution between the observed endpoints. The vehicle benchmark is the best two-hop execution through intermediary $k$, allowing each leg to use its best priced venue,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:direct}
Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t) \;=\; \max_{p \,\in\, \mathcal{P}_{ab,t}} \; q_{p}\!\left(a \to b,\, s;\, S_{p,t}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vehicle}
Q^{k}_{ab}(s,t) \;=\; \max_{p' \,\in\, \mathcal{P}_{kb,t}} \; q_{p'}\!\left(k \to b,\; \max_{p \,\in\, \mathcal{P}_{ak,t}} q_{p}\!\left(a \to k,\, s;\, S_{p,t}\right);\; S_{p',t}\right).
\end{equation}
The vehicle-route shortfall relative to direct execution is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gap}
G^{k}_{ab}(s,t) \;=\; \frac{Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t) - Q^{k}_{ab}(s,t)}{Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t)},
\end{equation}
reported in basis points as $10^{4}G^{k}_{ab}(s,t)$.

\begin{definition}[Cost-dominated route]
\label{def:dominated}
A route with endpoints $(a,b)$, intermediary $k$ and size $s$ is \emph{cost dominated} at reconstructed state $t$ when $G^{k}_{ab}(s,t)>0$: the direct benchmark returns more output than the vehicle benchmark before gas.
\end{definition}

Let $\mathcal G^{D}_{ab}(s,t)$ and $\mathcal G^{I}_{ab,k}(s,t)$ denote the direct and indirect routes' dollar gas expenditures evaluated at the realised transaction's settlement block. Gross and all-in cost-dominance indicators are
\begin{align}
\label{eq:dom}
D^{\mathrm{g}}_{ab,k}(s,t) &\;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left\{ G^{k}_{ab}(s,t) > 0 \right\}, \\
\label{eq:domallin}
D^{\mathrm{a}}_{ab,k}(s,t) &\;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left\{ G^{k}_{ab}(s,t) > -\gamma(s,t) \right\}, \qquad
\gamma(s,t) \;=\; \frac{\mathcal G^{I}_{ab,k}(s,t)-\mathcal G^{D}_{ab}(s,t)}{Q^{\ast}_{ab}(s,t)}.
\end{align}
Each $\mathcal G$ combines gas units calibrated by year, topology, venue sequence and intermediary identity with the transaction's effective gas price and the native asset's dollar price at settlement. The maximisations in \eqref{eq:direct} and \eqref{eq:vehicle} cover the priced venue set. These indicators measure costs at settlement. An interpretation about route choice additionally requires the router's quote time, venue access and any costs outside the observed perimeter. Section~\ref{sec:executability} evaluates those conditions before using the counterfactual to interpret route choice.

Constant-product pools carry reserves $R_{a,p,t}$ and $R_{b,p,t}$ whose product is constant within a trade, and their exact-input quote is closed form,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cp}
R_{a,p,t} \, R_{b,p,t} \;=\; \kappa_{p,t},
\qquad
q^{\mathrm{cp}}_{p}(a \to b,\, x) \;=\; \frac{(1-\phi_{p}) \, x \, R_{b,p,t}}{R_{a,p,t} + (1-\phi_{p}) \, x},
\end{equation}
with the swap fee $\phi_{p}$ fixed at 30 basis points on both constant-product venues. One pricing routine serves both of them, since they run the same invariant and the same event schema. \citet{LeharParlour2024Uniswap} study liquidity provision on exactly this invariant.

Concentrated-liquidity pools distribute reserves over a grid of ticks, so a quote walks the tick map using the protocol's continuous swap identities \citep{AdamsZinsmeisterRobinson2021UniswapV3}. The implementation applies those identities in Q64.96 integer arithmetic with direction-specific rounding; Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} records the port and its realised-swap validation. Index the pool's two tokens as $0$ and $1$, let $P$ be the price of token $0$ in units of token $1$, and let $L$ be the liquidity in range. The token amounts spanned between two prices, and the tick grid the ranges are cut on, are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clamounts}
\Delta_{0} \;=\; L \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{\alpha}}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{\beta}}} \right),
\qquad
\Delta_{1} \;=\; L \left( \sqrt{P_{\beta}} - \sqrt{P_{\alpha}} \right),
\qquad
\sqrt{P(i)} \;=\; 1.0001^{\,i/2},
\end{equation}
and one swap step of gross input $x$ moves the price within the current range by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:clstep}
\sqrt{P}' &\;=\; \sqrt{P} + \frac{(1-\phi_{p}) \, x}{L}
&& \text{when token } 1 \text{ is paid in}, \\
\label{eq:clstepdown}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{P}'} &\;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{P}} + \frac{(1-\phi_{p}) \, x}{L}
&& \text{when token } 0 \text{ is paid in}.
\end{align}
When a step exhausts the range, the price lands on the boundary tick $i$ and the pool's liquidity is updated by that tick's net position before the next step,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cross}
L \;\longleftarrow\; L + \varepsilon \, \Delta L_{i},
\qquad \varepsilon = -1 \text{ downward and } +1 \text{ upward},
\end{equation}
which reproduces realised output at 0.0000\% median error in all four combinations of trade direction and tick crossing. Direction is where this routine is fragile, and the fragility is silent. Taking the tick just crossed as the next target under-quotes tick-crossing upward swaps by a median 62.6\%, it leaves every non-crossing quote exact, and a validation set drawn only from non-crossing swaps would therefore pass the routine unchanged.
% docs/venue-coverage-bounds.md; src/ddvc/pricing/v3quote.py, the direction tie-break in _next_init_tick

Curve pools interpolate between a constant-sum and a constant-product curve. For $n$ tokens with normalised balances $x_{i}$, amplification coefficient $A$ and invariant $D$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stableswap}
A n^{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i} \;+\; D \;=\; A D n^{n} \;+\; \frac{D^{\,n+1}}{n^{n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}},
\end{equation}
and an exact-input quote solves the same equation for the output balance once the input has been added,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ssquote}
q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{p}(a \to b,\, x) \;=\; (1-\phi_{p}) \left( x_{b} - y \right),
\qquad
y \;\text{ solving \eqref{eq:stableswap} at } D \text{ with } x_{a} \text{ replaced by } x_{a} + x .
\end{equation}
Both loops are the contract's own Newton iterations in integer arithmetic, so the quote is exact given the balances and $A$. The amplification coefficient is not served by the raw layer, and it is identified from trades instead. Every quantity in \eqref{eq:stableswap} except $A$ is observed, and every realised swap reports its input and its output. The coefficient is therefore recovered by fitting the pool's own trades at a high quantile of absolute quote error,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:amp}
\hat{A}_{p,t} \;=\; \arg\min_{A} \; \mathcal{Q}_{0.90} \!\left( \left\{ \left| \frac{q^{\mathrm{ss}}_{p}(x_{j};\, A) - y_{j}}{y_{j}} \right| \right\}_{j} \right),
\end{equation}
where $(x_{j}, y_{j})$ are the day's realised amounts. Validation uses the ninetieth percentile because large errors can be concentrated in the tail even when median error is small. In a neighbouring venue family, one pool cleared a 0.1\% median threshold but produced a 34\% median error on held-out trades. A Curve pool is excluded when its best achievable error exceeds 1\%; the excluded crypto-pools use a different invariant and return a 36\% median error when fitted to StableSwap.
% docs/venue-coverage-bounds.md; src/ddvc/pricing/stableswap.py, MAX_CALIBRATION_ERROR and FIT_QUANTILE

Balancer pools keep a weighted geometric mean of their balances constant, which nests the constant product at equal weights,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted}
\prod_{i} B_{i,p,t}^{\, w_{i,p}} \;=\; \bar{V}_{p,t},
\qquad \sum_{i} w_{i,p} = 1,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wquote}
q^{\mathrm{w}}_{p}(a \to b,\, x) \;=\; B_{b,p,t} \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{B_{a,p,t}}{B_{a,p,t} + (1-\phi_{p}) \, x} \right)^{ w_{a,p} / w_{b,p} } \right].
\end{equation}
Only the ratio $w_{a,p}/w_{b,p}$ enters, so an equal-weight Balancer pool and a constant-product pair on the same reserves quote identically. The ratio and fee are served at the head block while balances come from historical snapshots; when those parameters fail to reproduce realised swaps, I estimate them from trades using the criterion in \eqref{eq:amp}. SushiSwap's concentrated-liquidity deployment is not priced. It accounts for 0.016\% of pooled priced-venue volume, although on fourteen sampled days 4.1\% of its own volume is on pairs hosted by no priced venue. These quantities bound its aggregate importance but do not assume that its omission is immaterial for every pair.
% src/ddvc/pricing/weighted.py, calibrate_fee and calibrate_weight_ratio
% docs/venue-coverage-bounds.md

Historical state is stored at period ends and recovered by unwinding subsequent events,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unwind}
S_{p,t} \;=\; S_{p,\bar{t}(t)} \;-\; \sum_{u \,\in\, \mathcal{E}_{p} \,:\, t \,<\, u \,\leq\, \bar{t}(t)} \delta_{p,u},
\end{equation}
where $\bar{t}(t)$ is the end of the period containing $t$, $\mathcal{E}_{p}$ the pool's event stream and $\delta_{p,u}$ the net balance change of event $u$. Replaying forward from the previous period's stored state instead accumulates every unrecorded liquidity event, and it returns a median 11.8\% error where backward unwinding returns 0.0000\%. The same result appeared independently on a second venue family, where a flat within-period assumption gave 1.7\%, an opening-state assumption 2.8\%, and backward unwinding 0.0000\%. The event stream in \eqref{eq:unwind} must carry joins and exits alongside swaps, because a liquidity event that moves balances without moving the price ratio is otherwise charged to the invariant, and the pool then looks like one running different arithmetic.

\subsection{The support restriction}
\label{sec:setting:support}

Each pricing routine is validated on realised swaps, which necessarily lie within the pool's executable depth. A hypothetical route can extend the same routine beyond the region in which its error has been measured. Smart-contract proxy execution can bundle trades across exchanges into one all-or-nothing transaction and revert the batch when one leg fails \citep{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys}. Atomic execution removes unmatched-leg risk but not financing, gas, competition, state latency or builder frictions. I therefore derive counterfactual support from the price impact observed on realised trades and do not interpret coarser-time cost gaps as executable arbitrage.

The restriction is applied before comparing routes. Filtering on the measured cost gap would condition on a monotone function of the outcome in \eqref{eq:gap} and would leave extrapolation unresolved. I instead retain each leg according to its own price impact relative to its own marginal price, with the threshold calibrated to realised trades,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:support}
\pi_{\ell}(x,t) \;=\; \frac{\tilde{q}_{\ell}(x,t) - q_{\ell}(x,t)}{\tilde{q}_{\ell}(x,t)} \;\leq\; \bar{\pi},
\qquad
\frac{x}{R_{a,p,t}} \;\leq\; \bar{\pi},
\qquad \bar{\pi} = 0.05,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{q}_{\ell}$ prices leg $\ell$ at the pool's marginal rate and the second form is the constant-product special case. Eligibility therefore depends on the state and size of the hypothetical leg before the outputs of competing routes are compared. Pooled over 932,270 validated swaps across eight sampled days, the median trade moves 0.34\% of the pool's input-side reserve, the 90th percentile 3.3\%, and the 99th 14.9\%, which places $\bar{\pi}$ between the 95th and the 99th percentile of realised behaviour.
% output/exhibits/quoter_support_bounds.jsonl

The restriction is applied across every venue-specific quoter. A reserve ratio alone is insufficient because most counterfactual quotes come from concentrated-liquidity pools, where executable depth depends on the active tick map. The price-impact condition in \eqref{eq:support} supplies one comparable support criterion across pool families.

\subsection{Interpreting counterfactual costs}
\label{sec:setting:validation}

Counterfactual costs are distinct from vehicle dominance. Equations~\eqref{eq:dominance} measure observed intermediary use; Equation~\eqref{eq:gap} compares hypothetical outputs at a reconstructed state. A cost gap does not identify the route available when the decision was made unless quote-time state and venue access are also established. For that reason, the descriptive cost ordering is deferred to Section~\ref{sec:executability}. Reported magnitudes use the continuous gap $G^{k}_{ab}$; a coefficient on the threshold indicator $D^{\mathrm{g}}$ has no basis-point interpretation without the density at the threshold.
