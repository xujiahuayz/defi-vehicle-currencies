\section{Setting and data}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Routes and notation}
\label{sec:setting:route}

In a limit order book, traders supply liquidity by posting orders at chosen prices. An \ac{amm} instead holds tokens in a pool and quotes mechanically against the pool's current state. Liquidity providers fund the pool, and a smart contract executes trades according to the protocol's pricing rule. \citet{LeharParlour2024Uniswap} describe the constant-product design and the economics of liquidity supply in these markets.

Multiple pools turn an exchange between two assets into a routing problem. A trader may use one pool, divide an order across pools, or trade through an intermediary asset. Routers compare and execute these paths within a single transaction. All calls settle together or the transaction reverts, eliminating the risk that only part of the route settles. Fees, price impact, gas, and venue access continue to vary across paths. When the available pools connect the endpoints only through an intermediary, market availability determines the route. When direct and indirect pools coexist, the router chooses between them. Protocol design therefore affects both the markets that form and the paths that routers can compose.

Blockchain logs record individual pool calls. We recover the economic route by following directed token flows within a transaction. Calls with a common source and destination form a split order. A token received in one call and spent in a later call links the two calls into a sequential route. The same procedure separates unrelated conversions bundled in one transaction.

A transaction on January 10, 2026 illustrates the difference between the user's instruction and the route visible in exchange pools. A public explorer describes the transaction as a 460,000 PYUSD--USDe swap executed through 0x.\footnote{Transaction \ethtx{0xbda1d07f17c503b5a9c8117c5d1383472006af8a9e22aa4e5e30804b9bf67ad9}{0xbda1d07f\ldots 9bf67ad9}.} The instruction therefore begins with PYUSD. The admitted pool calls begin when 459,929.81 USDC enters Fluid, continue when that pool returns 460,331.05 USDT, and end when Uniswap v4 returns 460,104.28 USDe. Our pool-level route is USDC--USDT--USDe: USDC and USDe are its endpoints and USDT is its vehicle. Keeping the transaction instruction separate from the connected pool component prevents an off-pool transfer or conversion handled by the aggregator from being assigned to an exchange pool. We apply the same flow rule throughout the sample. A comparison with direct USDC--USDe execution additionally requires the state of the pools before this transaction.
% output/exhibits/route_replay.json; data/unified/20260110.parquet; Blockscout transaction page retrieved 2026-08-14

Write $\mathcal{A}_{t}$ for the assets tradable on day $t$, $\mathcal{P}_{t}$ for the pools live on that day, and $\mathcal{V}$ for the priced venues, with $v(p) \in \mathcal{V}$ the venue of pool $p$ and $\chi_{p,t}$ its reconstructed pre-transaction state. A route $r$ carries an ordered pool sequence $(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m(r)})$ and token sequence $(k_0,\ldots,k_{m(r)})$, where $k_0=i$ is the input and $k_{m(r)}=o$ is the output. The dollar input notional $q$ corresponds to $\Delta_i(q,t)=q/P_{i,t}$ units of the input token. Let $Q_p(i\to j,\Delta_i;\chi_{p,t})$ denote the units of token $j$ returned by pool $p$ for an exact input of $\Delta_i$ units of token $i$. Composing the pool quotes recursively gives the route's token output and its dollar value,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:route}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{k_h}&=Q_{p_h}\!\left(k_{h-1}\to k_h,\Delta_{k_{h-1}};\chi_{p_h,t}\right),
&&h=1,\ldots,m(r),\\
Q_r(q,t)&=\Delta_o,
&O_r(q,t)&=P_{o,t}Q_r(q,t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:setting:pricing} applies the venue-specific forms of $Q_p$. The distinction between the dollar notional $q$ and the token input $\Delta_i$ is necessary because pool invariants take token quantities as arguments. Equation~\eqref{eq:route} describes an executed route and, once market state has been reconstructed, a hypothetical route.

These routes provide an automated-market counterpart to the classical volume--cost mechanism. Greater use of a vehicle expands trading in its two bilateral markets and can lower the cost of using it again \citep{Krugman1980VehicleCurrencies}. In \citet{KiyotakiWright1989MoneyMedium}, traders accept an asset for indirect exchange when its marketability, which depends on other traders' strategies and inventories, compensates for its storage cost. \citet{DowdGreenaway1993CurrencyCompetition} emphasize the fixed costs of learning, accounting, and quoting in a new currency. In automated markets, the corresponding coordination appears in liquidity placement, venue access, and the paths available to routing software.

Vehicle dominance measures intermediary use along an observed trading path. Work on invoicing measures the currency named in trade contracts \citep{GopinathEtAl2020DCP,AmitiItskhokiKonings2022Dominant,Mukhin2022InternationalPriceSystem}; work on funding studies the denomination of liabilities \citep{GopinathStein2021Making,ErenMalamud2022DominantDebt}; and dealer turnover measures trading activity \citep{Somogyi2026DollarDominanceFX}. These roles can reinforce one another, but each comes from a different economic decision. Equation~\eqref{eq:route} identifies the asset used between the currency sold and the currency bought. We observe its settlement block, while the router may have formed its quote earlier. Section~\ref{sec:executability} examines the implications of that timing difference for counterfactual price comparisons.

\subsection{Measuring vehicle dominance}
\label{sec:setting:definitions}

For route $r$, let $\mathcal{M}(r)$ denote its set of intermediary assets. We record the intermediary status of asset $k$ as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vehicle-status}
Z_{r,k} \;=\; \mathbf{1}\{k \in \mathcal{M}(r)\}.
\end{equation}
Our primary measure uses exact two-leg routes, for which the intermediary is uniquely defined. Routes with several intermediaries enter separate measures of network reach. Vehicle dominance is asset $k$'s share of exact two-leg routes. We report an equally weighted share and a value-weighted share,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dominance}
S^{N}_{k,t} \;=\;
\frac{\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}} Z_{r,k}}
     {|\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}|},
\qquad
S^{V}_{k,t} \;=\;
\frac{\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}^{(2),V}_{t}} \nu_{r} Z_{r,k}}
     {\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}^{(2),V}_{t}} \nu_{r}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{t}$ is the two-leg sample, $\mathcal{R}^{(2),V}_{t}$ contains the routes with supported dollar values, and $\nu_r$ is route $r$'s supported value. $S^N_{k,t}$ measures how often the asset is used as the intermediary; $S^V_{k,t}$ measures the value routed through it.

Endpoint frequency provides a benchmark for the ordinary demand to buy or sell an asset. We compare the asset's share of intermediary positions with its share of endpoints in the same transactions. Let $n^{I}_{k,t}$ and $n^{E}_{k,t}$ denote the corresponding counts. The two shares are
\begin{equation}
I^{N}_{k,t}=\frac{n^{I}_{k,t}}{\sum_j n^{I}_{j,t}},
\qquad
E^{N}_{k,t}=\frac{n^{E}_{k,t}}{\sum_j n^{E}_{j,t}},
\label{eq:role-shares}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding share gap and excess-use ratio are
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{N}_{k,t}=I^{N}_{k,t}-E^{N}_{k,t},
\qquad
X^{N}_{k,t}=\frac{I^{N}_{k,t}}{E^{N}_{k,t}}\quad\text{when }E^{N}_{k,t}>0.
\label{eq:excess-use}
\end{equation}
The difference $\Delta^N_{k,t}$ is positive when the asset appears more often as an intermediary than as an endpoint; $X^N_{k,t}$ expresses the same comparison as a ratio. The value-weighted measures replace counts with supported dollar values.

The analysis sample requires different source and destination assets. Routes that return to their starting asset are classified as round trips and removed. They may contain cyclic arbitrage or other self-returning activity. Path geometry identifies the cycle; trader intent remains unobserved. \citet{DaianEtAl2020FlashBoys} show that several arbitrage trades can be bundled atomically. On the median of 79 sampled days, round trips account for 12.7\% of multi-leg routes by count and 21.7\% by value. Appendix~\ref{app:route-construction} reports their distribution across the sampled dates.
% output/exhibits/round_trip_share_by_day.jsonl, via scripts/measure_round_trip_share.py

Within this route sample, we classify assets by the claims they represent: native, staked native, stable, imported, and other. The native category contains the platform's settlement asset. We combine ETH and WETH because WETH is redeemable one-for-one for ETH and routers perform the wrapping step mechanically. Liquid-staking claims remain separate because they also convey staking returns. Stable assets target a fiat value, imported assets are wrapped claims on off-chain or cross-chain value, and the remaining assets enter the residual category. The economic claim represented by the asset determines its category.

The stable category includes economically different designs. Reserve-backed stablecoins rely on backing and redemption, making confidence in those arrangements relevant to acceptance and run risk \citep{GortonZhang2023Wildcat}. Fiat-backed, crypto-collateralized, and algorithmic stablecoins also differ in their solvency and peg-restoration mechanisms \citep{CataliniDeGortariShah2022Stablecoins,LyonsViswanathNatraj2023Stablecoins}. Section~\ref{sec:rivals} therefore examines the major stablecoins and backing groups separately.

\subsection{The route panel}
\label{sec:setting:data}

The route panel covers swaps on eight Ethereum \ac{amm} deployments: Curve, Uniswap v2, v3 and v4, Balancer, SushiSwap v2 and v3, and Fluid. It spans 2,332 calendar dates from February 11, 2020 through June 30, 2026. The sample begins with 472,254,909 pool-level swap records and yields 471,616,269 directed legs after harmonising token direction, event identity, and units. These deployments cover several major venues and constitute a subset of Ethereum trading. Each protocol enters after launch, so both the active venues and the routes available to traders change over the sample. The source records are complete for all sample dates. We retain 55 early dates with no observed swaps as dates with zero activity on the covered deployments.
% output/exhibits/unified_route_quality.jsonl; docs/research-workflow.md section 2

We harmonise protocol-specific event identities, token units, and trade direction before ordering pool calls by their position in the transaction. The flow rule described above then separates connected routes from unrelated calls and distinguishes single-pool trades, split orders, and sequential paths. Appendix~\ref{app:route-construction} gives the reconstruction algorithm and reports the token units that cannot be verified in the historical records.

Every observed token sequence enters the count measure. The value-weighted measure uses the subset for which the source, intermediary, and destination amounts can be valued consistently. This separation preserves observed intermediary choice when the dollar value is noisy. It also keeps venue-level depth apart from aggregate allocation when orders split across exchanges \citep{ChenDuffie2021Fragmentation}. Both measures follow the conversion executed across pools.

Transactions and contract-state changes are publicly observable \citep{Schar2021DeFi}. Reconstructing a route still requires matching pool events, venues, and token units. This procedure identifies the executed route and its calling contract. The party or software that selected the route is generally anonymous. On a 2024 snapshot of 74,323 swaps, 241 distinct senders appear, and a registry of the ten largest accounts for 67.7\% of those swaps. By a 2025 snapshot, 397 senders appear across 130,282 swaps and the same registry covers 11.8\%. We therefore report route-level results without attributing them to routers.

\subsection{Pricing the counterfactual}
\label{sec:setting:pricing}

We construct two counterfactual benchmarks at a common reconstructed settlement state and dollar input notional $q$. The direct benchmark is the best single-hop execution between the observed endpoints. The vehicle benchmark is the best two-hop execution through intermediary $k$, allowing each leg to use its best priced venue,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:direct}
O^{D}_{i,o,q,t} \;=\; P_{o,t}\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,o,t}}Q_p\!\left(i\to o,\Delta_i(q,t);\chi_{p,t}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vehicle}
O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t} \;=\; P_{o,t}\max_{p'\in\mathcal P_{k,o,t}}Q_{p'}\!\left(k\to o,\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{i,k,t}}Q_p\!\left(i\to k,\Delta_i(q,t);\chi_{p,t}\right);\chi_{p',t}\right).
\end{equation}
The vehicle-route shortfall relative to direct execution is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gap}
\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,t} \;=\; \frac{O^{D}_{i,o,q,t}-O^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t}}{O^{D}_{i,o,q,t}},
\end{equation}
reported in basis points as $10^{4}\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,t}$.

Positive values of $\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,t}$ mean that the direct benchmark returns more output than the vehicle benchmark before gas.

Let $\mathcal G^{D}_{i,o,q,t}$ and $\mathcal G^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t}$ denote the direct and indirect routes' dollar gas expenditures evaluated at the realised transaction's settlement block. We define gross and all-in indicators as
\begin{align}
\label{eq:dom}
D^{\mathrm{g}}_{i,o,k,q,t} &\;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left\{ \Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,t} > 0 \right\}, \\
\label{eq:domallin}
D^{\mathrm{a}}_{i,o,k,q,t} &\;=\; \mathbf{1}\!\left\{ \Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,t} > -\gamma_{i,o,k,q,t} \right\}, \qquad
\gamma_{i,o,k,q,t} \;=\; \frac{\mathcal G^{I}_{i,o,k,q,t}-\mathcal G^{D}_{i,o,q,t}}{O^{D}_{i,o,q,t}}.
\end{align}
Each $\mathcal G$ combines gas units calibrated by year, route structure, venue sequence, and intermediary identity with the transaction's effective gas price and the dollar price of the native asset. The direct and vehicle benchmarks use the same input and search the same declared set of priced venues. They therefore measure the difference in output at a common reconstructed settlement state. Interpreting the difference as evidence about the route chosen by a trader also requires the earlier state observed by the router, its venue access, and any costs outside the measured perimeter. Section~\ref{sec:executability} examines those requirements.

The priced venues use constant-product, concentrated-liquidity, StableSwap, or weighted-pool rules. Each design allocates liquidity differently over the price curve. Trade-size comparisons therefore require the full input to pass through the pool's state transition. We reconstruct that state and apply the corresponding pricing rule. Appendix~\ref{app:quoters} presents the pricing equations, parameter recovery, event ordering, validation against realised swaps, and venue coverage. SushiSwap v3 remains in the route panel but outside the price comparison because its historical tick state is unavailable.

\label{sec:setting:support}

Validation on realised swaps establishes accuracy over the trade sizes that executed. Larger hypothetical trades require an additional restriction. We retain each hypothetical leg when its price impact is no greater than 5\%, a threshold calibrated to the realised validation sample and imposed before the direct and vehicle outputs are compared. For leg $\ell$,
% output/exhibits/quoter_support_bounds.jsonl
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:support}
\pi_{\ell}(\Delta_i,t) \;=\; \frac{\widetilde Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,t)-Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,t)}{\widetilde Q_{\ell}(\Delta_i,t)} \;\leq\; \bar{\pi},
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_i}{R_{i,p,t}} \;\leq\; \bar{\pi},
\qquad \bar{\pi} = 0.05,
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde Q_{\ell}$ prices leg $\ell$ at the pool's marginal rate and the second expression is the constant-product special case. The state and size of each leg therefore determine support before we compare the outputs of competing routes.
% output/exhibits/quoter_support_bounds.jsonl

The price-impact condition applies to every pool family. In concentrated-liquidity pools, executable depth depends on the active tick map, so the reserve-ratio expression in Equation~\eqref{eq:support} applies only to constant-product pools. Appendix~\ref{app:support} reports the 932,270-swap calibration sample and the observations excluded by the restriction.

\label{sec:setting:validation}

Vehicle dominance and the counterfactual cost gap answer separate questions. Equation~\eqref{eq:dominance} measures how often an asset is used as an intermediary; Equation~\eqref{eq:gap} compares the outputs of two hypothetical paths at reconstructed settlement state. We report magnitudes using the continuous gap $\Delta C^{D}_{i,o,k,q,t}$. A coefficient on the threshold indicator $D^{\mathrm{g}}$ measures a change in incidence. Converting it to basis points would additionally require the density of the gap at zero. We interpret the gap as a route-choice trade-off only for observations with established quote-time state and venue access.
