# Candidate Framing #1 — "Liquidity Provision Makes (and Unmakes) the Vehicle"

Scope respected throughout: liquidity provision and vehicle currencies in DeFi/AMM markets only. No revival of the CDOM/asset-pricing/speculative-attack leg — I deliberately did **not** reach for Goldstein-Ozdenoren-Yuan (2011) or Ozdenoren-Yuan-Zhang (2023) even though the manifest flags them as usable templates, because both sit on the attack/asset-pricing side of the retired boundary, not the LP side.

**ROUND 2 (2026-08-04, post loop-back — see `03_scoring/round_1_rejection.md`).** Phase 4 built and ran real evidence for both propositions; Phase 5 independently verified it. P1's design cleared computationally but not on its own causal-design gate, and produced one robust contra-finding (disclosed below, not buried). P2's specific empirical implementation (the V2→V3 event study) was found to be confounded by a real, diagnosed 2021 macro-volatility episode coinciding with the launch date — a framing-level design problem, not a fixable bug. Both propositions below are revised to state what the evidence actually, honestly supports, rather than what was originally hypothesized.

## 1. Headline mechanism — the one or two claims this talk makes

**P1 (formation, self-reinforcing — theory, then what the evidence actually shows).** Vehicle-currency status in an AMM market is hypothesized as a liquidity-provision equilibrium: LP capital concentrates around a candidate vehicle's pools (Yuan 2005's benchmark-liquidity logic, theoretical analogy only), which deepens the pool and plausibly lowers price impact on vehicle-routed trades (motivated by, not derived from, Lehár & Parlour's V1/V2-only Prop. 3 — an analogy, since $L_{k,t}$ here is a V3 construction), lowering the cost of indirect routing (echoing Krugman 1980's three-currency mechanism as analogy only), which feeds back into more LP capital — a volume↔depth loop.

**VERIFIED RESULT (Phase 4/5, 2026-08-04).** The exogenous supply-side shock the referee required (Section 1, original) was scoped and found infeasible in the time available (the one clean candidate, the September 2020 UNI liquidity-mining launch, predates this repo's V3-only $L_{k,t}$ construction). What was actually run is the disclosed fallback: a lead-lag/Granger-style panel (token+date FE, Driscoll-Kraay/cluster/block-bootstrap inference), independently verified computationally correct (exact rerun plus two independent from-scratch reimplementations match to 4 decimal places). Of the loop's four legs: **S-on-L (deeper liquidity → more future indirect share) is robust** — correct sign, DK p<0.001 at all 4 horizons, and holds across all 32 cells of the robustness battery (every depth/volatility/period/measure split). **L-on-S is partial** — correct sign, significant at 3 of 4 horizons. **S-on-D is null** — correct sign, never significant. **D-on-L is wrong-signed and robustly so** — significant in the wrong direction at 2 of 4 horizons, and this contradiction persists across nearly every robustness variant (3 of 4), not a fluke of one specification. Honest statement of what this establishes: temporal precedence for the S↔L half of the feedback loop under a design that — by its own admission — cannot rule out a common demand shock driving liquidity, cost, and volume together; and a genuine, unresolved contradiction on the D-on-L leg that must be stated in the talk, not omitted. This is a modest, partially-supported, suggestive finding, not a closed causal loop.

**P2 (persistence and architecture — reframed to what the evidence supports, not the original elasticity claim).** The original proposition — architecture that cheapens LP repositioning makes vehicle status more elastic to stress — was tested via the V2→V3 concentrated-liquidity launch (the cross-chain CCK design was scoped and found infeasible: no multi-chain data or L2 fetch path exists in this repo, not closeable before the deadline; Java confirmed within-chain is the intended design, not a tolerated fallback, with chronological sample splits as the robustness check). **VERIFIED RESULT (Phase 4/5, 2026-08-04):** the elasticity test came back null (no significant day-to-day responsiveness in route share, vehicle HHI, or direct depth, either window), and independent verification found this is not a bug — the headline and placebo estimators share identical code, ruling out a selective error — but a genuine, diagnosed confound: the true V3 launch date (2021-05-05) sits almost exactly on top of a real, well-documented Jan–May 2021 market-wide volatility episode, and the panel carries enough of that generic calendar-time variation that this specific design cannot distinguish a true architecture effect from it (placebo dates produce effects as strong as the real one; the joint pretrend Wald test rejects flat pretrends despite a flat linear slope — the same signature a market-wide step-shock would produce). **This means the elasticity claim cannot be supported or refuted by this design in this sample — it is not being claimed either way.** What the same event study robustly does show: **direct-pool depth improves significantly post-V3** (p<0.001, both windows) while vehicle-candidate route share and vehicle HHI do not move — a clean, honest, capital-efficiency finding, distinct from a vehicle-currency-formation finding, consistent with RQ4's own pre-existing decision rule ("better execution with unchanged topology is a capital-efficiency result without a vehicle-currency result"). **P2's actual contribution, revised:** architecture that improves execution quality does not automatically change which asset serves as the vehicle — capital efficiency and vehicle-currency status are separable margins, and this within-chain design cleanly shows the former moving without the latter.

Both propositions are stated purely in liquidity-provision terms — capital allocation, pool depth, price impact, repositioning cost — with no invocation of invoicing, reserve-currency, or asset-pricing channels.

- Lehár & Parlour (2024) — cite *only* for the narrow claim that constant-product (V1/V2) AMM pool size rises in noise trading and falls in volatility/informed-trading probability, and that under bounded conditions AMM price impact can be lower and less volatile than a comparable LOB's, tested only in their own 43-pair Binance comparison — not a general "AMMs beat order books" claim, not V3.
- Klein, Kozhan, Viswanath-Natraj & Wang (2026) — cite *only* for: net LP minting/burning near price has statistically significant short-horizon return-predictive content in one ETH/USDC pair (May 2021–Jul 2022), concentrated in the low-fee pool, via a recursive SVAR (predictive, not causal) — used here only to motivate that LP capital allocation is strategic/information-responsive, not passive, which is a load-bearing assumption behind P1's feedback loop.
- Caparros, Chaudhary & Klein (2024) — cite *only* for their own result (gas costs causally raise LP repositioning intensity/precision and liquidity concentration, professional not retail LPs, small-trade slippage falls on cheap chains) as the identification template for P2, not as evidence already obtained for this paper's vehicle-candidate set.
- Krugman (1980) — cite *only* for the specific three-country transaction-cost vehicle-selection mechanism and the volume→lower-cost→more-volume feedback loop under an endogenized cost function, used purely as a motivating theoretical analogy for what P1's regression is testing. Must not be presented as extending beyond three currencies, must not be treated as proving persistence-after-dominance-fades (Krugman explicitly disclaims the dynamic analysis), must not be treated as empirical.
- Yuan (2005) — cite *only* for the theoretical analogy that a liquid benchmark/common-factor asset can raise liquidity and price informativeness economy-wide, conditional on the specific assumption that market makers observe order flow across markets — flag explicitly as pure theory, not DeFi, not empirical, and conditional (not an assumption-free law).
- Somogyi (2026) — cite *only* as the TradFi-FX motivating comparator: ~13% of interdealer USD-pair volume is vehicle-currency (price-impact-reducing, not spread-reducing) routing, rising to 25–38% in majors, from a quasi-experimental holiday design — but note the C1–C3 panel-regression evidence behind the mechanism is explicitly non-causal and modest relative to an ~18–20% overall R² dominated by fixed effects (a caveat only one of two independent reads caught, so treat as live, not settled) — used to open the talk, not as DeFi evidence.
- Klein & Song (2021) and He, Khorrami & Song (2022) — cite *only* as brief TradFi structural analogies (liquidity commonality across venues; intermediary-capacity/inventory constraints driving liquidity-commonality patterns) — both explicitly disclaim any DeFi/crypto extension in their own safe-to-cite statements, so use only as "this pattern has a TradFi precedent," never as evidence for this paper's claims.

## 3. Narrative arc for a ~20-minute talk

Calibrated against the Phase 0-prime exemplars: classical-Beamer skeleton (title → outline → content under persistent section headers → conclusions → references) given the JFE-style target and mixed audience, but keeping the crypto-talk's visual discipline (one exhibit per slide, no dense equations live).

1. **Motivation (≈2 min).** Open with the FX vehicle-currency stylized fact (Somogyi 2026's ~13%/25–38% figures, from the paper's quasi-experimental holiday design — kept separate from the paper's own non-causal C1–C3 mechanism test) and pivot: "In FX, vehicle status is presumed to trace to fundamental dollar demand. In DeFi, transaction-level LP data lets us ask directly whether it instead traces to liquidity provision itself."
2. **The question, stated honestly (≈2 min).** State both propositions and, on the same slide, name the two constraints the evidence has to be read against: no true exogenous shock for P1 (disclosed fallback), and a diagnosed 2021 confound that blocks P2's original elasticity claim in this sample. Setting this expectation before the data appears is what keeps the honest-null results from reading as a bait-and-switch later in the talk.
3. **Mechanism scaffold (≈3 min).** One slide each for the Krugman feedback loop, Lehár & Parlour's V1/V2 analogy, and the Yuan benchmark-liquidity analogy — labeled as borrowed theory/motivation throughout.
4. **Headline exhibit — formation, mixed evidence (≈5 min).** The lead-lag panel result: lead with S-on-L (robust across 32/32 robustness cells), state L-on-S as partial support, and name D-on-L's robust wrong sign directly on the slide — "one leg of the loop runs backward, and it survives every check we ran." This is the moment the talk either earns or loses credibility with a referee-literate audience; do not bury the contra-finding in a footnote.
5. **Architecture: capital efficiency, not vehicle rotation (≈4 min).** The V2→V3 event study, reframed: lead with the significant direct-pool-depth result (capital efficiency, real and robust), then the honest null on route share/vehicle HHI, then the placebo/pretrend diagnostics that explain *why* the elasticity claim isn't testable here (the 2021 alt-season confound) — presented as a methodological finding in its own right, not an apology.
6. **Recap and contribution (≈2 min).** The honest version: this paper shows liquidity depth and route-volume are linked in the predicted direction (with an unresolved contra-result), and that architecture can improve execution quality without changing which asset serves as the vehicle — a real distinction between two previously conflated margins, established with disclosed, verified diagnostics rather than a clean two-proposition story.
7. **Backup budget:** roughly matched to core content — full regression tables for all four P1 relationships and the full P2 robustness battery (placebo, pretrend, volatility split, version construction), the D-on-L robustness detail for anyone who pushes on it, references slide(s) at full density.
