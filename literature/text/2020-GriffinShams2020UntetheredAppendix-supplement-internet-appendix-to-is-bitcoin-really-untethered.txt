I. Identifying and Verifying the Wallet Labeling Procedure
This appendix explains our labeling and veriﬁcation algorithm for wallets
on Tether blockchain and their corresponding clusters of wallets on the Bitcoin
blockchain.
A. Identifying Exchanges
For the Tether Blockchain, the identities of major Tether entities are identiﬁed
based on the Tether rich list reported by Tether Limited.1 At each point in time, the
rich list reports the top-50 wallets in terms of balance and the exchange identity
that the rich wallets belong to. Because the list changes over time, we go back
through Internet Archive and collect the identity of any known wallet in the top-
50 addresses at any point in time.
For the Bitcoin blockchain, after we cluster wallets that are likely to be con-
trolled by the same entities based on the algorithm explained in Section II.B, we
assign valid labels to major clusters using extensive public data on the identities
of representative wallet addresses within a given cluster. We then verify the ac-
curacy of these labels by matching and correlating the ﬂow of Bitcoin between
these labeled clusters with the ﬂow of Tether between corresponding Tether play-
ers whose identities are extracted from the Tether rich list. We explain these two
steps in detail below.
We extensively search public forums such as Reddit and BitcoinTalk for the
wallets associated with our major exchanges. For example, we ﬁnd several wallet
addresses that are associated with Bitﬁnex. When we match these wallets with our
clusters, we ﬁnd that all of these wallets belong to the same cluster, so we label
that cluster including all of these wallet addresses as Bitﬁnex. Following this pro-
cedure, we are able to label most major exchanges. Note that the readers can label
these exchanges by ﬁnding the identity of any small subset of the wallets in their
associated cluster and would not need all of the wallet addresses. Nevertheless, to
make the procedure replicable and veriﬁable for future research, in Internet Ap-
pendix Section II we provide a list of representative Bitcoin addresses that can be
matched with the clustered wallets to identify major exchanges.
B. Cross-Verifying Tether and Bitcoin Flows
Next, to verify our labeling accuracy, we compare the correlation and magni-
tude of the Bitcoin ﬂows between major exchanges labeled based on our algorithm
with the Tether ﬂows between exchanges labeled based on the Tether rich list. If
Tether is used mainly to purchase Bitcoin, we should see a correlated ﬂow mim-
icking the exchange of Tether for Bitcoin across the two blockchains. We proceed
as follows.

C. Cross-Verifying Tether Flows with Exchange Volume
As a further check on the relation between Tether and exchange transactions,
we examine the three-hour moving average inﬂow of Tether to Poloniex and Bit-
trex and the ofﬁcial reported exchange volume. Figure IA14 shows that the in-
ﬂow of Tether to Poloniex is highly correlated with the Poloniex exchange vol-
ume for the Bitcoin-Tether pair. When the exchange volume on Poloniex is re-
gressed on the inﬂow of Tether to Poloneix, the adjusted R2 is 0.30 as shown in
the top-left panel of Figure IA14. (The R2 is 0.48 using daily data.) We next
estimate placebo regressions with the same explanatory variable (Tether inﬂow to
Poloniex) but we use Binance exchange volume as the dependent variable. The
second panel shows that the adjusted R2 is only 0.02. Similarly, the other panels
show that while there is a general positive relationship, the adjustedR2s are signif-
icantly smaller and close to zero when we replace the exchange trading volume of
Poloniex and Bittrex with that of other Tether exchanges. TheR2s from regressing
Binance, HitBTC, Huobi, Kraken, and OKEx exchange volume on Tether inﬂow
to Poloniex are 0.02, 0.01, 0.00, 0.00, and 0.00, respectively. The last panel shows
that even the R2 from regressing Bittrex volume on Tether inﬂow to Poloniex is
very small at 0.01.
For Bittrex, regressing Bittrex volume on Bittrex Tether inﬂow yields an ad-
justedR2 of 0.23 (0.48 when using daily data). However, the adjusted R2s from
the placebo regressions of Binance, HitBTC, Huobi, Kraken, and OKEx exchange
volume on Bittrex Tether inﬂow are close to zero at 0.02, 0.05, 0.00, 0.00, and
0.00, respectively. The R2 from regressing Poloniex volume on Tether inﬂow to
Bittrex is 0.06. The results demonstrate that the strong linear relation is present
only when we match Poloniex and Bittrex ﬂows to their respective exchanges and
thus is not simply due to time-varying volatility or overall trading volume.

III. Timing and Characteristics of Currency Movements
In this appendix, we examine the timing and characteristics of the ﬂow of coins
between Bitﬁnex and other exchanges. First, we examine the timing of the ﬂow
relative to the time when Tether is printed. As described in Figure 1, the transfer of
Tether from Tether authorizer (account labeled 3MbY) to Tether treasuries (1NTM
and 3BbD), all colored in red, is referred to as Tether “authorization.” There are
cases where Tether moves quickly in and out of Bitﬁnex right after authorization,
but it is also common to see a delay of a few days between Tether authorization
and the ﬂow of coins from Bitﬁnex to other Tether exchanges, mainly Poloniex
and Bittrex. For example, 50 million Tether was printed on December 20, 2017
and was subsequently moved to Bitﬁnex the following day, increasing the Bitﬁnex
net balance of Tether by almost 50 million.3 However, it took almost four days for
this amount to be fully moved out of Bitﬁnex.
To better understand the timing of the Tether ﬂows, we examine inﬂows of
Tether to Bitﬁnex as well as outﬂows of Tether from Bitﬁnex to the two exchanges
in response to printing of Tether by Tether Limited. Here we estimate a V AR
model with ﬁve lags and examine the impulse response functions, which have
been used extensively to examine the ﬂows of capital between countries (Froot,
O’Connell, and Seasholes (2001)). The impulse response of Tether ﬂows shows
the response of ﬂows to a one-standard-deviation shock to printing of Tether. The
regression used to estimate the V AR model is
yt =c +A1yt−1 +A2yt−2 +... +A5yt−5 +εt, (IA8)
where yt is a 2-by-1 vector of Tether issuance and Tether ﬂow, Ai is a time-
invariant 2-by-2 coefﬁcient matrix, c is a 2-by-1 vector of constants, and εt is
a 2-by-1 vector of error terms.
Panel A of Figure 5 indicates that the inﬂow to Bitﬁnex increases signiﬁcantly
after Tether is printed at Tether Limited, but not all of the Tether ﬂows into Bitﬁnex
immediately. On average, it can take between three and four days for most of
the Tether to move to Bitﬁnex. Similarly, Panel B shows that the ﬂow of Tether
from Bitﬁnex to the other exchanges increases on the day of Tether authorization,
though not as much as the inﬂow to Bitﬁnex in Panel A. Even after three days,
ﬂows are still moving out from Bitﬁnex to the other exchanges. Throughout the
paper we use this delay in the ﬂow of Tether to formally test how Bitcoin prices
are affected when there is an oversupply of Tether in the system.
