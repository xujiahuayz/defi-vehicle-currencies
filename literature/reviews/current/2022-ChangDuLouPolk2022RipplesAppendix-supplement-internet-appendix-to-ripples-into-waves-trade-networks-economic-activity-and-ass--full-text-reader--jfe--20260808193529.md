### Chang, Du, Lou, and Polk (2022), Internet Appendix to “Ripples into Waves”

- Status: Complete full-text independent second read of the 20-page saved supplement, including the unnumbered cover, internal pp. 1–19, all three substantive sections, Tables A1–A7, Figures A1–A2, equations, notes, and captions.
- Roles: Independent full-text reader; claim verifier; supplementary-methods and JFE research-communication exemplar.
- Source: Jeffery (Jinfan) Chang, Huancheng Du, Dong Lou, and Christopher Polk, “Internet Appendix to Ripples into Waves: Trade Networks, Economic Activity, and Asset Prices,” saved as 2022-ChangDuLouPolk2022RipplesAppendix-supplement-internet-appendix-to-ripples-into-waves-trade-networks-economic-activity-and-ass.pdf, 20 PDF pages (cover plus 19 numbered pages).
- Version: Internet appendix bearing “First Draft: March 2018” and “This Draft: June 2021” on PDF p. 1; the saved filename associates it with the 2022 article, but this artifact itself is the June 2021 supplement rather than a self-contained typeset journal article.
- Uses: Supports verification of the paper’s variance-decomposition mechanism comparison at Section I and Eq. (1), internal pp. 1–2, and Figure A1, internal p. 18; its trade-linked shock-transmission and causal-language claims at Section II and Tables A3–A4, internal pp. 3–6 and 12–13; return-predictability robustness at Table A2, internal pp. 10–11; global-volatility prediction at Section III, Table A5, and Figure A2, internal pp. 6, 14, and 19; and the limits of real-activity evidence at Table A7, internal pp. 16–17.
- Scientific: The supplement asks whether sovereign-CDS comovement reflects granular-country volatility or propagation through trade links, whether destination-country shocks transmit causally to exporters, and whether SCDS returns predict asset-market and real outcomes. Section I decomposes export-weighted global monthly SCDS-return variance into own-country variance and pairwise covariance components, reporting that covariance supplies 92% of global variance and that regional export concentration explains over 60% of regional direct-component shares (Eq. (1), internal pp. 1–2; Figure A1, internal p. 18); bilateral trade intensity predicts pairwise SCDS correlation with t = 2.91, although this remains associational (internal p. 2). Section II residualizes weekly country SCDS returns against the export-weighted global return, estimates pair/year two-country structural VARs by splitting weeks into high- and low-volatility regimes, and relates off-diagonal transmission coefficients to trade (internal pp. 3–5). Table A3 finds export share predicts transmission—0.4451 percentage points in the controlled specification, t = 3.52—whereas import share does not, using 2,442 G20 pair-year observations with pair and year fixed effects and two-way clustered inference (internal p. 12). The composite-destination design finds larger transmission for vulnerable and eigenvector-central countries, with full-sample coefficients 1.454 (t = 2.59) and 1.885 (t = 3.32), respectively (Table A4, internal p. 13). These patterns discriminate trade propagation from undifferentiated common exposure more credibly than correlations alone, but the appendix’s “causal evidence” conclusion is stronger than its disclosed identification defense: parameter stability across volatility regimes is stated, while structural-shock orthogonality, admissibility of regime assignment, relative variance-shift conditions, weak-identification diagnostics, and tests of regime invariance are not reported (Section II.A, internal p. 4; causal statement, internal p. 6). Table A2 documents a monthly destination-news long-short SCDS alpha of 0.21% (t = 2.64), robust to market adjustment, volatility scaling, and seven exclusions (internal pp. 10–11), supporting prediction rather than causal identification. Lagged global SCDS return predicts global equity volatility with coefficients 0.00303 (t = 3.29) and 0.00288 (t = 2.43) after one or six volatility lags (Table A5, internal p. 14), again predictive. Sentiment predicts future equity but not SCDS returns (Table A6, internal p. 15). Table A7 provides limited support for SCDS-based real-outcome forecasting: own SCDS return does not predict next-year export growth, and upstream SCDS return does not significantly predict import growth, although own-country SCDS return does (internal pp. 16–17). Boundary conditions include uneven country start dates, G20 restriction for pairwise ITH, annual estimation from only 52 weekly observations split into two regimes, median-dichotomized vulnerability and centrality, and dependence on sovereign-CDS availability (Tables A1, A3–A4, internal pp. 7–13).
- Structure: The cover identifies the supplement and draft dates (PDF p. 1). Section I introduces the variance decomposition, interprets direct and link components, and connects them to Gabaix-style granularity and Acemoglu-style network propagation (internal pp. 1–2). Section II motivates endogeneity, formulates the simultaneous system and transmission matrix (internal pp. 2–3), then Section II.A explains heteroscedastic identification and regime construction (internal pp. 3–4), Section II.B maps pairwise transmission estimates to export and import shares (internal pp. 4–5), and Section II.C aggregates destinations and studies vulnerability and centrality heterogeneity (internal pp. 5–6). Section III adds global-equity-volatility forecasting evidence (internal p. 6). Table A1 documents country coverage, start dates, indices, and regions (internal pp. 7–9); Table A2 supplies SCDS-return-prediction robustness (internal pp. 10–11); Tables A3–A4 report the two ITH applications (internal pp. 12–13); Table A5 reports global-volatility forecasts (internal p. 14); Table A6 examines sentiment (internal p. 15); Table A7 examines real outcomes (internal pp. 16–17); Figures A1–A2 visualize concentration/variance and lagged-SCDS/global-volatility relations (internal pp. 18–19).
- Depth: Analytical and identification effort is concentrated in Section II’s simultaneous-equation derivation, transmission-matrix interpretation, exact-identification moment count, volatility-regime construction, and pair/country panel designs (internal pp. 3–6). Measurement depth is moderate: export weights, destination portfolios, vulnerability, centrality, controls, sample dates, and standardization are defined, but the appendix does not fully document SCDS vendor construction, liquidity filters, missing-data treatment, credit-rating mapping, or the exact global-return residualization regression (internal pp. 1, 3, 5–9, and table captions). Robustness is broad for portfolio prediction and subgroup stability (Table A2, internal pp. 10–11; Table A4, internal p. 13), but shallow for ITH assumptions and finite-sample reliability. Institutional interpretation is light, centering on exports, credit vulnerability, and investor attention without direct attention measurement (internal pp. 5–6). Tables A6–A7 are empirically substantial but receive no surrounding interpretation in the supplied prose (internal pp. 15–17).
- Breadth: The supplement spans granular volatility, production/trade-network propagation, sovereign-credit and equity pricing, investor attention, sentiment, and real activity; it explicitly contrasts Gabaix (2011) with Acemoglu et al. (2012), follows di Giovanni and coauthors on decomposition, uses Rigobon (2003) for identification, and invokes network-centrality work in Section II.C (internal pp. 1–6). It addresses common exposure through controls, fixed effects, residualization, and heteroscedastic identification, and addresses sample composition through exclusions in Tables A2 and A4. It does not directly test alternative financial, banking, commodity, geopolitical, or global-risk transmission networks; distance, language, and colonial ties appear only in the Section I correlation exercise (internal p. 2), not the reported ITH regressions. Declared and revealed boundaries are sovereign-CDS-covered countries, G20 pairs for Table A3, trade-weighted links, contemporaneous transmission, and predictive—not structural—macro evidence.
- Optics: The supplement inherits a strong mechanism-oriented title but opens directly with variance decomposition rather than an abstract or roadmap (PDF pp. 1–2). The exhibit ladder is coherent: decomposition and regional concentration, structural identification, pairwise transmission, heterogeneity, then financial and real-outcome extensions. Captions are unusually self-contained, defining samples, variables, transformations, fixed effects, weighting, clustering, and significance conventions (internal pp. 10–17). Equations make the transmission object visible, though extraction/rendering is degraded and equations are mostly unnumbered except Eq. (1). Prose is compact and economically interprets magnitudes, especially the Table A3 effect relative to the transmission coefficient’s standard deviation (internal p. 5). Credibility benefits from null import effects, subgroup exclusions, extensive controls, and reported t-statistics, but is weakened by the unqualified causal summary, limited discussion of ITH assumptions, annual estimation from small within-year samples, median splits of continuous heterogeneity, and the absence of a references section in this artifact. Page allocation prioritizes exhibits—13 of 19 numbered pages—consistent with a supplement designed for audit and robustness rather than standalone exposition.

- Implication: The appendix materially strengthens the parent paper’s claim that trade links organize sovereign-risk comovement and predict SCDS returns, and it provides mechanism-discriminating evidence beyond simple correlation. It does not, on its own, close the stronger claim that trade links causally transmit country shocks: that conclusion requires validation or fuller defense of the heteroscedastic-identification assumptions and the precision of pair/year estimates. The real-activity tables also counsel calibration—financial-market propagation is the clear result, whereas destination/upstream SCDS measures do not robustly forecast the corresponding export or import growth outcomes.
