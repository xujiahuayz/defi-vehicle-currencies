- Scientific: The scientific question is why the dollar dominates FX trading despite the smaller US share of underlying economic activity; the proposed mechanism is strategic avoidance of price impact through indirect, dollar-mediated exchange. Appendix A establishes a contingent-demand benchmark in which cross-pair conditioning makes price impact proportional to fundamental covariance and independent of the distribution of initial demands, Theorem A1 and Eqs. (A.6)–(A.12), pp. 42–43. Appendix B derives an uncontingent-market equilibrium in which pairs clear separately, cross-pair price impact is zero, but quantities and diagonal own-pair impacts depend jointly on return covariance, demand covariance, and inference, Theorem B2 and Eqs. (B.9)–(B.16), pp. 46–48. Simulations with 15 traders and three-pair triplets show volume increasing with fundamental demand and its variance, while the effect of return variance is parameter-dependent, Figs. B.1–B.3, pp. 50–53. Empirically, the appendix uses CLS activity from September 1, 2012 through September 30, 2022 across 25 pairs and 15 triplets; interdealer volume, absolute net customer flow, intraday flow variance, realized return variance, and Amihud price impact are the principal measures, Appendix C, pp. 57–58 and Tables D.8–D.9, pp. 77–78. Identification of vehicle trading comes from pair and time fixed-effects event studies around holidays affecting one nondollar country but not the other triplet countries, Eq. (D.1), p. 60. The pooled estimate is a $1.9 billion decline relative to $22.8 billion average daily interdealer volume, interpreted as at least 8%, p. 60 and Fig. D.2, p. 61; hourly timing produces an approximately 7% decline, Fig. D.3 and surrounding text, pp. 60–62; Table D.3 reports an 11.04% volume-weighted mean customer-volume VCT share, p. 65. Uncertainty is shown with 95% confidence intervals and Driscoll–Kraay or heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-robust inference, Figs. D.2–D.4 and Tables D.4, D.8, and D.9. The dominance and elasticity panels are explicitly noncausal, Eq. (D.2) discussion, p. 75: jointly, flow, flow variance, and volatility have standardized coefficients 0.24, 0.19, and 0.21 in Table D.8 col. 8, p. 77, while flow variance predicts lower price impact at −0.43 but return volatility is statistically insignificant in Table D.9, p. 78. Credible alternatives remain holiday-specific reductions in underlying trading or dealer participation, mechanical dependence of Amihud on volume, and latent common liquidity conditions. Time effects, controls, hourly timing, rolling estimates, and the treated-versus-control trend display address but do not eliminate these readings, pp. 60–64. CLS classification also misses look-through on prime-brokered trades and excludes market-maker-to-market-maker and price-taker-to-price-taker transactions, pp. 57–58. Model conclusions are bounded by linear schedules, symmetric risk preferences, normal demand components, three-pair structures, and uniqueness established only for symmetric markets with independent fundamental demands, pp. 45–48. Corollaries 2–3 differentiate while holding the endogenous inference coefficient B fixed, Eqs. (B.21)–(B.26), pp. 55–57, so the claimed general-equilibrium comparative statics require additional justification; moreover, footnote 21’s general assertion that a product of symmetric positive-semidefinite matrices is positive semidefinite is false without commutation, although the locally imposed symmetric case with scalar-diagonal price impact may restore the needed property, p. 54.

- Depth: Analytical effort is concentrated in the equilibrium fixed point, endogenous inference coefficient, simulations, and proposition proofs, pp. 41–57. Identification and empirical robustness receive extensive exhibit coverage through daily and hourly event studies, rolling windows, trend plots, triplet-level regressions, de-seasonalization, and detrending, pp. 60–78. Measurement depth is lighter: Appendix C candidly explains CLS coverage but provides no validation statistics for the network classifier, pp. 57–58; Amihud alternatives are asserted as robust but not tabulated, note 28, p. 77. The proof depth is uneven because fixed-B derivatives are used to support claims about an equilibrium in which B is endogenous, pp. 55–57.
- Breadth: The appendix connects thin-market demand-schedule theory, decentralized exchange, vehicle-currency trading, triangular arbitrage, customer order flow, dealer funding, transaction costs, and dominant-currency finance; the relevant literatures appear throughout pp. 41–58, 60, 75–80. It considers contingent and uncontingent designs, symmetric and nonsymmetric equilibria, means, variances, covariances, intraday and intertemporal variation, euro dominance, and volume- versus price-impact-based outcomes. Declared boundaries include symmetric risk preferences, Gaussian demand components, sufficient rather than necessary dominance conditions, sign restrictions in Eqs. (B.23) and (B.26), pp. 55–56, and noncausal elasticity regressions, p. 75. Less developed alternatives are endogenous route choice, holiday-driven participation changes, classifier error, and the mechanical volume component of Amihud.
- Optics: The supplement uses a functional inherited title and no abstract, opening immediately with the contingent benchmark on p. 41. Section-purpose paragraphs, theorem statements, equation numbering, and mostly self-contained captions make the exhibit ladder auditable. Page allocation signals priorities clearly: approximately 17 pages of theory and proofs, two pages on CLS measurement, 20 pages of empirical exhibits, and two pages of references. Claim calibration is strongest where the text says “at least 8%,” p. 60, labels the volume regressions noncausal, p. 75, and reports insignificant volatility effects on price impact, p. 78; it is weaker where associative R² comparisons are translated into vehicle motives being “almost equally important” and support for the decentralized model, pp. 76 and 78. Dense matrix notation, the compressed Table D.4, p. 67, inconsistent equilibrium terminology, and occasional index or prose errors impede readability. The appendix is credible as a detailed technical supplement but cannot serve as a JFE publication-style exemplar.

- Implication: The appendix materially strengthens auditability of the mechanism, CLS measurement, holiday design, and reduced-form evidence, but it does not close the published paper’s full-text record or independently recover its 13% headline estimate. Load-bearing use should preserve three qualifications: holiday exclusion is plausible rather than definitive, the dominance panels are descriptive associations, and the proposition proofs do not fully propagate the endogeneity of B. The source should therefore be cited for specific appendix derivations, measurements, and robustness locations, not as unqualified proof that strategic price-impact avoidance uniquely causes dollar dominance.
