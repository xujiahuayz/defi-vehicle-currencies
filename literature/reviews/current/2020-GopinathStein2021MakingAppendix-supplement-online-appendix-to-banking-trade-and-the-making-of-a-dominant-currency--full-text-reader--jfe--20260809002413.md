- Uses: Supports verification of Proposition 2’s safe-asset-demand cutoff (Appendix A.1, p. 2); Proposition 3’s four banking/invoicing regimes (A.2, eqs. 1–4 and Cases 1–4, pp. 2–3); Proposition 4’s feedback-induced threshold reversal and potential multiplicity (A.3, p. 3); Proposition 5’s no-dominant, single-dominant, and dual-dominant currency regions (A.4, eqs. 5–12, pp. 4–6); and asymmetric dollar–euro comparative statics and path dependence (A.5–A.5.2, eqs. 13–14 and Figure A.I, pp. 7–10). It does not support empirical, causal, welfare, or calibrated quantitative claims.
- Scientific: The appendix asks which parameter restrictions sustain dominant-currency equilibria when importer hedging demand, bank funding, trade invoicing, and foreign safe-asset supply interact. A.1 derives the import-demand cutoff above which domestic banks create dollar claims; A.2 separates four regimes ranging from zero dollar intermediation to currency mismatch; A.3 makes imports depend on invoicing and shows how sufficiently strong feedback can reverse cutoff ordering and permit multiplicity; A.4 extends the model to symmetric dollar–euro competition; and A.5 permits asymmetric trade shares and outside asset supplies. The contribution is analytical characterization of equilibrium existence and comparative statics, not estimation or mechanism discrimination using data. There are no observations, measured variables, standard errors, confidence intervals, statistical uncertainty, or empirical identification strategy. The principal alternative reading is therefore important: the results establish that equilibria are sustainable under stated inequalities, not which equilibrium will be selected, how probable it is, or whether the mechanism caused historical dollar dominance.
- Structure: Page 1 is the title leaf. A.1 proves Proposition 2 using importer demand, market clearing, and a two-scenario cutoff (p. 2). A.2 states the core equilibrium system and proves Proposition 3 through four exhaustive ranges of import demand (pp. 2–3). A.3 introduces endogenous import demand and proves Proposition 4’s cutoff ordering result (p. 3). A.4 derives the two-currency importer problem and Proposition 5’s three symmetric equilibrium types (pp. 4–6). A.5 generalizes to asymmetric dollar and euro trade shares and safe-asset supplies (pp. 7–8). A.5.1 uses Figure A.I(a) for trade-share paths (pp. 9–10), and A.5.2 interprets Figure A.I(b) for asymmetric safe-asset supply (p. 10). There is no appendix conclusion or reference list.
- Depth: Analytical effort is concentrated in equilibrium accounting, first-order conditions, threshold derivations, and case-by-case sustainability restrictions (pp. 2–8). Institutional depth is light: Eurozone integration and fragmented European bond markets motivate parameter movements but are not modeled institutionally (pp. 9–10). Measurement and identification are absent because this is a theory supplement. Robustness consists mainly of moving from one currency to symmetric two-currency competition and then to asymmetric trade shares and asset supplies (A.4–A.5); equilibrium selection, transition dynamics, and sensitivity to complete parameter calibrations remain light. The “computational details” section supplies inequalities and selected paths but no parameter table, code reference, or algorithm sufficient to reproduce Figure A.I from this appendix alone (pp. 7–10).
- Breadth: The artifact covers importer portfolio demand, bank balance-sheet currency mismatch, endogenous invoicing, safe-asset supply, dollar–euro competition, multiplicity, and path dependence. It treats both exogenous trade-share asymmetry and exogenous safe-asset-supply asymmetry. It does not engage alternative literatures within the appendix, consider more than two candidate currencies, endogenize public safe-asset issuance or market liquidity, model explicit equilibrium-selection dynamics, quantify welfare, or test the mechanism against historical or microeconomic evidence. Novel digital currencies and other broader applications are outside this supplement.
- Optics: The title page clearly labels the artifact as an Online Appendix, after which it moves directly into proofs without an abstract or roadmap. The exhibit ladder is logical—single-currency proofs, endogenous feedback, two-currency extension, asymmetric cases, then Figure A.I—but equation numbering is selective and the lone caption, “Sustainable Equilibria Regions,” does not report parameter values or fully decode colors and regions (p. 9). Most claims are appropriately framed as equilibrium conditions, although “more likely to emerge” on p. 10 exceeds what a deterministic sustainability-region exercise establishes. Audit friction comes from notation inconsistencies and apparent typographical errors, including repeated currency subscripts in Case 1 headings (pp. 4, 7), variation between \(E\) and \(\bar E\) (pp. 3, 6, 8), “Path 1” used for two different trajectories (pp. 9–10), and several prose errors. Roughly seven pages are algebra and three are title/interpretation, making the artifact dense but appropriately proof-centered.
