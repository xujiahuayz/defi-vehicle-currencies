- Scientific: The appendix develops evidence for the proposition that dealer-intermediated FX liquidity is elastic in unconstrained states but becomes less elastic when major dealers’ constraints tighten. Its data combine hourly CLS aggregate volume and customer order flow, with no trader identities or execution prices, across 15 currency triplets from 1 November 2011 through 30 September 2022 (pp. 45, 50, 55–56); dealer states are assembled from Euromoney top-ten rankings and bank VaR, debt-funding costs, VaR breaches, leverage, CDS, and CIP data (pp. 45–47). VLOOP is the triangular-law-of-one-price component and is interpreted as a lower bound on intermediary shadow costs, while positive TCOST aggregates round-trip bid-ask costs and, under no arbitrage, supplies an upper bound (Eqs. (B.1)–(B.2) and Fig. B.1, pp. 48–49). Identification is observational and state-dependent rather than based on exogenous causal variation: LSTAR models estimate different volume slopes under a common lagged dealer-constraint regime, generally with currency-triplet and daily fixed effects, while the SVAR labels supply and demand shocks through two-period sign restrictions, a weak Normal-Wishart prior, lag length two, and 500-by-500 parameter/rotation draws (p. 66). Triplet-level estimates show significantly negative constrained-minus-unconstrained volume slopes for only 5 of 15 VLOOP triplets and 6 of 15 TCOST triplets, alongside some significant positive differences and only small adjusted-R² gains over OLS (Tables C.1–C.2, pp. 55–56), so cross-sectional support is heterogeneous. Panel robustness is stronger: counterparty splits yield VLOOP differences around −0.07 for non-bank customers and −0.12 to −0.13 for banks, and TCOST differences around −0.02 and −0.06 respectively (Table C.3, p. 58); market-share weighting, alternative lags, London hours, and euro triplets broadly preserve reduced elasticity, although euro-triplet VLOOP differences of about −0.09 to −0.10 are statistically insignificant (Table C.6 and Figs. C.4–C.5, pp. 61, 63; Tables C.8–C.9, pp. 64–65). VIX, gold, TED, and LIBOR-OIS placebos do not produce the same negative state dependence (Table C.7, p. 62), which discriminates the dealer-state interpretation but does not establish causality. Credible alternatives remain: DCM may proxy for stress correlated with liquidity demand; the no-time-fixed-effect LSTAR differences are insignificant in Table C.5 (p. 60); non-bank-liquidity-provider substitution is inferred from a 2017 sample split rather than observed identities (p. 57 and Table C.4, p. 59); and spread validation is uneven despite near-unit bid/ask-level correlations, with bid-ask-spread correlations as low as 0.001 and materially divergent average spreads for several pairs (Table E.2, p. 71).
- Structure: Internal p. 44 is a companion title page. Appendix A defines CLS coverage, participant categories, top-ten dealer selection, and each constraint variable, then documents dealer-market-share history and rankings (pp. 45–47). Appendix B derives VLOOP and TCOST, illustrates triangular trading, and extends the measures to forwards and swaps (pp. 48–50). Appendix C supplies principal-component and fitted-regime exhibits before concentrating on LSTAR robustness across individual triplets, counterparties, subsamples, fixed effects, market-share weights, placebo states, lag lengths, London hours, and euro triplets (pp. 51–65). Appendix D explains Bayesian sign-restricted SVAR estimation and then presents rolling-correlation evidence across constraint states and the Covid-19 episode (pp. 66–69). Appendix E provides summary statistics, EBS–Olsen quote comparisons, unconditional and conditional correlations, forwards/swaps extensions, volatility conditioning, and volume persistence (pp. 70–80). Appendix-only references occupy p. 81.
- Depth: Analytical depth is greatest in liquidity-cost construction and nonlinear-state robustness: Eqs. (B.1)–(B.2) tie prices and spreads directly to the two outcomes (pp. 48–49), while fifteen pages test LSTAR behavior along multiple margins (pp. 51–65). Institutional and measurement discussion is substantial for CLS classification, bank selection, constraint-variable provenance, and frequency mismatches (pp. 45–47), but trader anonymity prevents direct dealer or non-bank-provider attribution. Identification depth is moderate: the appendix documents LSTAR specifications, robust standard errors, fixed effects, state-variable placebos, lag sensitivity, and the SVAR draw algorithm, yet provides no exogenous instrument and no appendix-level posterior SVAR estimates or sensitivity to alternative sign horizons/priors. Robustness is broad but some exercises expose fragility, particularly heterogeneous triplet estimates (pp. 55–56), insignificant LSTAR differences without time fixed effects (p. 60), and insignificant euro-triplet VLOOP differences (p. 65).
- Breadth: The evidence spans spot, one-week and one-month forwards and swaps; inter-bank and customer-bank volume; dollar and euro triplets; 2011–2016 versus 2017–2022 subsamples; full-day versus London-hour aggregation; equal- versus market-share-weighted bank constraints; dealer-specific versus market-wide state variables; and lags through 90 days (pp. 49–50, 57–65, 77–78). It addresses measurement-source disagreement with an EBS–Olsen comparison (pp. 71–74), liquidity-demand confounding through market-state placebos (pp. 58, 62), volatility as an alternative activity driver (p. 79), and persistent volume through autocorrelations (p. 80). Declared boundaries include aggregate CLS data without identities or prices (p. 45), exclusion of privately held non-bank providers from the bank constraint index (pp. 45, 47), incomplete bank coverage for VaR and breaches (pp. 45–46), VaR-breach availability only from 2015 (pp. 46, 50), and inability to observe non-bank providers directly (footnote 24, p. 57).

- Implication: The appendix materially supports the measurement transparency and robustness of a state-dependent dealer-liquidity mechanism, especially the claim that volume–liquidity-cost comovement weakens in high-constraint states. It supports mechanism discrimination and predictive state dependence, not an unqualified causal claim that dealer constraints generate the observed liquidity changes. Any project use should preserve the heterogeneous triplet evidence, dependence on time fixed effects, inability to identify traders, and suggestive status of non-bank substitution; the main article must be read before closing headline claims or the paper’s source set.
