### Krugman (1979/1980), Vehicle Currencies and the Structure of International Exchange

- Status: The independent reader evaluated the complete published article without the existing card, bibliography or project characterization. The official NBER working paper was then materialized and both versions were read sequentially in full for source reconciliation. The verdict below records only claims verified in the two durable extracts.
- Roles: Independent second read of the central theory source; material working-paper-to-journal reconciliation; audit of every live manuscript and deck attribution.
- Source: Paul R. Krugman, “Vehicle Currencies and the Structure of International Exchange,” NBER Working Paper 333, April 1979, 36 PDF pages, 298,862 bytes, final Journal of Money, Credit and Banking 12(3), August 1980, pp. 513–526, DOI 10.2307/1991725.
- Version: The NBER artifact is the official superseded working paper, not an appendix. The final article retains the three-country flow model, partial-versus-total indirect-exchange taxonomy, clockwisdom, volume-dependent cost mechanism, conditional multiplicity, dominant-country examples and fixed-cost proof appendix. It materially removes the working paper's formal dynamics, so working-paper claims about a developed dynamic model cannot be attributed to the journal version.
- Companions: No separate appendix, supplement, correction, corrigendum, data or code package was disclosed by the final article, DOI/Crossref, JSTOR or NBER records. The proof appendix is embedded in both versions. The working paper is retained in the source family because it changes scientific depth and the status of the dynamics claim.
- Uses: The source supports current transaction volume lowering proportional brokerage cost, vehicle use expanding the two bilateral markets in which the vehicle trades, conditional multiple equilibria, payment-importance bounds, qualitative persistence and abrupt displacement. It does not define continuous dominance, pair a realised trade with an executable alternative at the same state and size, establish that usual data prohibit such a measure, model provider capital or returns, or derive an AMM slow-capital channel.
- Scientific: With zero transaction costs, the payment structure is determined but the exchange structure is not, and non-bilateral payment balance requires some indirect exchange. With fixed proportional costs, the two cheaper bilateral markets select the vehicle. Partial indirect exchange arises when retaining value over two vehicle legs is below direct retention; only the payment imbalance is routed indirectly and exchange-rate clockwisdom compensates the higher brokerage cost until marginal traders are indifferent. Total indirect exchange arises when the two vehicle legs retain more value and the direct market disappears. Endogenizing each market's proportional cost as the same decreasing function of that market's current transaction volume creates feedback: vehicle use expands two markets, lowers their costs and can sustain the vehicle choice. Symmetric payment structures permit multiple vehicle equilibria; a sufficiently important country can make its currency uniquely feasible.
- Strict-domination boundary: The paper contains no agent knowingly choosing a route that delivers less currency than an available executable alternative after exchange rates and costs. In the partial-exchange case that can look closest to the project's state, the two-leg route has higher brokerage costs but clockwisdom gives its user a better exchange rate that exactly offsets them. The project's strict realised-route domination object is scientifically interesting, but it is a new empirical extension, not the missing state on which Krugman's theory rests.
- Working-paper dynamics: Working-paper Section IV, manuscript pages 23–30, specifies perceived bilateral costs that adjust toward actual costs in equations (11)–(13). It solves two special cases: competition between alpha and beta as vehicle under partial indirect exchange, and partial versus total indirect exchange with alpha fixed as vehicle. Figures 9–10 show two locally stable equilibria, threshold-triggered displacement and path dependence after a temporary payments shift. The final article deletes the equations, both figures and the section, and says a definitive dynamic analysis lies beyond scope. It keeps only the qualitative conclusions that history matters, established structures can persist, and change may be “catastrophic.” The working paper therefore supports a specific adaptive-cost special case, while the published source supports qualitative persistence and abrupt change only.
- Other version differences: The working paper includes a graphical no-cost exchange-rate determination and explicit payment functions of exchange rates and exogenous variables; the final compresses both. The working paper offers two possible microfoundations for economies of market size, dealer working balances that diversify with order flow and fixed physical exchange resources, but leaves industrial organization unresolved. The final removes those stories, adds a contemporary market quotation, and expressly distinguishes economies of market size from economies of a large individual transaction. The final also adds an opening distinction among store-of-value, unit-of-account and medium-of-exchange roles, a sharper three-contribution ladder, the result that partial indirect exchange minimizes the cost of necessary imbalance routing, and the warning that clockwisdom does not generalize to more than three currencies.
- Structure: The working paper has an introduction, four substantive sections, notes, a proof appendix and references. The final has an introduction, three substantive sections, the proof appendix and references. The deleted dynamics section is the only material scientific contraction; most other changes compress exposition, remove speculative microfoundations and sharpen the contribution ladder.
- Depth: The published article is deep on the special three-country equilibrium and compact on everything else. The working paper adds a genuine dynamic system, but it remains a special-case adaptive process with exogenous payments and technology, no estimated transition duration and no general nonlinear solution. Neither version contains data, empirical identification, more than three currencies, routing technology, aggregators, provider capital, welfare or a continuous dominance measure.
- Breadth: Both versions connect payment imbalances, indirect exchange, triangular arbitrage, economies of market size, multiple equilibria, international media of exchange and historical persistence. The working paper adds loss-of-confidence and working-balance interpretations in notes. Neither version supplies the project's market integration, execution-state, search-efficiency, routing-complexity or LP-return objects.
- Optics: The final's fourteen journal pages are the appropriate cited artifact. Its opening role distinction and three-contribution ladder are stronger than the working paper's summary, and its deletion of an underdeveloped dynamics section improves claim calibration. The paper should emulate that calibration: a result removed from the journal version cannot be smuggled back through an index summary, and a new exact-state test should be sold as the project's extension instead of attributed to the canonical source.
- Locations: Final pages 513–514 state the role and contributions; pages 514–519 build exchange structure, fixed costs, clockwisdom and indifference; pages 520–523 contain equations (6)–(10), conditional multiplicity, dominant-country examples and qualitative persistence; pages 523–526 contain the proof appendix. Working-paper manuscript pages 15–18 supply the omitted market-size stories; pages 23–30 contain Section IV, equations (11)–(13), Figures 9–10 and the dynamic interpretation; manuscript pages 30–31 state tipping, persistence after temporary dominance and the loss-of-confidence extension.
- Live-use verdict: The introduction's basic volume-cost feedback passes. Every statement that the exact-state strictly dominated route is the state Krugman's theory rests on, or that Krugman establishes an empirical identification limit around that state, requires correction. The slow-capital account must be labeled the project's extension because Krugman's primitive is current transaction volume, not deposited liquidity. The rent-incidence paragraph must separate Krugman's low-cost-market mechanism from the project's provider-return evidence. The conclusion and identification slide must visually distinguish the source's mechanism from the new test. “Lowest-cost route” is imprecise: the paper is about proportional brokerage costs in bilateral currency markets, with cross-rates adjusting route indifference.
- Implication: Retain Krugman as the central mechanism source under exact boundaries. Cite the published article for volume-cost feedback, payment-structure limits, conditional multiplicity and qualitative persistence. Cite the working paper only when the adaptive perceived-cost dynamics themselves matter, and mark it as superseded. Present continuous dominance, normalized excess use, matched route domination, LP capital and return incidence as project contributions.
